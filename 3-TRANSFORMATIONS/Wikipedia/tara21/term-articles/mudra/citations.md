# Citations — mudra (ཕྱག་རྒྱ)

**Mode B revision.** This citations.md was rewritten in place from the v1 article's own
citations.md (`wiki-article-from-claims`, dated 2026-08-14) following the
`wiki-article-from-claims-v2` skill's Mode B procedure. No claim ID, quotation, or
`Cite:` target was re-derived from `2-RAILS/Claims/` or the raw tree-guided files — every
claim ID below was already present in the v1 citations.md and is treated as settled
ground truth. The only file-content lookups performed were **frontmatter-only** reads of
the nine raw claims files, to resolve `author_in_use` for Rule 17 (see "Author-naming
corrections" below).

Scope: unchanged from v1 — this article covers ཕྱག་རྒྱ (mudrā) as the ritual
hand-gesture attested in this corpus, principally the two-handed gesture-pair of homage 9
(a Three-Jewels-symbolizing mudrā on the left hand, a supreme-giving mudrā on the right),
its doctrinal reasoning, its divergent readings (Tāranātha's samaya-mudrā tradition; the
"hidden meaning" completion-stage readings; the light-source divergence), its role paired
with mantra as protective "armor," and its reuse as a recurring iconographic template
across other deity-forms. It continues to treat ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (mahāmudrā) only as a
named, out-of-scope homonym. Of the sixteen commentaries that attest ཕྱག་རྒྱ in some
form, this article draws on the same nine as v1; the other seven's attestations remain
resolved-but-not-incorporated (see below).

  say" framing in favor of stating the fact directly, backed by refs. Individual-position
  material (the three explanations of why the mudrā symbolizes the Three Jewels; every
  position in `གཞུང་ལུགས་སོ་སོའི་བཤད་པ།`) keeps inline attribution, now via
  `author_in_use` throughout (see below).

    Gyatso), present in the raw claims file's `author:` field. Both are fixed to carry
