# Citations — mudra (ཕྱག་རྒྱ)

Scope: this article covers ཕྱག་རྒྱ (mudrā) as the ritual hand-gesture attested in this
corpus — principally the two-handed gesture-pair of homage 9 (a Three-Jewels-symbolizing
mudrā on the left hand, a supreme-giving mudrā on the right), its doctrinal reasoning,
its divergent readings (Tāranātha's samaya-mudrā tradition; the "hidden meaning"
completion-stage readings; the light-source divergence), its role paired with mantra as
protective "armor," and its reuse as a recurring iconographic template across other
deity-forms. It deliberately treats ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (mahāmudrā) only as a named,
out-of-scope homonym, per `2-RAILS/Claims/mudra.md`'s own scope note. Of the sixteen
commentaries that attest ཕྱག་རྒྱ in some form, this article draws directly on nine;
the other seven's attestations were resolved and considered but judged redundant with
material already drafted (see "Claims resolved but not incorporated" below).

## Claims resolved but not incorporated

The consolidated page cites material from `dharmabhadra`, `gendun-drub`, `gendun-gyatso`,
`lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `sangye-nyentrul`, and `tenga-tulku` under "Homage 9's
mudrā (Three Jewels)" and "Hand-configuration." All were resolved against their raw
files during drafting but not drawn into the final prose, because each restates the same
thumb-and-ring-finger/three-raised-fingers configuration already established by the three
representative witnesses quoted (yama-sonam, drakpa-gyaltsen, karma-maitri) without
adding a materially new detail — including them would have made the དབྱེ་བ / ངེས་ཚིག
sections a long list of near-duplicate quotations rather than a due-weight backbone (Rule
5 of the wiki-article-from-claims skill). Their attestation counts remain visible on
`2-RAILS/Claims/mudra.md` itself for any reviewer who wants the full sixteen-way tally.

Also not incorporated: the many repetitive appendix instances of the supreme-giving/
Three-Jewels mudrā-pair template already logged on `2-RAILS/Claims/mudra.md`'s own
"Claims reviewed, not separately cited" section (representative palden-sherab and
tsultrim-namdak instances only are used here); and every claim listed under that page's
"Scope note: mahāmudrā" and "Out of scope" headings, which this article's own scope note
excludes on the same grounds.
