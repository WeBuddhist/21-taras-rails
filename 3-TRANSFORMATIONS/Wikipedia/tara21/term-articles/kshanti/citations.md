# Citations — kshanti (བཟོད་པ)

**Revision note (Mode B, 2026-08-20).** This file and `article.md` were revised
in place from the v1 draft (`wiki-article-from-claims`, dated 2026-08-14) to
v2 register, per `4-SYSTEM/Skills/wiki-article-from-claims-v2/SKILL.md`
Procedure — Mode B. No claim ID, quotation content, or `Cite:` target was
re-derived from `2-RAILS/Claims/` or the raw tree-guided files; the sole
exception, per Rule 17, was frontmatter-only lookups of `author` and
`author_in_use` on the raw claims files already listed above, to fix three
`<ref>`/bibliography entries (see *Rule 17 fixes* below). The set of claim
IDs cited is byte-identical to the v1 draft — nothing added, nothing dropped.

Scope: this article covers *kṣānti* (བཟོད་པ, "patience"), one of the six
pāramitās, as attested in the twenty-one-praise commentarial corpus: its
appearance in the root verse's six-pāramitā list, its classification as
antidote to anger, an extended ten-pāramitā placement, two hidden-meaning
(sbas don) readings, an ultimate/non-referential (Madhyamaka) definition, a
threefold subdivision, one commentary's own labeled alternate reading, and —
kept clearly separated, per `2-RAILS/Claims/kshanti.md`'s own scope note — the
doctrinally adjacent but distinct "acceptance of the non-arising of
phenomena" (anutpattika-dharmakṣānti) narrated in Tārā's origin story. Nine of
the sixteen commentaries in the consolidated page are drawn on directly in
this article's prose (the remaining seven are cited on the consolidated page
for the same "six-pāramitā list" consensus facet but were not selected as one
of this article's representative refs — see `2-RAILS/Claims/kshanti.md` for
their citations).

  by more than one commentary is now stated in plain wikivoice — no
  commentator named, no "འགྲེལ་པ་བཅུ་བཞིས" / "འགྲེལ་པ་མང་པོའི་ནང་དུ" /

  body. This affected the lead, ངེས་ཚིག, one sentence of དབྱེ་བ, the whole of
  ཁོང་ཁྲོའི་གཉེན་པོར་བཤད་པ, the whole of སྦས་དོན་གྱི་བཤད་པ, the summary sentence
  in དབྱེ་བ recapping the ten-pāramitā placement, the first sentence of
  མི་སྐྱེ་བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ, and all three sentences of བསྡུས་དོན།. Two spots stay
  attributed by design (Rule 5): the Madhyamaka ultimate-definition reading in
