# Citations — brahma (ཚངས་པ)

Scope: this article covers Brahmā (ཚངས་པ) as an independent deity within the
commentarial tradition on the Twenty-One Homages to Tārā — his recurring
appearance among the worldly gods who worship or serve Tārā, his explicit
identification as "creator of the world," tantric hidden-meaning readings
of his name (elemental, central-channel), his use as an exemplar of worldly
power, three distinct narrative/iconographic episodes, and the "Brahmā-voice"
epithet — as attested by all sixteen commentaries surveyed in
`2-RAILS/Claims/brahma.md`. Not every one of that page's 68 attestations is
individually quoted or paraphrased below (the "worshipper-list" facet alone
carries ~40 near-duplicate attestations of the same underlying fact); the
article draws a representative, due-weight subset per Rule 5 of
`wiki-article-from-claims`, while every commentary that contributed to the
consolidated page is cited at least once in the article.

## Claims used but not quoted

Most claims above entered the article as paraphrase (still individually
`<ref>`-cited). This is deliberate: the "worshipper-list" facet on the
consolidated page carries ~40 near-duplicate attestations of the same
underlying fact (Brahmā named among the worldly gods who worship Tārā), so
the article quotes the canonical fivefold verse once in full
(`anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-3`) and paraphrases the remaining attestations by
commentary, citing each one's own `<ref>`.
