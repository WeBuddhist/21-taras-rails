# Citations — vayu (རླུང་ལྷ)

Scope: this article covers Vāyu (རླུང་ལྷ) as an independent minor deity within the
commentarial tradition on the Twenty-One Homages to Tārā — his recurring appearance
among the worldly gods who worship Tārā in the fivefold homage-verse (consistently
placed as guardian of the northwest), three independent single-commentary
characterizations (an epithet, a cosmogonic function, and a cosmological power), his
place among a fuller set of ten directional guardians summoned into Tārā's service in
two commentaries, and a tantric hidden-meaning identification with the wind element —
as attested by all sixteen commentaries surveyed in `2-RAILS/Claims/vayu.md`. Not
every one of that page's 32 consulted claims is individually quoted or paraphrased
below (the "worshipper-list" facet alone carries 21 near-duplicate attestations of the
same underlying fact); the article draws a representative, due-weight subset per Rule
5 of `wiki-article-from-claims`, while every commentary that contributed to the
consolidated page is cited at least once — in fact at least twice — in the article.

## Claims used but not quoted

Most claims above entered the article as paraphrase (still individually `<ref>`-cited).

near-duplicate attestations of the same underlying fact (Vāyu named among the worldly
gods who worship Tārā, consistently as guardian of the northwest), so the article
quotes the canonical fivefold verse once in full (`yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`) and the
bare directional gloss once (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`), then paraphrases the
remaining attestations by commentary, citing each one's own `<ref>`.
