# Citations — vayu (རླུང་ལྷ)

**Revision note (Mode B, 2026-08-21).** This is a register-only rewrite of the v1
article at the same path (`method: wiki-article-from-claims`, dated 2026-08-14; the
prior version is recoverable via the vault's git backup, not kept as a side copy). No
claim ID, quotation source, or `Cite:` target was re-derived — every claim ID below
already appeared in the v1 `citations.md`. The rewrite: (1) converted the sixteen-way
"worshipper-list" ref pile-up and its duplicate "fivefold-verse" paragraph into a
single wikivoice consensus statement in the lead, capped to 3 representative refs
(`yama-sonam`, `dharmabhadra`, `tenga-tulku`), with the other 12 attesting
commentaries' claim IDs moved to *Full attestation beyond in-article refs* below; (2)
cut the quotation count from 10 to 2 (plus the root-verse quotation in the lead, which
is exempt from the budget) — retaining only the `yama-sonam` epithet quote and the
`taranatha` elemental-mapping quote, both unchanged, character-for-character
substrings of quotations already marked PASS in the v1 file; (3) applied the
punctuation contract (sentence-final shad, paragraph-final double shad, no commas,
punctuation before `<ref>` tags); (4) confirmed every in-prose commentator name against
its raw claims file's `author_in_use` frontmatter value — all seven commentaries named
in-body already had `author_in_use` populated, and it already matched what v1 used in
prose in every case, so no name was changed and no fallback warning is needed.

Scope (unchanged from v1): this article covers Vāyu (རླུང་ལྷ) as an independent minor
deity within the commentarial tradition on the Twenty-One Homages to Tārā — his
recurring appearance among the worldly gods who worship Tārā in the fivefold
homage-verse (consistently placed as guardian of the northwest), three independent
single-commentary characterizations (an epithet, a cosmogonic function, and a
cosmological power), his place among a fuller set of ten directional guardians
summoned into Tārā's service in two commentaries, and a tantric hidden-meaning
identification with the wind element — as attested by all sixteen commentaries
surveyed in `2-RAILS/Claims/vayu.md`.
