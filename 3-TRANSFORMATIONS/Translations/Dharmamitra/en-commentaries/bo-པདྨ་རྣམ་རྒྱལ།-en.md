# The Delightful Grove of Nectar: A Commentary on the Praise in Twenty-One Homages ^0

## Homage ^I-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^I-1]]

Oṃ Svasti! ^I-1

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^I-2]]

The Dharmakāya is the Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom;
The Sambhogakāya is the Samaya Tārā, the Yoginī;
To the twenty-one [Tārās] of the Net of Magical Manifestation,
I prostrate with the stirring of a joyful garden of faith. ^I-2

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^I-3]]

Since merely reciting the outer, inner, and secret commentaries and so forth of this praise
Without understanding them is of no benefit to one’s practice,
I shall explain in an easy-to-understand way those parts that are difficult to grasp
From within the direct teachings, including the preliminaries, the main body, and the conclusion. ^I-3

## First, the preliminary of accumulating merit ^II-0

### Inviting the field of merit ^II-1-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-1]]

Furthermore, if one wishes to praise the qualities of the Noble Tārā in an increasingly exalted manner, there are three parts: the preliminaries, the main body, and the conclusion. The first, the preliminary of the sequence of accumulating merit, is the invitation of the field of merit: ^II-1

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-2]]

From the supreme realm of Potala,
Born from the green syllable TĀṂ,
Liberating transmigrating beings through the light of the syllable TĀṂ,
I invite the green Tārā together with her retinue of twenty-one. ^II-2

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-3]]

Thinking thus, visualize them abiding in the space before you within a garden of lotus and moon-disc seats. ^II-3

### The actual accumulation of merit ^II-2-0

#### First, prostrating by manifesting infinite bodies ^II-2-1-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-4]]

Regarding the seven branches of the actual accumulation of merit, the first, prostrating by emanating countless bodies, is as follows: ^II-4

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-5]]

With the crowns of gods and demigods,
Bowing down before your lotus feet,
To her who liberates from all misfortune,
Homage to the Mother Tārā. ^II-5

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-6]]

To the Noble Lady Tārā,
And to all the Victors and their heirs
Residing in the ten directions and three times,
I pay homage with complete devotion. ^II-6

#### Second, the branch of making offerings to the Noble Lady ^II-2-2-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-7]]

Second is the branch of making offerings to the Noble Lady. ^II-7

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-8]]

Flowers, incense, lamps, perfumes,
Food, music, and so forth—
I offer these, both actually performed and mentally emanated;
May the assembly of Noble Ladies please accept them. ^II-8

#### Third, the branch of confessing the misdeeds of oneself and others ^II-2-3-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-9]]

The third is the branch of confessing the misdeeds of oneself and others: ^II-9

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-10]]

From beginningless time until the present,
The ten non-virtues and the five crimes of immediate retribution,
All misdeeds committed while the mind
Was under the power of the afflictions, I confess. ^II-10

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-11]]

The three misdeeds of killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct; the four of lying, slander, harsh speech, and idle gossip; and the three of covetousness, ill will, and wrong views—these constitute the ten non-virtues. The five crimes of immediate retribution consist of killing one's father, killing one's mother, killing an Arhat, creating a schism in the Sangha, and maliciously drawing blood from the body of a Victor, and so forth. ^II-11

#### Fourth, the branch of rejoicing in the virtues accomplished by oneself and others ^II-2-4-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-12]]

The fourth is the branch of rejoicing in the virtue accomplished by oneself and others: ^II-12

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-13]]

In the merit of whatever virtue has been accumulated
Throughout the three times by Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas,
Bodhisattvas, ordinary beings, and so forth,
I rejoice. ^II-13

#### Fifth, the branch of requesting the turning of the wheel of Dharma ^II-2-5-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-14]]

The fifth is the branch of requesting the turning of the wheel of Dharma: ^II-14

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-15]]

According to the intentions of sentient beings,
And their various levels of mental capacity,
I pray you turn the wheel of Dharma of the Great Vehicle (the Bodhisattva vehicle of both Sūtra and Tantra), the Small Vehicle (the two vehicles of Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas), and the Common Vehicle (the two vehicles of gods and humans of the higher realms).
I beseech you to turn the wheel of Dharma. ^II-15

#### Sixth, the branch of requesting not to pass into nirvana ^II-2-6-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-16]]

The sixth is the branch of requesting that you do not pass into nirvana: ^II-16

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-17]]

Until saṃsāra is emptied,
Do not pass into nirvana, but through your compassion,
I pray that you look upon all sentient beings
Who are sinking in the ocean of suffering. ^II-17

#### Seventh, the branch of dedication and aspiration ^II-2-7-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-18]]

The seventh is the branch of dedication and aspiration: ^II-18

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-19]]

In this regard, through the first two branches, merit is accumulated; through the third, negative obscurations are purified; and through the remaining three, it is increased. Thus, having attained the enlightenment in which all the merit of these three—accumulation, purification, and increase—is ultimately merged as one with the wisdom mind of the Jetsunma, the aspiration to accomplish the benefit of others together with you in the interim is: ^II-19

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-20]]

Whatever merit I have accumulated,
May it all become the cause for enlightenment in which my mind is merged as one with the Jetsunma,
And before long, may I become a glorious guide for wandering beings,
Together with you, O Jetsunma. ^II-20

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^II-21]]

In this way, it is taught in the sutras that by performing a single prostration, one obtains the benefit of attaining the sovereignty of a universal monarch as many times as there are atoms in the great golden foundation of the earth, extending up to the surface covered by one’s own body. Furthermore, it is explained that the remaining branches possess benefits that increase a hundredfold, one after the other; thus, this seven-branch practice is essential as a means to quickly complete the accumulation of merit. This concludes the sequence of the preliminary accumulation of merit. ^II-21

## Second, the actual praise ^1-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-1]]

The second part, the praise extracted from the Tārā Tantra, which stirs the rain-cloud of blessings, is to be recited while keeping in mind the meaning of the actual words of praise, and from within the expanse of faith and devotion—where tears well up in the eyes and the hairs of the body stand on end—offering praise through the gate of the four immeasurables. ^1-1

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-2]]

First, to state the title in the Indian Sanskrit language: Namaḥ Tārā Ekaviṃśati Stotra Guṇahita Sāka. ^1-2

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-3]]

Second, to state it in the Tibetan language: The Homage to Tārā with Twenty-One Verses and its Benefits. ^1-3

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-4]]

Third, to state it in the combined languages: Namaḥ means "homage," Tārā means "Savior," Ekaviṃśati means "twenty-one," Stotra means "praise," and Guṇahita Sāka means "with benefits." ^1-4

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-5]]

Fourth, the meaning of the title is easy to understand as it is directly stated. ^1-5

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-6]]

Oṃ is the introductory syllable. Because she has become the supreme mother of all the victorious ones, the Venerable Lady is exalted above the level of ordinary beings, and thus she is the Noble Lady (Ārya). Because she liberates those who have faith from cyclic existence, the lower realms, and fears, she is the Savior (Tārā); thus, "Homage to Tārā." This is the translator's homage. To whom is the homage paid? Tāre is the Savior; because she accomplishes [tasks] in an instant or in a single day, she is the Heroine who is swifter in her enlightened activity than other deities and who quickly turns back obstacles. Tuttāre is she who dispels fears. Ture is the Savior who grants all benefits—one's own, others', and the ultimate goal. The syllable Svāhā means "I bow to you." It appears that this is a blessed [verse] that arose in the manner of a summary of Tārā, which is renowned as having been supplemented from other tantras by the glorious Jowo Rje and others. ^1-6

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-7]]

To whom is the homage paid? It is to that Savior who, through her wisdom, liberates those with faith from all fears; who, out of love for the one offering praise, does not delay even for an instant in her enlightened activity, and is thus the "Swift" one of compassion; and who, being capable, is the "Heroine" because she is certain of her ability to protect from all the fears of cyclic existence and peace. In her wrathful aspect, her wisdom eyes are like a momentary flash of lightning. She is the one who arose from the opening stamens of the lotus face of the Lord of the Three Worlds—the subterranean realm of the lower states, the terrestrial realm of humans, and the celestial realm of the higher states—the Saṃbhogakāya Avalokiteśvara. From that lotus face, which is like a blooming water-born flower, and from the stamens of those eyes, there arose the ocean of tears of compassion for beings, and from within the lotus that swirled from the right and left, she appeared as the white and blue Tārā, born by transformation, having pledged to be a companion in liberating beings. "To her, I offer homage"—apply this likewise to those that follow. ^1-7

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-8]]

To whom is the homage paid? It is to that beautiful one who, in terms of the qualities of her body, is like a hundred full autumn moons gathered together, her face white, beautiful, and fully radiant. If one contemplates that she, who is blazing with the nectar-like light that is brilliantly emitted by the rays of light that are even greater than those of a thousand gathered stars, dissolves into oneself, filling and stabilizing one's body, one will attain the siddhi of longevity and wisdom. ^1-8

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-9]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to that delightful one whose left hand is beautifully adorned with a lotus that has grown from the water, possessing blue petals and stamens of a reddish-yellow golden hue, and which is a lotus stalk that is free from aging and decay, complete with leaves and fruit. Because she makes the six perfections—the causes of buddhahood, namely the perfection of generosity, diligence, austerity (which is ethical discipline), the wisdom of peace, patience, and meditative concentration—her sphere of activity, she is the one who acts in the manner of a Bodhisattva and a daughter of the Victorious Ones. ^1-9

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-10]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who is the jewel of the uṣṇīṣa of the assembly of the five families of the Victorious Ones, the Tathāgatas of the ten directions; who is the sovereign of the families, having been empowered as the infinite crown ornament; and who, because she is the one who acts in the manner of being victorious over all directions as if she were superior even to the Victorious Ones themselves, is the one who is most respectfully relied upon by the assemblies of the sons of the Victorious Ones—the Bodhisattvas who have attained the ten perfections in sequence throughout the world-realms of the ten directions—as the Mother who generates the wisdom of the path of seeing, the Mother who nurtures the wisdom of the path of meditation, and the Mother who always fulfills the aspirations of the path of no-more-learning and the rest. ^1-10

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-11]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who, by performing the application of the syllables oṃ tuttāre hūṃ, and relying upon the deity, mantra, and samādhi of the red Tārā, is able to summon—having trampled with her compassionate feet the great ones of the seven worlds, including the desire realm, the form realm, the formless realm, the space that fills them, and the six classes of beings along with the antarābhava—by bringing them under her power without exception. ^1-11

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-12]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who is worshipped with reverence even by the particles of dust at her feet by the ten guardians of the directions, headed by the great gods such as the great lord Maheśvara—who acts as the father even to the great protectors bearing various names, such as Indra, the greatest among the thirty-three; the fire deity Agni, the greatest among the sages; Brahmā, the greatest in the form realm; and the wind deity Vāyu, who is able to shake the three realms. Furthermore, it is to the one who is praised from before, with fear and trembling reverence, by hosts of such beings who are peerless in their greatness and power, including the bhūtas such as the Lord of Hosts (Gaṇapati), the vetālas such as the terrifying black one, the gandharvas such as the king of gandharvas Druma and those with horse-heads, and the yakṣas such as Vaiśravaṇa. If one pays homage to her, one will be free from the fear of those beings, both now and in all lifetimes to come. ^1-12

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-13]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who, relying upon the deity mantra and meditative absorption such as oṃ tāre trat phaṭ svāhā, utterly destroys all the magical wheels—the suppressing, burning, hurling, and rotating devices—of the adversary’s evil mantras by means of the killing mantra trat and the repelling mantra phaṭ. It is to the one who, trampling with her right leg drawn in and left extended, acts with the fierce power of subjugation, blazing amidst a swirling mass of fire. ^1-13

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-14]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who, by the great and fearsome power of the deity mantra oṃ ture svāhā, instantly destroys all the champions of Māra and their retinues who manifest as the four Māras to cause one harm. Because she makes her face, which resembles a blooming water-born lotus, appear with a frowning expression, she is the one who, without even needing to be specifically invoked, naturally slays all the enemies of the practitioner who takes Tārā as their yidam, just as the sun dries the dew on the tip of a blade of grass. ^1-14

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-15]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one whose left hand, with the middle, ring, and little fingers raised, forms the mudrā of granting refuge, symbolizing that all three Jewels are complete within the Venerable Lady. It is to the one who is adorned with a young utpala flower, held at her heart by the fingers of her thumb and ring finger joined together to symbolize the measuring of the sacred commitment within the continuum, and with the flower blossoming at the level of her left ear to symbolize that one’s own continuum is liberated through hearing. It is to the one who is adorned with a turbulent mass of her own light, which clearly illuminates all the circular realms of the entire world in the ten directions without exception. ^1-15

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-16]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who is completely filled with the supreme joy of an internal, stainless mental continuum, and who, on her external body, radiates splendor. She is the one who, even with the garlands of light from her precious crown, captivates and brings under her power all that is stationary and moving, cyclic existence and nirvana, and who spreads this delight. By the power of the vidyā-mantra tuttāre, she laughs with the laughter of speech to bring the world of cyclic existence under her power, and she laughs supremely to bring the supramundane world under her power. She is the one who, possessing the strength to subjugate the three realms, brings all demons and the world, both great and small, under her power, and thereby causes them to ripen and be liberated. ^1-16

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-17]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who has the power to summon all the sentient beings who protect the earth, and in particular, the hosts of human kings, as they all have faith in you. By the power and strength of gathering and dissolving—emanating from the countless hosts of the syllable hūṃ, which is the source of all desired attainments, along with light rays and the blessings of nectar, all while she moves her frowning expression in a wrathful manner—she is the one who completely liberates the practitioner from all destitution regarding Dharma, material wealth, physical form, and the sense fields. ^1-17

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-18]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who is adorned with a crown of a crescent moon, a beautiful quality, and who is also completely decorated with the abundance of other precious ornaments and garments, all of which blaze with intense light. From within her matted locks, she is the one who constantly radiates a light that causes the life, merit, qualities, power, realization, and the enlightened activity of all beings to be fully expanded. ^1-18

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-19]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who dwells in the midst of a blazing garland of fire—like the fire at the end of an eon—due to the lamp of primordial wisdom blazing within, while she manifests a wrathful appearance externally. Because she delights in being surrounded by various wrathful postures, such as having her right leg extended and her left bent, even if one is surrounded on all sides by the fourfold army of enemies, if one prays to the noble lady without hesitation, she is the one who immediately destroys them. ^1-19

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-20]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who, with a wrathful expression toward the malicious, strikes the surface of the earth—including Mount Meru and the continents—with the palms of her hands and tramples it with her feet. With a wrathful frown, and by the sound of the syllable HŪṂ from her nose that causes the three realms to tremble, she shatters the seven underworld levels—which are like the layers of roof tiles on a house, consisting of the abodes of the spirits, the superior abodes, the baseless abodes, the essential abodes, the abodes of essence, the excellent abodes, and the pure abodes—along with the Nāgas, Asuras, Rākṣasas, hungry ghosts, creeping beings, and the servants of the guardians of hell who dwell therein. ^1-20

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-21]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the noble lady, the great mother of the expanse of the dharmakāya, the peaceful one who establishes beings in the happiness of the higher realms, the virtuous one who establishes them in liberation, and the one who establishes them in omniscience, whose very sphere of experience is the realization of the peace of nirvana in a single lifetime. By reciting her essence, the vidyā-mantra TĀRE TUTTĀRE—which is perfectly endowed with SVĀHĀ at the end and OṂ at the beginning—ten thousand, one hundred thousand, ten million, or one hundred million times, one destroys all the causes, such as the ten non-virtuous acts and the five heinous crimes, and all the results, such as the suffering of illness, demonic interference, and physical pain, as well as the fears of mental derangement, agitation, and fainting; she is the one who performs the activity of pacification. ^1-21

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-22]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to you, the goddess who delights in being surrounded by an assembly of vidyādharas, realized beings, and bodhisattva heirs, and who shatters the bodies of enemies by scattering them into pieces. It is to the very Tārā who is generated from the HŪṂ of the essence and the seed syllable that generates the vidyā-mantra, upon whose speech the ten-syllable mantra of the wrathful Tārā is arranged. ^1-22

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-23]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the Ture, the Tārā who, by manifesting the miraculous display of her form, stamps her feet upon the ground, which is like Mount Meru, the king of mountains, and the continents. She is the one whose essence is the seed-syllable in the form of Hūṃ, which is the cause for generating you, the wrathful one, and the life-force of your heart. She is the one who, by shaking the three realms—the subterranean world of the lower realms, the terrestrial world of humans, and the celestial world of the higher realms—through the ten types of earth-shaking, suppresses and overcomes all that is cruel and harsh, including Mount Meru, the king of mountains at the center, the mountains Mandara and Vindhya that reside within the surrounding oceans where the gods and great sages dwell. ^1-23

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-24]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one who holds in her hand the deer-marked moon, which has the form of a clear celestial lake, and which is visualized in the space before Tārā’s form. By contemplating that nectar flows from it, washing and filling one’s own body inside and out, one recites the Oṃ tāra tāra phaṭ, which is the direct instruction of the tantra, or alternatively, the Oṃ tāre tuttāre ture phaṭ svāhā as the main practice. By pronouncing the two tāras and the syllable phaṭ, she is the one who pacifies all existing poison-illnesses, such as those from mad dogs and snakes, without exception, and who clears them away for the future as well. ^1-24

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-25]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to the one whom even the kings of the hosts of gods of the desire and form realms, such as Brahmā and Indra, as well as the kings of the gods, humans, and kinnaras belonging to their retinues—who possess power, strength, miraculous abilities, and majesty—rely upon as their guru, even for the smallest particle of dust at her feet. By the majesty of her joyful, complete armor of the deity, mantra, and primordial wisdom of the Venerable Lady, which she maintains at all times through her three doors, she is the one who dispels all religious disputes, worldly quarrels, and legal arguments, as well as all bad omens and nightmares, thereby making one victorious in all directions. ^1-25

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-26]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to Tārā, who possesses eyes as brilliant as the full sun and moon—the right eye, like the sun, having rays of fire that burn away all enemies, obstructors, illnesses, demonic spirits, and obscurations, and the left eye, like the moon, having rays of nectar that increase all lifespan, wisdom, and merit—and who is the one endowed with such eyes of supreme radiance. Having meditated upon her in the sky before oneself or performed the self-generation, one should recite the mantra oṃ hara hara tuttāre svāhā. As indicated by the literal meaning of the mantra, by reciting the two haras and tuttāre, she is the one who dispels extremely violent epidemics and contagious diseases, such as earth-anthrax, sky-anthrax, smallpox, virulent fevers, heat fevers, bilious fevers, and common colds, as well as other illnesses of heat, cold, and combined disorders arising from past karma or immediate conditions, and in particular, she is the one who pacifies previously contracted epidemic diseases and prevents them from arising in the future. ^1-26

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-27]]

Furthermore, to whom is the homage paid? It is to her who is perfectly endowed with the power of pacification through the arrangement of the three suchnesses—the tāre, tuttāre, and ture—which, when the seven syllables are combined with oṃ at the beginning and svāhā at the end, constitute a vidyāmantra capable of accomplishing all activities, and which are specifically arranged as the three suchnesses to accomplish the activities of pacifying illnesses, spirits, and obscurations. She is the one who pacifies the eighteen great spirits, such as the namgru, as well as the rolang (zombies) of the charnel grounds—including those of flesh, blood, skin, moles, channels, and bones—and all the cruel and fierce worldly spirits, such as the byungpo and gnod sbyin (yakṣas), who steal away the radiance of one’s life force. Specifically, as the Buddha has said: "To the Mother, the Blessed Tārā, who is beautiful like the light of the moon and who pacifies the fears of lions, elephants, fire, snakes, robbers, iron chains, the ocean, and flesh-eating demons, I pay homage," and as it is said: "She who thoroughly destroys all harm from lions, tigers, elephants, great oceans, fire, bonds, thieves, and poisonous snakes," she is the supreme ture herself, who swiftly destroys the eight fears and all harms. ^1-27

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-28]]

In this way, these verses of praise, which are based on the ten-syllable root mantra of Tārā, and the twenty-one homages are thus presented. In this manner, the direct meaning of the tantra has been explained in a way that is easy to understand. ^1-28

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-29]]

Furthermore, according to the hidden meaning: "Amidst the matted hair, Amitābha" refers to the vase empowerment; "a crescent moon as a diadem" refers to the secret empowerment of drawing the bindu; the four—bdemā, dgemā, zhimā, and mya ngan 'das zhi—refer to the four empowerments of the wisdom-knowledge empowerment; the three suchnesses refer to the fourth empowerment, which possesses the three liberations; and "a diadem of light garlands" refers to the crown ornament and other meanings of empowerment. Moreover, "circling in all directions, supreme joy" and "right leg extended, left drawn in, circling in all directions with joy" refer to the generation stage of the principal deity and retinue, and the way of teaching the completion stage through the bindu and prāṇa of the sun, moon, and so forth. Although these could be explained in great detail, since they are taught completely in other tantras, in this context of a concise tantric text, I have chosen to extract only the clear, unclouded essence of the direct meaning and have explained it accordingly. ^1-29

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^1-30]]

If the meaning taught in this tantra is expanded, it also reveals the sādhana of Tārā as peaceful by day and wrathful by night, the twenty-one sādhanas, and the twenty-one ritual activities. With this, the meaning of the actual praise is concluded. ^1-30

## Third, the concluding benefits ^2-0

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-1]]

Third, the meaning of the concluding benefits: ^2-1

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-2]]

For any intelligent person who possesses genuine devotion to the Goddess, the object of praise, by reciting the tantric words of praise with a mind that is thoroughly purified in the manner of praising, ^2-2

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-3]]

The time for praise is at dusk, when spirits and demons, who are inclined toward darkness, cause harm, and at dawn, the time when the gods give prophecies; one should rise from bed and, by merely recollecting the Noble One, be granted all power of fearlessness and confidence. The cause, all misdeeds, is thoroughly pacified, and the result—sickness, demons, misfortune, bad omens, and the doors of birth into the three lower realms—is blocked, thereby destroying all miserable rebirths. ^2-3

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-4]]

By the seventy million Conquerors, one will be granted the empowerment to swiftly master all wind, mind, and phenomenal existence, whether in actual experience, meditative absorption, or dreams. One will attain all the qualities greater than those of this life, and in the next, one will proceed to that ultimate state of buddhahood. ^2-4

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-5]]

Regarding the temporary poisons for that person who praises: these include violent, virulent poisons, great insect poisons, and conditions like colic; poisons that exist in the manner of disturbing the elements in sentient beings who are the basis; or other poisons from the harm of sentient beings such as nāgas, mad dogs, and snakes. Furthermore, even if one has eaten or drunk substances of innate poison such as black aconite, or compounded poisons such as goat bile and rotten dog flesh, or transformed poisons such as frozen goat meat, if one has previously completed the praise of the Venerable Lady well, such as by reciting it a hundred thousand times, then by subsequently recollecting the mantra, one will completely eliminate them and attain the state of having transformed them into nectar. ^2-5

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-6]]

One will attain a great peace and joy in body and mind that thoroughly abandons the masses of suffering caused by spirits, epidemics, and poisons; and if one wishes to accomplish purposes for other sentient beings, not just for oneself, those will be accomplished as well. When reciting the praise regularly, if one clearly pronounces it two, three, or seven times, ^2-6

![[bo-པདྨ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ།#^2-7]]

those desiring a child will obtain a child, students, servants, and so forth; those desiring wealth will obtain the very wealth they desire. In short, one will obtain everything that is desirable to the mind, and may all obstacles be absent and individually destroyed. ^2-7
