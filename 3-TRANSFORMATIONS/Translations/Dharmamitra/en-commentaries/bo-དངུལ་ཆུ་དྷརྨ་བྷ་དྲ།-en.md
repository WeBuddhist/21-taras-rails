# A Bouquet of Utpala Flowers Captivating the Mind: An Explanation of the Praise to Tārā in Twenty-One Homages ^0

## Homage ^I-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^I-1]]

Namo guru ārya tāraye. ^I-1

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^I-2]]

Having bowed at the feet of the Guru, who possesses the three kāyas and the five primordial wisdoms,
And of Tārā, the Mother of the Victors, who is inseparable from him,
I shall explain in brief the meaning of the tantra of the praise
To her twenty-one manifestations. ^I-2

## First, the brief thematic summary ^1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^1-1]]

Regarding the explanation of the meaning of the Praise to Tārā in Twenty-One Verses, there are three parts: the brief presentation, the detailed explanation, and the presentation of the benefits. The first is: ^1-1

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^1-2]]

Oṃ. Homage to the Venerable Noble Tārā. ^1-2

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^1-3]]

As for the meaning: Oṃ, when analyzed through its components, consists of the three letters A, U, and Ma, which symbolize the body, speech, and mind of the object of homage. Because she is the supreme Mother of the Victors and possesses the three vows, she is the Venerable Lady (rje btsun ma). Because she does not abide in either saṃsāra or nirvāṇa, she is the Noble Lady (’phags ma). Because she saves all sentient beings from suffering, she is Tārā (sgrol ma). Prostrating at her feet signifies bowing with devotion through the three doors. ^1-3

## Second, the detailed explanation ^2-0

### First, praise by way of historical account ^2-1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-1]]

The second, the detailed explanation, has three parts: praise by way of history, praise by way of the form of the body, and praise by way of enlightened activity. The first is: ^2-1

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-2]]

Homage to Tārā, the swift and heroic,
Whose eyes are like a flash of lightning,
She who arose from the opening stamens
Of the lotus-face of the Lord of the Three Worlds. ^2-2

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-3]]

This means: "Homage" signifies bowing with devotion. To whom does one bow? She is Tārā (sgrol ma) because she delivers sentient beings from saṃsāra. She is the Swift One (mgyur ma) because her enlightened activity is swifter than that of other Victors. She is the Heroic Lady (dpal mo) because she vanquishes the hosts of māras without remainder. Her wisdom eyes are like a momentary flash of lightning, as she perceives all knowable objects in a single instant. The protector and refuge of the three worlds—the subterranean world of the nāgas, the terrestrial world of humans, and the celestial world of the gods—is the Noble Avalokiteśvara, and she is the one who possesses the history of having arisen from the opening stamens of a lotus flower that was born from the tears resting upon the maṇḍala of his face; thus, one bows to her. As the Great Translator Namzang emended the third line to read "the lotus of the face of the Lord of the Three Worlds," it is easier to understand, and so it is explained accordingly. ^2-3

Homage to her whose face is like

This means: Homage. To whom? To her who possesses a face that is supremely white and beautiful, like a hundred full autumn moons—the season free from dust, clouds, and sheep-wool-like mists—gathered and piled together, the moon being at its full, having completed its waxing and not yet having begun to wane. To her who is blazing and radiating brilliant white light, as if that face had illuminated and clarified everything by means of light rays resembling a thousand stars gathered together in one place. ^2-6

She is the one who possesses the power not only to fill those seven worlds of existence with light and sound, but also to trample them under the feet of her body, and is able to summon all beings dwelling therein under her power, leaving none behind. ^2-15

Homage to you, who with the syllables Traṭ and Phaṭ,

This means: Homage. To whom? To her who, merely by the sound of the vidyā-mantras "Traṭ" (meaning to tear apart, or conquer) and "Phaṭ" (meaning to burst) from her mouth, utterly destroys in an instant all the power of the magical wheels created by the evil thoughts and actions of hostile sorcerers. With her right leg drawn in and left extended—symbolizing emptiness and compassion—she tramples underfoot the sorcerers, the rbad-’dre spirits, and the like. From her body, a fire of primordial wisdom blazes and swirls, and by its intense burning, she consumes them completely, leaving not even ash behind. Although her posture is that of a peaceful deity, her dwelling in the midst of a blazing fire represents a slightly wrathful aspect. ^2-21

###### First, praise by way of purifying the two obscurations and the maras. ^2-2-1-2-1-0

There are seven parts to the praise by way of the wrathful form: praise by way of purifying the demons and the two obscurations, praise by way of the hand symbols on the right and left, praise by way of the crown ornament and the sound of laughter, praise by way of accomplishing enlightened activity through the ten directional guardians, praise by way of the crown ornament, praise by way of the wrathful posture, and praise by way of light rays radiating from the syllable Hāṃ. ![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-23]]

Homage to Ture, the Great Fearful One,

Who forms a frowning expression upon her lotus face,

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-24]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the one who is Ture (the Swift One) and so forth, by way of the sound of her speech, and the Great Fearful One, by way of the manifestation of her extremely wrathful body. She who utterly destroys the champions of Mara—the champions among the demons who are difficult to tame—is the one who destroys the four Maras of mental affliction. By means of her lotus face—beautiful like a blossoming lotus—which she makes to have a frowning expression, she is the one who slays all external enemies as well, without leaving any remainder. Here, if one interprets the enemies of liberation as the afflictive obscurations and the enemies of omniscience as the cognitive obscurations, it accords with the summary meaning. ^2-24

This means: Homage. To whom? To the one who strikes the surface of the great earth, including Mount Meru and the continents, with the palms of her hands—which are accompanied by the threatening mudrā—and tramples it with the soles of her feet. Furthermore, she assumes a highly wrathful appearance, such as by contracting her face into a frown, and from the blue syllable Hūṃ residing in the center of each of her palms and soles, she emanates a rain of vajra fire. Thereby, she shatters—that is, she destroys—the malevolent beings such as the Nāgas, Asuras, and Yamas who dwell in the seven levels beneath the earth, namely: the basis, the superior basis, the baseless, the basis itself, the basis of essence, the good basis, and the pure basis. ^2-42

#### Second, praise by way of the aspect of the dharmakāya ^2-2-2-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-43]]

The second section is the praise by way of the aspect of the Dharmakāya. ^2-43

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-44]]

Homage to the Blissful, Virtuous, and Peaceful One,
Whose very sphere of experience is the peace of nirvāṇa;
Perfectly endowed with Oṃ and Svāhā,
She who utterly destroys great negativity. ^2-44

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-45]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the one who is "Blissful" because she is free from the result of suffering; "Virtuous" because she does not accumulate the cause of non-virtue; and "Peaceful" because she is free from the afflictions to be abandoned. Because she constantly rests in equipoise within the supreme expanse of peace that has passed beyond the sorrow of the two obscurations, she has become one whose very sphere of experience is that. By the power of that, the mantra she has spoken—Tāre Tuttāre Ture—when practiced like the waxing moon, having been made perfectly endowed with Svāhā at the end and Oṃ at the beginning, and consisting of ten syllables, utterly destroys without remainder the great negativities of the practitioner’s continuum, such as the five inexpiable sins and the abandonment of the Dharma; their causes, the great afflictions such as attachment and aversion; and their results, the great sufferings such as heat and cold. To that essence of a mother, I pay homage. ^2-45

### Third, praise by way of enlightened activity ^2-3-0

#### First, praise by way of the enlightened activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras ^2-3-1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-46]]

The third section, praise by way of enlightened activity, has six parts: praise by way of the enlightened activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras; praise by way of the enlightened activity of the wrathful female deity shaking the three worlds; praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling the poisons of the stationary and moving worlds; praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling disputes and bad dreams; praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling epidemics; and praise by way of the enlightened activity of destroying malevolent spirits and zombies. The first is: ^2-46

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-47]]

Homage to the One who is surrounded by intense joy,
Who utterly shatters the bodies of enemies;
She who is the Tārā arising from the Hūṃ of awareness,
In which the speech of the ten-syllable mantra is arranged. ^2-47

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-48]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who utterly shatters and destroys the bodies of the outer and inner enemies in the mental continuums of those to be tamed who take great delight in the turning of the Dharma wheel in every way. Regarding that, for the peaceful aspect, at the heart is the Tāṃ syllable, followed by the ten-syllable speech, that is, the mantra: OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ. Because it is this, it is by way of arranging that. For the wrathful aspect, at the heart is the awareness, that is, the awareness mantra: OṂ NAMO TĀRE NAMO HARE HŪṂ HARE SVĀHĀ. By way of the Hūṃ syllable placed in the center of that which is surrounded by this, light rays and so forth are emanated, and thus it is to that Tārā herself who destroys enemies, obstructers, and so forth. ^2-48

#### Second, praise by way of the enlightened activity of shaking the three realms through a wrathful form ^2-3-2-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-49]]

The second is the praise by way of the enlightened activity of the wrathful one causing the three realms to tremble: ^2-49

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-50]]

Homage to the One who stamps Her feet with the syllable Ture,
Who is the very essence of the seed-syllable in the form of Hāṃ;
She who causes Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya,
And all the three worlds, to tremble. ^2-50

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-51]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who stamps Her two feet—the Ture, meaning the Swift One—violently. What is that Swift One like? She is the embodiment of the wrathful one born from the seed-syllable in the form of the syllable Hāṃ. What does She do by stamping Her feet? She causes the king of mountains, Mount Meru, the mountain Mandara, the black mountain Vindhya, and so forth—in short, the three worlds of the subterranean, the terrestrial, and the celestial, along with their inhabitants—to tremble. Therefore, it is to that One who is the embodiment of such power and strength. ^2-51

#### Third, praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling the poisons of the stationary and the moving ^2-3-3-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-52]]

The third is the praise by way of the enlightened activity of clearing away the poisons of the stable and the moving: ^2-52

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-53]]

Homage to the One who holds in Her hand
The deer-marked moon, which has the form of a celestial lake;
By reciting the two Tāras and the syllable Phaṭ,
She clears away every poison without exception. ^2-53

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-54]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who, holding in Her hand the moon mandala—which is white and round like the lake of the gods—marked with the sign of the deer (the hare), clears away the poison of the stable. By reciting the mantra—which is the ten-syllable mantra indicated by the two Tāras, with the syllable Phaṭ attached in place of Svāhā—She clears away the hosts of the poisons of the moving without leaving any remainder. It is to that One. ^2-54

#### Fourth, praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling disputes and bad dreams ^2-3-4-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-55]]

(The fourth) is the praise by way of the enlightened activity of clearing away disputes and bad dreams: ^2-55

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-56]]

Homage to the One who is the queen of the hosts of gods,
Whom gods and kinnaras rely upon;
With the majesty of joyful and complete armor,
She clears away disputes and bad dreams. ^2-56

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-57]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who is served at Her lotus feet with devotion by the crowns of the kings of the hosts of gods of the desire and form realms—such as Śakra and Brahmā—as well as by ordinary gods and the kinnara king Druma and others. By the splendor of being extremely joyful in wearing the armor of meditating on the Goddess’s body and reciting Her mantra in every way, She clears away all disputes from others and all bad dreams. It is to that One. ^2-57

#### Fifth, praise by way of the enlightened activity of dispelling epidemics ^2-3-5-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-58]]

The fifth is the praise by way of the enlightened activity of clearing away infectious diseases: ^2-58

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-59]]

Homage to the One whose two eyes,
Like the full sun and moon, radiate brilliant light;
By uttering the two haras and tuttāra,
She clears away the most violent infectious diseases. ^2-59

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-60]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who possesses light that is brilliantly radiant and cool from Her two wrathful eyes, which are like the full sun, and Her two peaceful eyes, which are like the full moon. By reciting the wrathful mantra, indicated by the two haras (meaning "to snatch away"), which is oṃ namaḥ tāre namo hare hūṃ hare svāhā, and the peaceful mantra, indicated by tuttāra, which is oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā, She clears away even all the most violent—that is, difficult to cure—infectious diseases without exception. It is to that One. ^2-60

#### Sixth, praise by way of the enlightened activity of subduing spirits and zombies ^2-3-6-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-61]]

The sixth is the praise by way of the enlightened activity of subduing spirits and zombies: ^2-61

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-62]]

Homage to the One who, by arranging the three suchnesses,
Is perfectly endowed with the power of pacification;
She is the supreme Ture who destroys
The hosts of spirits, zombies, and yakṣas. ^2-62

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^2-63]]

This means: Homage. To whom? To the One who is perfectly endowed with the power and ability to pacify, without remainder, all obstacles of the three doors, by meditating upon the arrangement of the three suchnesses at the three places—the suchness of body, oṃ, at the crown; the suchness of speech, āḥ, at the throat; and the suchness of mind, hūṃ, at the heart—of the practitioner who abides in the yoga of the Goddess. She is the supreme mistress who swiftly destroys the hosts of harmful beings, such as the eighteen types of spirits including the nam-gru spirits, the evil mantras such as the ro-langs (zombie) rituals of casting spells, and the yakṣas who steal one's vitality. This is explained according to the emendation made by the Lord, the Protector of Beings, in the fourth line: "She is the supreme Ture who destroys," because ture means "the swift one," or, depending on the context, it also functions as "the swift [act of] destroying." ^2-63

## Explanation of the benefits ^3-0

### First, the presentation of the distinction of intention ^3-1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-1]]

The third section, on the explanation of the benefits, has four parts: the explanation of the specific intention, the explanation of the specific time, the explanation of the actual benefits, and the summary explanation by way of number. The first is: ^3-1

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-2]]

This praise with the root mantra,
And these twenty-one homages,
By any intelligent person endowed with genuine devotion to the Goddess,
Who recites them clearly, ^3-2

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-3]]

This means: Because it is combined with the two root mantras of the peaceful and wrathful [Tārās], this praise of the twenty-one Tārās contains twenty-one verses, and the text itself, which contains twenty-one "homages," should be recited with great devotion and the thought that the benefits mentioned below will arise for any person who possesses supreme intelligence capable of distinguishing good from bad, who is endowed with genuine devotion and remembrance of the kindness of the Goddess Tārā, and who recites it with firm, supreme faith that remembers her qualities according to the meaning of the words. ^3-3

### Second, the presentation of the distinction of time ^3-2-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-4]]

The second section, the explanation of the specific time, is: ^3-4

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-5]]

Having arisen at dusk and at dawn,
Through mindfulness, all fearlessness is fully bestowed;
All misdeeds are thoroughly pacified,
And all lower realms are utterly destroyed. ^3-5

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-6]]

This means: If one prays because of fear of illness, malevolent spirits, enemies, thieves, and so forth in this life, one should recite the praise at dusk while primarily recollecting the wrathful form and so forth. If one prays because of fear of the suffering of lower realms in the future, one should recite the praise at dawn, having arisen from bed, while primarily recollecting the peaceful form and so forth. Because this destroys the eight great internal and external fears, it "fully bestows all fearlessness," and because it thoroughly pacifies all misdeeds, such as the ten non-virtues which are the causes of lower realms, it has the benefit of "utterly destroying" all lower realms, such as the hells, which are the results. This shows that one must act in accordance with the specific time. ^3-6

### Third, the actual explanation of the benefits. ^3-3-0

#### The benefits that arise for the practitioner themselves. ^3-3-1-0

##### The benefits of attaining qualities. ^3-3-1-1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-7]]

The third section, the explanation of the actual benefits, has two parts: the benefits that arise for the practitioner themselves, and the benefits that arise for others who are to be helped. The first of these has two parts: the benefits of attaining qualities, and the benefits of reversing faults. The first is: ^3-7

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-8]]

Seventy million Victorious Ones
Will swiftly bestow empowerment,
They will attain even greater excellence than this,
And proceed to that ultimate state of Buddhahood. ^3-8

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-9]]

This means: The seventy million Victorious Ones—the emanated forms who reside together with the Bhagavatī herself—will swiftly, that is, in this very life, bestow empowerment through a stream of light rays or nectar. By the power of that, one will attain, in the temporary sense, a state of excellence in terms of life, body, enjoyments, and retinue that is greater and more supreme than one's current situation. In the ultimate sense, one will swiftly traverse the path and proceed to that state of Buddhahood. This is the meaning; some translations of the fourth line explain it as "proceed to the ultimate state of Buddhahood." ^3-9

##### Second, the benefits of reversing faults. ^3-3-1-2-0

###### First, the benefits of reversing the causes of suffering. ^3-3-1-2-1-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-10]]

The second section, the benefits of reversing faults, has two parts: the benefits of reversing the causes of suffering, and the benefits of reversing the result, which is suffering. The first is: ^3-10

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-11]]

Their poisons, which are extremely fierce,
Whether stationary or moving,
Even if they have been eaten or drunk,
Are completely eliminated through mere recollection. ^3-11

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-12]]

This means: The poisons such as attachment, which are afflictions that obstruct the attainment of the temporary and ultimate benefits and cause great suffering, are "fierce" because they rob one of the life of high status and definite goodness, and they are "great" because they generate the suffering of cyclic existence and the lower realms. Regarding these, as well as poisons that are "stationary," such as aconite found in the inanimate world, or "moving," such as snake venom found in sentient beings—even if one has eaten, drunk, or touched them, by merely remembering the Goddess’s form and reciting the praise, one will attain complete purification. ^3-12

##### Second, the benefits of reversing the result, which is suffering. ^3-3-1-3-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-13]]

The second section, the benefits of reversing the result, which is suffering, is: ^3-13

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-14]]

The masses of suffering of those tormented
By demons, epidemics, and poisons, are utterly cast off. ^3-14

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-15]]

This means: The masses of suffering and harm generated by being tormented by demons such as the nam-gru, epidemics such as the one-day fever, and external or internal poisons, are capable of being utterly cast off, because their causes can be easily eliminated. ^3-15

#### Second, the benefits that arise for others to be accomplished. ^3-3-2-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-16]]

The second section, the benefits that arise for others, is: ^3-16

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-17]]

It applies to other sentient beings as well. ^3-17

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-18]]

This means: Not only do the benefits arise for the practitioner themselves, but even if one recites the words of praise while focusing on the sake of other sentient beings, such as a benefactor, the benefits explained above and below will arise just as they are. This is what is meant. ^3-18

### Fourth, a brief presentation by way of enumeration. ^3-4-0

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-19]]

The fourth section, a brief presentation by way of enumeration, is: ^3-19

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-20]]

If recited two, three, or seven times,
Those desiring a child will obtain a child,
And those desiring wealth will obtain wealth.
All desires will be attained,
And obstacles will be non-existent and individually destroyed. ^3-20

![[bo-དངུལ་ཆུ་དྷརྨ་བྷ་དྲ།#^3-21]]

This means: If one divides each of the two parts of the day and night into three sessions each, and recites the words of praise seven times in each session, those who desire a child to hold their lineage and religious tradition will obtain a child or a student; those who desire wealth to accomplish the happiness of this and future lives will easily obtain wealth and abundant resources; and so forth. In short, one will obtain all the desires one may have, whether they are temporary or ultimate, within the favorable circumstances. Regarding that, obstacles and hindrances of the unfavorable circumstances will not arise anew, and those that have already arisen will be individually destroyed without remainder by the power of the Jetsunma, depending on the occasion. This is what is meant. ^3-21
