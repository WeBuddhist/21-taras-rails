# Notes on Karma Maitri's Praise to Tārā ^0

## Expression of Homage ^I-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^I-1]]

Namo guru ārya-tārāyai! ^I-1

## First, the actual praise ^1-0

### First prostration ^1-1-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-1]]

The Praise to the Twenty-One Tārās consists of the actual praise and the benefits. Regarding the first, there are twenty-one [verses], and as for the first: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā. If one asks what qualities or distinctions she possesses, it is because she liberates sentient beings in an instant that she is "Swift" (nyur ma); because she vanquishes the demonic hosts of defilements that she is "Heroic" (dpa' mo). Because the light of her eyes is like a momentary flash of lightning, that Tārā is the one who has emerged from the opening stamens of the lotus flower of the face of Avalokiteśvara, the protector of the three worlds—the subterranean, the terrestrial, and the celestial—which was born from a water-born [lotus]. ^1-1

### Second prostration ^1-2-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-2]]

Regarding the second: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who possesses a face that is white and beautiful, like a hundred full autumn moons gathered together, and who blazes with the brilliant white light of a thousand gathered stars that cannot be rivaled. ^1-2

### Third prostration ^1-3-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-3]]

Regarding the third: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, whose body is beautiful like gold and slightly blue in color, and whose hand is adorned with a water-born lotus that is fully blossomed. Furthermore, she is the one who pacifies defilements through generosity, diligence, and austerity—that is, ethical discipline—as well as patience and meditative concentration. ^1-3

### Fourth prostration ^1-4-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-4]]

Regarding the fourth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who has become an object of veneration like the crown protuberance (uṣṇīṣa) of the Tathāgatas, and who acts to be completely victorious over infinite adversaries such as the Maras. She is the very essence of having attained the state of the ten perfections and the ten stages without exception; because she has become the mother of all the great bodhisattvas—the heirs of the Victors—she is Tārā, who is relied upon intensely with great devotion. ^1-4

### Fifth prostration ^1-5-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-5]]

Regarding the fifth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who fills the desire realm, the directions—that is, the form realm—and space—that is, the formless realm—with the syllables of TUTTĀRE HŪṂ, and who tramples the seven worlds—the five classes of beings of the desire realm and the two higher realms—underfoot, and who, by the power of her mantra (vidyā), is able to, or acts to, summon all without exception under her control. ^1-5

### Sixth prostration ^1-6-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-6]]

Regarding the sixth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and the various Īśvaras—that is, Maheśvara—and who is constantly praised from before by hosts of Bhūtas, Vetālas dwelling in charnel grounds, Gandharvas such as those with five topknots, and hosts of Yakṣas such as Smu-g-'dzin. ^1-6

### Homage to the seventh ^1-7-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-7]]

Regarding the seventh: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who utterly destroys the magical wheels of adversaries with fierce sounds such as TRAT and PHAT from her mouth, who tramples the seven worlds with her feet—the right bent and the left extended—and who dwells in the midst of a swirling, intensely blazing mass of fire. ^1-7

### Homage to the eighth ^1-8-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-8]]

Regarding the eighth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who protects from the eight great fears through her speech, such as TURE, and who completely destroys the four Māra-champions, such as the Devaputra-māra. She manifests a frowning expression upon her face, which is as beautiful as a lotus born from water, and she slays all enemies, such as the mental afflictions, without leaving even one remaining. ^1-8

### Homage to the ninth ^1-9-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-9]]

Regarding the ninth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is adorned at her heart with the fingers of her left hand in the mudrā of granting refuge, which is exceedingly beautiful and symbolizes the Three Jewels. She is the one who fills all directions without exception with a turbulent mass of her own light, which radiates from the part of her right hand—adorned with a wheel on its palm—that performs the mudrā of supreme generosity, thereby overwhelming all other lights. ^1-9

### Homage to the tenth ^1-10-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-10]]

Regarding the tenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is adorned with a precious crown and a garland of jewels that spread a swirling and blazing garland of light, which causes supreme joy to arise, fulfilling the hopes of all beings. Through the sound of the mantra TUTTĀRA and others, which she utters with laughter and great mirth, she brings under her power the Māras—the lords of the Heaven of Controlling Others' Emanations—and furthermore, all the worlds. ^1-10

### Homage to the eleventh ^1-11-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-11]]

Regarding the eleventh: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who possesses the power to summon all the hosts of those who protect the earth, such as the ten guardians of the directions, without exception, as her servants. Through the light of the syllable HŪṂ, which marks her heart while her wrathful face quivers with a frown, she completely liberates all beings who have become terrified and destitute from their suffering, and establishes them in supreme bliss. ^1-11

### Homage to the twelfth ^1-12-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-12]]

Regarding the twelfth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is adorned upon her head with a crescent moon—that is, a half-moon—and who is the embodiment of all ornaments that blaze with an intensely white light. From within her matted locks, where the Lord of the Family, Amitābha, resides, she constantly emits a most brilliant light for the sake of sentient beings. ^1-12

### Homage to the thirteenth ^1-13-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-13]]

Regarding the thirteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who dwells in the midst of a garland of fire that blazes intensely like the fire at the end of an eon—that is, the time of destruction. By the posture of her right leg extended and her left bent, she creates joy by bestowing happiness, surrounded by a garland of light, and she completely destroys all the armies of enemies. ^1-13

### Homage to the fourteenth ^1-14-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-14]]

Regarding the fourteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who strikes the surface of the earth with the palm of her hand, accompanied by the threatening mudrā, and who tramples it with her feet. She assumes a wrathful, frowning expression, and by the light radiating from the syllable HŪṂ in the palm of her right hand, she shatters the seven levels of the earth and removes the obstacles of all sentient beings. ^1-14

### Fifteenth Homage ^1-15-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-15]]

Regarding the fifteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is the Blissful One of an uncontaminated nature; the Virtuous One who has abandoned the afflictions; the Peaceful One who is free from rough contact; and who is the Tārā whose very sphere of experience is the supreme, utterly peaceful state of nirvāṇa that does not abide. To Tārā, who is capable of destroying all great evils and obscurations by means of the mantra that is perfectly endowed with the syllables SVĀHĀ and OṂ. ^1-15

### Sixteenth Homage ^1-16-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-16]]

Regarding the sixteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who, by the light of the Tārā that creates great joy—surrounded at the center of the wheel at her heart by OṂ and on the spokes by the syllables of the mantra—completely shatters the bodies of enemies. From the HŪṂ of the awareness that arranges the speech of the ten syllables, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ, in the form of a wheel, she manifests as Tārā herself in the manner of a wrathful deity who destroys the hosts of Māra, saying OṂ NAMAḤ TĀRE NAMO HĀRE HŪṂ HĀRE SVĀHĀ. ^1-16

### Seventeenth Homage ^1-17-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-17]]

Regarding the seventeenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, for whom "Ture" means the Swift One, and who, by stamping her feet upon the ground, is endowed with the very essence of the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ. By that, she is the one whose nature is to cause the mountains located outside the three-thousandfold world system—such as Mount Meru, Mandara, and the Vindhya mountain—and, in short, the three worlds of existence, to tremble. ^1-17

### Eighteenth Homage ^1-18-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-18]]

Regarding the eighteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who holds in her hand the moon—the one marked with a deer—which has the nature of the form of a celestial lake possessing special qualities such as being clear and cool. By the syllable PHAṬ at the end of pronouncing TĀRA twice, she is the Tārā who clears away, without exception, all poisons of the stable and the moving for any sentient being. ^1-18

### Nineteenth Homage ^1-19-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-19]]

Regarding the nineteenth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is served at her feet by the kings of the hosts of gods—Brahmā, Indra, and the common gods—and by all the hosts of Kinnaras. She is the Tārā who, by the majesty of her joyful and complete armor of divine form and mantra, dispels all disputes and bad dreams. ^1-19

### Twentieth Homage ^1-20-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-20]]

Regarding the twentieth: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, whose two eyes are like the fully expanded sun and moon, radiating and blazing with brilliant light, and who, by the utterance of two hara syllables and tuttāre—the syllables of the vidyā-mantra—acts to dispel all contagious diseases through their extreme intensity. ^1-20

### Twenty-First Homage ^1-21-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-21]]

Regarding the twenty-first: "Homage." To whom? To Tārā, who is perfectly endowed with the power to pacify afflictions through the light of the three suchnesses—that is, the arrangement of the three syllables—and who destroys the hosts of spirits, zombies, and yakṣas, as well as fears and harms, by means of the ten-syllable mantra, the supreme ture. ^1-21

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^1-22]]

This praise, adorned with the root mantra—that is, the peaceful and wrathful mantras previously taught—and the "homage" are directed toward Tārā, who possesses the twenty-one emanations. ^1-22

## Second: The Benefits ^2-0

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-1]]

The second part concerns the benefits: A person endowed with an intellect possessing genuine devotion to the Goddess Tārā should recite this king of praises with a mind of intense clarity. ^2-1

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-2]]

Having risen at dusk for wrathful [practices] and at dawn for peaceful [practices] and so forth, with devotion to her form, one should recollect her in the mind, which protects from fears and thoroughly dispels all fearlessness. Furthermore, all sins that are the causes of miserable rebirths are thoroughly pacified, and all the sufferings of miserable rebirths, which are the results, are destroyed. ^2-2

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-3]]

Seventy million Victors will swiftly confer empowerment, and in this very life, one will attain great happiness; ultimately, one will proceed to that very ultimate state of Buddhahood. ^2-3

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-4]]

The poisons that act as obstacles to attaining that are the great and violent ones, namely the view of a self, thieves, and flesh-eaters and the like, which are dependent and abiding, meaning they are constantly harmful; furthermore, the poisons of beings such as snakes and the like are also completely neutralized merely by remembering her. ^2-4

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-5]]

The masses of suffering and immediate harm caused by being afflicted by demons, epidemics, and poisons are thoroughly cast off, and this benefit also arises for sentient beings other than oneself. ^2-5

![[bo-ཀརྨ་མཻ་ཏྲི།#^2-6]]

If one recites this praise seven times during the two periods of day and night, or three times during the day and three times during the night, those desiring a child will obtain a child, those desiring wealth will obtain wealth, and in short, all desires contemplated in the mind will be fulfilled; all obstacles and hindrances will be absent and will not arise, and even if they do arise, they will be individually destroyed by the power of the Jetsunma herself. ^2-6

This is written as a personal memorandum, recorded as notes from what was spoken by the accomplished masters, under the name of Karma Maitri. Śubham. ^a-1
