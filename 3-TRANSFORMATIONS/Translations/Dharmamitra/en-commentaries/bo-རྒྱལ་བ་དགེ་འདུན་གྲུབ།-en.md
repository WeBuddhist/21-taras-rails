# The Precious Garland Tikka: A Commentary on the Twenty-One Homages to Tārā ^0

## Expression of homage ^I-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^I-1]]

Homage to the Noble Triple Gem. ^I-1

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^I-2]]

To the Bhagavatī Tārā, who is the mother of the Victors
And the embodiment of the enlightened activity of all the buddhas of the three times,
I prostrate with devotion of body, speech, and mind. ^I-2

## Indication of the tantric source ^II-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^II-1]]

The Praise in Twenty-One Homages was spoken by the perfectly enlightened Buddha Vairocana, who is extremely skilled in explaining things according to one’s capacity. Regarding this, most of the scholars of the past have stated that this tantra belongs to the Kriyātantra among the four classes of tantra. Furthermore, this Praise in Twenty-One Homages was spoken in the third chapter of the thirty-five-chapter tantra entitled "The Praise to Tārā, Mother of All the Tathāgatas." One should examine whether or not this is Kriyātantra, for it is also said that this Praise in Twenty-One Homages has been interpreted as Anuttarayogatantra. ^II-1

## First, the introductory setting ^1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^1-1]]

This has two parts: the prologue and the actual explanation. The first is: Then the Blessed One said to the youthful Mañjuśrī, “Mañjuśrī, this Mother is the mother of all the buddhas of the three times. Mañjuśrī, for that reason, you should keep in mind this praise offered by all the buddhas of the three times.” Then the Blessed One spoke the dhāraṇī-mantra of praise, and so forth. ^1-1

## Second, the actual explanation ^2-0

### Brief presentation ^2-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-1]]

Regarding the second part, it is divided into two: "This praise with the root mantra" and "the twenty-one homages." Some say that the praise with the root mantra is Namaḥ sarva-tathāgatānām, tadyathā, oṃ namo su-ka-sa-nama tāre pāramitā. However, the Great Translator Panglo says that the version found in the Sanskrit manuscript, which he translated, is: Oṃ, homage to Tārā, the heroic Tāre; with Tuttāre you dispel all fears; with Ture you grant all purposes; I bow deeply to you, along with the syllable Svāhā. Yet, the masters state that the praise with the root mantra is the set of twenty verses of praise interspersed with the mantras presented here, and the twenty-one homages are the twenty-one verses of homage that are interspersed with them. ^2-1

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-2]]

Therefore, there are three parts: the brief presentation, the detailed explanation, and the presentation of the benefits. The first is: Oṃ, homage to the Noble and Venerable Tārā. Regarding this, Oṃ signifies taking refuge, offering worship, or purifying what is impure. Regarding Venerable Lady (rje btsun ma), Venerable (rje) means the supreme among the mothers who give birth to all the buddhas of the three times. Lady (btsun) means possessing the vows: externally, the individual liberation vows; internally, the bodhisattva vows; and secretly, the mantra vows. Noble (phags ma) means having gone far from the non-virtues abandoned through seeing, and having transcended cyclic existence and nirvāṇa. Tārā (sgrol ma) means one who liberates from the ocean of suffering. Mother (ma) means one who performs the benefit of beings equally, without partiality toward any sentient being. Homage (phyag) means to sweep away the obscurations of karma and afflictions. To (tshal) means the act of paying respect with body, speech, and mind. ^2-2

### Second, the detailed explanation ^2-2-0

#### First, praise by way of historical account ^2-2-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-3]]

The second part consists of three sections: praise based on history, praise based on the form of the body, and praise based on the nature of enlightened activity. The first is: Homage to Tārā, the swift and heroic, whose eyes are like a momentary flash of lightning, she who arose from the opening stamens of the lotus face of the Lord of the Three Worlds. This is to be understood as "Homage to Tārā." To whom? To Tārā. How did she arise? When the Noble Avalokiteśvara, the protector and refuge of the three realms—the desire, form, and formless realms, which are based on the five or four aggregates of fear—saw that although he had liberated countless beings from cyclic existence, their number had not diminished, he wept. She arose from the opening stamens of the utpala flower that was born from the water of his tears. Because she is swift in the purpose of sentient beings, she said, "I shall perform the service of swiftly liberating them from cyclic existence, so please do not shed tears." Having requested this, she is "heroic" in turning back the battle of cyclic existence. Therefore, her two eyes are like lightning that flashes in an instant, gazing upon the three worlds. Regarding this, the Paṇḍita Nyima Bepa (Sūryagupta) called her "Tārā, the supremely heroic." ^2-3

#### Second, praise by way of the aspects of the form ^2-2-2-0

##### First, praise from the perspective of the sambhogakāya. ^2-2-2-1-0

###### First, praise from the perspective of the peaceful form. ^2-2-2-1-1-0

####### First, praise from the perspective of a clear complexion and radiating light. ^2-2-2-1-1-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-4]]

The second part consists of two sections: praise based on the nature of the enjoyment body and praise based on the nature of the truth body. The first of these consists of two sections: praise based on the peaceful form and praise based on the wrathful form. The first of these consists of six homages; the first is praise based on the clarity of her facial complexion and the radiating light: Homage to her whose face is like a hundred full autumn moons gathered together, and so forth. This refers to the autumn moon, which is free from dust due to clouds and wind, and is "full" in the sense of being a full moon that has completed its growth and has not yet begun to wane. "A hundred gathered" means she possesses a face that radiates light even more than a hundred such moons piled together. From that face, she is the one who blazes with light that is more brilliant than even a gathering of a thousand stars. Alternatively, if one reads it as "the two eights are arranged," it means that within her face, sixteen moon-like teeth are arranged, and her face radiates light exceeding that of a gathering of a thousand stars; and by saying "those two eights are arranged," it signifies the thirty-two excellent marks. This is explained as Tārā, the White One Possessing Radiance. ^2-4

####### Second, praise from the perspective of body color, hand symbols, and causes. ^2-2-2-1-1-2-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-5]]

The second part consists of praise based on her body color, hand emblem, and causes: Homage to her whose hand is beautifully adorned with a gold and blue water-born lotus, and so forth. "Blue" refers to her body color, and "gold" signifies the purity of her complexion, as it is stated that she is Tārā who possesses a golden color. Her left hand is beautifully adorned at the heart with a water-born lotus, that is, an utpala; holding it so that it blossoms near her ear is a sign of the perfection of the ten perfections. Therefore, it is the sphere of activity of generosity, diligence, austerity—which is ethical discipline—patience, meditative concentration, and the perfection of wisdom. "Peace" refers to the pacification of discordant factors such as stinginess, laziness, unethical conduct, anger, distraction, and wrong views; this is the sphere of activity of Tārā, meaning it arises from those causes. ^2-5

####### Third, praise from the perspective of being respectfully served by the Victors and their heirs. ^2-2-2-1-1-3-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-6]]

The third part consists of praise based on how she is revered by the Victors and the Victors' heirs: Homage to the Ushnīṣa of the Tathāgatas, and so forth. Because she is the mother of all Tathāgatas, she is borne like the crown protuberance (uṣṇīṣa) on their heads. Therefore, she is the one who acts with complete victory over the infinite obscurations of the knowable, such as the misfortunes of this life, the obscurations of the afflictions, and those pertaining to the three spheres. "Revered by the Victors' heirs" means she is the essence of the ten stages without exception, and is revered with the utmost respect by the bodhisattvas, who are the heirs of the Victors having attained the ten perfections. She is known as Tārā who is adorned with the uṣṇīṣa. ^2-6

####### Fourth, praise from the perspective of suppressing opposing forces. ^2-2-2-1-1-4-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-7]]

The fourth part consists of praise based on how she suppresses the discordant: Homage to you who fill the realms of desire, the directions, and space with the syllables TUTTARA and HUM, and so forth. The speech of TUTTARA and HUM and the syllable HUM at her heart signify emptiness and compassion. By means of this, she tramples with her feet—which have the essence of emptiness and compassion—the seven worlds: the three lower realms of desire, the two of gods and humans in the desire realm, the form realm, and the space of the formless realm. She is the one who has the power to summon all of them to happiness without exception. She is known as Tārā who utters the sound of HUM. ^2-7

####### Fifth, praise by the great worldly gods. ^2-2-2-1-1-5-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-8]]

The fifth part consists of praise by the great worldly deities: Homage to you. To whom? To the Tārā who is worshipped by the lords of various kinds, such as Indra, the lord of gods, Agni, Brahmā, and Vāyu. Furthermore, she is the one who is praised from before by the lord of bhūtas, Gaṇapati; the lord of vetālas, Maheśvara; the gandharva Pañcaśikha and others; and the lord of yakṣas, Vaiśravaṇa, along with their many hosts of retinues. She is known as Tārā who is victorious. ^2-8

####### Sixth, praise from the perspective of conquering adversaries. ^2-2-2-1-1-6-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-9]]

The sixth part consists of praise based on how she vanquishes adversaries: Homage to you who utterly destroy the magical wheels of others with the sounds TRAT and PHAT, and so forth. She is the one who destroys the power invoked by others through fierce mantras, as well as the magical wheels of sorcery and the like that obstruct temporary and ultimate happiness. She is known as Tārā who vanquishes the disputatious. Regarding her peaceful posture, the right leg drawn in signifies the realization of emptiness, and the left leg extended signifies compassion. Having trampled the three realms with such feet of emptiness and compassion, the line blazing amidst a swirling mass of fire refers to her having become a wrathful figure externally, sitting majestically in the midst of a conflagration with a slightly wrathful demeanor even now. ^2-9

###### Second, praise by way of the wrathful form. ^2-2-2-1-2-0

####### First, the qualities of overcoming the māras and the two obscurations. ^2-2-2-1-2-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-10]]

The second part consists of praise based on her wrathful form, which contains seven homages. The first, concerning the quality of overcoming the demons and the two obscurations, is: Homage to TURE, the great fearsome one, who completely destroys the champions of Mara, and so forth. TURE means Tārā; the great fearsome one is the wrathful female; the champions of Mara are the mental afflictions, and thus she is the one who completely destroys the four Maras, such as those. Furthermore, she makes her lotus-like face possess a wrathful frown, adopting the appearance of a wrathful female. By adopting this wrathful appearance, she is the one who slays—along with their latent tendencies—the enemies that obstruct the attainment of liberation, namely the obscurations of mental afflictions, and the enemies that obstruct omniscience, namely the obscurations of knowledge, without exception. She is known as Tārā who bestows the supreme empowerment. ^2-10

####### Second, praise by way of the hand symbols on the right and left. ^2-2-2-1-2-2-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-11]]

The second part consists of praise based on the hand symbols of her right and left hands. Regarding the hand symbol of the left hand: Homage to her whose fingers, in the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels, beautifully adorn her heart, means that she holds an utpala with her thumb and ring finger, and is adorned by the manner in which her index, middle, and little fingers are extended at her heart. Regarding the hand symbol of the right hand: Homage to her, adorned with a wheel in every direction without exception, radiating a turbulent mass of her own brilliance, means that the palm of her right hand, which performs the mudrā of supreme generosity, is adorned with a wheel, and by the light of the wheel, which is her own hand symbol, she makes the other masses of light turbulent, meaning she overwhelms them. She is known as the Tārā of the Khadiravaṇa forest. ^2-11

####### Third, praise by way of the crown ornament and the sound of laughter. ^2-2-2-1-2-3-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-12]]

The third part consists of praise based on her crown ornament and the sound of her laughter. Homage to her whose crown ornament radiates with supreme joy, spreading garlands of light, means that through the Noble One’s ornament, she fulfills the desires of the disciples who have faith and devotion, and her majesty is the garland of light that overwhelms other lights, interspersed with her jewel crown. Laughing, she laughs aloud with the sound of the mantra TĀRA, means she brings the maras and the eight great gods of the world under her power. She is known as the Tārā who dispels sorrow. ^2-12

####### Fourth, praise by way of the ten directional protectors accomplishing enlightened activity. ^2-2-2-1-2-4-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-13]]

The fourth part consists of praise based on her summoning the ten guardians of the directions to perform enlightened activities. Homage to her who guards the earth refers to the ten guardians of the directions, and all the hosts means she has the power to summon them together with their retinues; having summoned them all, she causes them to perform enlightened activities. With a quivering frown means that, while she abides in her wrathful form, by the power of the light rays from the syllable HŪṂ at her heart, she completely liberates all those who are destitute and tormented by suffering from that suffering. She is known as the Tārā who summons. ^2-13

####### Fifth, praise by way of the crown ornament. ^2-2-2-1-2-5-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-14]]

The fifth part consists of praise based on her crown ornaments. Homage to her whose head is adorned with a crescent moon, and whose every ornament blazes intensely, refers to the actual crown ornament, the crescent moon, meaning she is adorned with the moon of the first day of the lunar month, and from all the moon ornaments, rays of light that dispel suffering blaze intensely. From Amitābha amidst her matted locks, she forever radiates a brilliant light, refers to the second crown ornament, meaning that Amitābha abides within the hair at the crown of her head, and for the sake of sentient beings, she forever—that is, abundantly—radiates light. She is known as the Tārā of Auspicious Radiance. ^2-14

####### Sixth, praise by way of the wrathful posture. ^2-2-2-1-2-6-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-15]]

The sixth part consists of praise based on her wrathful posture. Homage to her who dwells within a garland of blazing fire like that at the end of an eon, with right leg extended and left bent, she who is surrounded by joy and completely destroys the armies of enemies, means that, just as the fire at the end of an eon burns the earth and stones with heat more intense than seven suns, she shows her wrathful manner of abiding in the midst of a garland of blazing flames of primordial wisdom; with her right leg extended and left bent, she completely destroys the armies of enemies—the mental afflictions—of those disciples who delight in turning the wheel of Dharma with faith. She is known as the Tārā who ripens. ^2-15

####### Seventh, praise by way of light rays radiating from the syllable Hūṃ. ^2-2-2-1-2-7-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-16]]

The seventh part consists of praise based on the radiating of light rays from the syllable HŪṂ. Homage to her who strikes the surface of the earth with her palms and tramples it with her feet; with a wrathful frown and the syllable HŪṂ, she shatters the seven levels, means that, regarding "she who makes a wrathful frown," she strikes the earth with the palm of her right foot in the manner of a wrathful deity, and by performing the threatening mudra, she shatters—that is, dries up—the ocean of existence of the seven levels of the world by means of the syllable HŪṂ at her heart. She is known as the Tārā who moves with a wrathful frown. ^2-16

##### Second, praise by way of the dharmakāya. ^2-2-2-2-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-17]]

The second part consists of praise based on the Dharmakāya. Homage to the Blissful, Virtuous, and Peaceful One, she whose very domain is the peace of nirvāṇa, means, according to Jetsun Drakpa: "Blissful" means she possesses uncontaminated bliss; "Virtuous" means she is virtuous because she is free from the afflictions to be abandoned; "Peaceful" means she is the peace of the result, suffering; and "She whose very domain is the peace of nirvāṇa" refers to the peace of conceptual thought. Furthermore, some scholars say: regarding the nature of the unborn, having cut through superimpositions through hearing and contemplation on the path of accumulation, she is "Blissful" because there is no unhappiness; on the path of preparation, having experienced it through meditative realization, she is "Virtuous" because there are no signs of the conceptual thoughts of coarse afflictions; on the path of seeing, having been freed from seeds, she is "Peaceful" because the suffering of the result—birth, aging, sickness, death, and so on—is pacified; and on the path of no-more-learning, she is the one who has manifested the Dharmakāya, the nirvāṇa in which even latent tendencies are pacified. Perfectly endowed with the syllables Oṃ and Svāhā, she completely destroys great negativity, means the ten-syllable mantra, with Svāhā at the end, Oṃ at the beginning, and eight syllables in the middle, perfectly endowed, she is the one who destroys the great negativity of the ignorance of grasping at true existence. This is the praise based on the Dharmakāya of the speech and mind. She is known as the Tārā of virtuous and peaceful excellence. ^2-17

#### Third, praise by way of enlightened activity. ^2-2-3-0

##### First, the enlightened activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras. ^2-2-3-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-18]]

Third, regarding the praise through the aspect of enlightened activity, there are six homages. The first, the enlightened activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras, is: "Homage to Her who is surrounded by intense joy and utterly shatters the bodies of enemies; the ten-syllable speech is arranged, and She is Tārā herself, arising from the HŪṂ of awareness." The arrangement of the ten-syllable speech mentioned here is the mantra: OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ. The HŪṂ of awareness is: OṂ NAMA TĀRE NAMO HARE HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ, which is the wrathful mantra. Through these two, for those disciples who are intensely joyful in turning the wheel of Dharma with complete devotion, she is the one who utterly shatters the enemies of liberation—those who cling to the internal body as a self and those who cling to external things as belonging to a self—and thus she appears as Tārā who destroys clinging. ^2-18

##### Second, the enlightened activity of the wrathful female deity shaking the three realms. ^2-2-3-2-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-19]]

Second, regarding the enlightened activity of the wrathful one shaking the three realms: "Homage to Her who stamps Her feet, the Ture, whose essence is the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ," and so forth. By stamping the feet of Ture, who is born from the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ, She is able to shake the three worlds, such as Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya, in the external realm. She is known as Tārā who accomplishes happiness. ^2-19

##### Third, the enlightened activity of dispelling the poisons of the stable and moving world. ^2-2-3-3-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-20]]

Third, regarding the enlightened activity of clearing away the poisons of the stable and the moving, it is: "Homage to Her who holds in Her hand the deer-marked one, which has the form of a celestial lake," and so forth. She holds in Her hand the moon mandala, which is like a celestial lake and marked with a hare; this is a sign of clearing away the poison of the stable, the torment of the mental afflictions. By reciting the two TĀRA syllables and the syllable PHAṬ, She clears away, without exception, the poisons of the moving. She is known as Tārā who is completely victorious. ^2-20

##### Fourth, the enlightened activity of dispelling strife and bad dreams. ^2-2-3-4-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-21]]

Fourth, regarding the enlightened activity of dispelling disputes and bad dreams: "Homage to the king of the hosts of gods," refers to the king of the desire-realm gods, Śakra, and the great Brahmā of the form-realm, who are the kings of the divine hosts; she is Tārā herself, who is respectfully served by the crowns of the kings of gods and kinnaras, such as the great Druma. If one focuses one's mind with single-pointed joy upon the greatness of her peaceful and wrathful form and the armor of her peaceful and wrathful mantras, then, through the majesty of her blessings, the disputes of others and bad dreams are dispelled. She is known as Tārā who burns away suffering. ^2-21

##### Fifth, the enlightened activity of Tārā in dispelling infectious diseases. ^2-2-3-5-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-22]]

Fifth, regarding the enlightened activity of Tārā in clearing away infectious diseases: "Homage to her whose right eye is like the full sun and moon, and whose left eye is like the full moon," although it is stated this way, it refers to the wrathful eye like the full sun and the peaceful eye like the full moon, both of which radiate brilliantly clear light. By reciting the wrathful mantra, the two HARA syllables, and the peaceful mantra, the TUTTĀRA mantra, she clears away even the most violent infectious diseases. She is known as Tārā who is the source of attainments. ^2-22

##### Sixth, the enlightened activity of subduing spirits and zombies. ^2-2-3-6-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-23]]

Sixth, regarding the enlightened activity of subduing spirits and zombies: "Homage to her, endowed with the three suchnesses," refers to the suchness of body, speech, and mind; the suchness of body is the Oṃ placed at the crown, the suchness of speech is the Āḥ placed at the throat, and the suchness of mind is the Hūṃ placed at the heart. She is the one who possesses the firm power of this arrangement, as well as the power to pacify the six afflictions. For subduing the shifting poisonous spirits, zombies, and hosts of yakṣas, the supreme one is Ture, Tārā herself. She is known as Tārā who brings everything to perfection. ^2-23

### Third, the explanation of the benefits ^2-3-0

#### First, the distinction of the intention regarding the benefits ^2-3-1-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-24]]

Third, regarding the benefits, there are four parts: the distinction of the intention, the distinction of the time, the explanation of the actual benefits, and the summary of the benefits through the count. Regarding the first: this praise of the twenty-one verses of the peaceful and wrathful root mantras, and the homage performed with respect through the three doors, is twenty-one. ^2-24

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-25]]

"Endowed with genuine respect for the Goddess Tārā," means not just with the mouth or just with words, but by one who possesses the wisdom of a one-pointed mind, and who recites them with supreme faith. ^2-25

#### Second, the distinction of time ^2-3-2-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-26]]

Second, regarding the time: having risen from bed at dusk and dawn, "recollection" means that, in general, by merely recollecting the body, the speech of the mantra, and the praise, and in particular by recollecting the state of the wrathful one at the time of dusk and the peaceful body at the time of dawn, one is granted total fearlessness from obstacles such as illnesses, spirits, and untimely death in this life. Because all sins that are the causes of miserable rebirths in the future are thoroughly pacified, it is the very destruction of all results of miserable rebirths. ^2-26

#### Third, the explanation of the actual benefits ^2-3-3-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-27]]

Third, regarding the benefits: Seventy million Conquerors will swiftly and continuously confer empowerment upon one. In this very life, one will attain the common greatness of the siddhis, such as bringing humans and non-humans under one's influence, and one will proceed to the ultimate attainment of the supreme siddhi, the state of Buddhahood. The poison that obstructs that state of Buddhahood is the great and violent poison of wrong views. That which brings this to completion and causes it to remain permanently is the dispelling of the stable poisons, which is described as destroying the actions of disparaging the mode of existence of phenomena through the dharma of ignorance, and the actions of having hostility toward the dharma and those who teach the dharma through intense hatred. ^2-27

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-28]]

Furthermore, the poison of moving creatures, such as the six types of insects and the six types of snakes, as well as what is eaten or drunk—the poison of food and drink itself—is also completely eliminated by merely recollecting the Goddess's body and so forth, thereby attaining the removal of the causes of suffering. ^2-28

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-29]]

The result of dispelling intense suffering is that the masses of suffering of birth, which are afflicted by spirits, epidemics, and poisons, are thoroughly cast off; this refers to one's own welfare. Regarding the welfare of others, it applies to other sentient beings as well. This means that if one, possessing devotion for the sake of others, rises at dusk and dawn and recites the praise while recollecting the Goddess's body and so forth in one's mind, one will attain the benefits as described above. ^2-29

#### Fourth, a summary presentation of the benefits by way of number ^2-3-4-0

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-30]]

The fourth point, summarizing the benefits by way of numbers, is the phrase "If this is recited two, three, or seven times." This is according to the well-known four-mandala system. Regarding its meaning, some poets say: "If recited twice, those desiring a child will obtain a child; if recited three times, those desiring wealth will obtain wealth; if recited seven times, all desires will be fulfilled." ^2-30

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-31]]

Panglo says regarding the phrase "two, three": "Two" refers to the distinction of the person who is the basis for the practitioner; the phrase "whoever is intelligent" indicates the sharp-facultied follower of dharma, and the phrase "with great faith" indicates the dull-facultied follower of faith. "Three" indicates the distinction of time; the phrase "rising at dusk and dawn" refers to the period from daybreak until the dusk session. The phrase "If recited seven times" refers to the distinction of number; for those three times, one recites seven times each, and if one recites twenty-one times in total, all desires—such as the granting of all fearlessness—will be attained. Since it is not possible for obstacles to arise in the attainment of that, it is said that there are no hindrances, and each of their antidotes will overcome each of the things to be abandoned. ^2-31

![[bo-རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ།#^2-32]]

Rinpoche Butön says: "Two" refers to the two parts of day and night. "Three" means that for each of those, there are three sessions; for the day, there are the morning, midday, and afternoon sessions, and for the night, there are the dusk, midnight, and dawn sessions. If one recites seven times for each of these six sessions—making forty-two in total—over the course of a single day and night, those benefits will arise. ^2-32
