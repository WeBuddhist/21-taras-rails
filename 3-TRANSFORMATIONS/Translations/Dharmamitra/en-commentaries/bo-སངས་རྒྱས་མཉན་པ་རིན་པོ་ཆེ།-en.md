# Rain-Cloud of the Noble Lady's Blessings: A Concise Word-Commentary and Visualization Sequence for the Twenty-One Homages to Ārya Tārā ^0

## Homage ^I-0

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^I-1]]

Venerable Lady, who is filled with the youthful vitality of primordial wisdom,
By the mere remembrance of your feet, you cause the wanderers in suffering,
Together with the causes of their misery, to become but a mere name,
You, who act thus, are my eternal refuge throughout all my lifetimes. ^I-1

Having preceded this with an expression of homage, I shall now provide a word-commentary on the root text, the Praise to the Twenty-One Tārās, along with the associated visualization practices, explained in concise language to suit the dispositions of those in the present age who are prone to laziness and distraction. First: ^1-1

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-2]]

Oṃ! Homage to the Venerable Noble Tārā! ^1-2

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-3]]

Regarding this, "Oṃ" signifies the three vajras of the Noble Tārā’s body, speech, and mind. Because she constantly protects [beings] with non-conceptual compassion and has abandoned all faults and coarseness, she is the "Venerable Lady" (rje btsun ma). Because she has completely transcended the extremes of cyclic existence and peace, she is the "Noble One" (’phags ma). Because she liberates beings from the eight and sixteen great fears, she is "Tārā" (sgrol ma). Thus, possessing these qualities, I prostrate to you, Noble One, with supreme devotion. ^1-3

#### Word-by-word commentary ^1-2-1-0

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-4]]

Homage to Tārā, the swift and heroic,
Whose eyes are like a momentary flash of lightning,
She who arose from the opening of the stamens
Of the lotus-face of the Lord of the Three Worlds. ^1-4

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-5]]

Regarding this: To whom is the homage paid? She is the embodiment of the enlightened activity of all the victorious ones. She is the one who liberates [beings] from the karma and afflictions of cyclic existence, along with their results, through her great, immeasurable love. Because she is never negligent in connecting all beings to benefit and happiness, she is "Swift" (myur ma). Because she has unobstructed power and capability to act accordingly, she is "Heroic" (dpa' mo). Her wisdom-eye, which perceives the nature of reality, is "like a momentary flash of lightning" because it perceives without attachment or obstruction in a single instant; thus, she is the "One Possessing Wisdom." She is the Noble One who arose from the tears of great compassion—which are like the stamens and filaments opening within the lotus-flower face of the Noble Avalokiteśvara, the protector of the three worlds (the subterranean, the terrestrial, and the celestial). This is the word-commentary. ^1-5

#### Method of visualization ^1-2-2-0

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-6]]

The method of visualization is as follows: one should meditate on this Tārā, the Swift and Heroic, who increases the bodhicitta and brings appearances under control. Her body is red in color, and she possesses an expression of wrathful smiling and passion. She sits in the half-lotus posture upon a lotus and moon seat. She has one face and two hands; her right hand is in the gesture of supreme generosity, and her left is in the gesture of giving protection. Between the thumb and ring finger of the hand in the gesture of the Three Jewels, she holds a fully blossomed utpala flower, upon which rests a right-turning conch. One should meditate on the bodhicitta as pervading all the worlds. This is the sequence of visualization. ^1-6

One should meditate on her as opening the treasure of wisdom, in which the power of the intelligence that realizes things as they are blazes forth. This is the sequence of visualization. ^1-9

Likewise, you are the object of praise for the Noble One, Tārā, before whom there respectfully gather and offer praise those who proclaim your greatness in this world: the spirits such as the obstacles in the northeast; the zombies in the southwest, who are the truthful and excellent ones; the gandharvas, who are the retinue of Indra in the east; and the hosts of wealth-lords, such as the yakṣa Vaiśravaṇa in the north. Regarding this: To whom is the homage paid? Ture means "swift as lightning," and because You arise in the form of the Great Fearful One in order to swiftly protect beings from the dangerous defiles of deluded appearances, You are the One who completely destroys the champions of Mara—who are extremely difficult to tame—including the conceptualizations of the three poisons, the mental afflictions, the aggregates, and the Lord of Death. Your face, which resembles a beautiful lotus, is assumed in a form with a frowning expression in order to tame those who are perverse and malicious. Having done so, You are the One who slays and conquers, without exception, all enemies—the two obscurations that obstruct the path to enlightenment—within the state of the equality of the dharmadhātu. #### Method of Visualization ^1-10-2-0

The sixteen great fears are: 1. the fear of the lion of holding to morality and views as supreme; 2. the fear of the elephant of ignorance; 3. the fear of the fire of hatred; 4. the fear of the snake of jealousy; 5. the fear of the thief of wrong views; 6. the fear of the iron chains of desire and attachment; 7. the fear of the ocean of desire; 8. the fear of the flesh-eating demon of doubt; 9. the fear of the punishment of kings, which is the arrogance of secondary afflictions and violence; 10. the fear of the enemy of the wild and deceitful; 11. the fear of the demon of hypocrisy and forgetfulness; 12. the fear of the leper of shamelessness, lack of conscience, and lack of mindfulness; 13. the fear of the lack of faith and loss of spiritual friends; 14. the fear of the poverty of miserliness; 15. the fear of the thunderbolt of anger, resentment, and jealousy; 16. the fear of the loss of purpose due to laziness, heedlessness, and distraction; these are the sixteen. ^1-31

### Homage to the Tenth ^1-11-0

#### Word-by-word Commentary ^1-11-1-0

![[bo-སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་པ་རིན་པོ་ཆེ།#^1-32]]

Homage to You, radiant with supreme joy,
Whose crown-ornament spreads garlands of light,
Who, with the laughter of TUTTĀRE,

Regarding this: To whom is the homage offered? It is to You, who possess a body of major and minor marks that generates supreme joy in sentient beings, and from whose majestic and beautiful crown-ornament of jewels, various garlands of light radiate. Since the extensive commentaries on the twenty-one homages have few readers and their meaning is difficult to understand, hoping to be of benefit to those who are diligent in the regular practice of Tārā, I have based this on the commentaries of Tāranātha, Kunkhyen Jikme Lingpa, Gyalse Tenzin Rabgye, and others. I have made the word-by-word commentary as easy to understand as possible, arranged it with a balanced level of detail, and written it as one called Sangye Nyen Trul. May it become a cause for the benefit of all. ^a-1
