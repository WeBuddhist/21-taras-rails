Readings that **support** the current Hindi:
- 1-1: *myur ma* = swift to benefit beings, *dpa' mo* = heroic in turning back the battle of saṃsāra → "त्वरित और शूरवीर" (swift and heroic). "जिनके नेत्र क्षणिक विद्युत के समान हैं" (whose eyes are like momentary lightning) matches "her two eyes, like lightning that flashes in an instant, look on the three worlds". Her birth from the opening stamens, "प्रस्फुटित केसर से" (from the opened stamens), matches.
- 1-2: *kun du gang ba* = the fully full autumn moon, not yet waning → "शरद ऋतु के सौ पूर्ण चन्द्रमाओं" (a hundred full autumn moons). *stong phrag* → "सहस्रों" (thousands).
- 1-3: *dka' thub* = "that is, ethical discipline" → "शील" (ethical discipline) is right. *spyod yul* = "the perfection of wisdom" → "प्रज्ञा के गोचर" (the sphere of wisdom) is supported. The utpala adorning her hand → "जिनके हाथ … कमल से पूर्णतः अलंकृत हैं" (whose hand is fully adorned with a lotus).
- 1-4: she is carried like the crown uṣṇīṣa, as mother of all tathāgatas → "जो तथागतों का उष्णीष हैं" (who is the uṣṇīṣa of the tathāgatas). *rgyal ba'i sras* = "the Victors' children, the bodhisattvas, who have attained the ten perfections" → "पारमिताओं को प्राप्त कर चुके जिनपुत्र" (the Jina-sons who have attained the perfections). *जिनपुत्र* is a clear Sanskrit term for bodhisattvas (no ordinary-follower reading, unlike Vietnamese *Phật tử*), and the bodhisattvas, not Tārā, are the ones who attained the perfections.
- 1-5: *'dod / phyogs / nam mkha'* = desire, form, formless realms → "काम, रूप और अरूप लोकों" is exactly his reading. "सप्त लोकों" (seven worlds) matches his count: three lower realms, desire-realm gods and humans, form and formless. *'gugs* → "आकर्षित करने में समर्थ" (able to draw in).
- 1-6: the order Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu is kept → "शक्र, अग्नि, ब्रह्मा, वायु" (the Vietnamese had swapped two). *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural ("the chiefs") → "विभिन्न ईश्वरों" (the various Īśvaras). *'byung po* (chief Gaṇapati) = "भूत" (spirits). *ro langs* (chief Maheśvara) = "वेताल" (vetālas). *dri za* (chief Pañcaśikha) = "गन्धर्व" (gandharvas). *gnod sbyin* (chief Vaiśravaṇa) = "यक्षों के समूहों" (hosts of yakṣas). *mdun nas bstod* = "जिनके सम्मुख स्तुति की जाती है" (who are praised in her presence).
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* = the sorcery (*mthu*) and effigy devices (*byad ma*) of others → "शत्रुओं के यन्त्रों" (the enemies' yantras) fits. The legs, right drawn in (emptiness) and left extended (compassion), match "दाहिना पैर सिकोड़कर और बायां फैलाकर" (right leg drawn in and left extended).
- 1-8: *tu re* = "Tārā" → "जो तुरे … हैं" (who is Ture — her name). *'jigs pa chen mo* = "the wrathful one" → "महाभयंकर" (greatly terrifying) fits and avoids the "afraid" reading. *bdud kyi dpa' bo* = the afflictions, and with them the four māras → the literal "मार के शूरवीरों" (Māra's champions) stands. *dgra bo* = the two obscurations, so "सभी शत्रुओं" (all enemies) stands.
- 1-9: left hand, with thumb and ring finger holding the utpala and three fingers extended at the heart, is the Three Jewels mudrā → "त्रिरत्न की मुद्रा से, अपनी अंगुलियों द्वारा हृदय को … अलंकृत" (with the Three Jewels mudrā, adorning her heart with her fingers). **One** wheel on the right palm → "चक्र" (wheel), singular.

- 1-18: "फट् बीजाक्षर" (the seed-syllable PHAṬ) uses *बीजाक्षर*, which the word list reserves for the named syllable HŪṂ. The lock for *phaṭ kyi yi ge* is just "फट्"; "फट् अक्षर" would match the list's split.

- 1-22: "ये इक्कीस नमस्कार हैं" (these are the twenty-one homages) adds a copula and closes the line. 2-1 picks the text up again, so this is harmless.
