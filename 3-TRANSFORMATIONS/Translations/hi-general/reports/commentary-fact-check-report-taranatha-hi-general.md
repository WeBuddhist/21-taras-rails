Method: a strict term-by-term alignment against the commentary's own glosses. It checks kāya, named entities, numbers, similes, agents and order, not just the gist. It also judges the Hindi word choices (त्रिलोकनाथ, जिनपुत्र, यन्त्र, महाभयंकर, वेताल, ग्रह, तत्त्व, स्थावर/जंगम, सात कोटि …) against the glosses. Tāranātha's commentary is layered. It gives a literal reading (*yi ge'i don* / *sgra ji bzhin pa*), then hidden (*sbas don*), definitive (*nges don*) and final (*mthar thug*) readings tied to the six-branch vajra-yoga. **Only the literal reading is used as ground truth.** The fact-checked English, the English consensus table, the Hindi word list and the Hindi back-translation report were used as context, not as ground truth. Where the Hindi follows a settled English translator decision (1-3 colours to the lotus; 1-8 Ture as her name and "champions of Mara"; 1-14 "seven levels"; 1-17 Vindhya; 1-22 "and"; 2-6 optative) and Tāranātha reads it differently, the row is a MISMATCH and says it is a translator decision. The same holds where the Hindi follows an English consensus fix that Tāranātha did not support (1-3 wisdom, 1-5 three realms, 1-9 palm). Locked Hindi words are kept in every suggested fix. English glosses of the Hindi are given in parentheses. This is a preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off. A domain specialist and a native Hindi reader must review it before it is treated as final, and an LLM never marks its own output complete.

Readings that **support** the current Hindi:
- 1-1: "from the opened stamens of the water-born (lotus) **of the face** (*zhal ras kyi chu skyes*) of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the Three Worlds". "जो त्रिलोकनाथ के मुख से प्रकट कमल के, / प्रस्फुटित केसर से उत्पन्न हुई हैं" ("who were born from the opened stamens of the lotus that appeared from the face of the Lord of the Three Worlds") fits: the lotus grows from his face and is not his face. "त्वरित और शूरवीर" ("swift and heroic") and "क्षणिक विद्युत के समान" ("like an instant's lightning") match *myur ma*, *dpa' mo* and the lightning-quick eyes.
- 1-2: line 2 is her face; lines 3–4 are her **body**, blazing with light rays like a thousand stars. The Hindi starts line 3 with a fresh "जो" ("who"), so the blazing belongs to her, not to her face: "जो … अत्यधिक प्रकाशमान आभा से प्रज्वलित हैं" ("who blaze with exceedingly bright radiance"). The problem the Vietnamese had here does not arise. "सौ पूर्ण चन्द्रमाओं" ("a hundred full moons") and "शरद ऋतु" ("autumn") match.
- 1-3: "golden stalk, blue flower, a water-born lotus" held in her hand. "जल से उत्पन्न, स्वर्णिम-नीले कमल" ("a golden-blue lotus born from water") fits the translator decision to give the colours to the lotus. *Dka' thub ste tshul khrims*: "शील" ("discipline") fits exactly. *Spyod yul*: "गोचर" ("sphere, domain") fits.
- 1-4: "*rgyal ba'i sras po sa bcu'i dbang phyug rnams*": the bodhisattvas, lords of the ten bhūmis. "जिनपुत्र" ("Jina-sons") is the Sanskrit term and names them unambiguously in Hindi (the Vietnamese *Phật tử* problem does not arise). "अनन्त और पूर्ण विजय से कार्य करती हैं" ("acts with infinite and complete victory") puts *mtha' yas* ("infinite") on the victory, as he does. "अत्यंत आश्रय लेते हैं" ("deeply rely on") fits *gus pas brten*.
- 1-5: the seven worlds are nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras and gods, pressed under her feet. "सप्त लोकों को आक्रांत कर" ("trampling the seven worlds") fits, as does "बिना शेष छोड़े सबको आकर्षित करने में समर्थ" ("able to summon all without exception"; he: even Maheśvara as a servant).
- 1-6: the gods are in root order: "शक्र, अग्नि, ब्रह्मा, / वायु" ("Śakra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu"), as he glosses them (Indra king of the gods, Agni chief of the ṛṣis, Brahmā the reputed maker, Vāyu). *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural, so "विभिन्न ईश्वरों" ("the various Īśvaras") fits. *'byung po* is "Gaṇapati and the like, obstructers and misleaders", so "भूत" ("spirits") fits. *ro langs* here is a class of beings named after the vetāla rite, so "वेताल" fits. "जिनके सम्मुख स्तुति की जाती है" ("who are praised before her face") fits *mdun nas bstod*.
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* is "the magical devices and mantra-power of adversaries' sorcerers", so "शत्रुओं के यन्त्रों" ("the enemies' yantras, magical devices") fits well. "दाहिना पैर सिकोड़कर और बायां फैलाकर" ("right leg drawn in, left extended") matches.
- 1-8: *bdud kyi dpa' bo* is "Māra's heroes, i.e. his **military commanders** (*dmag dpon*)". "मार के शूरवीरों" ("Māra's champions") fits, and the translator decision "champions of Mara" is supported. *'jigs pa chen po'i sku*: "महाभयंकर" ("greatly terrifying") fits exactly; the Vietnamese softening problem does not arise. The lotus face with a frown and "वध करती हैं" ("slays") match.
- 1-10: "a body that **brings** supreme joy to beings". "परम आनंद देती हैं" ("gives supreme joy") fits; the majesty goes to the crown ornament ("तेजोमय मुकुट", "radiant crown"), as he reads it. *dbang du mdzad*: "वश में करती हैं" ("brings under control") fits. He reads *bdud* as Māra who controls other worlds, and "मार और सम्पूर्ण लोकों" ("Māra and all the worlds") fits.
- 1-11: *sa gzhi skyong ba'i tshogs* is "the hosts of earth-protecting kings, **or** earth-goddesses, underground nāgas …", whom she summons and controls. "पृथ्वी के रक्षकों के समूहों" ("the hosts of the earth's protectors") fits. "दरिद्रता से … मुक्त करती हैं" ("frees from poverty") matches *phongs pa*.
- 1-12: the crescent crown ornament, the blazing ornaments, and Amitābha in the matted locks radiating light constantly all match ("अर्धचन्द्र का मुकुट", "half-moon crown"; "जटाओं के मध्य में स्थित अमिताभ", "Amitābha dwelling within the matted locks").
- 1-14: the palms strike the earth and the feet pound it. *Rim pa bdun* are the "seven levels **underground**", whose malevolent beings are crushed and "scattered into pieces". The neutral "सात स्तरों" ("seven levels"), a translator decision, is compatible, and "छिन्न-भिन्न करती हैं" ("shatters to pieces") matches *dum bur 'thor*.
- 1-15: "nirvāṇa, supreme peace, resting in the sphere of the truth of **cessation**" fits "जिनका गोचर निर्वाण की शांति है" ("whose sphere is the peace of nirvāṇa"). "स्वाहा और ॐ" keeps the root order.
- 1-16: "*rig pa* = the **vidyā-mantra** syllable hūṃ, from which arises the **lamp** (*sgron ma*), a body blazing with rays". "हूँ विद्याक्षर से उत्पन्न दीपक स्वरूप हैं" ("are a lamp arising from the vidyā-syllable hūṃ") fits exactly. "चारों ओर आनंद से घिरी हैं" ("surrounded all round by joy") is acceptable: he reads siddhas, vidyādharas and bodhisattvas of joyful mind surrounding her (see style notes).
- 1-17: "*tu re* = the **swift one**, the goddess who stamps her feet in wrath". "तुरे, जो अपने चरणों से प्रहार करती हैं" ("Ture, who strikes with her feet") fits. She shakes the mountains and the three worlds: "जो मेरु, मन्दर और विन्ध्य पर्वतों को, / तथा तीनों लोकों को कम्पित करती हैं" ("who make Meru, Mandara and the Vindhya mountains, and the three worlds, tremble") has the mountains as objects, as he reads it. The error found in the Vietnamese does not recur. On *'bigs byed*: "most Tibetan editions have *'bigs byed*, the king of mountains (Vindhya); the Sanskrit has Kailāśa; either is acceptable". **विन्ध्य** is supported.
- 1-18: the divine-lake simile attaches to the moon: "देवों के सरोवर के रूप वाले, / हरिण के चिह्न वाले चन्द्रमा" ("the deer-marked moon that has the form of the gods' lake") fits. He removes "stationary and moving poisons"; "सम्पूर्ण विषों" ("all poisons") covers both (विष is general, unlike Vietnamese *nọc độc*). At 1-20 he glosses "*ta ra gnyis brjod*" as "**tāre** twice, plus tuttāre", i.e. the ten-syllable mantra. This supports the recitation form **तारे** for ཏཱ་ར.
- 1-19: gods, kings and kinnaras "rely with respect on your lotus feet". The direction (they serve her, "सेवित हैं") is right. *Rtsod pa* = "opponents' disputes" fits "विवादों" ("disputes").
- 1-20: the right eye is like the sun (it frightens harmers) and the left like the moon (it bestows life). "पूर्ण सूर्य और चन्द्रमा के समान … दोनों आँखों से" ("with both eyes, like the full sun and moon") fits. "अत्यंत भयंकर महामारियों" ("extremely fierce epidemics") keeps *shin tu*; he glosses them as "extremely fierce, hard to cure".
- 1-21: *gdon* = "the eighteen *gdon*, such as the planetary *gdon*", so the locked **ग्रह** ("graha") fits exactly. *Mthu* fits the locked **शक्ति** ("might"). "वे तुरे अत्यंत श्रेष्ठ हैं" ("she, Ture, is most supreme") is compatible with his alternative "*ture* = the swift one".
- 1-22: "the praise by way of the ten-syllable root mantra **is this one**, and the praise with twenty-one homages **is also this one**; *dang* is a joining word". "मूल मंत्र द्वारा यह स्तुति, / और ये इक्कीस नमस्कार हैं" ("this praise by the root mantra, and these are the twenty-one homages") points both descriptions at this text with "यह … ये" ("this … these"), so it is compatible with his reading. It follows the translator decision (1-22 "and").

Style notes (not errors):
- 1-2 "तारों के समूहों के … आभा से" ("with the radiance of the star clusters") gives her the stars' own light. He glosses *kyis* as "like" (*lta bu*): light like thousands of stars. *Rab tu phye ba* ("spread out, radiating") is not rendered. The literal Hindi is acceptable. Also, तारा means both "Tārā" and "star"; the back-translation report already flags this.

Readings that **support** the current Hindi:
- 2-1: the object of devotion is "the goddess, i.e. the Bhagavatī", so "देवी" ("the goddess") fits. The agent is "any intelligent one", so "जो भी बुद्धिमान" ("whoever is intelligent") fits. "सच्ची भक्ति से युक्त" ("endowed with true devotion") fits *gus pa yang dag par ldan pa*. The comma running into 2-2 is right, since *brjod pas* continues.
- 2-2: "having risen at dusk **and** at dawn … in the six sessions". "संध्याकाल और उषाकाल में उठकर" ("rising at dusk and at dawn") has "और" ("and"); the "or" problem found in the Vietnamese does not arise.
