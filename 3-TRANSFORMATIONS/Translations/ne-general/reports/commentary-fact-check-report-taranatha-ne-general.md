Method: a strict term-by-term alignment against the commentary's own glosses. It checks kāya, named entities, numbers, similes, agents, list order and what modifies what, not just the gist. Each verse was aligned Tibetan | gloss | Nepali | MATCH/MISMATCH before a verdict; only ⚠ ERROR and MISMATCH rows are shown. Tāranātha's commentary is layered. It gives a literal reading (*yi ge'i don* / *sgra ji bzhin pa*), then hidden (*sbas don*), definitive (*nges don*) and final (*mthar thug*) readings tied to the six-branch vajra-yoga. **Only the literal reading is used as ground truth.** The fact-checked English, the English consensus table, the Hindi consensus, the Nepali word list and the Nepali back-translation report were used as context, not as ground truth. The Nepali was checked on its own words; the Hindi report on the same commentary gave the structure only. Where the Nepali follows a settled English translator decision (1-3 colours to the lotus; 1-8 Ture as her name and "champions of Mara"; 1-14 "seven levels"; 1-17 Vindhya; 1-22 "and"; 2-6 optative) or a decision Tenkal made for the Hindi (1-11 "destitution"; 1-7 "amid fire"; ग्रह kept and flagged; बोधिसत्त्व at 1-4 and बुद्ध at 2-3), and Tāranātha reads it differently, the row is a MISMATCH and says so. The same holds where the Nepali follows an English consensus fix that Tāranātha did not support (1-3 wisdom, 1-5 three realms, 1-9 palm, 1-16 and 1-21 "set"). Locked Nepali words are kept in every suggested fix. English glosses of the Nepali are given in parentheses. This is a preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off. A domain specialist and a native Nepali reader must review it before it is treated as final, and an LLM never marks its own output complete.

Readings that **support** the current Nepali:
- 1-1: "from the opened stamens of the water-born (lotus) **of the face** (*zhal ras kyi chu skyes*) of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the Three Worlds". "तीन लोकका नाथको मुखबाट प्रकट कमलको / प्रस्फुटित केशरबाट उत्पन्न भएकी" ("born from the opened stamens of the lotus that appeared from the face of the Lord of the Three Worlds") fits: the lotus grows from his face. "द्रुत र शूरवीर" ("swift and heroic") and "क्षणभरको बिजुली जस्ता" ("like an instant's lightning") match *myur ma*, *dpa' mo* and her lightning-quick wisdom-eyes.
- 1-2: line 2 is her face ("a hundred full moons stacked"); lines 3–4 are her **body**, blazing with rays like thousands of stars. The Nepali ends lines 3–4 with the participle "छर्ने" ("who spreads"), which attaches to her, not to her face. "सय पूर्ण चन्द्रमाहरू खप्टिए जस्तो" ("like a hundred full moons stacked") and "शरद् ऋतु" ("autumn") match *brgya … brtsegs* and *ston ka*. "छर्ने" also renders *rab tu phye ba* ("spread out"), which the Hindi missed.
- 1-3: "golden stalk, blue flower, a water-born lotus" held in her hand. "सुनौलो-नीलो जलज / कमलद्वारा" ("with a golden-blue water-born lotus") fits the decision to give the colours to the lotus. *Dka' thub ste tshul khrims*: "शील" ("discipline") fits exactly. *Spyod yul*: "आचरणको क्षेत्र" ("sphere of conduct") fits "her domain, in which she acts".
- 1-4: "from the uṣṇīṣa of **the Tathāgata Vairocana**". "तथागतको उष्णीष स्वरूप" ("in the form of the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa") is singular, which is exactly his reading (the English and Hindi have the plural). "अनन्त र पूर्ण विजयी आचरण गर्ने" ("who acts in infinite and complete victory") fits "victorious over all adverse forces, with infinite benefits". *Rgyal ba'i sras po* = "the lords of the ten bhūmis"; "बोधिसत्त्वहरू" names them (Tenkal's Hindi decision). "अत्यन्त सेवित" ("deeply served") fits *gus pas brten*.
- 1-5: the seven worlds are nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras and gods, pressed under her feet. "सातै लोकलाई चरणले कुल्चेर" ("trampling all seven worlds with her feet") and "सबैलाई निःशेष तान्न सक्ने" ("able to summon all without exception"; he: even Maheśvara as a servant) fit. "भर्ने" ("fills") is *gang*.
- 1-6: the gods are in root order: "इन्द्र, अग्निदेव, ब्रह्मा, / वायुदेव" ("Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu"), as he glosses them (Śakra king of the gods, Agni chief of the ṛṣis, Brahmā the reputed maker, Vāyu). *Sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural (Rudra, Viṣṇu, Sūrya …), so "विभिन्न ईश्वरहरू" ("the various Īśvaras") fits. *'Byung po* = "Gaṇapati and the like, obstructers and misleaders": "भूत" fits. *Ro langs* here is a class of beings named after the vetāla rite, so "वेताल" fits. *Dri za* = gandharvas such as Citraratha: "गन्धर्वहरू" fits. "अगाडि स्तुति गरिएकी" ("praised before her") fits *mdun nas bstod*.
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* is "the magical devices and mantra-power of adversaries' sorcerers", so "शत्रुको यन्त्रहरू" ("the enemy's yantras") fits. "दाहिने खुम्च्याएको र देब्रे तन्काएको चरणले कुल्चेर" ("trampling with right leg drawn in and left extended") matches.
- 1-8: *bdud kyi dpa' bo* = "Māra's heroes, i.e. his **military commanders**": "मारका शूरवीरहरू" ("Māra's champions") fits the decision. *'Jigs pa chen po'i sku*: "महाभयङ्करी" ("the Great Terrifying One") fits. "कमल-मुखमा क्रोधित भृकुटी" ("a wrathful frown on her lotus face") and "अशेष वध गर्ने" ("who slays without exception") match.
- 1-9: the utpala mudrā at her heart "symbolizes the Three Jewels": "त्रिरत्नको प्रतीक मुद्राको / औंलाहरूद्वारा हृदयमा … सजिएकी" ("adorned at the heart by the fingers of the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels") fits. "the masses of light rays from her own body **stir intensely and radiate** (*rab tu 'khrugs shing 'phros pas*)": "आफ्नै प्रकाशका उर्लँदा समूह फैलाउने" ("who spreads the surging masses of her own light") has both the surging and the radiating. The Hindi problem here (प्रज्वलित) does not arise.
- 1-10: *bzhad pa rab bzhad* and the sound of *oṃ tuttāre* bring Māra, who controls other worlds, and all worlds under control: "मार र लोकलाई वशमा गर्ने" ("who brings Māra and the world under control") fits, with no added "all" (the Hindi problem does not arise).
- 1-11: *sa gzhi skyong ba'i tshogs* = "the hosts of earth-protecting kings, **or** earth-goddesses, underground nāgas …". "पृथ्वीको रक्षा गर्ने समूहहरू / सबैलाई तान्न समर्थ भएकी" ("able to summon all the hosts who protect the earth") fits. He reads the frown and the wrathful mantra with hūṃ as the power, so "चलायमान क्रोधित भृकुटी र हूँ अक्षरद्वारा" ("by the quivering wrathful frown and the syllable hūṃ") is compatible. "दरिद्रताबाट … तार्ने" ("delivers from destitution") keeps Tenkal's decision and matches *phongs pa*.
- 1-12: the crescent crown ornament, the blazing ornaments, and Amitābha in the matted locks giving light constantly all match ("अर्धचन्द्रले मुकुट सजिएकी", "whose crown is adorned with a half-moon"; "जटाको बीचमा अमिताभबाट", "from Amitābha amid the matted locks").
- 1-13: "dwelling in the midst of a garland — a mass of fire — blazing like the fire of the aeon's end". "कल्पको अन्त्यको अग्नि जस्तै / प्रज्वलित मालाको बीचमा रहने" ("dwelling amid a garland blazing like the fire at the aeon's end") has the simile on the garland, as he reads it. "शत्रुको सेना … नष्ट गर्ने" ("destroys the enemy's army") fits (human and non-human harmers).
- 1-14: the palms strike the earth and the feet pound it; hūṃ is shouted with a wrathful frown. *Rim pa bdun* are "seven levels **underground**", whose malevolent beings are "scattered into pieces". The neutral "सातै स्तरहरू" ("all seven levels", a translator decision) is compatible, and "तहसनहस पार्ने" ("who smashes to pieces") matches *'gems … dum bur 'thor*.
- 1-15: "always blissful, virtuous, of peaceful nature; nirvāṇa, supreme peace, resting in the sphere of the truth of cessation" fits "सुखी, कल्याणी, शान्ता, / निर्वाणको शान्ति जसको आचरणको क्षेत्र हो" ("blissful, virtuous, peaceful, whose sphere is the peace of nirvāṇa"). "स्वाहा र ॐ" keeps the root order.
- 1-16: "*rig pa* = the **vidyā-mantra** syllable hūṃ, from which arises the **lamp** (*sgron ma*), a body blazing with rays". "विद्या हूँबाट उत्पन्न दीप स्वरूप भएकी" ("who is a lamp arising from the vidyā hūṃ") fits exactly. "चारैतिर परम आनन्दले घेरिएकी" ("surrounded all round by supreme joy") is acceptable: he reads siddhas, vidyādharas and bodhisattvas of joyful mind surrounding her (see style notes). "शत्रुको शरीर" ("the enemy's body") is singular, as his Māra Pāpīyān.
- 1-17: "*tu re* = the **swift one**, the goddess who stamps her feet in wrath". "तुरे जसको चरणको प्रहारले" ("Ture, by the stamping of whose feet") fits. She shakes the mountains and the three worlds: "मेरु, मन्दर र विन्ध्य पर्वत, / तीनै लोकहरूलाई हल्लाउने" ("who shakes Mount Meru, Mandara and Vindhya, and all three worlds") has the mountains as objects, as he reads it. On *'bigs byed*: "most Tibetan editions have *'bigs byed*, the king of mountains (Vindhya); the Sanskrit has Kailāśa; either is acceptable". **विन्ध्य** is supported.
- 1-18: the divine-lake simile attaches to the moon: "देवतालको स्वरूपको / चन्द्रमा हातमा धारण गर्ने" ("who holds in her hand the moon that has the form of the gods' lake") fits. He removes "stationary and moving poisons"; "सम्पूर्ण विषहरू" ("all poisons") covers both. At 1-20 he glosses "*ta ra gnyis brjod*" as "**tāre** twice, plus tuttāre", which supports the recitation form **तारे**.
- 1-19: gods, kings and kinnaras "rely with respect on your lotus feet": "सेवित" ("served") has the right direction. The armour is "the armour of mantra and mudrā blessed by you … which the practitioner binds on **all round**", and the majesty is the practitioner's. "चारैतिरबाट कवचको आनन्दमय तेजले" ("by the joyful majesty of the armour, from all sides") keeps *kun nas*, *go cha*, *dga' ba* and *brjid*, with no possessive. The Hindi problem here ("her own armour") does not arise. *Rtsod pa* = "opponents' disputes": "विवाद" fits.
- 1-20: the right eye is like the sun (it frightens harmers) and the left like the moon (it bestows life). "जसका दुई नेत्र पूर्ण सूर्य र चन्द्रमा जस्तै / अत्यन्त उज्यालो प्रकाशले चम्किन्छन्" ("whose two eyes shine with very bright light like the full sun and moon") puts the simile on the eyes. "अत्यन्त भयंकर महामारी" ("extremely fierce epidemics") keeps *shin tu drag po*; he glosses them as "hard to cure".
- 1-21: *gdon* = "the eighteen *gdon*, such as the planetary *gdon*", so the locked **ग्रह** fits (kept, flagged for the native reviewer, as decided). *Zhi ba'i mthu*: "शान्तिको शक्ति" ("the might of peace") fits the locked शक्ति. "नष्ट गर्ने तुरे परम उत्तम भएकी" ("Ture, most supreme, who destroys") is compatible with his alternative "*ture* = the swift one".
- 1-22: "the praise by way of the ten-syllable root mantra **is this one**, and the praise with twenty-one homages **is also this one**; *dang* is a joining word". "मूल मन्त्रद्वारा गरिएको यो स्तुति र, / एक्काइस वन्दनाहरू" ("this praise made by the root mantra, and the twenty-one homages") points the praise at this text with "यो" ("this") and follows the translator decision (1-22 "and").

Style notes (not errors; for the native Nepali reader):
- 1-2 "ताराहरूको समूहद्वारा" ("by the hosts of stars") makes the stars the agent of the blazing. He glosses *kyis* as "like" (*lta bu*): light like thousands of stars. "हजारौं ताराहरू जस्तै" ("like thousands of stars") would follow him. Also, तारा means both "Tārā" and "star"; a reader may pause here.
- 1-3 "सुनौलो-नीलो" ("golden-blue") suggests one mixed colour. He has a gold stalk and a blue flower. Optional.
- 1-4, 1-6: the refrain changes form. Most verses end in a participle (-ने, -की), but 1-4 "सेवितलाई" and 1-6 "पूजितलाई" add -लाई ("to"). A native reader should decide whether to make them uniform.
- 1-7 "शत्रुको यन्त्रहरू" ("the enemy's yantras"): with a plural head, standard Nepali uses -का: "शत्रुका यन्त्रहरू".
- 1-10 "तुत्तारेको अट्टहास" ("the great laughter of tuttāre") merges the laughter and the mantra. He lists laughter, great laughter and the sound of oṃ tuttāre. Fine, as in the English.
- 1-13 "दाहिने तन्काएको र देब्रे खुम्च्याई" mixes a participle (-एको) and a conjunctive (-ई). "दाहिने तन्काई देब्रे खुम्च्याई" (or -एको twice, as at 1-7) reads better. The line is also long.
- 1-15 "महान् पाप" ("great sin"): महान् usually praises ("noble, great"). "महापाप" keeps the locked महा stem and reads naturally.
- 1-16 "चारैतिर परम आनन्दले घेरिएकी" ("surrounded all round by supreme joy") leaves the joyful retinue implicit. "आनन्दित अनुचरहरूले घेरिएकी" ("surrounded by joyful attendants") would say what he says. Optional.
- 1-17 "हूँ को स्वरूपको बीज" ("the seed of the form of hūṃ") makes the form own the seed; the root has identity ("the seed that is in the form of hūṃ"). "हूँ-स्वरूप बीज भएकी" is closer. Also "हूँ को" should be written "हूँको".
- 1-18 "देवताल" ("gods' lake") can be read as a place-name. "देवताहरूको ताल" or "दिव्य ताल" is clearer. The deer-mark epithet (*ri dwags rtags can*) is not rendered; it is locked to चन्द्र, and he glosses it as the moon, so this is fine.
- 1-22 "यो स्तुति र," has a comma after र ("and"). Move or drop it.

Textual notes (his edition or remarks versus our root; the Nepali follows our root, so none of these are errors):
- 1-1: he prefers Nam mkha' bzang po's revised translation, "*'jig rten gsum mgon zhal gi chu skyes / bye ba'i ge sar las ni byung ma*", as clearer. He calls the dharmakāya → rūpakāya reading non-literal.
- 1-12: he reads *zla ba'i dum bu* ("half-moon") for our *zla ba'i rtse mo*. The meaning (crescent) is the same, and "अर्धचन्द्र" fits both.
- 1-15: *svāhā dang oṃ dang* (same order as our root, with an extra *dang*).
- 1-16: he reads **སྒྲོན་མ** ("lamp"), the same as our root and the Nepali "दीप". DG, GD and TT read སྒྲོལ་མ.
- 1-17: he reports *'bigs byed* in most Tibetan editions and *Kailāśa* in the Sanskrit.
- 1-18: he reads **ཧ་ར** twice (our root ཏཱ་ར, Nepali तारे) and gives the mantra *oṃ namaḥ tāre namo hare hūṃ hare svāhā*.
- 1-20: he reads **ཏ་ར** twice (our root ཧ་ར, Nepali हर), explaining it as "tāre twice + tuttāre", the ten-syllable mantra. His opening sentence says *hā ra*, so his own text is not fully consistent here.

- 2-4: he cites Gnyan lo's and Nam mkha' bzang po's variant renderings of *brtan gnas pa'am gzhan yang 'gro ba*, and a line-order variant of *zos pa dang ni* (Sanskrit *khāditaṃ pītam eva ca*).

Readings that **support** the current Nepali:
- 2-1: the object of devotion is "the goddess, i.e. the Bhagavatī": "देवीप्रति" ("towards the goddess") fits. The agent is "any intelligent one": "जुन बुद्धिमानले" ("whichever intelligent one") fits. "पूर्ण रूपले भक्तिले युक्त" ("fully endowed with devotion") fits *gus pa yang dag par ldan pa*. The clause ends with "पाठ गर्नाले," ("by reciting,") and runs into 2-2, which is right, since *brjod pas* continues.
- 2-2: "having risen at dusk **and** at dawn … in the six sessions". "साँझ र बिहान उठेर" ("rising at evening and morning") has "र" ("and"). "उहाँको स्मरण" ("recollecting her") matches "recollecting the Noble Lady's qualities" (the Hindi fix #1 is already in). *Sdig pa* is glossed as the **fruits** of misdeeds; the literal "पापहरू" ("sins") is acceptable.
- 2-3: "seventy million (*bye ba phrag bdun*) Victors, nirmāṇakāya buddhas who accompany the Bhagavatī" confer empowerment on the practitioner. "सात करोड बुद्धहरूद्वारा" ("by seven crore buddhas") is exactly seventy million, and बुद्ध is the referent he gives (Tenkal's decision for बुद्ध over जिन). The recipient is the reciter, which is right, and "पुग्नेछ" ("he will reach") is singular. "यसभन्दा पनि महानता" ("greatness even beyond this") fits "many qualities greater than this … mahāmudrā siddhi". "अन्तिम बुद्ध पद" ("the ultimate state of buddhahood") fits *mthar thug rdzogs pa'i sangs rgyas kyi go 'phang*.
- 2-4: "**stationary** poisons such as aconite (*nag po brtsegs*), and **moving** ones — snakes, bites, stings"; "*gnas pa* is a synonym of *brtan pa*". "स्थावर होस् वा जङ्गम" ("whether stationary or moving") is the exact Sanskrit pair. "eaten or drunk … removed by recollecting the vidyā" matches "खाएको र पिएको भए तापनि, / उहाँको स्मरण गर्नाले पूर्ण रूपले हट्नेछ" ("even if eaten and drunk, by recollecting her it will be fully removed"). The verb is intransitive here, so the problem at 2-2 does not arise.
- 2-5: "harms of the various *gdon*, illnesses such as epidemics, torment by poison" fits "ग्रह, महामारी र विषद्वारा पीडित" ("tormented by grahas, epidemics and poisons"). The last line closes the sentence: "अन्य प्राणीहरूका लागि पनि त्यस्तै हुनेछ" ("it will be the same for other beings too") matches his "the mantra-holder removes that suffering for other beings too".
- 2-6: "two = day and night; three = three sessions" fits "दुई, तीन, सात पटक" ("two, three, seven times"). *Nor* = "wealth and enjoyments": "धन" fits, and "धनहरू नै" keeps *nor rnams nyid*. "विघ्नहरू" ("obstacles") fits *bgegs*, and "एक-एक गरी" ("one by one") keeps *so sor* ("each").

Style notes (not errors; for the native Nepali reader):
- 2-1 "राम्ररी पाठ गर्नाले" ("by reciting well") stands for *rab tu brjod*. He glosses it as "with great faith and diligence" and prefers "*brtson pas klog*" ("reciting diligently"). "श्रद्धा र उद्यमसाथ पाठ गर्नाले" would follow him. Optional. A native reader should also confirm "जुन बुद्धिमानले" (जुन usually "which") against "जो बुद्धिमान्‌ले".
- 2-2 "सम्पूर्ण पापहरू … शान्त हुन्छ": a plural subject needs a plural verb, "शान्त हुन्छन्". "गर्छ" and "गर्दछ" also alternate in one verse.
- 2-4 "महा विष" should be written as one word, "महाविष".
- 2-5 "ग्रह, महामारी र विषद्वारा पीडित भएको, / सम्पूर्ण दुःखका समूहहरू … हटाइनेछ": "पीडित भएको" has no head noun (the Hindi had the same dangling line, fixed as पीडित जनों के), and the plural subject needs "हटाइनेछन्". e.g. "…पीडित भएकाहरूका / दुःखका समूहहरू पूर्ण रूपले हटाइनेछन्". "सम्पूर्ण" ("all") is not in the Tibetan (*sdug bsngal tshogs*). Optional.
- 2-6 "सम्पूर्ण इच्छाहरू प्राप्त हुनेछ": plural subject, so "हुनेछन्".
- 2-6 "पुत्र" ("son") for *bu*: he glosses it as a lineage heir for householders, or disciples for renunciants. The English has "child"; "सन्तान" would match it. Optional.
- 2-6 "दुई, तीन, सात पटक": he reads *bdun bdun* (seven times in each session). Our root has *bdun du*, so the Nepali is fine.
