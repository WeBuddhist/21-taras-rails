- **Commentary (ground truth):** `1-SOURCES/Commentaries/New raw data/bo-ཏཱ་ར་ན་ཐ།.md`, Tāranātha (1575–1634), *ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པའི་རྣམ་པར་བཤད་པ།* (commentary id `taranatha`)

- **Date:** 2026-09-25

Method: a strict term-by-term alignment against the commentary's own glosses. It checks kāya, named entities, numbers, similes, agents, list order and what modifies what, not just the gist. Each verse was aligned Tibetan | gloss | Thai | MATCH/MISMATCH before a verdict; only ⚠ ERROR and MISMATCH rows are shown. Tāranātha's commentary is layered: a literal reading (*yi ge'i don* / *sgra ji bzhin pa*), then hidden (*sbas don*), definitive (*nges don*) and final (*mthar thug*) readings tied to the six-branch vajra-yoga. **Only the literal reading is used as ground truth.** The fact-checked English (and the translator decisions in its frontmatter), the English, Nepali and Mongolian consensus files and the Thai word list (`termbase-th-general.md`) were used as context, not as ground truth. Where the Thai follows a settled English translator decision (1-3 colours to the lotus; 1-8 Ture as her name; 1-14 "seven levels"; 1-17 Vindhya; 1-22 "and"; 2-6 optative), one of Tenkal's decisions (1-11 "destitution"; 1-7 "amid fire") or an English consensus fix Tāranātha did not support, and he reads it differently, the row is a MISMATCH and says so. Where the Thai departs from a settled decision, that is said too. Locked Thai words are kept in every suggested fix unless the row says the lock is wrong. English glosses of the Thai are given in parentheses. This draft had not been meaning-checked, so it carries more issues than the other languages did at this stage. This is a preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off. **A domain specialist and a native Thai reader must review it** before it is treated as final, and an LLM never marks its own output complete.

Style notes (not errors; for the native Thai reader):
- The refrain "ขอนอบน้อมด้วย…" at 1-5, 1-7, 1-8, 1-9 reads "I pay homage by means of …". Elsewhere the draft uses "ขอนอบน้อมพระผู้…" ("homage to you who …"); one pattern throughout would match the English "Homage to you, …".
- 1-2 "ฤดูใบไม้ร่วง" is the plain word for autumn; "สารทฤดู" is the literary word Thai liturgy uses. Optional.
- 1-3 "สีน้ำเงินทอง" reverses the root's gold-blue order; "สีทองและน้ำเงิน" (gold and blue) is clearer.
- 1-4 "พระเกตุมาลา": please confirm it will be read as the uṣṇīṣa (Thai iconography sometimes means the flame above it, พระรัศมี); "พระอุษณีษะ" is the learned alternative.

- 1-9 "เปล่งประกายเจิดจ้า" drops *'khrug* ("stirring, surging"); "เปล่งประกายพลุ่งพล่าน" would keep it.
