# The Clear Mirror: The Twenty-One Homages Together with Their Benefits ^0

## First, the brief thematic summary. ^1-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^1-1]]

Regarding the praise with twenty-one homages, there are three parts: a brief presentation, a detailed explanation, and a conclusion showing the benefits. The first, the brief presentation, is: ^1-1

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^1-2]]

Oṃ, homage to the Noble and Venerable Tārā. ^1-2

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^1-3]]

This means: Oṃ is the essence of the five kāyas and the five wisdoms, which purifies the stains of the minds of sentient beings; it is uttered as an expression of taking refuge and rendering homage. "Venerable" (rje btsun ma) is used because she has become the protector and refuge of the three worlds and is perfectly endowed with the three sets of vows. "Noble" (phags ma) is used because she has far transcended the level of ordinary beings. "Tārā" (sgrol ma) is used because she causes all sentient beings to be liberated from the level of samsara to the level of Buddhahood. ^1-3

## Second, the detailed explanation. ^2-0

### First, prostration by way of the historical context. ^2-1-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-1]]

The second part, the detailed explanation, also consists of three sections: prostrating based on the general history, prostrating based on the form of the body, and prostrating based on the display of enlightened activity. The first is: ^2-1

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-2]]

Homage to Tārā, the swift and heroic,
Whose eyes are like a flash of lightning,
Born from the opening stamens of the lotus face
Of the Lord of the Three Worlds. ^2-2

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-3]]

This means: If one asks how the body of Tārā first arose and what kind of benefit she performs for sentient beings in the interim, it is as follows: Although the Noble Lord Avalokiteśvara, the protector of the three worlds, established all sentient beings to be tamed in the state of Buddhahood, he saw that the realms of sentient beings were not emptied and, furthermore, that they possessed great suffering. Relying on this, he shed many tears, which became a stream of water. From that, a lotus or utpala flower grew, and because she was born from its stamens, it is said, "Born from the opening stamens." ^2-3

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-4]]

If one wonders what kind of benefit she performs for those to be tamed after her birth, it is indicated by the words, "Homage to Tārā, the swift and heroic." She is "swift" because she acts quickly for the benefit of sentient beings, and she is a "heroine" because her courage is unyielding. That very Venerable Tārā, being thus swift and heroic, does not neglect to gaze with her wisdom eyes for the sake of sentient beings throughout the six times of the day and night. Because there is no obstruction to her vision in seeing who is in decline, who is flourishing, and so forth, it is said, "Whose eyes are like a flash of lightning." This means that I prostrate with devotion of the three doors to the Venerable One who possesses such qualities. ^2-4

##### First, prostration by way of the peaceful form. ^2-2-1-1-0

###### First, praise by way of a clear complexion and radiating light. ^2-2-1-1-1-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-5]]

The second section concerns prostrating by way of the aspects of the body, which is divided into two: prostrating by way of the aspect of the Sambhogakāya and prostrating by way of the aspect of the Dharmakāya. The first of these, prostrating by way of the aspect of the Sambhogakāya, is further divided into two: prostrating by way of the peaceful aspect and prostrating by way of the wrathful aspect. Regarding the first, prostrating by way of the peaceful aspect, this is praised through six verses of homage. The first of these, which praises her by way of her clear, radiant complexion and the light rays she emits, is: ^2-5

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-6]]

Homage to her whose face is like
A hundred full autumn moons gathered together,
Blazing with the brilliant light
Of a thousand gathered stars. ^2-6

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-7]]

This means: In general, the moon is something that possesses a brilliance and luster that is superior to other objects. During the summer, it is obscured by clouds and mist, and during the winter, it is obscured by smoke and dust, making its brilliance unclear; however, during the autumn, it is free from both of these, and thus its brilliance is clear. Just as the moon on the fifteenth day possesses the quality of being full, the face of the Venerable One is also extremely clear and possesses brilliance, showing that it is of the same nature as the full autumn moon. Furthermore, regarding the instruction that light rays radiate from it, the phrase "by a thousand gathered stars" indicates that when a thousand stars are gathered together, they radiate light that is unbearable for the eyes to behold; in the same way, light and rays of light radiate from the face of the Venerable One that are unbearable for the eyes to behold. Thus, this is a prostration by way of the qualities of having a beautiful face and radiating light. ^2-7

###### Second, praise by way of body color, hand symbols, and physical form. ^2-2-1-1-2-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-8]]

The second section is the praise by way of body color, hand symbols, and the body itself: ^2-8

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-9]]

Homage to her whose hand is adorned
With a gold and blue water-born lotus,
She whose sphere of activity is generosity, diligence, austerity, peace,
Patience, and meditative concentration. ^2-9

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-10]]

This means: Regarding the body color, the phrase "gold and blue water-born" indicates that, in terms of being extremely bright, it is of the same nature as the luster of gold, while showing that it possesses a blue appearance without having the actual luster of a colored pigment. Regarding the hand symbols, the phrase "water-born" refers to the lotus, and "her hand is perfectly adorned with a lotus" indicates that she holds a blue utpala flower—which is born from water and has very wide and expansive petals—at her heart with her left hand, with the petals blossoming at the level of her ear, thereby showing that she possesses the qualities of the body. ^2-10

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-11]]

Regarding the body, if one wonders from what the Noble Lady’s body arises, it arises from the six perfections: first is the perfection of generosity, and "austerity" is the perfection of ethical discipline, the perfection of patience, the perfection of diligence, the perfection of meditative concentration, and the phrase "sphere of activity of meditative concentration" is the perfection of wisdom. Thus, it is generated by relying on the six perfections. Not only is it generated by the six perfections as causes, but if we take the term "peace" in terms of its direct indication, it means that the six perfections are not tainted by any form of defect. ^2-11

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-12]]

In the context of generosity, it is untainted by the defect of stinginess; regarding ethical discipline, it is untainted by the defect of immoral conduct; regarding patience, it is untainted by the defect of anger; regarding diligence, it is untainted by the defect of laziness; regarding meditative concentration, it is untainted by the defect of distraction; and regarding wisdom, it is untainted by the defect of foolishness. By relying on the six perfections, she has perfectly attained complete buddhahood and performs the welfare of sentient beings to be tamed. ^2-12

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-13]]

In this way, she performs the praise or prostration by way of the body color, the hand symbols, and the body. ^2-13

###### Third, the manner in which the Victors and the Sons of the Victors rely with devotion. ^2-2-1-1-3-0

####### First, the manner in which the Buddhas rely with devotion. ^2-2-1-1-3-1-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-14]]

Third, because the victorious ones and the children of the victorious ones rely upon her with devotion, regarding the phrase "prostration," there are three indicators for the beginning, middle, and end. In this context, "prostration" is taught as the indicator for the beginning. To whom? To the Noble Lady. ^2-14

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-15]]

What qualities does she possess that warrant prostration? Because the victorious buddhas and the great bodhisattvas rely upon her with devotion and prostrate to her, she has become an object of prostration. Regarding this, there are two ways: the way in which the buddhas rely upon her with devotion, and the way in which the great bodhisattvas rely upon her with devotion. The first, the way in which the buddhas rely upon her with devotion, is: ^2-15

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-16]]

Homage to the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas, ^2-16

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-17]]

This means: if one asks what the reason is for the Tathāgatas to prostrate to the Noble Lady, it is because the Noble Lady is the embodiment of the Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom. This is because all buddhas are born from relying upon the Perfection of Wisdom. ^2-17

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-18]]

If one realizes the meaning of the Perfection of Wisdom, the expanse of reality, in the correct way and manifests it, one attains the state of Buddhahood; if one does not manifest it, one does not attain Buddhahood. Therefore, because the Noble Lady is the actual form of the Perfection of Wisdom, the Tathāgatas prostrate to her. ^2-18

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-19]]

She who acts with infinite, complete victory. ^2-19

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-20]]

Furthermore, she is called "She who acts with infinite, complete victory" because she purifies the two obscurations, which are the adverse conditions preventing all infinite sentient beings from realizing the Perfection of Wisdom as it is, and because she teaches that one relies upon realizing the Perfection of Wisdom as it is. ^2-20

####### Second, the manner in which the great Bodhisattvas rely with devotion. ^2-2-1-1-3-2-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-21]]

The second [section] is the manner in which great Bodhisattvas rely upon her with reverence: ^2-21

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-22]]

She who is deeply relied upon by the Victors' heirs,
Who have attained every single perfection. ^2-22

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-23]]

Regarding the Bodhisattvas, there are two types: those who are ordinary beings and those who abide on the noble stages; of these, it is the Bodhisattvas who abide on the noble stages who are the actual [referents]. It is shown that the Bodhisattvas who abide on the stages—having attained the qualities of the ten stages through the perfections of the ten stages—along with the Victors and their heirs, prostrate to the Noble Lady with a mind of supreme purity. ^2-23

###### Fourth, the manner of praising the Jetsunma by way of suppressing all unfavorable conditions. ^2-2-1-1-4-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-24]]

The fourth [section] is the praise showing how the Noble Lady suppresses all discordant factors: ^2-24

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-25]]

Homage to you who, with the syllables TUTTĀRA and HŪṂ,
Fill the realms of desire, the directions, and space;
Trampling the seven worlds under your feet,
You are able to summon all without exception. ^2-25

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-26]]

This means: "Homage to TUTTĀRA" is shown as the initial clarifier. "Desire," "directions," and "space" refer to the desire realm, the seventeen classes of abodes of the form realm, and the abodes of the formless realm, respectively. Light rays emanate from the heart of the Noble Lady into these three realms, filling all these abodes with light. The "seven worlds" are the three lower realms of the desire realm, the abodes of gods and humans—making five classes of abodes—plus the form realm and the formless realm above them, all of which she suppresses under her feet. ^2-26

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-27]]

She suppresses them with her two feet: the foot that perceives emptiness and the foot that does not abandon others out of compassion. By doing so, she protects them from the suffering of cyclic existence and the lower realms, and she possesses the quality of being able to summon all sentient beings of cyclic existence—leaving none behind in the lower realms—onto the path of higher status and liberation; thus, it is shown that one pays homage by way of her suppressing all discordant factors. ^2-27

###### Fifth, the instruction on how the great world protectors pay homage with devotion. ^2-2-1-1-5-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-28]]

The fifth section, which shows how the great protectors of the world pay homage with devotion, is: ^2-28

This means: Indra, the lord of the gods; Brahmā, the master of the trichiliocosm; or alternatively, Indra who protects the eastern direction, Agni in the southeast, Brahmā who protects the zenith, Vāyu in the northwest, and so forth, along with the great protectors of the directions. Not only that, but also those in the world who have been born through the connection of negative karma and wrong aspirations, and who possess harmful intentions toward others, and ^2-30

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-31]]

the bhūtas who have become a type of preta wandering in the sky, constantly tormented by fear and suffering; the vetālas who are disguised as living beings by the demons of the dark side; the gandharvas who wander in the intermediate state of existence; and the hosts of yakṣas who constantly harbor harmful intentions toward others and possess miraculous powers and strength—even they, with the intention of going for refuge to the noble lady, constantly attend to her from the front, and thus she is shown as an object of homage. ^2-31

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-33]]

Not only that, but she is praised for the way in which the fire of primordial wisdom blazes from her body, burning and destroying all demons and obstacles. ^2-34

###### Second, praise by way of the right and left hand symbols ^2-2-1-2-2-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-39]]

The second is the praise by way of the hand symbols on the right and left. ^2-39

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-40]]

Homage to her whose fingers, in the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels,

This means: Her left hand, with the thumb and ring finger joined at her heart and the index, middle, and little fingers raised, is the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels; with the thumb and ring finger, she beautifully adorns her heart with the stem of an utpala flower. With her right hand, she performs the mudrā of supreme generosity, which symbolizes the granting of supreme and common accomplishments to all sentient beings without exception; the palm is beautifully adorned with the design of a thousand-spoked wheel, and because the light rays from it dispel all the masses of harm to all sentient beings and bestow all aspects of benefit and happiness, this is a praise by way of her causing a boundless, swirling mass of light from the wheel itself. ^2-41

###### Third, praise by way of the crown ornament and laughter ^2-2-1-2-3-0

The third is the praise by way of the crown ornament and laughter. ^2-42

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-43]]

Homage to her whose crown ornament,

The praise by way of laughter means that, through laughter, great laughter, and so forth, she strikes terror into all hosts of maras and enemies, and brings all three worlds under her power or subjugation. ^2-44

###### Fourth, praise by way of the ten directional protectors accomplishing enlightened activity ^2-2-1-2-4-0

The fourth is the praise by way of accomplishing enlightened activities through the ten directional protectors. ^2-45

Regarding the hosts of powerful spirits who do not abide by the vows of the commands given in this way, the noble lady causes the wrinkles on her fiercely wrathful face to quiver like clouds, and from the HŪṀ at her heart, she emanates immeasurable hosts of HŪṀs and wrathful ones, thereby praising her by way of establishing all those beings to be tamed—who are destitute of emptiness and compassion, who possess negative karma, and who engage in suffering and non-virtue—in the state of the omniscient Buddha. ^2-48

"From the midst of her matted locks, adorned by the Protector Amitābha," from whom rays of light constantly and intensely radiate, she praises her by way of pacifying all the sufferings of all sentient beings. ^2-51

Following that, the praise offered by way of the aspect of the Dharmakāya is: ^2-58

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^2-59]]

This means: Because she has already abandoned all that is to be abandoned by the path of seeing, she is free from suffering and possesses perfect bliss; thus, she is the "Blissful One." Because she uproots all that is to be abandoned by the path of meditation, she manifests the fruit through the path of perfect virtue; thus, she is the "Virtuous One." Because all stains of habitual tendencies have become completely pacified, she is the "Peaceful One." Being free from all stains of the three obscurations, she is the Noble Lady herself, who possesses the sphere of experience of the peace of nirvāṇa. The manifestation of the wisdom of the path of seeing, the wisdom of the path of meditation, and ultimately the wisdom of all that is and all that exists, constitutes the qualities of her heart, the Dharmakāya. ^2-60

The ten syllables of the Noble Lady’s mantra, adorned at the end with SVĀHĀ and at the beginning with OṂ, protect those to be tamed from the eight and sixteen fears in the short term, and ultimately from all the sufferings of cyclic existence and the lower realms; this constitutes the qualities of her speech, the Sambhogakāya. Following that, because one confesses in the proper manner before the Noble Lady—who possesses a body that is a collection of the major and minor marks—all the great negativity, the ten non-virtuous actions, and the five inexpiable crimes that I and all sentient beings have committed, she utterly destroys all negativity and obscurations from the root; this constitutes the qualities of her body. Thus, the praise is offered by way of the qualities of body, speech, and mind. ^2-61

From the sound of the mantra of reciting TĀRA twice from her mouth, light rays like droplets of nectar emerge, and from the full moon as well, a stream of nectar descends. By this stream of nectar pervading all the poisons of the moving—the ignorance and the collection of mental afflictions of all sentient beings—and all the collections of types of poisons, such as the compounded poisons of the stable, this constitutes the praise by way of eliminating all collections of harm from mental afflictions and poisons. ^2-70

Thus, by way of eliminating strife—such as conflicts arising from unhappy minds—and all evil omens and bad dreams, this constitutes the praise. ^2-73

By way of these, she dispels all the hosts of infectious diseases that cause the most intense and unbearable suffering for the individuals who pray to her, and this constitutes the praise. ^2-76

## Third, concluding by way of demonstrating the benefits. ^3-0

### Second, from the perspective of time ^3-2-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-4]]

Having arisen at dusk and at dawn,
Through mindfulness, all fearlessness is fully bestowed; ^3-4

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-5]]

This means that one makes supplication at the time of dusk to that very Bhagavatī who abides in a wrathful manner, and one arises or stands up at the time of dawn to that very Bhagavatī who abides in a peaceful manner; thus, it primarily demonstrates two times. By way of remembering her qualities at all times, one makes supplication, and by mere recollection, she fully grants or bestows all fearlessness. In terms of temporary circumstances, this means fearlessness from the eight and sixteen fears, and ^3-5

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-6]]

In terms of the ultimate, it means protection from the causes of non-virtuous misdeeds. ^3-6

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-7]]

"All misdeeds are thoroughly pacified," means that she causes all non-virtuous misdeeds to be thoroughly pacified; and in terms of the result, "she destroys all miserable rebirths," means that by merely making supplication to the Bhagavatī, one is liberated from the realms of miserable rebirths without having to experience the results, as all the suffering of the miserable rebirths is destroyed. ^3-7

### Third, the direct explanation of the benefits ^3-3-0

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-8]]

Third, the explanation of the actual benefits: ^3-8

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-9]]

Seventy million Victorious Ones
Will swiftly confer empowerment,
And one will attain even greater significance than this,
And proceed to that ultimate state of Buddhahood. ^3-9

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-10]]

This means that, by relying on the power of making single-pointed supplication to that very Bhagavatī—who was empowered by seventy million Victorious Ones throughout the worlds of the ten directions as the Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom—one will, in this very life, attain the temporary qualities of the higher realms. In terms of attaining "greater significance," this refers to being like a universal monarch; in terms of merit, it is like being Indra or Brahmā; and in terms of the ultimate, it refers to proceeding to or attaining the state of Buddhahood, the ultimate fruit of all phenomena, at the eleventh stage of Universal Radiance. ^3-10

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-11]]

Their poisons, which are extremely fierce,
Whether stationary or moving,
Even if eaten or drunk,
Are completely eliminated by remembering [her]. ^3-11

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-12]]

This means that, regarding the time for any person, the obscurations of poison that obstruct the path to attaining the state of Buddhahood—the three obscurations that destroy all the happiness of the state of liberation, which are like the fierce and great [poisons]—are all abandoned by relying on making single-pointed supplication to the Bhagavatī. Furthermore, regarding "stationary or moving," all the obscurations accumulated in the minds of all sentient beings since beginningless time remain fixed until they are destroyed by the vajra-like concentration of the end of the continuum. ^3-12

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-13]]

"Moving," refers to all other types of poisons that move and circulate, such as those of snakes, as well as the poisons of fangs, the poisons of flesh, the poisons of fur, and so forth; all such types of poison are pacified. "Even if eaten or drunk," means that in the same way, for all sentient beings who have eaten or drunk such things and are experiencing unbearable suffering, if those individuals make single-pointed supplication to the Bhagavatī, ^3-13

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-14]]

"Are completely eliminated by remembering [her]," means that by merely remembering the blessings of the Bhagavatī, one attains a supreme and special happiness, and thus one attains the complete elimination of those sufferings. ^3-14

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-15]]

Tormented by demons, infectious diseases, and poisons,
The masses of suffering are thoroughly abandoned,
And this applies to other sentient beings as well. ^3-15

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-16]]

Furthermore, "Tormented by demons, infectious diseases, and poisons, the masses of suffering are thoroughly abandoned," means that the masses of suffering—constantly tormenting and afflicting one through demons that constitute a host of harms, infectious diseases caused by the disturbance of the elements, and various types of poisons—are completely and thoroughly abandoned the moment one makes supplication to the Bhagavatī. "And this applies to other sentient beings as well," means that by merely making supplication to the Bhagavatī through the twenty-one prostrations, one not only accomplishes one's own benefit, but also acts to protect all other sentient beings from whatever suffering they may have. ^3-16

And obstacles will be non-existent and individually destroyed. ^3-18

![[bo-བསྟན་དགའ་སྤྲུལ་སྐུ།#^3-19]]

This means that if one recites this to the Bhagavatī with a mind of supreme faith two, three, or seven times, the reciter will attain all that they desire in the interim, such as children and wealth. The ultimate desire, the permanent happiness of the state of Buddhahood, will also be attained, and until that is attained, may all obstacles and hindrances be individually destroyed by relying upon the blessings of the Bhagavatī. ^3-19
