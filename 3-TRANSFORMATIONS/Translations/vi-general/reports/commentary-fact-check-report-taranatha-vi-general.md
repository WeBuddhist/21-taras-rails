Method: a strict term-by-term alignment against the commentary's own glosses. It checks kāya, named entities, numbers, similes, agents and order, not just the gist. It also judges the Vietnamese-specific word choices (quỷ thần, khởi thi, ác quỷ, Nữ Thần, nhiếp phục, tà thuật, bảy tầng …) against the glosses. Tāranātha's commentary is layered. It gives a literal reading (*yi ge'i don* / *sgra ji bzhin pa*), then hidden (*sbas don*), definitive (*nges don*) and final (*mthar thug*) readings tied to the six-branch vajra-yoga. **Only the literal reading is used as ground truth.** The English fact-check, the English consensus table and the Vietnamese termbase were used as context, not as ground truth. Where the Vietnamese follows a settled English translator decision (1-3 colours to the lotus; 1-8 Ture as her name and "champions of Mara"; 1-14 "seven levels"; 1-17 Vindhya; 1-22 "and"; 2-6 optative) and Tāranātha reads it differently, the row is a MISMATCH and says it is a translator decision. English glosses of the Vietnamese are given in parentheses. This is a preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off: a domain specialist reviews it before it is treated as final, and an LLM never marks its own output complete.

Readings that **support** the current Vietnamese:
- 1-1: "from the opened stamens of the water-born (lotus) **of the face** (*zhal ras kyi chu skyes*) of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the Three Worlds". "Sen mọc từ gương mặt Hộ Chủ ba thế giới / Từ nhụy hoa bừng nở mà ngài đản sinh" fits: the lotus grows from his face and is not his face. *Myur ma*, *dpa' mo* and lightning-quick eyes all match.
- 1-3: "golden stalk, blue flower, a water-born lotus" held in her hand. *Sen thủy sinh sắc vàng pha xanh* ("a gold-and-blue water-born lotus") fits the translator decision to give the colours to the lotus. *Dka' thub ste tshul khrims*: "trì giới" ("discipline") fits, and the old "austerity" problem is gone. *Spyod yul*: "hành xứ" ("sphere of activity") fits.
- 1-5: the seven worlds are nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras and gods, pressed under her feet. "đạp lên bảy thế giới" fits, as does "năng lực triệu thỉnh" (able to summon even Maheśvara as a servant).
- 1-6: *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural ("the various ones famed as makers or Īśvaras"), so **chư Tự Tại** fits. *'byung po* is "Gaṇapati and the like, obstructers and misleaders", so **quỷ thần** fits. *ro langs* here is a class of beings named after the vetāla rite (deva-rākṣasa mantra-adepts, the nine terrifiers …), so **khởi thi** fits. *mdun nas bstod* fits "hiện tiền tán thán".
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* is "the magical devices and mantra-power of adversaries' sorcerers", so **tà thuật** ("sorcery") fits well.
- 1-8: *bdud kyi dpa' bo* is "Māra's heroes, i.e. his **military commanders** (*dmag dpon*)". **dũng tướng ma** ("Māra's valiant generals") fits exactly, and the translator decision "champions of Mara" is supported. The lotus face with a wrathful frown and *gsod pa* ("slays") both match.
- 1-10: "a body that **brings** supreme joy to beings". "ban niềm cực hỷ" fits; the earlier "her own joy" problem is gone. *dbang du mdzad*: **nhiếp phục** fits. He reads *bdud* as Māra who controls other worlds, and "ác ma" is acceptable.
- 1-11: *sa gzhi skyong ba'i tshogs* is "the hosts of earth-protecting kings, **or** earth-goddesses, underground nāgas, asuras, vidyādharas, ḍākinīs", whom she summons and controls. "chúng thần hộ địa … triệu thỉnh" fits his alternative. Liberation from poverty matches.
- 1-12: the half-moon crown ornament, the blazing ornaments, and Amitābha in the matted locks radiating light constantly all match.
- 1-14: the palms strike the earth and the feet pound it. *Rim pa bdun* are the "seven levels **underground**", whose malevolent beings are crushed. The neutral **bảy tầng** ("seven levels"), a translator decision, is compatible. "bảy tầng dưới lòng đất" would follow him exactly, but it is not needed.
- 1-15: "nirvāṇa, supreme peace, resting in the sphere of the truth of **cessation**" fits "Niết-bàn tịch diệt".
- 1-16: "*rig pa* = the **vidyā-mantra** syllable hūṃ, from which arises the **lamp** (*sgron ma*), a body blazing with rays". "ngọn đèn từ minh chú chữ Hum" fits; the earlier "awareness" problem is gone. "hoan hỷ vây quanh" fits: he reads siddhas, vidyādharas and bodhisattvas of joyful mind surrounding her.
- 1-17: "*tu re* = the **swift one**, the goddess who stamps her feet in wrath". "Kính lễ Ture dậm bàn chân" fits; the earlier "syllable ture" problem is gone. On *'bigs byed*: "most Tibetan editions have *'bigs byed*, the king of mountains (Vindhya); the Sanskrit has Kailāśa; either is acceptable". **Vindhya** is supported.
- 1-18 / 1-20: at 1-20 he glosses "*ta ra gnyis brjod*" as "**tāre** twice, plus tuttāre", i.e. the ten-syllable mantra. This supports the Vietnamese recitation form **Tare** for ཏཱ་ར.
- 1-19: gods, kings and kinnaras "rely with respect on your lotus feet". The direction (they serve her) is right; the earlier reversal is gone. *Rtsod pa* = "opponents' disputes" fits "tranh chấp".
- 1-20: the right eye is like the sun (it frightens harmers) and the left like the moon (it bestows life). "đôi mắt … như mặt trời, mặt trăng" fits.
- 1-21: *gdon* = "the eighteen *gdon*, such as the planetary *gdon*", so **ác quỷ** fits. *Mthu* fits **uy lực**. "Ture tối thắng" is compatible with his alternative "*ture* = the swift one".
- 1-22: "the praise by way of the ten-syllable root mantra **is this one**, and the praise with twenty-one homages **is also this one**; *dang* is a joining word". "**Đây là** lời tán thán bằng căn bản chú, / Cùng với hai mươi mốt đoạn kệ kính lễ" fits this identification reading.

Style notes (not errors):
- 1-3 "vàng pha xanh" ("gold blended with blue") suggests one mixed colour. He has a gold stalk and a blue flower ("cọng vàng, hoa xanh"). Optional.
- 1-4 "Hành động vô biên, hoàn toàn chiến thắng" ("acting boundlessly, completely victorious") puts *mtha' yas* ("infinite") on the action. He puts it on the victory and benefits: "victorious over all adverse forces, with infinite benefit".
- 1-12 "vô lượng quang minh" ("immeasurable light") stands for *shin tu 'od* ("intense light"). It echoes Vô Lượng Quang but adds a word. "ngự **trên** búi tóc" ("on the topknot") is *khrod na* ("within the locks").
- 1-15 "Om và Soha" reverses the root order (*svāhā oṃ*). He quotes the root order but gives it no meaning. Not flagged, as in the English audit.
- 1-16 "thân thể **mọi** kẻ thù" ("the bodies of all enemies"): *mọi* ("all") is not in the Tibetan.
- 1-17 "Mạn-đà" can be confused with *mạn-đà-la* (the flower or the maṇḍala); "núi Mandara" is clearer. "Chủng tử **của ngài**" ("your seed-syllable") is possessive, where the root has identity ("she who is the seed in the form of hūṃ").
- 1-18 "Mang dấu ấn hươu như hồ nước chư thiên" needs a comma or a reorder so that the divine-lake simile attaches to the moon, not to the deer-mark.
- 1-20 "dịch bệnh hung hiểm" drops *shin tu* ("extremely"). He glosses the disease as "extremely fierce, hard to cure".
- 2-1 "Nữ Thần": he glosses *lha mo* as the Bhagavatī (Tārā). Capitalised, it points to her. Fine.
- 2-2 "thức giấc" ("wake from sleep") stands for *langs pa*, which he glosses as "rising, promptly". Waking at dusk reads oddly. Also "**luôn** ghi nhớ" ("always recollecting", here and in 2-4): *luôn* is not in the Tibetan (*dran pas*, "by recollection").
- 2-2 "ác nghiệp" ("evil karma"): he glosses *sdig pa* here as the **fruits** of misdeeds (illness, *gdon*, misfortune). The literal rendering is acceptable.
- 2-3 "Đấng **Như Lai** Tôn Thắng": the Tibetan has only *rgyal ba* ("Victors"). The referent, the nirmāṇakāya buddhas who accompany Tārā, matches.
- 2-4 "**lỡ** ăn vào hay lỡ uống vào" ("accidentally eaten or drunk"): *lỡ* is added.

Textual notes (his edition or remarks versus our root; the Vietnamese follows our root, so none of these are errors):
- 1-1: he prefers Nam mkha' bzang po's revised translation, "*'jig rten gsum mgon zhal gi chu skyes / bye ba'i ge sar las ni byung ma*", as clearer. He calls the dharmakāya → rūpakāya reading non-literal.
- 1-12: he reads *zla ba'i dum bu* ("half-moon") for our *zla ba'i rtse mo*. His closing survey quotes *rtse mos*. The meaning (crescent) is the same.
- 1-15: *svāhā dang oṃ dang* (same order as our root, with an extra *dang*).
- 1-16: he reads **སྒྲོན་མ** ("lamp"), the same as our root. DG, GD and TT read སྒྲོལ་མ.
- 1-17: he reports *'bigs byed* in most Tibetan editions and *Kailāśa* in the Sanskrit, and glosses "the snowy mountain where Maheśvara dwells".
- 1-18: he reads **ཧ་ར** twice (our root ཏཱ་ར) and gives the mantra *oṃ namaḥ tāre namo hare hūṃ hare svāhā*.
- 1-20: he reads **ཏ་ར** twice (our root ཧ་ར), explaining it as "tāre twice + tuttāre", the ten-syllable mantra. His opening sentence says *hā ra*, so his own text is not fully consistent here.
- 1-21: he suggests emending the last line to *myur du 'joms pa'i mchog rab nyid ma*.
- 2-1/2-2: on *rab tu brjod*, he says the Sanskrit has *pa*, so "*brtson pas klog cing*" ("reciting diligently") is better.
