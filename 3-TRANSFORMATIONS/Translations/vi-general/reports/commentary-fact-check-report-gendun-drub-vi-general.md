Preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off — a domain specialist and a native
Vietnamese reader review before this is treated as final (an LLM never marks its own
output complete).

Extraction notes: 29 buckets (I-3, 1-1 to 1-22, 2-1 to 2-6), none empty, no cascading
shift. Each bucket opens with Gendun Drub's outline heading, quotes the root line or

not gloss. The a-1 marker holds his own colophon (which names Vairocana as the

Readings that **support** the current Vietnamese:
- 1-1: *myur ma* = swift to benefit beings, *dpa' mo* = heroic in turning back the battle of saṃsāra → "nhanh chóng, dũng mãnh" (swift, heroic). "Đôi mắt ngài tựa tia chớp" (her two eyes like lightning) matches "her two eyes, like lightning that flashes in an instant, look on the three worlds". Her birth from the opening stamens, "từ nhụy hoa bừng nở" (from the blossoming stamens), matches.
- 1-3: *dka' thub* = "that is, ethical discipline" → "trì giới" (keeping the precepts) is right. *spyod yul* = "the perfection of wisdom" → the added "trí tuệ" (wisdom) is supported. Both fix problems the English draft had ("austerity"; wisdom missing).
- 1-5: *'dod / phyogs / nam mkha'* = desire, form, formless realms → "dục giới, sắc giới, vô sắc giới" is exactly his reading. "bảy thế giới" (seven worlds) matches his count: three lower realms, desire-realm gods and humans, form and formless.
- 1-6: *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural ("the chiefs") → "chư Tự Tại" (the Īśvaras). Each class matches its chief. *'byung po* (chief Gaṇapati) = "quỷ thần" (spirits). *ro langs* (chief Maheśvara) = "khởi thi" (risen corpses). *dri za* (chief Pañcaśikha) = "càn-thát-bà" (gandharvas). *gnod sbyin* (chief Vaiśravaṇa) = "dạ-xoa" (yakṣas). *mdun nas bstod* = "hiện tiền tán thán" (praise in her presence).
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* = the sorcery (*mthu*) and effigy devices (*byad ma*) of others → "tà thuật của kẻ thù" (the black magic of enemies) fits. The legs, right drawn in (emptiness) and left extended (compassion), match "chân phải co lại, chân trái duỗi".
- 1-8: *tu re* = "Tārā" → "Độ Mẫu Ture" (Ture as her name). *'jigs pa chen mo* = "the wrathful one" → "đại uy mãnh" (greatly fierce and mighty) fits. *bdud kyi dpa' bo* = the afflictions, and with them the four māras → the literal "dũng tướng ma" (champions of Māra) stands. *dgra bo* = the two obscurations, so the literal "kẻ thù" (enemies) stands.
- 1-9: left hand, with thumb and ring finger holding the utpala and three fingers extended at the heart, is the Three Jewels mudrā. The right hand in the supreme-giving gesture has **one** wheel on its palm → "Lòng bàn tay trang nghiêm bánh xe" (palm adorned with the wheel). This fixes the earlier English "wheels in every direction".
- 1-10: the joy is that of "disciples with faith, whose wishes she fulfils" → "ban niềm cực hỷ" (bestows supreme joy) fits. *dbang du mdzad* → "nhiếp phục" (subjugate).
- 1-11: *sa gzhi skyong ba* = the ten direction-guardians with their retinues → "chúng thần hộ địa" (hosts of earth-guardian gods). *'phongs pa* = the destitute and those tormented by suffering → "nghèo khó" (poverty).
- 1-13: right leg extended, left bent → "Chân phải duỗi, chân trái co". The fire at the end of the eon is the blaze of wisdom-fire, and "vòng lửa" (ring of fire) fits.
- 1-14: *rim pa bdun* = "the seven levels of the world" → "bảy tầng" (seven levels), the translator decision, is supported by this commentary.
- 1-15: *bde ma* = undefiled bliss, *dge ma* = free of afflictions, *zhi ma* = suffering pacified, nirvāṇa as her sphere → "an lạc, thiện lành, tịch tĩnh … Niết-bàn tịch diệt". The ten-syllable mantra has OṂ first and SVĀHĀ last, so "Om và Soha" (Om and Soha) is in the right order.
- 1-16: *yi ge bcu pa'i ngag* = oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā → "câu chú mười chữ" (the ten-syllable mantra). *rig pa hūṃ* = the wrathful mantra (oṃ nama tāre namo hare hūṃ hara svāhā) → "minh chú chữ Hum" (the knowledge-mantra HUM). This fixes the earlier English "hum of awareness".
- 1-17: Ture is Tārā herself stamping ("the feet of Ture, arisen from the seed in the form of HŪṂ") → "Kính lễ Ture dậm bàn chân" (homage to Ture who stamps her feet). This fixes the earlier English "stamp your feet with the syllable ture". *'bigs byed* is listed as a mountain with Meru and Mandara → "núi Vindhya" (Mount Vindhya).
- 1-19: the kings of the hosts of gods are Indra (desire realm) and Mahābrahmā (form realm), and Druma is king of the kinnaras. They **serve** her → "được các vua của chúng trời phụng sự" (served by the kings of the hosts of gods). This fixes the earlier English "sovereign of the gods".
- 1-20: right eye sun-like (wrathful), left eye moon-like (peaceful). HARA twice is the wrathful mantra and TUTTĀRA the peaceful one → matches. *rims* = infectious disease → "dịch bệnh" (epidemic).
- 1-21: *de nyid gsum* = the suchnesses of body, speech and mind: OṂ at the crown, ĀḤ at the throat, HŪṂ at the heart, set on her → "ba chân như an lập nơi thân" (three suchnesses set on her body). "nơi thân" (on her body) is exactly his reading. This fixes the earlier English "established by". The supreme destroyer is Ture Tārā herself → "Ture tối thắng" (supreme Ture).

Style notes (not errors):
- 1-3 *zhi ba*: Gendun Drub reads it as the pacifying of the six perfections' opposites (miserliness, laziness …), not as a seventh item. The Vietnamese keeps "tịch tĩnh" (peace) inside the list, in the root's order. Harmless.
- 1-7 *'khrugs pa* (swirling, turbulent) is not rendered. "Ngự trong ngọn lửa rực rỡ bừng cháy" (abides amid brilliantly blazing fire) keeps the substance.
- 1-8: the termbase note mentions *đại bố úy*, but the text has *đại uy mãnh*. Uy mãnh fits Gendun Drub's "wrathful one". *Bố úy* would risk the "afraid" reading that the English fact-check rejected.
- 1-12: "vô lượng quang minh" (immeasurable light) inflates *shin tu* (glossed "much"). "trên búi tóc" (atop the hair-knot) vs his "amid the hair at the crown". Both are minor, and "vô lượng" is a nice echo of A Di Đà.
- 1-13 and 1-16 *kun nas bskor (rab) dga'*: he reads "disciples with faith who delight in turning the Dharma wheel", and the enemies are theirs (afflictions; self-grasping). "hoan hỷ vây quanh" (surrounded by joy) is the settled literal reading.
- 1-14: his paraphrase has "the sole of her right foot strikes the ground" and her hand in the threatening mudrā. The root says *phyag gi mthil* (palm of the hand). The Vietnamese follows the root, and no change is suggested.
- 1-16: "mọi kẻ thù" adds *mọi* ("all") to *dgra yi lus*.
- 1-18: "như hồ nước chư thiên" (like a lake of the gods) comes after "dấu ấn hươu", so the simile could attach to the mark. He makes the moon the lake-like thing. Also "nọc độc" (venom) is narrower than *dug* (he covers stationary and moving poisons), so consider "chất độc" (poison). "Tare" for *tA ra* is a locked termbase choice.
- 1-19: he reads the "armour of joy" as the peaceful and wrathful mantras held with a joyful, one-pointed mind. "Áo giáp ngài rạng ngời hào quang hoan hỷ" (her armour shines with joyful radiance) is the literal reading. The Tibetan order "disputes and bad dreams" is reversed.
- 1-21: "có ba chân như" can be misparsed as *ba chân* ("three legs") + *như* ("like"). Consider "chân như thân, khẩu, ý" (the suchness of body, speech and mind — Gendun Drub's own gloss) or "tam chân như" to block that reading.
- 1-22: "Đây là …" (This is …) adds a copula and makes the line a closed statement. 2-1's "những lời tán thán này" picks it up again, so this is harmless.
