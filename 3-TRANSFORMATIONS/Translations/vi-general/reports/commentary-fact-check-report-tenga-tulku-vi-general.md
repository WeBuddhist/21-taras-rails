#### Chapter I — I-3

He glosses *oṃ* (the nature of the five kāyas and five wisdoms), *rje btsun ma*
(protector and refuge of the three worlds, holding the three vows), *'phags ma*
(raised far above the level of ordinary beings) and *sgrol ma* (she who frees all
beings to the level of buddhahood). "Om! Kính lễ Chí Tôn Thánh Độ Mẫu!" ("Om!
Homage to the Venerable Noble Tara-Mother"). *Thánh* (ārya, holy as opposed to
ordinary, *phàm*) fits *'phags ma*. *Độ* (to ferry across, to liberate) fits
*sgrol ma*. Clean.

Readings that **support** the current Vietnamese (including places where earlier drafts had problems):
- 1-1: Avalokiteśvara, "the protector of the three worlds", shed many tears. They became a stream, an utpala grew from it, and she was born from its filaments. "Sen mọc từ gương mặt Hộ Chủ ba thế giới, / Từ nhụy hoa bừng nở mà ngài đản sinh" ("a lotus grew from the face of the Protector of the three worlds; from its opening stamens she was born") has the lotus coming from him, not his face as a lotus. That was the earlier error. *myur ma* and *dpa' mo* are "acts quickly for beings" and "courage undaunted": "nhanh chóng, dũng mãnh" fits.
- 1-2: A thousand stars gathered give "light unbearable to the eye", and her face's light is the same. "Như ngàn vì tinh tú cùng hội tụ, / Rực rỡ tỏa ánh hào quang chói lòa" ("like a thousand stars gathered, radiating dazzling light") keeps her face's light as what is being described. The simile is right.
- 1-3: *dka' thub* = "the perfection of ethical discipline". "trì giới" ("keeping the precepts") is right; *khổ hạnh* ("austerity") would have been the earlier English error. *spyod yul* ("domain of meditation") = the perfection of wisdom, and the Vietnamese names "trí tuệ" (wisdom). The English second draft left it out.
- 1-5: *'dod* = desire realm, *phyogs* = the seventeen abodes of the form realm, *nam mkha'* = the formless realm. "dục giới, sắc giới, vô sắc giới" fits exactly. The seven worlds are the five abodes of the desire realm plus form and formless, and "bảy thế giới" has the number right.
- 1-6: *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is read as "the great direction-protectors" (plural), so "chư Tự Tại" (the Īśvaras) fits. *'byung po* = "a class of pretas that roam the sky, always tormented by fear and suffering": *quỷ* (ghost, as in *ngạ quỷ*, preta) fits, so "quỷ thần" is supported. *ro langs* = "corpses made to act alive by the Māra of the dark side": "khởi thi" (risen corpse) fits. *dri za* = bardo wanderers, and "càn-thát-bà" (gandharva) is the standard term.
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* = the "machinations of the dark-side māras" behind adversaries' diseases and harm. "tà thuật của kẻ thù" ("the sorcery of enemies") fits. Right leg drawn in, left extended: the order is right.
- 1-8: "*phyag 'tshal tu re* gives the Venerable One's name", so "Độ Mẫu Ture" fits. The Mara line is glossed as the army of the four māras (divine-son, lord of death, aggregates, afflictions), and "các dũng tướng ma" ("Mara's champions") fits the translator decision.
- 1-9: The left hand at her heart makes the Three-Jewels mudrā, holding the utpala stem. The right hand is in the supreme-giving gesture, **its palm** adorned with a thousand-spoked wheel. "Lòng bàn tay trang nghiêm bánh xe" ("the palm adorned with the wheel") has one wheel, on the palm. The English second draft had "wheels in every direction".
- 1-10: "overwhelms or brings under control all three worlds" — "Nhiếp phục" (subjugates) fits. The five-family crown fits "vương miện".
- 1-15: *bde ma* / *dge ma* / *zhi ma* are glossed as bliss, virtue and pacification of habitual stains, and "an lạc, thiện lành, tịch tĩnh" fits. The mantra is "adorned with oṃ at the beginning and svāhā at the end", so "Om và Soha" is in the right order.
- 1-16: "The peaceful mantra is the ten syllables; the mantra practised in the fierce way is *oṃ tāre hūṃ hara hara hūṃ*." *rig pa hūṃ* is a mantra, and "minh chú chữ Hum" ("the knowledge-mantra Hum") fits. The English second draft had "awareness".
- 1-17: "The Venerable One herself, in fierce wrath, stamps her feet." "Kính lễ Ture dậm bàn chân" has Ture as her, not a syllable she uses. The three worlds are gods above the earth, humans on it and nāgas below, and "ba thế giới" fits.
- 1-19: "The great world-protectors — the kings of the hosts of gods — and gods and kinnaras rely on her." "được các vua của chúng trời phụng sự" ("served by the kings of the hosts of gods") is the right way round. The English second draft had her as their sovereign. *rtsod pa* = quarrels from unhappy minds, and "tranh chấp" fits.
- 1-20: *rims nad* are contagious diseases causing unbearable suffering, and "dịch bệnh hung hiểm" fits. The two eyes are like sun and moon.
- 1-21: "the suchness of body, white OṂ; of speech, red ĀḤ; of mind, blue HŪṂ, set at **the three places of the body**". "ba chân như an lập nơi thân" ("the three suchnesses set at her body") fits. The English second draft had "established by".
- 1-22: "the praise by the root mantra … **and** the twenty-one homages", counted 1 + 6 + 7 + 1 + 6. That is two items, which fits "Cùng với" (the translator decision). He treats the line as a summary of the praise, which fits the Vietnamese closing statement "Đây là…".
- 2-1: "*lha mo de la*" ("to **that** goddess") points back to Tārā. "Nữ Thần", capitalised, fits.
- 2-2: Temporary fearlessness from the eight and sixteen fears; all non-virtue pacified; freed from the lower realms. "vô úy", "ác nghiệp" and "ác đạo" fit.
- 2-6: "… *so sor 'joms par gyur cig*" ("may they be destroyed one by one"), optative. "Nguyện mọi ma chướng tiêu tan" follows it (translator decision, which agrees with this commentary).

Style notes (not errors):
- 1-5 "triệu thỉnh" (to summon or invite, normally said of deities) is used for the beings she draws to the higher realms and liberation. "dẫn dắt" or "thu nhiếp" would fit this commentary's objects better. At 1-11, where the objects are guardian deities, "triệu thỉnh" is apt.
- 1-8 *'jigs pa chen mo* → "bậc đại uy mãnh" ("the one of great fierce majesty") is milder than "Great Terrifying One". The termbase's own example was *đại bố úy*. Tenga Tulku does not gloss the word.
- 1-9 "mười phương" ("ten directions") adds a number not in *phyogs*. It is the usual Vietnamese idiom for all directions. "rực rỡ" softens *'khrugs* ("turbulent"). Tenga Tulku has the light as the wheel's own rays, and "hào quang tự thân" can read as her body's light.
- 1-11 "chúng thần hộ địa" ("earth-protecting deities") is literal. Tenga Tulku identifies them as the ten direction-guardians, and Vietnamese readers may take *hộ địa* as local earth gods (*thổ địa*). "chúng thần hộ thế / hộ phương" is closer.
- 1-12 "vương miện trăng khuyết" ("crescent-moon crown"): the Tibetan says her **head** is adorned with the moon, and Tenga Tulku puts it on the top of her uṣṇīṣa. *vương miện* is also the word used at 1-10 for *dbu rgyan*. "vô lượng quang minh" ("immeasurable light") for *shin tu 'od* repeats Amitābha's name rather than "very". Amitābha as the one who radiates the light matches the commentary.
- 1-13, 1-16 "hoan hỷ vây quanh": Tenga Tulku reads *kun nas bskor* as joyful, faithful disciples who circle her. The Vietnamese ("joyfully surrounded") allows that.
- 1-14 "Vỗ" (pat, slap) for *bsnun* (strike; Tenga Tulku: "press") is light. "đập" would be closer.
- 1-19 "rạng ngời hào quang hoan hỷ" adds *hào quang* (halo) and drops *kun nas*. Tenga Tulku reads the armour as protecting those who rely on her from all ill omens at all times.
- 2-3 "Đấng Như Lai Tôn Thắng": *Như Lai* is added, and *Tôn Thắng* is also the name of Uṣṇīṣavijayā. "chư Thắng Giả" or "chư Phật" avoids the clash. The number (bảy mươi triệu = 70 million) is right.
- 2-4 "lỡ" ("accidentally") is added to eaten/drunk. "Nhờ luôn ghi nhớ" ("through constant recollection") differs from Tenga Tulku's *dran pa tsam gyis* ("by merely recollecting"). His first reading of line 1, the three obscurations as poison ("*chos mtshungs pa*", "comparable to"), is figurative elaboration and is not flagged.
- 2-5 The block ends with a comma. "Cả đối với tất thảy những chúng sinh khác," can then run into 2-6's benefits. Tenga Tulku attaches it to 2-5 ("not only for oneself — she protects other beings from all suffering too"). A full stop ("… cũng vậy.") would fix it.
- 2-6 "trì tụng rõ" ("recite clearly") for *mngon par brjod*: Tenga Tulku reads it as "recite with manifest faith".

Second pass (kāya / named entity / number): no kāya terms at issue. Named entities are right: Avalokiteśvara, Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Amitābha, Meru, Mandara, the kinnaras. Vindhya is the translator decision. The *Phật tử* class issue (1-4) was found on this pass and is already a row. Numbers are right: 100 moons, 1,000 stars, seven worlds, ten syllables, seven levels, three worlds, 21 homages, 70 million, 2/3/7 times. The exceptions are the seven-item perfection list at 1-3 (row) and the added "ten" at 1-9 (style note).
