Method: a strict term-by-term alignment against the commentary's own glosses. It checks kāya, named entities, numbers, similes, agents, list order and what modifies what, not just the gist. It also judges the Chinese word choices (菩薩, 鬼神, 起屍, 邪魅, 攝伏, 天女, 真如, 七千萬 …) against the glosses. Tāranātha's commentary is layered. It gives a literal reading (*yi ge'i don* / *sgra ji bzhin pa*), then hidden (*sbas don*), definitive (*nges don*) and final (*mthar thug*) readings tied to the six-branch vajra-yoga. **Only the literal reading is used as ground truth.** The fact-checked English, the English consensus table, the Chinese word list and the Chinese back-translation, light-check and QA reports were used as context, not as ground truth. Where the Chinese follows a settled English translator decision (1-3 colours to the lotus; 1-8 Ture as her name and "champions of Mara"; 1-14 "seven levels"; 1-17 Vindhya; 1-22 "and"; 2-6 optative) and Tāranātha reads it differently, the row is a MISMATCH and says it is a translator decision. The same holds where the Chinese follows an English consensus fix that Tāranātha did not support (1-3 wisdom, 1-5 three realms, 1-9 palm). Locked Chinese words are kept in every suggested fix. English glosses of the Chinese are given in parentheses. This is a preliminary self-check, not a scholarly sign-off. A domain specialist and a native Chinese reader must review it before it is treated as final, and an LLM never marks its own output complete.

Readings that **support** the current Chinese:
- 1-1: "from the opened stamens of the water-born (lotus) **of the face** (*zhal ras kyi chu skyes*) of Avalokiteśvara, Lord of the Three Worlds". "三世界怙主面容所生蓮花，/ 從其花蕊綻放之中化現者" ("the lotus born from the face of the Lord of the Three Worlds; who appeared from within its opening stamens") fits: the lotus grows from his face and is not his face. "迅捷勇猛者" ("the swift and heroic one") and "雙目猶如剎那閃電光" ("eyes like an instant's lightning") match *myur ma*, *dpa' mo* and the lightning-quick eyes.
- 1-2: line 2 is her face; lines 3–4 are her **body**, blazing with light rays. The Chinese starts line 3 with "她" ("she"), so the blazing belongs to her, not to her face. "一百輪 / 秋季滿月層層相疊" ("a hundred autumn full moons heaped layer on layer") matches *brgya brtsegs*.
- 1-3: "golden stalk, blue flower, a water-born lotus" held in her hand. "手中以金藍色 / 水生蓮花為莊嚴" ("whose hand is adorned with a gold-blue water-born lotus") fits the translator decision to give the colours to the lotus. *Dka' thub ste tshul khrims*: "持戒" ("keeping discipline") fits exactly. *Spyod yul*: "行境" ("sphere of activity") fits.
- 1-4: "from the uṣṇīṣa of **the Tathāgata Vairocana**". "如來頂髻" ("the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa") has no plural in Chinese, so it fits his single named tathāgata; the Hindi plural problem does not arise. "*rgyal ba'i sras po sa bcu'i dbang phyug rnams*": the bodhisattvas, lords of the ten bhūmis. "諸菩薩" ("the bodhisattvas") names them exactly. "以無邊的全然勝利而行持" ("acts with boundless, complete victory") puts *mtha' yas* on the victory, as he does. "極力依止" ("rely on deeply") fits *gus pas brten*.
- 1-5: the seven worlds are nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras and gods, pressed under her feet. "以足踏住七個世界" ("pressing down the seven worlds with her feet") fits, as does "能將一切無餘召集而來" ("able to summon all without exception"; he: even Maheśvara as a servant).
- 1-6: the gods are in root order: "帝釋、火神、梵天、/ 風神" ("Śakra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu"), as he glosses them (Indra king of the gods, Agni chief of the ṛṣis, Brahmā the reputed maker, Vāyu). *sna tshogs dbang phyug* is plural, so "諸自在天" ("the various Īśvara gods") fits. *'byung po* is "Gaṇapati and the like, obstructers and misleaders", so "鬼神" ("spirits") fits. *ro langs* here is a class of beings named after the vetāla rite, so "起屍" fits. "都在她面前讚頌" ("all praise her before her face") fits *mdun nas bstod*.
- 1-7: *pha rol 'khrul 'khor* is "the magical devices and mantra-power of adversaries' sorcerers", so "敵方咒術幻輪" ("the enemy's sorcery and magic wheels") fits well (the light-check fix). "右屈左伸以足踏" ("right drawn in, left extended, trampling with her feet") matches.
- 1-8: *bdud kyi dpa' bo* is "Māra's heroes, i.e. his **military commanders** (*dmag dpon*)". "魔的勇士" ("Māra's champions") fits, and the translator decision "champions of Mara" is supported. *'jigs pa chen po'i sku*: "令人大怖畏" ("causing great terror") fits exactly: she terrifies, she is not afraid. "蓮花面容現忿怒顰眉" ("her lotus face shows a wrathful frown") and "誅殺" ("slays") match.
- 1-9: "the masses of light rays from her own body **stir intensely and radiate** (*rab tu 'khrugs shing 'phros pas*)". "自身的光聚翻騰湧動" ("her own light-masses churn and surge") keeps the stirring; the Hindi problem (a "blazing" word for འཁྲུག) does not arise. "三寶手印之指，/ 莊嚴於心間" ("fingers in the Three Jewels mudrā, adorning her heart") fits the utpala mudrā at the heart.
- 1-10: "a body that **brings** supreme joy to beings". "賜予極喜者" ("who bestows supreme joy") fits; the majesty goes to the crown ornament ("其威嚴 / 頭冠散發光鬘", "her majestic crown spreads garlands of light"), as he reads it. *dbang du mdzad*: "攝伏" ("bring under control") fits. He reads *bdud* as Māra who controls other worlds, and "令魔與世間皆受攝伏" ("brings Māra and the world under control") fits.
- 1-11: *khro gnyer g.yo ba* is "the quivering frown, i.e. the wrathful vidyā with the syllable hūṃ". "以顫動的忿怒顰眉和吽字" ("with a quivering wrathful frown and the syllable hūṃ") fits. "使人從一切貧困中解脫" ("frees people from all poverty") matches *phongs pa* (see style notes for 神眾).
- 1-12: the crescent crown ornament, the blazing ornaments, and Amitābha in the matted locks radiating light constantly all match ("以月牙莊嚴頭頂", "head adorned with a crescent"; "從她髮髻中的阿彌陀佛 / 恆常放出極其燦爛的光芒", "from the Amitābha in her matted locks, intensely brilliant light constantly radiates").
- 1-13: "the garland of fire, a mass of flames like the fire at the time of destruction; she sits in its centre". "安住於如劫末之火般 / 熾燃的火鬘中央" ("who dwells in the centre of a garland of fire blazing like the fire at the end of the eon") fits. "摧毀敵人的軍隊" ("destroys the enemies' armies") matches his "hosts of human and non-human harmers".
- 1-14: the palms strike the earth and the feet pound it. *Rim pa bdun* are the "seven levels **underground**", whose malevolent beings are crushed and "scattered into pieces". The neutral "七層" ("seven levels"), a translator decision, is compatible.
- 1-15: "bliss, virtue, peace; nirvāṇa, supreme peace, resting in the sphere of the truth of **cessation**". "安樂、善妙、寂靜者，/ 以涅槃寂靜為行境" ("the blissful, virtuous, peaceful one, whose domain is the peace of nirvāṇa") fits. "摧毀大罪障" ("destroys great negativity") fits (he adds its fruit, great suffering). On the order of 嗡梭哈 see the style notes.
- 1-16: "surrounded all round by siddhas, vidyādharas and bodhisattvas **of supremely joyful mind**". "周遭環繞極喜者" ("the one surrounded all around, of supreme joy") is acceptable (see style notes). "*rig pa* = the **vidyā-mantra** syllable hūṃ, from which arises the **lamp** (*sgron ma*), a body blazing with rays". "從明咒吽字生起的明燈" ("the lamp arising from the vidyā-mantra syllable hūṃ") fits exactly. "徹底粉碎敵人之身" ("utterly shatters the enemy's body") matches.
- 1-17: "*tu re* = the **swift one**, the goddess who stamps her feet in wrath". "都咧頓足者" ("Ture, who stamps her feet") fits: Ture is Tārā herself. "她即是吽字形相的種子字" ("she is the seed-syllable in the form of hūṃ") keeps the root's identity. She shakes the mountains and the three worlds: "令須彌山、曼陀羅山、頻闍山 / 與三世界皆震動" ("makes Meru, Mandara, the Vindhya and the three worlds all tremble") has the mountains as objects, as he reads it. On *'bigs byed*: "most Tibetan editions have *'bigs byed*, the king of mountains (Vindhya); the Sanskrit has Kailāśa; either is acceptable". **頻闍山** is supported.
- 1-18: the divine-lake simile attaches to the moon: "手持形如天湖、/ 帶有鹿紋的月亮者" ("who holds the deer-marked moon shaped like a divine lake") fits. He removes "stationary and moving poisons"; "所有毒物，無一遺漏" ("all poisons, none left out") covers both. At 1-20 he glosses "*ta ra gnyis brjod*" as "**tāre** twice, plus tuttāre", i.e. the ten-syllable mantra. This supports the recitation form **達咧** for ཏཱ་ར.
- 1-19: gods, kings and kinnaras "rely with respect on your lotus feet". The direction (they serve her, "所侍奉", "所依止") is right. The armour: "the armour of mantra and mudrā, the cause of joy, which the practitioner binds on **all round** … the majesty of body, speech and mind". "以周遍喜樂鎧甲的威嚴" ("by the majesty of the all-round armour of joy") keeps *kun nas* (周遍), makes 威嚴 ("majesty") its own noun, and does not make the armour hers; the Hindi problem does not arise. *Rtsod pa* = "opponents' disputes" fits "爭鬥" ("strife").
- 1-20: the right eye is like the sun (it frightens harmers) and the left like the moon (it bestows life). "雙目如圓滿日月" ("both eyes like the full sun and moon") fits. "極其猛烈的傳染病" ("extremely fierce contagious diseases") keeps *shin tu*; he glosses them as "extremely fierce, hard to cure" epidemics.
- 1-21: *gdon* = "the eighteen *gdon*, such as the planetary *gdon*", so the locked **邪魅** ("harmful spirits") fits. *Mthu* fits the locked **威力** ("might"). "摧毀它們的最勝都咧" ("the supreme Ture who destroys them") is compatible with his alternative "*ture* = the swift one".
- 1-22: "the praise by way of the ten-syllable root mantra **is this one**, and the praise with twenty-one homages **is also this one**; *dang* is a joining word". "這篇以根本咒所作的讚頌，/ 以及二十一偈敬禮" ("this praise made with the root mantra, and the twenty-one verses of homage") points both descriptions at this text, so it is compatible with his reading. It follows the translator decision (1-22 "and").

Style notes (not errors):
- 1-2 "星辰匯聚所放射的光芒" ("the light radiated by the gathered stars") gives her the stars' own light. He glosses *kyis* as "like" (*lta bu*): light like thousands of stars. The literal Chinese is acceptable.
- 1-3 "金藍色" ("gold-blue colour") suggests one mixed colour. He has a gold stalk and a blue flower. "金莖藍花的水生蓮花" would say it. Optional.
- 1-9 "湧動" ("surging") only half covers *'phros* ("radiating"). The QA suggestion "翻騰四射" ("churning and radiating in all directions") says both. Optional.
- 1-10 "以都達咧的大笑聲" ("with the great laughter of tuttāre") merges the laughter and the mantra. He lists laughter, great laughter and the sound of oṃ tuttāre. Fine, as in the English.
- 1-11 "守護大地的神眾" ("the hosts of deities who guard the earth") adds 神 ("deity"). He reads "the hosts of **kings** who protect the earth, **or** earth-goddesses, underground nāgas, asuras, vidyādharas and ḍākinīs". "守護大地者眾" ("the hosts of earth-guardians") would cover both. Optional.
- 1-15 "嗡梭哈" puts oṃ before svāhā; the root has "svāhā, oṃ" and he keeps that order ("*svāhā dang oṃ dang*"). He also glosses svāhā as the ending of the pacifying mantra, which fits oṃ first. The consensus left this (DG only). A reader may take 嗡梭哈 as one mantra; "梭哈與嗡字" would keep the root order. Optional.
- 1-16 "周遭環繞極喜者" ("surrounded all around, of supreme joy") leaves the joyful retinue implicit. "為極喜眷屬所環繞" ("surrounded by a joyful retinue") would say what he says. Optional. "敵人之身" ("the enemy's body") matches his single enemy (Māra Pāpīyān and the like).
- 1-19 "爭鬥" ("strife, fighting") is a little stronger than *rtsod pa* ("disputes"). "爭訟" or "爭論" would be closer. A native reader should judge.

Textual notes (his edition or remarks versus our root; the Chinese follows our root, so none of these are errors):
- 1-1: he prefers Nam mkha' bzang po's revised translation, "*'jig rten gsum mgon zhal gi chu skyes / bye ba'i ge sar las ni byung ma*", as clearer. He calls the dharmakāya → rūpakāya reading non-literal.
- 1-2: he quotes "*skar ma stong phrag **mang po** tshogs pa*" ("many thousands of gathered stars"). See the 1-2 row.
- 1-12: he reads *zla ba'i dum bu* ("half-moon") for our *zla ba'i rtse mo*. His closing survey quotes *rtse mos*. The meaning (crescent) is the same, and "月牙" ("crescent moon") fits both.
- 1-15: *svāhā dang oṃ dang* (same order as our root, with an extra *dang*).
