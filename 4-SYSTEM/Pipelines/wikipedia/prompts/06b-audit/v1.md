Audit the following bo.wikipedia article draft about "$term" sentence by sentence against
its atomic claims table. You are the last reader before publication: your job is to catch
what the drafting and polishing models changed, added, or dropped.

## Findings to detect

- **added-fact** — a factual assertion no cited claim covers (a sentence with an empty
  `citations` array that nonetheless asserts a fact counts).
- **dropped-qualifier** — a claim's qualifier weakened or gone (ཙམ།, ཁོ་ན།, negations,
  conditions, scope words).
- **terminology-drift** — one of the locked glossary terms paraphrased: $glossary
- **attribution-loss** — a sub-consensus claim (`claim_type` below `consensus`) sitting in
  Wikipedia's neutral voice, or an attribution softened. A school-position silently promoted
  to neutral voice is an audit failure.
- **wrong-claim** — a `citations` entry pointing at a claim that does not support the
  sentence.
- **meaning-shift** — the sentence no longer says what its claims say.

## Severity

`blocking` — added-fact and attribution-loss are always blocking; any other finding that
changes what a reader would believe is too. `advisory` — style-level drift worth a look.

## Output

JSON only:

```
{
 "term": "...",
 "findings": [
  {
   "sentence": "…the sentence, quoted…",
   "claim_indices": [3],
   "finding": "added-fact | dropped-qualifier | terminology-drift | attribution-loss | wrong-claim | meaning-shift",
   "severity": "blocking | advisory",
   "note": "…one line…"
  }
 ],
 "verdict": "publish | fix | return-to-drafting"
}
```

`verdict` is `publish` only if there are no blocking findings. With blocking findings, use
`fix` when they are localized, `return-to-drafting` when the draft misuses the claims
throughout. An empty findings list with verdict `publish` is a legitimate answer — do not
invent findings to seem thorough.
