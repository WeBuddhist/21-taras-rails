**Expanding the Digital Footprint of Tibetan: A Semi-Automatic Pipeline for Wikipedia Article
Generation Using LLMs**

Tashi Tsering, The OpenPecha Project (tashitsering@dharmaduta.in)

Tibetan, despite its profound historical and cultural importance, remains underrepresented in the
field of Natural Language Processing (NLP). Unlike major world languages, Tibetan lacks the
large-scale digital footprint that fuels academic research and commercial interest. This scarcity
of digital resources has limited the development of tools and models that could support Tibetan
language technologies. Addressing this gap requires not only advances in computational methods but
also the systematic creation of high-quality, open digital content.

This paper presents a semi-automatic pipeline for generating Tibetan-language Wikipedia articles
with proper citations, leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs). The pipeline combines machine
assistance with human oversight to ensure linguistic accuracy, contextual appropriateness, and
source reliability. By automating key stages—such as information extraction, structuring, and
citation alignment—while retaining a human-in-the-loop editorial process, the approach lowers the
barrier for producing encyclopedic content in Tibetan. Beyond contributing to Tibetan Wikipedia,
this initiative strategically increases the language's digital footprint, thereby creating
training data that can further support Tibetan NLP research. Crucially, a richer Tibetan digital
footprint also ensures that Tibetan knowledge is more visible to LLM-powered applications—such as
Perplexity and other AI chat systems—enabling broader access to Tibetan cultural and scholarly
resources in the global digital sphere.

We argue that such a cyclical process—where digital content creation feeds computational research,
and improved tools in turn accelerate content production—offers a sustainable model for minority
and under-resourced languages. The presentation will demonstrate the pipeline, evaluate its early
outputs, and discuss implications for Tibetan studies, digital humanities, and the broader field
of computational linguistics.
