**Tārā (Drolma)** — regarding the etymology of the name, three different scholars agree
in explaining it as: she who delivers and protects sentient beings from the ocean of
suffering, or from the suffering of saṃsāra.⟦1⟧⟦2⟧⟦3⟧

## Etymology (ངེས་ཚིག)

As for the etymology of the name "Drolma": three different scholars explain it in
agreement — as delivering from the ocean of suffering; or as acting to deliver all
sentient beings to the level of buddhahood ⟨the Tibetan reads ས་ལས་, literally "from
the buddha-level"; the intended sense is evidently "to" it — an awkwardness inherited
from the claim's own wording⟩; or as protecting from the suffering of saṃsāra. All
three agree on the meaning of the name.⟦1⟧⟦2⟧⟦3⟧

## Characteristics (མཚན་ཉིད།)

In the commentary composed by the First Dalai Lama, Gendün Drub, separate alternative
names are given for the Tārās of several verses of the praise: for verse 2, "White Tārā
of Radiant Countenance"; for verse 11, "Tārā Who Accomplishes Bliss" and also "Tārā Who
Proclaims the Sound of HŪṂ"; for verse 17, "Tārā Who Bestows the Supreme
Empowerment."⟦1⟧×4 ⟨the renderer emits one ref per supporting passage; four passages
from the same ṭīkā back this sentence, so the same citation appears four times — a
cosmetic point for the pre-publication review⟩

For the name of the ninth verse of praise, both the Geluk master Gendün Drub and the
Nyingma master Khenchen Palden Sherab agree in calling her "Tārā of the Khadira Forest"
(Khadiravaṇī Tārā).⟦1⟧⟦4⟧

Khenchen Palden Sherab also calls the Tārā of the tenth verse "the Venerable Noble Tārā
Who Dispels Poverty and Bestows Wealth."⟦4⟧

## Summary (བསྡུས་དོན།)

On Tārā's history, two Geluk commentaries agree in relating: long ago, in the era of
the Tathāgata "Drum-Sound" (Dundubhisvara), a king's daughter named Yeshe Dawa ("Moon
of Wisdom") presented great offerings to that buddha and generated the resolve for
awakening. Then, by the power of her accomplishing the samādhi that delivers beings
from saṃsāra, her former name Yeshe Dawa became "Drolma," the Deliverer.⟦3⟧⟦5⟧

One Geluk commentary states that if, at the moment one recalls Tārā's qualities and
the occasion arrives ⟨phrase is slightly compressed in the Tibetan⟩, one prays with
one-pointed mind, she protects from the eight fears and the sixteen fears.⟦3⟧

## The schools' respective explanations (གཞུང་ལུགས་སོ་སོའི་བཤད་པ།)

Sermé Tsang Geshé Tendzin Dönzang states: although the Praise in Twenty-One Homages is
generally famed as a praise of Khadiravaṇī Tārā, chief of all the Tārās, when analyzed
in detail it comes down to a praise of the twenty-one Tārās.⟦5⟧

On this point, some scholars hold that all twenty-one Tārās are emanations of
Khadiravaṇī Tārā;⟦5⟧

while certain other scholars hold that the twenty-one Tārās are twenty-one distinct
families — and the two positions disagree.⟦5⟧
