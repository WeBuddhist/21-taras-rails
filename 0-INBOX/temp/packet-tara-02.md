**English:** On this point, some commentators explain that the goddess's face is clear like the moon, and that rays of star-like light — or light even more radiant than that — emanate from her face or body; this is not the tantra's own meaning, because starlight is an example of dim, unclear light, and joining it with the goddess's face becomes an ornament of denigration for the goddess, which is not good. This is known as a fault of some commentators who explained the tantra's word "rab tu phye" [thoroughly opened/surpassing] in the sense of "surpassing," while others explained it in the sense of "adorned by."

#### c-3-2-6 Correct reading of "rab tu phye": made vividly manifest, adorned at the edge by stars

**English:** Therefore, "rab tu phye" [thoroughly opened] means "made vividly manifest to view" — that is, the moon's clear and radiating splendour is brought vividly before the eyes, adorned at its edge by the cluster of stars.
