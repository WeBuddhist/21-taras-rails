#### c-3-3-6 Etymology: austerity as moral discipline, peace as wisdom, and the six perfections as her domain
**བོད་ཡིག:** 8 ཐོག་མར་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཀྱི་མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་བསྲུང་དཀའ་ཞིང་དེ་བསྲུང་ཐུབ་པའམ་ནུས་པས་ན་བཀའ་ཐུབ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། ཞི་བ་མྱང་འདས་ཐོབ་པའི་རྩ་བར་གྱུར་པས་ན་ཞི་བའམ་ཤེས་རབ་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་བཅས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་དུ་བྱས་ནས་བསླབ་ཅིང་བརྩོན་པས
**English:** First, generosity and diligence; and moral discipline — because it is difficult to guard against what is discordant with it, yet she is able to guard it, hence "austerity" [means] moral discipline; and "peace" — because it becomes the root of attaining the peace of nirvana, hence "peace" [means] wisdom; together with patience and meditative concentration, she has made the ten perfections her domain, training in and striving at them.

**English:** Now, as a sign that these [perfections] have been brought to completion and her intelligence has fully expanded with respect to the knowable, she holds, as a hand-symbol, a water-born lotus flower grasped at the end of its stem between ring finger and thumb, its tip opened level with her ear and fully blossoming — seeing her thus adorned, how could one possibly remain free of faith? — that is the meaning.

**English:** Moreover: generosity comprises three — the giving of material goods, the giving of protection from fear, and the giving of Dharma. Diligence comprises five — armor-like diligence, the diligence of application, the diligence that does not grow discouraged, the diligence of non-reversion, and the diligence that is not satisfied with little. Ethical discipline comprises three — the discipline of restraining misconduct, the discipline of gathering virtuous qualities, and the discipline of benefiting sentient beings. Wisdom comprises three — wisdom realizing the ultimate, wisdom realizing the conventional, and wisdom realizing beings' welfare. Patience comprises three — the patience of accepting suffering, the patience of being unperturbed by harm, and the patience of certainty regarding the Dharma. Meditative stability comprises three — the stability causing body and mind to abide blissfully in this life, the stability that manifestly accomplishes qualities, and the stability that benefits sentient beings.
