## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but they mark it three different ways. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the eighteenth" (བཅོ་བརྒྱད་པ/བཅུ་བརྒྱད་པ) or its numeral, verified in the claim text itself (not merely in the packet's own node-header metadata): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-1` (numeral "༼༡༨༽"), `taranatha:c-18-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-1` (spelled བཅུ་བརྒྱད་པ, a variant of བཅོ་བརྒྱད་པ).

— explicit global ordinal attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-1`, `taranatha:c-18-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-1` (8 commentaries)

Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the third" (གསུམ་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the third member of a "dispelling the poisons of the moving and unmoving [animate and inanimate]" sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-1`. These are close but not character-identical: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-1` ("གསུམ་པ་བརྟན་གཡོའི་དུག་རྣམས་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི") and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-1` ("གསུམ་པ་བརྟན་གཡོའི་དུག་ཐམས་ཅད་སེལ་བར་མཛད་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི") both use the "སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ...ནི" ("praising by way of...") construction; `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-1` drops that construction ("གསུམ་པ་བརྟན་གཡོའི་དུག་རྣམས་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ནི" — "the third, the activity of dispelling..."); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-1` gives only the bare ordinal ("གསུམ་པ་ནི") with the sub-group content ellipted (editorially bracketed in the packet as "[dispelling poisons of the mobile and immobile]"). `sungrab-tulku:c-21-1` also uses this "dispelling all moving and stationary poisons" thematic phrasing but pairs it with the global numeral "(18)" rather than "the third" — already counted above under the explicit-ordinal group.

The remaining four (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no ordinal marker — global or local — within the claims in this packet; their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match (three are flagged ⓘ in the packet as "routed by claim, not by node"), and `sangye-nyentrul`'s own node is externally numbered "19" with "༡༨" appearing only in the packet's node-title metadata, not inside any cited claim's own text.

Eight commentaries quote all four lines of the root verse verbatim: seven give one continuous verbatim block, and one (drakpa-gyaltsen) splits the verbatim quotation cleanly across two adjacent claims, each followed by its own gloss. A ninth (gendun-drub) verbatim-quotes only the verse's first half; on inspection, its second-half claim proves to be a gloss rather than a quotation (see below) — corrected here from an earlier miscount that had placed it, and lobsang-dawa, in the split-verbatim group:

— full verbatim quotation (single block) attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-61` (7 commentaries)

— full verbatim quotation split across two claims attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-88` (lines 1–2, followed by the boundary marker ཞེས་པ་ནི and its own gloss), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-90` (lines 3–4, followed by the verse-number marker "40" and its own gloss) (1 commentary)

`anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-2`'s quotation reads "ཏྭ་ར" (TVARA) where the root text (`1-SOURCES/Text/སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པ།.md#^1-18`) and every other commentary in this packet read "ཏཱ་ར" (TĀRA) — a genuine textual variant, discussed further under "The vidyā-mantra," below. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-2` quotes the verse's first two lines verbatim, character-identical to the root ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པའི། །རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན་ཕྱག་ན་བསྣམས་མ།"); its own claim title labels this half of the quotation "Verse 19," even though the spine map routes it here as verse 18's content — an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's own extraction, not evidence that gendun-drub disputes which verse this is (the same pattern was noted for this commentary on `tara-09`). Its next claim, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-4` ("ངག་ཏུ་ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེས་གཡོ་བའི་དུག་རྣམས་མ་ལུས་པར་ནི་སེལ་མའོ།"), is *not* a verbatim continuation of lines three and four: it interpolates "ངག་ཏུ" ("verbally") and "གཡོའི" ("of the moving," specifying which poison) into the root's own wording and alters the closing particle — a gloss built on root vocabulary, cited for its actual content under "Scope of poisons and benefits dispelled," below, rather than counted here as a verbatim quote. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-2` and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-3` are the same kind of gloss throughout, not merely at a joint: root vocabulary is interspersed with the commentary's own additions from the start of each claim ("ལྟར་དཀར་ཞིང་ཟླུམ་པ་ཡི," "ཟླ་བའི་དབུས་སུ," "བརྟན་པའི་དུག་སེལ་མ" in the first; "ཀྱི་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པའི་སྔགས་ཀྱི་སྭཧཱ་ཡི་གནས་སུ" in the second), so neither supplies a clean verbatim run of either half; both are counted in the gloss/paraphrase group below, not here.

The remaining seven commentaries give only a gloss or paraphrase in this packet, without a clean or split verbatim four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-1` (through `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-5`, word-by-word), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-2`–`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-2`–`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-3` (see above), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-48`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49` (no verse text quoted verbatim in either claim, routed by claim-content match), `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-1`–`sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-2`, `taranatha:c-18-1`, `taranatha:c-18-3` (the verse's opening words only, with the mantra portion replaced by taranatha's own HARA-based reading rather than a verbatim quotation of the root's TĀRA-PHAṬ line — see "The vidyā-mantra," below).

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name or classificatory label for this homage gives one of several genuinely different labels; this facet is a set of divergent traditions, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"Tārā of Victory"** (རྣམ་རྒྱལ་སྒྲོལ་མ / རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-1` ("རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་རྣམ་རྒྱལ་སྒྲོལ་མ"), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-2` (same phrase), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-5` ("འདི་ལ་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་འབྱུང་ངོ") — a near-identical but not character-identical epithet, the noun form རྣམ་རྒྱལ་ ("Victory") versus the verb-phrase form རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ ("Utterly Victorious"). Two commentaries.
2. **"Great Peacock/Peahen, who pacifies and protects from all kinds of poison"** (རྨ་བྱ་ཆེན་མོ, paired with དུག་རིགས་...ཞི་ཞིང་སྲུང་བ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-0-1` ("བཅོ་བརྒྱད་པ་དུག་རིགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཞི་ཞིང་སྲུང་བའི་རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་རྨ་བྱ་ཆེན་མོ"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-1` ("དུག་རིགས་ཞི་ཞིང་སྲུང་བའི་འཕགས་མ་རྨ་བྱ་ཆེན་མོ") — the two share the "དུག་རིགས...ཞི་ཞིང་སྲུང་བའི" + "རྨ་བྱ་ཆེན་མོ" construction closely, though `palden-sherab` adds "ཐམས་ཅད" ("all") where this specific `sangye-nyentrul` claim does not — a close paraphrase, not a verbatim match. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-21` independently names her "སྒྲོལ་མ་རྨ་བྱ་ཆེན་མོ" ("Tārā, Great Peahen") without the poison-pacifying qualifier phrase attached in this claim. Three commentaries.
3. **"Dispeller of Poison"** (དུག་སེལ་མ), a general (not nāga-specific) epithet — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-1` ("བཅུ་བརྒྱད་པ་དུག་སེལ་མ་ལ་བསྟོད་པ"), heading-level. `sungrab-tulku:c-21-3` independently uses the identical two-word epithet mid-gloss ("...རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་དུག་སེལ་མ་ལ་སྒོ་གསུམ་གྱི་གུས་པས་འདུད་པའོ" — "...venerable Tārā, the Dispeller of Poison, [homage] with respectful bowing through the three doors"; already cited above under "Structural placement" and under 4(a) below) — so the epithet is attested by two commentaries, not unique to tenzin-dhonzang as an earlier draft of this page stated.
4. **Functional/thematic classification by the poisons dispelled, rather than a proper name** — two sub-patterns: (a) "praise by way of dispelling the poisons of the moving and unmoving [animate/inanimate]" — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-1` (already cited under "Structural placement," above); (b) "homage by way of dispelling nāga poison" — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-1`, corroborated in substance (though not in a shared epithet) by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4` ("chiefly worshipped and praised through dispelling nāga-caused illness, symbolised by poison-illness") and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-5` (citing Nyima Sbaspa's own colophon-line naming this a "sādhana of the venerable Tārā of Victory for curing nāga-caused illness"). `anon-trinle-char` and `konchok-thabkhe` independently single out nāga-poison as this verse's specific application, though from different commentaries, in different wording, and without either using the other's proper-name epithet.

The remaining commentaries — `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha` — supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims in this packet. (`taranatha:c-18-1`'s heading, "eighteenth, homage to [she of] the divine [lake]," simply quotes the root verse's own opening words as a section marker and is not treated as an interpretive name.)

### Unique
None beyond the divergent traditions enumerated above.

## The animal-mark on the moon: deer or hare

### Consensus
The root text itself reads "རི་དགས་རྟགས་ཅན" (deer-marked; `1-SOURCES/Text/སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པ།.md#^1-18`). Ten commentaries retain "deer" (རི་དྭགས་) without glossing or substituting a different animal in the claims consulted for this slot:

— deer-only attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-88`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-89`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-3`, `taranatha:c-18-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-3` ("རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན་ནམ་ཟླ་བ་ཉ་གང་བ" — "the deer-marked [moon], that is, the full moon"; consulted for this slot but previously undisposed on this page), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-61`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-21` (10 commentaries; `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-7` was removed from this list on review — that claim describes body colour, arms, seat and mudrās only and never mentions the animal-mark at all, so it does not belong here; it remains correctly cited under "Iconographic form," below)

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries independently and explicitly gloss the root's "deer-marked" as "hare-marked" (རི་བོང་), substituting rather than merely adding the second animal:
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-2` quotes the root's own "རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན" verbatim, then its very next claim, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-3`, glosses the same moon-disc as "རི་བོང་གི་རྟགས་ཅན" ("marked with the sign of the hare") without repeating "རི་དྭགས" at all.
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-1` does the same within a single claim: "…རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན་ཏེ། རི་བོང་ཅན་ཟླ་བ་ཕྱག་ན་བསྣམས་ལ" — "deer-marked, that is, hare-marked moon, held in hand" — using the explanatory particle ཏེ ("that is") to equate the two terms explicitly.

Three further commentaries name *both* animals together in a single compound phrase, rather than substituting one for the other — a distinct pattern from the clean substitution above, and one whose grammatical function (apposition of two terms, or a compound meaning something closer to "wild hare") cannot be resolved from the claims alone: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-3` ("རི་དྭགས་རི་བོང་གི་རྟགས་མཚན་ཅན"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-2` ("རི་དྭགས་རི་བོང་གི་རྟགས་ཅན"), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-3` ("རི་དྭགས་རི་བོང་གི་རྟགས་མཚན་ཅན") — all three character-identical or near-identical in this specific compound.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-48` names only "deer," with no hare and no elaboration beyond the bare epithet ("རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན་གྱི་ཟླ་བ" — "the deer-marked moon") — counted among the deer-only group by its wording, but listed separately here because, unlike the other ten, it supplies no surrounding iconographic elaboration to compare.

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## The vidyā-mantra: wording variants

### Consensus
None as a single wording — this facet is a genuine multi-way divergence, comparable in kind to the mantra-wording note internal to `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-6` itself (below). (This mention is a structural analogy only, not an attestation of any position from that claim; its substantive content — the four masters' readings — is discussed under Divergences, below, which is the only place it is cited as evidence.)

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **Root-matching short formula, "TĀRA (uttered twice) + PHAṬ,"** given either as a bare restatement of the root's own line or folded into a longer gloss, with no extended ten-syllable form supplied in the claim: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-90`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-4`. Seven commentaries.
2. **Extended ten-syllable form, "OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE (SVĀHĀ)," with a further PHAṬ-based extension appended:** `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-2` (announces the "profound ten-syllable vidyā-mantra...adorned with PHAṬ" without quoting it in full) + `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-2` (quotes it in full: "ཨཱོྃ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སརྦ་བི་ཥ་ཏཱ་ར་ཧཱུྃ་ཕཊཱ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ"), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49` (gives two acceptable forms: "ཨོཾ་ཏ་ར་ཏ་ར་ཕཊ" as "the tantra's own direct teaching," or "ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་ཕཊ་སྭཱཧཱ" as "the main recitation"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-2` (announces the ten-syllable mantra "adorned with PHAT" without quoting it), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-62` ("ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སརྦ་བི་ཥ་ཏཱ་ར་ཕཊ་སྭཱཧཱ" — near-identical to `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-2`'s wording but omitting the "ཧཱུྃ" syllable palden-sherab includes), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-4` (gives both the ten-syllable base "ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུ་ཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སྭ་ཧཱ" and an extended fifteen-syllable form adding "ཏཱ་ར་ཏཱ་ར་ཕཊ"). Six commentaries. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-2` gives a further extended form ("ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུདྷཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སྦཱ་ཧཱ") whose wording ("ཏུདྷཱ་རེ" rather than the more usual "ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ") and whose surrounding syntax ("ཏཱ་རེ་པའོ") are uncertain in this extraction; it is not folded into the count above but is noted as a probable further witness to the same tuttāre-based tradition.
3. **TVARA (ཏྭ་ར), not TĀRA, as the doubled syllable** — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-2` (root-verse quotation itself reads "ཏྭ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད"), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-5` (mantra gloss: "ཏྭ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཀྱིས་མཚོན་པའི...ལ་སྭཱཧཱའི་གནས་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེ་བཏགས་པའི་སྔགས"). Unique to this commentary among the packet's claims for this slot; it diverges from the root text's own "ཏཱ་ར" reading (`1-SOURCES/Text/སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པ།.md#^1-18`).
4. **HARA (ཧ་ར), not TĀRA, as the doubled syllable** — `taranatha:c-18-4` ("དེའི་རིག་སྔགས་ཧ་ར་ཞེས་པ་ལན་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཅིང..."), `taranatha:c-18-6` (quotes the mantra in full: "ཨོཾ་ན་མཿཏཱ་རེ་ན་མོ་ཧ་རེ་ཧཱུྃ་ཧ་རེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ"), `taranatha:c-18-7` (an appended action-mantra, "སརྦ་བི་ཛཿ ཤནིཾ་ཀུ་རུ་ཕུཊ," said to belong to "wrathful Tārā"), `taranatha:c-18-12` (the definitive-meaning gloss below reads "ཧ་ར" as "seizing" — see "Hidden meaning," below). This is a striking departure from the root text's own "ཏཱ་ར." Unique to taranatha among the packet's claims for this slot.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-6` itself reports this kind of disagreement directly, as an inventory of "four masters'" differing readings of "the vidyā-mantra containing tāra twice": Jonangpa's "ཨོཾ་ན་མཿ ཏཱ་རེ་ན་མོ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཧཱུྃ་ཧ་རེ་སྭཱཧཱ" with the PHAṬ-support syllable folded into a separate mantra "སརྦ་བི་ཛཿ ཤནྟིངྐུ་རུ་ཕཊ"; Ngulchu's instruction to apply PHAṬ in place of SVĀHĀ at the end of the ten-syllable tāra-marked mantra; Co ne pa's ten-syllable-plus-doubled-TĀRA-plus-bare-PHAṬ reading; and Gedun Drub's "TĀRA TĀRA PHAṬ" (doubled TĀRA with a terminal PHAṬ), which `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-6` itself judges to match the tantra's own marginal wording most closely.

Read together with position 4 above, `taranatha:c-18-6`'s own quoted mantra — "ཨོཾ་ན་མཿཏཱ་རེ་ན་མོ་ཧ་རེ་ཧཱུྃ་ཧ་རེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ" — is very close to, but not character-identical with, the "Jonangpa" reading `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-6` itself reports ("ཨོཾ་ན་མཿ ཏཱ་རེ་ན་མོ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཧཱུྃ་ཧ་རེ་སྭཱཧཱ"): the two differ in a single word, "ན་མོ་ཧ་རེ" (taranatha's own text) against "ན་མོ་ཏཱ་རེ" (the Jonangpa reading anon-trinle-char reports). Similarly, `taranatha:c-18-7`'s appended mantra ("སརྦ་བི་ཛཿ ཤནིཾ་ཀུ་རུ་ཕུཊ") closely echoes, without exactly matching, the PHAṬ-support mantra `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-6` attributes to Jonangpa ("སརྦ་བི་ཛཿ ཤནྟིངྐུ་རུ་ཕཊ") — the differences (ཤནིཾ/ཤནྟིངྐུ, ཕུཊ/ཕཊ) read as orthographic variants of the same Sanskrit "śāntiṃ kuru phaṭ" rather than as a different mantra. Because taranatha is himself a Jonang-tradition master, this near-match is a plausible identification of taranatha's own reading with the "Jonangpa" position anon-trinle-char reports at second hand — but it is reported here as a close, non-identical echo, not as a verbatim corroboration, per the character-level differences just noted.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-18-7`'s classification of the appended PHAṬ-mantra as "the action-mantra of wrathful Tārā" (སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྲོ་མོའི་ལས་སྔགས) is not paralleled by any other claim in the packet for this slot.

## Iconographic form: body colour, arm-count, and the moon's placement

### Consensus
Where a body colour is given, all agree it is white — no commentary in this packet gives a different colour for this Tārā (unlike, e.g., the red/green divergence recorded for `tara-09`):

— white body colour attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-21` (4 commentaries)

Four commentaries independently and explicitly place the moon atop an opened utpala flower held in Tārā's *left* hand — a detail not present in the root verse's own wording, which names only "holds in her hand," without specifying the hand or an utpala:

— left-hand utpala placement attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-1` ("ཕྱག་གཡོན་ན་བསྣམས་པའི..."), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-1` (left hand forms the Three-Jewels mudrā at the utpala's pollen-centre), `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-2` ("གཡོན་ཨུཏྤལ་འཛིན་པའི་སྟེང་ན...ཟླ་བ..."), `sungrab-tulku:c-21-3` ("རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མའི་ཕྱག་གཡོན་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་ཨུཏྤལ་ཁ་ཕྱེ་བའི་སྟེང་དུ...ཟླ་བ..."), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-3` ("ཕྱག་གཡོན་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་ཨུཏྤལ་ཁ་ཕྱེ་བའི་སྟེང་དུ...ཟླ་བ..."). Four commentaries (`palden-sherab` cited via two of its own claims for two distinct sub-points of the same placement).

### ⚑ Divergences
Arm-count is a genuine two-way divergence: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-7` gives this Tārā one face and **four** arms ("ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་བཞི་མ"), seated on a swan atop white lotus and moon, in bodhisattva posture; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-1` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-2` both give one face and **two** hands ("ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས"), right hand in supreme-giving mudrā and left holding the utpala-and-moon described above. All three agree the body is white (see Consensus, above); the divergence is arm-count and seat/posture only. (These same three claims are cited above under Consensus for body-colour or utpala-placement and here for arm-count: two distinct, non-conflicting facts drawn from the same claim text, not two sides of one divergence.)

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char`'s own two claims describing this four-armed form are not fully consistent with each other: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-7` (its own synthesis) states that the first pair of hands forms "the mudrā of joy" (དགའ་བའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ), while `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-8`, citing Nyima Sbaspa's own verse in support, instead describes the first pair of hands as holding "an iron hook above the crown" (གཉིས་ཀྱི་སྤྱི་བོར་ལྕགས་ཀྱུ་འཛིན) — a different gesture for the same hand-pair, with no iron hook mentioned in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-7`'s own description and no joy-mudrā mentioned in the Nyima Sbaspa verse quoted at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-8`. Both claims agree on the second right hand's supreme-giving mudrā and (with `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-8` adding a held volume) the second left hand's implement. No other commentary in the packet supplies a parallel four-armed description against which to resolve this internal tension; it is recorded here as a finding about `anon-trinle-char`'s own two claims, not assigned to either the Consensus or Divergence lists above.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-1` additionally specifies both legs in archer's posture (རྟ་བབས), a detail not paralleled elsewhere in the packet's claims for this slot.

## Scope of poisons and benefits dispelled

### Consensus
The most widely attested classificatory scheme divides the totality of poisons into "moving" (གཡོ་བ, animate) and "unmoving/stationary" (བརྟན་པ, inanimate):

— moving/unmoving classification attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-90`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-1-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-4` (9 commentaries — anon-utpala, drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, gendun-gyatso, karma-maitri, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, tenga-tulku; several of the claims listed pair only one half of the distinction — "dispels the moving" or "dispels the unmoving" alone — rather than stating both halves in a single claim, consistent with each half being extracted as its own claim by the source commentary)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the "outer/inner" scheme below is an additional classificatory axis, not a contradiction of the "moving/unmoving" scheme, since a commentary could hold both simultaneously.

### Unique
Two commentaries instead classify the totality of poisons as "outer" (ཕྱི) and "inner" (ནང), a distinct axis from the moving/unmoving pairing above: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-4` ("ཕྱི་ནང་གི་དུག་གི་ནད་རྣམས་མ་ལུས་པར་ནི་སེལ་བར་མཛད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-63` ("ནང་གི་དུག་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དུག་གསུམ་དང་ཕྱི་ཡི་དུག་སྦྱར་དུག་རེག་དུག་སོགས" — inner poison as the three mental poisons, outer poison as administered/contact poison). No other claim in the packet uses this specific outer/inner framing for this verse.

Several claims single out specific named ailments beyond the general moving/unmoving or outer/inner schemes: nāga-caused illness (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4`, corroborated by the Nyima Sbaspa colophon-citation at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-5`, and by `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-1`'s classification of the whole verse as "homage by way of dispelling nāga poison" — already discussed under "Name, epithet, and classification"); snake-venom generally (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4`, `taranatha:c-18-5`); rabies and snakebite specifically (`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49`, unparalleled elsewhere in the packet); and epidemic disease alongside ignorance and affliction (`sungrab-tulku:c-21-4`, likewise unparalleled elsewhere). `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-88` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-3` both gloss the dispelled poison as, at root, "the poison of the afflictions" (ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་དུག / ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་གདུང་བ) rather than a literal toxin — a shared move toward a figurative reading, though the two commentaries use non-identical phrasing and are not claiming to quote one another.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-48` additionally describes a specific visualization practice — nectar flowing from the hand-held moon to wash and fill the practitioner's own body, inside and out — not paralleled by any other claim in the packet for this slot.

## Hidden meaning and the general doctrine of mantra

### Consensus
`palden-sherab` and `taranatha` each give a multi-tier non-literal exegesis of this verse, and their tiers converge on one specific point: both read the doubled mantra-syllable as removing or liberating from "the two obscurations" (སྒྲིབ་པ་གཉིས), though they name the syllable differently (palden-sherab keeps "TĀRA," taranatha's own reading substitutes "HARA," per "The vidyā-mantra," above):

— "two obscurations" reading attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-3-1` ("ལན་གཉིས་བརྗོད་པས་ཉོན་མོངས་དང་ཤེས་བྱའི་སྒྲིབ་པ...ལས་སྒྲོལ་ཞིང" — "saying it twice liberates from the afflictive and cognitive obscurations"), `taranatha:c-18-12` ("ལན་གཉིས་བརྗོད་པའི་བརྡས་སྒྲིབ་པ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཉེས་སྐྱོན་འཕྲོག" — "by the sign of uttering it twice, it removes the faults of the two obscurations") (2 commentaries)

Beyond this shared point, the two commentaries' full expositions do not overlap in content or terminology and are not claimed here to corroborate one another further: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-3-1` frames its reading as "completion-stage-with-marks" (རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས), glossing "the divine lake" as the vajra-body's citadel and "held in hand" as the non-dual bliss-emptiness drop (ཐིག་ལེ); `taranatha:c-18-9`–`taranatha:c-18-11` instead frames "divine" as arisen from the body, "the lake" as innate bliss with all its bases, and "held in hand" as the stainless wisdom of emptiness — different imagery reaching the same "two obscurations" conclusion by a different route.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement between the two hidden-meaning systems — see "Unique," below, for their independent, non-overlapping continuations, which are elaborations rather than incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-4-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-4-3` extend the exegesis to a fourth tier, "completion-stage-without-marks" (རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་མེད): "the divine lake" as the abiding-nature Great Perfection itself, and the dispelling of poison as the purification of dualistic stationary/moving conceptual thought within primordially pure dharmadhātu — a Great-Perfection-styled culmination not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the corpus for this slot. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-4-2` supports this tier with a citation from Jigme Lingpa's *Yum-kā* Inner Sādhana (discussed further under "Cited textual and legendary authorities," below).

`taranatha:c-18-13` adds, beyond the shared "two obscurations" point, that the same non-dual bliss-emptiness "destroys the poison of the impure body" — a further step in taranatha's own sequence, not paralleled in `palden-sherab`.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-5`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-8` gives an extended doctrinal excursus on the nature and classification of "mantra" itself, prompted by this verse but not confined to it: mantra as protection from suffering and ordinary appearance-clinging (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-5`); a fourfold classification — dhāraṇī-mantra, secret mantra, awareness-mantra, and (via a further gloss) essence/near-essence/root-or-garland mantra — attributed to "the great Jowo" (Atiśa) (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-7`); and the mantra's ultimate essence as the two form-bodies, clear light, and the wisdom-dharmakāya (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-8`). No other commentary in the packet gives a comparable general treatment of mantra-classification at this slot; it is recorded here as unique to `tenzin-dhonzang` and not folded into the "two obscurations" consensus above, since its scope (what mantra *is*, in general) is distinct from palden-sherab's and taranatha's scope (what *this* mantra's doubled syllable specifically accomplishes).
