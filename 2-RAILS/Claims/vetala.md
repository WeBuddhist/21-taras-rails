## Questions asked

1. Does each commentary name vetāla among the fourfold host (bhūta, vetāla, gandharva, yakṣa) who bow/praise Tārā, and in what wording?
2. Does each commentary name vetāla among the beings or forces destroyed or pacified by Tārā's mantra or power?
3. How does each commentary identify or explain what a vetāla actually is — chief deity, dwelling-place, directional/maṇḍala position, or relationship to rākṣasas?
4. Does any commentary associate vetāla with a specific harmful ritual practice, and if so, which one?
5. Does any commentary classify vetāla within a broader taxonomy of corpse- or zombie-type beings?
6. Does any commentary attribute a specific protective benefit to prostration with respect to vetāla?
7. Does any commentary give a tantric or completion-stage symbolic equivalence for vetāla?
8. drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, and tsultrim-namdak identify Maheśvara (དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ) as chief/ruler of the vetālas — do other commentaries corroborate, contest, or simply not address this?
9. anon-utpala, lobsang-dawa, and sungrab-tulku identify vetālas as the retinue of rākṣasas (སྲིན་པོའི་འཁོར) — is this corroborated elsewhere, and is it stated as compatible with the Maheśvara-headed reading?
10. konchok-thabkhe reads the term not as an independent being-class but as the corpse-mount ridden by a rākṣasa — does any other commentary share or contradict this reading?
11. palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul place the vetāla in the south-west, headed by a figure named Legden (ལེགས་ལྡན / བདེན་དང་བྲལ་བ) — do other commentaries corroborate this directional/named identification?
12. anon-utpala, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, and tenzin-dhonzang name a specific vetāla-associated sorcery practice, རྦོད་གཏོང་ (curse-sending) — do other commentaries corroborate this specific practice, or only describe generic harm?
13. pema-namgyal lists a taxonomy of six named "-ལངས" corpse-demon subtypes alongside vetāla — is this classification paralleled in any other commentary?
14. palden-sherab offers two different completion-stage tantric equivalences for vetāla (ཐིག་ལེ་/"drop" in two passages, ལས་/"karma" in a third) within its own commentary — is either equivalence corroborated by any other commentary?

## Textual attestation — vetāla among the fourfold host who praise Tārā

### Consensus
The verse describing Tārā's worship by the great mundane gods (Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Īśvara/Maheśvara) also names hosts of bhūta, vetāla, gandharva, and yakṣa as beings who bow and praise her "from in front":

འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། །གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།
("...praised in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas.")

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-10`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4` (11 commentaries)

Note: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3` fills this slot with the phrase "རོ་ལངས་ཞོན་པའི་སྲིན་པོ" (a rākṣasa riding a corpse) rather than a bare "vetāla" — the same structural position in the fourfold list, but a distinct textual/etymological reading; see the Identity facet below.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on whether vetāla belongs in this fourfold list — only on what a vetāla *is* (see Identity facet).

### Unique
None beyond the identity variants discussed below.

## Textual attestation — vetāla among the harms destroyed or pacified by Tārā's mantra

### Consensus
In the verse(s) on the power that destroys harmful forces, vetāla is regularly named — most often alongside dön-spirits (གདོན) and yakṣas — as one of the classes of harmful being Tārā's mantra/power overcomes:

གདོན་དང་རོ་ལངས་གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་རྣམས། །འཇོམས་པ་ཏུ་རེ་རབ་མཆོག་ཉིད་མ།
("...she who destroys the hosts of spirits, vetālas, and yakṣas — Ture, supreme and excellent, herself.")

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-21-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-6-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-99`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-21-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-21-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-21-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-2-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-24-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-71`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-74`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` (12 commentaries)

Note: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-1` (a structural header, "Sixth: the activity of destroying spirits and zombies") names only spirits and vetāla, not yakṣa, in that specific line; `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-2` completes the fuller statement including yakṣa. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-21-3` again uses "corpse-riding demons" rather than a bare "vetāla."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on whether vetāla belongs among what is destroyed — only on what precisely is destroyed *about* it (generic harm vs. the specific sorcery-practice named in the facet below).

### Unique
None beyond the sorcery-specific claims discussed below.

## Identity and etymology of vetāla

### Consensus
None. Every commentary that goes beyond bare textual attestation (facets above) to explain what a vetāla actually *is* gives a different further identification; none of these are stated as mutually exclusive by the commentaries themselves, but none restate another's specific identification either, so no shared position is recorded here.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position 1 — headed/exemplified by Maheśvara (དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ), an independent being-class with its own chief deity:**
- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-41`: "རོ་ལངས་ནི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པའོ།" ("'Vetālas' means [beings] such as Maheśvara.")
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "རོ་ལངས་ཀྱི་གཙོ་བོ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ" ("Maheśvara, chief of the vetālas").
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22`: "རོ་ལངས་ནི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པ" ("the vetālas [are ruled by] Maheśvara and others").

**Position 2 — vetāla as the retinue/subclass of rākṣasas (སྲིན་པོའི་འཁོར):**
- `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`: "སྲིན་པོའི་འཁོར་རོ་ལངས་རྣམས" ("the vetālas, retinue of the rākṣasas").
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "སྲིན་པོའི་འཁོར་རོ་ལངས" ("the vetālas — the retinue of the rākṣasas").
- `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`: "སྲིན་པོའི་འཁོར་རོ་ལངས་དང་" ("the vetālas, retinue of the rākṣasas").

**Position 3 — vetāla identified by dwelling-place, charnel grounds (དུར་ཁྲོད), with no chief named:**
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`: "དུང་ཁྲོད་ན་གནས་པའི་རོ་ལངས་རྣམས" ("the vetālas dwelling in charnel grounds").
- `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-10`: "དུར་ཁྲོད་ན་གནས་པའི་རོ་ལངས" ("a vetāla dwelling in the charnel ground").

**Position 4 — vetāla as a directional maṇḍala-retinue in the south-west, headed by a named figure Legden (ལེགས་ལྡན / བདེན་དང་བྲལ་བ), possessed of mantra-siddhi, associated with rākṣasas:**
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-2`: "རོ་ལངས་ནི་ལྷོ་ནུབ་བདེན་དང་བྲལ་བ་ལེགས་ལྡན་འཁོར་ལྷའི་སྲིན་པོ་རིག་སྔགས་མཐུ་གྲུབ་པ་ཅན" ("'vetālas' are the powerful mantra-siddhi-possessing rākṣasa retinue-deities of the untrue south-west, headed by Legden").
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`: "ལྷོ་ནུབ་ཏུ་རོ་ལང༵ས་ཏེ་བདེན་དང་བྲལ་བ་ལེགས་ལྡན" ("in the south-west, the vetāla, [namely] Legden, 'free from truth'").

**Position 5 — konchok-thabkhe's reinterpretation: not an independent being-class at all, but the corpse-mount ridden by a rākṣasa:**
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`: root phrase reads "རོ་ལངས་ཞོན་པའི་སྲིན་པོ" ("the corpse-riding rākṣasa"), not a bare "vetāla."
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-6`: "དེས་ཞོན་པའི་མགོ་ཅན་དུ་གསུངས་པས་རོ་ལངས་ཞེས་པས་སྲིན་པོ་ཞོན་པའི་མིང་གིས་བཏགས་ནས་བསྟན་པར་བྱས།" ("'corpse-rider' names a rākṣasa riding a corpse-headed mount" — i.e. this commentary explains "ro langs" here as the name for what is *ridden*, not as the independent being-class every other commentary in this packet treats it as).

The page observes (this is the page's own reading, not asserted by any single claim): Positions 1–4 are not stated as contradicting one another by their own commentaries — a being-class headed by Maheśvara, dwelling in charnel grounds, serving as a rākṣasa-retinue in the south-west under Legden, could in principle be the same being described from different angles — but no commentary in the packet states this harmonization itself. Position 5 stands apart: it is the only reading that does not treat "རོ་ལངས" as naming a being-class at all.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## The vetāla's own harmful practice: curse-sending sorcery (རྦོད་གཏོང་)

### Consensus
Several commentaries specify that the harm associated with vetāla, destroyed by Tārā's mantra, is not generic but a named sorcery/curse-sending ritual, རྦོད་གཏོང་ ("rbod gtong" / "curse-sending"):

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-6-5` ("རོ་ལངས་ཀྱི་ལས་རྦོད་གཏོང་ལ་སོགས་པའི་ངན་སྔགས" — "evil mantras, such as the vetāla's curse-sending ritual"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-6-3` ("རོ་ལངས་ཀྱི་རྦོད་གཏོང་སོགས་ངན་སྔགས" — "zombie-sorcery ... evil mantras"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3` ("རོ་ལངས་ཀྱི་ལས་རྦོད་གཏོང་སོགས་ངན་སྔགས" — "zombie-sorcery such as curses and hexes"), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7` ("རོ་ལངས་ཀྱིས་ལས་སྦོད་གཏོང་བ" — "zombies ... sent to perform sorcery") (4 commentaries)

## Classification: vetāla within a broader taxonomy of corpse-demons

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-55` gives a taxonomy not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: alongside "cemetery corpse-spirits" (དུར་ཁྲོད་ཀྱི་རོ་ལངས), it lists six further named "-ལངས" ("risen") corpse-demon subtypes — flesh-zombies (ཤ་ལངས), blood-zombies (ཁྲག་ལངས), skin-zombies (པགས་ལངས), birthmark-zombies (སྨེ་ལངས), sinew-zombies (རྩ་ལངས), and bone-zombies (རུས་ལངས) — grouped together with elemental spirits and yakṣas as "malicious worldly-karma spirits that steal life-force and vitality." No other commentary's claims in this packet name these six subtypes.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.

## Benefit: protection from vetāla through prostration

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-25` states that prostrating to Tārā, before whom spirits, cannibal-demons, gandharva-kings, and yakṣas (including "cannibal-demons such as Kālabhairava" in a phrase naming vetāla-type beings as objects of dread) tremble and bow, frees one "from the fear of those spirits in this life and in all lives." No other commentary in this packet ties a specific vetāla-related protective benefit to the act of prostration.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.

## Tantric completion-stage symbolism: vetāla as thig-le / las

### Unique
`palden-sherab` offers three completion-stage symbolic equivalences for vetāla, not paralleled by any other commentary in the packet, and not fully consistent even within its own exposition:
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2` (glossing verse 6's fourfold host in a completion-stage "dissolution into the great mother's dharmadhātu" reading): "རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་" — vetāla = the [red/white] drop (ཐིག་ལེ).
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-3-2` (glossing verse 21's "peace" as clear-light dzogchen "near-attainment"): "རོ་ལངས་ནི་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་" — vetāla = drop (ཐིག་ལེ) again, in a different symbolic set (spirit = channel, yakṣa = conceptual thought).
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2` (glossing "peaceful, endowed with power" as self-liberation of dualistic obscuration, for sharpest-faculty disciples): "རོ་ལངས་ནི་ལས་དང་" — vetāla = karma (ལས), in yet another symbolic set (spirit = suffering, yakṣa = affliction).

The page notes (its own observation, not stated by the claims themselves): the same commentary gives vetāla two different completion-stage equivalences — "drop" twice, "karma" once — across three separate passages of its own multi-layered exegesis of verses 6 and 21; the claims do not explain the relationship between these readings, and this page does not attempt to harmonize them.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as cross-commentary disagreement — this is internal to a single commentary's own layered exposition, not a disagreement between commentaries.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claims.
