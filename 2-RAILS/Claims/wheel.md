> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation

The term འཁོར་ལོ། (*'khor lo*, wheel/cakra) surfaces across this corpus in several recurring but textually distinct contexts: root verse 9's own line describing a "wheel of every direction" — whose sense the commentaries themselves read two different ways; several alternate multi-armed iconographic forms in which a wheel appears among other hand-implements; a wheel visualized at Tārā's heart bearing a mantra; Tārā's own activity of "turning the wheel of Dharma"; and a scatter of further, unrelated occurrences — a tantric completion-stage reading, a bodily-mark enumeration, wheel-related epithets drawn from an external text, a request-verse addressed to other buddhas, and an auspicious-substances verse. These are treated as separate facets below rather than folded into one, since the claims address different textual loci and, in one central case, genuinely disagree about what the term itself refers to.

## Root verse 9 — quoting "adorned by the wheel of every direction"

### Consensus
The verse's own line — that her own mass of light, adorned by/agitated at a wheel [reaching] every direction without exception, is agitated, as part of the fuller four-line verse honoring the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels at her heart — is quoted or closely paraphrased across a wide sample of commentaries, independent of how each commentary goes on to gloss "wheel" itself (see the following facet):

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32` (7 commentaries)

Of these, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-4` already glosses the wheel, within its own verse-citation, as the "right-hand emblem" (གཡས་པའི་ཕྱག་མཚན) — a first, lighter locational note that its own follow-on claim (cited under "What does 'wheel' mean," below) develops into a full palm/mudrā identification.

### ⚑ Divergences
None at the level of the bare verse text — every citation above quotes or closely paraphrases the same line without contradiction. (The genuine divergence concerns what the line's "wheel" refers to; see the next facet.)

### Unique
None beyond the framing note above.

## What does "wheel" mean in this line? — cosmological totality vs. physical palm-mark

### Consensus
None. This is a genuine, unreconciled divergence in how commentaries construe the compound phrase "wheel of every direction" (ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ) from root verse 9 — not a disagreement over doctrine, but over what the word denotes.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position 1 — cosmological/spatial totality.** Several commentaries explicitly gloss "wheel of every direction" as denoting the encompassing totality of the ten directions (and the buddha-fields within them) that her light pervades and adorns, rather than any physical wheel-shaped object:

- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-6`: "དེ་བཞིན་མ་ལུས་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་སྟེ་ཕྱོགས་བཞི་མཚམས་བཞི་སྟེང་འོག་གཉིས་ཏེ་ཕྱོགས་བཅུ་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་...," glossed in the claim's own English as "the wheel of all directions without exception — the four cardinal, four intermediate, upper, and lower, all ten directions."
- `taranatha:c-9-7`: "the wheel of directions — that is, all the buddha-fields of the ten directions without exception."
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-3`: "adorns the wheel of all directions and intermediate directions without exception."
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-31`: reads the same phrase as "all the maṇḍala buddha-fields (འཁོར་ལོའི་ཞིང་ཁམས) of every world in the ten directions."

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-6`, `taranatha:c-9-7`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-31` (4 commentaries)

**Position 2 — a physical wheel-mark on the right (boon-giving) palm.** A larger group of commentaries instead explicitly locates a wheel-shaped mark or design on the palm of her right hand, within the mudrā of supreme/boon-giving, as the source of the light described in the verse:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-57`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (8 commentaries)

Within this group, two commentaries independently add that the palm's wheel is specifically "thousand-spoked" (རྩིབས་སྟོང་), a matching specific detail not stated by any of the other six: `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` — "འཁོར་ལོ་རྩིབས་སྟོང་གི་རི་མོས་རབ་ཏུ་མཛེས་པའི་འོད་ཟེར་" — and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` — "མཐིལ་དུ་འཁོར་ལོ་རྩིབས་སྟོང་གི་རི་མོས་རབ་ཏུ་མཛེས་པར་བརྒྱན་པའི་འོད་ཟེར་."

— thousand-spoked detail attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` (2 commentaries)

Also within this group, four commentaries go further than the root verse's own neutral "agitated" (འཁྲུག) and explicitly state, using the verb ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་ ("outshine"/"overpower"), that the wheel's light outshines or overpowers all other light: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` ("འོད་གཞན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་མ"), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-58` ("འོད་གཞན་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་པའོ"), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-5` ("འོད་ཀྱི་ཚོགས་གཞན་རྣམས་འཁྲུག་པ་སྟེ་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་མའོ"), and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2` ("འོད་གཞན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་མ་ལའོ").

— explicit "outshines other light" (ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་) attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-58`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2` (4 commentaries)

By contrast, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32`'s own verse-citation (cited under "Root verse 9," above) renders the same root-verse verb འཁྲུགས་མ in its own English gloss as "disturbs [overwhelms] all other lights" — the bracketed "[overwhelms]" is that raw extraction's own English wording, not a distinct Tibetan verb like ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་; it is noted here as a translation choice internal to that one raw extraction, not counted among the four explicit ཟིལ་གྱིས་གནོན་ attestations above.

Two further commentaries tie the palm-wheel's radiance specifically to protection from a numbered set of fears: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-3` (protecting the beings of the ten directions "from the eight great fears" via "the entire expanse of the wheel of directions"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-2` (protecting from "the two sets of eight saṃsāric fears," i.e. sixteen, via light radiating "from her body and the wheel"), and `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` (light-rays from the thousand-spoked wheel "dispel the eight and sixteen fears harmful to beings").

— protection from the eight/sixteen fears attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab`'s own two claims above are carried through transparently rather than assigned to either Position 1 or Position 2: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-3`'s language — protecting "the entire expanse of the wheel of directions" (ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོའི་ཁྱོན་ཀུན) — leans toward Position 1's spatial-totality sense, while `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-2` treats "the wheel" (འཁོར་ལོ) as a light-source named alongside her body in a generation-stage visualization context, closer to Position 2's object-sense. Neither claim is explicit enough on its own to place palden-sherab confidently on either side, so both are recorded here as ambiguous rather than counted toward either position's total.

### Unique
None beyond the sub-points recorded above.

## Alternate multi-armed iconographic forms bearing a wheel among other implements

### Consensus
None — every form catalogued here differs from every other in the number of arms/faces and the specific set of accompanying implements; no two commentaries in the packet describe an identical form.

### ⚑ Divergences
Not framed as a divergence in the doctrinal sense (these are not competing claims about a single image); recorded as Unique, below.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char` alone in this packet attests four distinct multi-armed forms bearing a wheel among other implements:
- An eight-armed form (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-22`): the first two hands raised in the mudrā of great joy, the next two lowered holding a bow and arrow, the right third hand a wheel, the right fourth a sword, the left third a conch, the left fourth a lasso — corroborated by the same commentary's own citation of a verse attributed to Nyima Sbaspa (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-23`: "arrow, bow, wheel, sword, conch, vajra, lasso"), though that cited verse's own seven-item list does not match c-3-1-22's eight-item description implement-for-implement (the verse adds a vajra not named in c-3-1-22).
- A three-faced, twelve-armed form, attested only via a second Nyima Sbaspa citation (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-11`): "khaṭvāṅga, wheel, and jewel... vajra, flower-garland... on the right; pill, blue lotus, bell, and vase and volume... on the left."
- An alternate six-right-handed form (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-9`): first two hands in equipoise, then khaṭvāṅga, wheel, jewel, vajra, and a garland of flowers.
- A four-implement form (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-7-8`): wheel and sword in the right hands, lasso and the threatening gesture in the left.

None of these four forms matches another, and none is corroborated by any other commentary's claims in this packet.

`sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-2` attests a further, distinct two-armed form: seated half-cross-legged on a lotus-and-moon seat, right hand in the supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding an utpala topped by a wheel that blazes with light-rays — a wheel borne atop a flower rather than held directly or marked on the palm, not paralleled by any other commentary's claims in this packet.

## The heart-wheel — mantra hub and seed-syllable

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently describe a wheel visualized at Tārā's heart, its hub bearing a seed-syllable encircled by the letters of a mantra arranged on its spokes:

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-3` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
The same three claims cited above for the general schema are cited again here for a narrower, distinct point: which specific syllable each says occupies the hub. Each claim sits on only one side of this specific disagreement (never both), so the repeat citation is not the "same claim padding both sides of one dispute" case the deterministic checker's Consensus/Divergences overlap check exists to catch — it is one claim supporting two different, non-contradictory-in-themselves propositions (the wheel-schema, and the wheel-schema's specific hub-syllable). The three commentaries above (plus karma-maitri's own second claim on this topic) do not agree on which syllable occupies the hub:

- **TAṃ at the hub:** `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1` — the packet itself flags this block as OCR-degraded, so it is treated here as tentative rather than fully certain — and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-3` both state the hub bears the syllable TAṃ ("ཐུགས་ཀའི་འཁོར་ལོའི་ལྟེ་བར་ཏཾ་ཡིག").
- **OM at the hub:** `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-2` instead states the hub bears OM — "ཐུགས་ཀར་འཁོར་ལོའི་ལྟེ་བར་ཨོཾ་དང་རྩིབས་རྣམས་ལ་སྔགས་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེས་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་བའི" — though the claim's own text is truncated mid-sentence in the raw extraction.
- **HŪṂ as the mantra's generative syllable:** karma-maitri's own following claim (`karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-5`) states that the ten-syllable mantra "arises from the awareness-syllable HŪM arranged in the wheel," in apparent tension with its own preceding OM-at-hub claim — a tension not resolved within the packet's excerpts. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-2` separately associates HŪM with a distinct wrathful form of the mantra/deity ("from the vidyā-syllable HŪṃ [arises] Tārā... the wrathful mantra") — a different structural role from karma-maitri's "arises from HŪM" statement.

These HŪM-mentions and the OM/TAṃ disagreement are recorded here as unreconciled, per the vault's rule against silently resolving ambiguous claims, rather than harmonized into one account.

### Unique
`gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-2` additionally states that the ten-syllable peaceful mantra (oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā) is itself "arranged at the heart-wheel," not paralleled verbatim by karma-maitri's or tenzin-dhonzang's claims. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-3` additionally states that light-rays emitted from this mantra-garland "utterly crush and destroy the bodies of outer and inner enemies" — a further activity claim not stated by gendun-gyatso's or karma-maitri's heart-wheel claims.

## The dharma-wheel — Tārā turning the wheel of Dharma, destroying enemies

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently describe, at a matching point in their own verse-numbering (their own "verse 30"), Tārā abiding with the right leg extended and the left leg bent, delighting in universally turning the wheel of Dharma, and thereby destroying or pacifying the host of outer and inner enemies in the mindstreams of those to be tamed — in closely matching wording:

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`: "ཞབས་གཡས་བརྐྱངས་གཡོན་བསྐུམས་པར་བཞུགས་ནས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་དགའ་བའི། །གདུལ་བྱའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཕྱི་ནང་གི་དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང་ནི་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་མ་ལའོ"
`sungrab-tulku:c-16-4`: "ཞབས་གཡས་བརྐྱངས་ཤིང་ཞབས་གཡོན་བསྐུམས་པའི་སྟབས་ཀྱིས་བཞུགས་ནས། སྒོ་ཀུན་ནས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་དགའ་བའི་གདུལ་བྱའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཕྱི་ནང་གི་དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་པའམ་ཞི་བར་མཛད་པའི"

The two share the same stance description (right leg extended, left bent), the same core phrase "delighting in turning the wheel of Dharma" (ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ...བསྐོར་བ་ལ་དགའ་བ), and the same target ("outer and inner enemies... in the mindstreams of those to be tamed").

— attested: `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-4` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`lobsang-dawa` additionally attests a second, separate verse (its own "verse 35," `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-3`) with closely related but textually distinct content — delighting the disciples by universally turning the wheel of Dharma, and thoroughly crushing (rather than destroying/pacifying) the bodies of outer and inner enemies. This is not corroborated by any other commentary's claims in this packet, and is a different verse from the "verse 30" material above (a different stance description is absent here, and the verb is "crushes bodies" rather than "destroys/pacifies the host").
