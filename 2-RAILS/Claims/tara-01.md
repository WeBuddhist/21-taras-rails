## Name and Etymology — Tārā (the Liberator)

### Consensus
སེམས་ཅན་འཁོར་བའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་བས་ན་སྒྲོལ་མ། — Because she liberates/delivers (*sgrol ba*) beings from the suffering of saṃsāra — in some readings, ultimately from the afflictive and cognitive obscurations — and is the very embodiment of the enlightened activity of the buddhas, she is called སྒྲོལ་མ (Tārā, "the Liberator/Deliverer").

The verse itself is quoted, in essentially identical form, as the basis for this and the following two etymologies by: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-0-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-3`, `taranatha:c-1-2`, `taranatha:c-1-4`.

— attested (etymology proper): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-5`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-7` (citing the *Tārā-tantra of a Hundred and Eight Names*), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-5`, `taranatha:c-1-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-4` (Abhidharmakośa-style supporting citation), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-1` (11 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on this specific point (contrast Pamo, below, where anon-utpala reads a different third epithet).

### Unique
None beyond what appears in Consensus.

## Name and Etymology — Myurma (the Swift One)

### Consensus
སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་དོན་ལ་སྐད་ཅིག་ཙམ་ཡང་མི་གཡེལ་བར་མྱུར་དུ་མཛད་པས་ན་མྱུར་མ། — Because she accomplishes the welfare of beings swiftly, without lagging even for an instant — swifter, several commentaries add, than the activity of other buddhas/victors, or than ordinary processes such as healing an illness or a sprout growing from a seed, which require time — she is called མྱུར་མ (Myurma, "the Swift One").

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-6` (13 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- anon-trinle-char connects her swift, salvific activity to the traditional stories of protection from the eight and sixteen great fears, reading the epithet as an implicit instruction that one should go for refuge to this goddess — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-9`.
- taranatha glosses the swiftness/compassion component under a fourth, unstated epithet, "Mother" (*ma*), rather than parsing *myur ma* directly: "by her mind's non-referential great compassion, swiftly accomplishing the welfare of beings, she is 'Mother'" — `taranatha:c-1-5`.
- tenzin-dhonzang frames her swift activity through a fourfold-deity analogy: just as Avalokiteśvara is the deity-form of all buddhas' love combined into one, Mañjughoṣa of their wisdom, and Vajrapāṇi (*phyag na rdo rje*) of their power, so Tārā is the deity-form of all buddhas' [swift] enlightened activity combined into one — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-5`.
- gendun-drub frames her swiftness through a narrative rather than a bare word-gloss: she asks Avalokiteśvara not to weep, and vows herself to render service in swiftly freeing beings from saṃsāra, heroic in "turning back saṃsāra's battle-front" — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-4` (see also Origin Narrative, below).

## Name and Etymology — Pamo (the Heroine)

### Consensus
ཉོན་མོངས་དང་བདུད་སྡེ་འཇོམས་ཤིང་འཇིགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པའི་ནུས་པ་ཐོགས་པ་མེད་པས་ན་དཔའ་མོ། — Because she destroys the afflictions (*nyon mongs*) and hosts of māras (*bdud sde*), inner and outer, and her power to protect beings from all fears is unobstructed and never wavers, she is called དཔའ་མོ (Pamo, "the Heroine").

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4` (combines this reason with her swiftness), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-11` (a second, parallel etymology: her power is certain to protect from all the terrors of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, not merely "swift of compassion") (13 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
anon-utpala reads the third epithet of the root verse itself differently: its own quotation of the verse gives "མྱུར་མ་དཔལ་མོ" — Myurma **Dpal-mo** ("the Glorious One"), not Myurma **Dpa'-mo** ("the Heroine") — and glosses it accordingly: "Because she utterly destroys the host of māras without remainder, she is 'the Glorious One.'" — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-2` (verse quotation), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-6` (etymology). No other commentary consulted here attests this reading of the third epithet; all others read དཔའ་མོ, "Heroine."

### Unique
anon-trinle-char gives an extended etymology tying "Heroine" to Tārā's threefold generation of bodhicitta, accumulation of the two collections, and attainment of buddhahood — all accomplished specifically on the basis of a female body (*bud med kyi rten*) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-10`; and, alternatively, to her benefiting migrating beings by means of a female form until saṃsāra is emptied — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-11` (this etymology is grounded in the extended narrative recorded under "The Vow to Benefit Beings Through a Female Body," below).

## The Wisdom-Eyes "Like an Instant of Lightning"

### Consensus
ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་སྐད་ཅིག་གློག་དང་འདྲ་བས་ཤེས་བྱ་མཐའ་དག་སྐད་ཅིག་ལ་གཟིགས་པ། — The verse's simile, "whose eyes are like an instant of lightning" (*spyan ni skad cig glog dang 'dra ma*), is read as describing her wisdom-eye (*ye shes kyi spyan*), which perceives the entirety of knowable things (*shes bya*) — across the three times and in all directions — instantaneously and without obstruction or attachment, like a flash of lightning.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-14`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-4` (simile applied to her instantaneous arising rather than her eyes specifically, but the same lightning-flash image), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-8`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-9`, `taranatha:c-1-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-5` (12 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- karma-maitri ties this simile directly into the etymology of the name "Tārā" itself, rather than treating it as a separate descriptive clause: "she is Tārā because the light of her eyes is like an instantaneous flash of lightning" — `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-7`.
- lobsang-dawa reads the simile as generating a further, otherwise-unattested epithet: "All-seeing" (*kun gzigs ma*) — `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-5`.
- sangye-nyentrul independently derives a different extra epithet from the same simile: "Possessor of Wisdom" (*ye shes can ma*) — `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-4`.
- pema-namgyal specifies that the wisdom-eye described belongs to her wrathful aspect (*khro ma'i tshul*) specifically — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-12`.
- tenga-tulku elaborates that she gazes with this wisdom-eye unceasingly through the six periods of day and night, seeing without obstruction who declines and who flourishes among beings — `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-6`.

## Origin Narrative — Arisen from Avalokiteśvara's Tears

### Consensus
འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་མགོན་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཞལ་གྱི་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མ་བྱུང་བ། — The verse's "protector of the three worlds" (netherworld/nāga-world below, human world on the earth, deva-world above) is identified as Avalokiteśvara (*spyan ras gzigs*). Seeing that, however many beings he freed, their numbers in saṃsāra never diminished, he wept; from those tears a lotus grew, and Tārā arose from the myriad pollen/filaments of its opened blossom.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-15`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-16`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-19`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-7`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-13`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-11`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-12`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-13`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14`, `taranatha:c-1-9`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6` (27 attestations across all 16 commentaries consulted — this is the single most broadly attested facet on this page)

### ⚑ Divergences
konchok-thabkhe's own text flags a tradition-internal divergence on whose tears are the source: "earlier commentaries" (unnamed within this source) are reported as reading the tantra's second line as referring to **Tārā's own eyes/tears** as the origin of her arising, whereas konchok-thabkhe explicitly departs from that reading, following mahāsiddha Nyima Sepa's own verse, and attributes the tears instead to **Avalokiteśvara's face** — the same reading given in the Consensus above — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-5`. None of the other fifteen commentaries consulted for this page independently attest the "Tārā's own eyes" reading konchok-thabkhe describes as older; every other commentary that addresses her origin agrees with konchok-thabkhe's own stated position (Avalokiteśvara's tears). The divergence is therefore preserved here as reported, without a directly attested opposing commentary within this corpus.

### Unique
- gendun-drub gives an expanded narrative in which Tārā herself intervenes at the moment of Avalokiteśvara's grief: she asks him not to shed tears and vows to render service in swiftly freeing beings from saṃsāra herself, her heroism framed as "turning back saṃsāra's battle-front" — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-4`.
- pema-namgyal describes **both** White and Blue Tārā arising together in emanated, miraculous form, "having vowed to be companions in liberating beings" — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-14`.
- Two commentaries independently record a named master's own summary epithet for her: gendun-drub cites Paṇchen Nyima Bepa calling her simply "Tārā, Utterly Heroic" (*rab tu dpa' ba'i sgrol ma*) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-5` — while anon-trinle-char cites the same or a closely related master (referred to as "Nyisbepa"/"Nyima Sbaspa") calling her "Noble Tārā, Swift and Greatly Heroic" — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-24` (see also Textual Variants and Attribution, below).

## The Vow to Benefit Beings Through a Female Body

### Consensus
Not applicable — only one commentary in this packet elaborates this narrative for Homage 1.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
anon-trinle-char alone preserves an extended narrative, attributed to Jonangpa, that grounds the "Heroine" etymology in Tārā's own vow. Monks are said to have once urged her to pray that her root of virtue would cause her to be reborn in a male body, so as to act more effectively for the doctrine — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-12`. In reply, she said that many wish for enlightenment on the basis of a male birth, but no one works for the welfare of beings by means of a woman's body; therefore, until saṃsāra is emptied, she vowed to work for the welfare of beings precisely by means of a female form — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-13`. This narrative is the basis for the same commentary's etymology of "Heroine" as courage expressed through generating bodhicitta, accumulating the two collections, and attaining buddhahood, all on a female basis (see Name and Etymology — Pamo, above: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-10`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-11`).

## Iconography — Body Colour, Seat, and Posture

### Consensus
Only four of the sixteen commentaries consulted supply generation-stage/sādhana-style iconographic detail for Homage 1 at all: anon-trinle-char, palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, and tsultrim-namdak (its second, independent commentary, "Work 3"). Where they overlap, they agree that she is seated on a lotus (or lotus-and-moon) seat, in the centre of the sky.

— attested (lotus/lotus-moon seat): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-21`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-23` (citing Nyima Sbaspa's sādhana: a golden-coloured lotus seat — the seat's colour, not a conflicting body-colour claim), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-2` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Body colour: anon-trinle-char (`c-3-1-21`: *sku mdog dmar mo*, "red-coloured, radiant with fire-like light"), palden-sherab (`c-3-1-1-2-1`: *sku mdog dmar mo*, "red-bodied"), and sangye-nyentrul (`c-2-1-2`: *sku mdog dmar mo*, "body red in colour") agree on plain red. tsultrim-namdak's Work 3, however, gives her body as **reddish-yellow** (*sku mdog dmar ser*) — `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4`.

Posture and expression: palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul both describe a lotus-and-moon seat, a half-vajra/"archer" leg posture (*rta 'babs skyil mo krung* / *zhabs phyed skyil*), and a semi-wrathful, passionately smiling expression — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-2`. anon-trinle-char's citation of Nyima Sbaspa's sādhana gives neither leg-posture nor facial-expression detail, stating only that she abides atop a red-lotus seat in the centre of the sky, with one face, two eyes, and eight arms — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-21`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-23`. This reads as a difference of detail/tradition rather than a stated contradiction (see also Iconography — Arms and Implements, immediately below, where the same eight-armed/two-handed split recurs).

### Unique
None beyond what appears above.

## Iconography — Arms and Implements

### Consensus
Not applicable — the commentaries that give hand-implement detail for Homage 1 do not agree on the number of arms (see Divergences).

### ⚑ Divergences
anon-trinle-char, citing Nyima Sbaspa's/Nyisbepa's sādhana, describes an **eight-armed** form: the first two hands raised in the mudrā of great joy, as if proclaiming the Dharma; the next two lowered, holding a bow and arrow drawn to the heart; the right third hand holding a wheel, the fourth a sword; the left third hand holding a conch, the fourth a lasso; adorned with every ornament — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-21`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-22`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-23`.

By contrast, palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, and tsultrim-namdak's Work 3 each independently describe a **two-handed** form: right hand in the mudrā of supreme giving, left hand in the mudrā of refuge-giving (symbolizing the Three Jewels) or holding an utpala, with a white conch coiling to the right resting between the fingers atop a fully opened blue utpala flower — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4`.

### Unique
- palden-sherab's generation-stage instructions add that the practitioner visualizes themselves as the ḍākinī Yeshe Tsogyal with Tārā at the heart-centre (or visualizes Tārā as self or in front), before reciting the approach-mantra — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-2`.
- sangye-nyentrul's parallel visualization instruction is simply that bodhicitta is meditated as pervading the entire world — `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-3`.

None observed.

### Unique
pema-namgyal glosses each syllable as an epithet matching Homage 1's own vocabulary: **Tāre** = "Tārā" — swift because her activity is accomplished in a single instant or day, and she swiftly wards off obstacles, hence "Swift One" and "Heroine" — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-6`; **Tuttāre** = "she who dispels fear" — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-7`; **Ture** = "Tārā who bestows one's own aim, others' aim, and the ultimate aim, all of them" — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-8`; **Svāhā** = "homage to you" — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-9`. The same commentary notes that this condensed mantra-form is traditionally said to have entered use via glorious Jowoje (Atiśa) and others, drawing on other tantras — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-10`.

## The Threefold Quality-Framework (Knowledge, Love, Power)

### Consensus
མཁྱེན་པ་དང་བརྩེ་བ་དང་ནུས་པ་གསུམ་གྱིས་ཡོན་ཏན་ཐམས་ཅད་མཚོན། — Several commentaries organize the whole of Homage 1's meaning under an explicit framework of qualities — most often knowledge (*mkhyen pa*), love/compassion (*brtse ba*), and power/capability (*nus pa*), understood as jointly indicated by the verse's description of her swift, powerful, compassionate liberation of beings and her lightning-swift wisdom-eye. anon-trinle-char states this most explicitly, deriving the framework from "skillful means and wisdom never being separated," and mapping knowledge to the wisdom-eye clause, love to her failure-to-procrastinate, and capability/power to her heroism.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-14`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-17`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-18`, `taranatha:c-1-8` (knowledge, love, power, and activity together as a summary indication of all infinite qualities), `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-1` (this Tārā "increases bodhicitta and draws appearances under control") (5 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
tenzin-dhonzang elaborates the wisdom-side of this framework in unusual detail: her wisdom-eye possesses "the two wisdoms" (knowing things as they are, and in their variety), the three qualities of knowledge/love/power, the five wisdoms, and "the twenty-one groups of undefiled wisdom" (among others), with her enlightened activity described as swiftly assisting the accomplishment of the four [types of] enlightened activity — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`.

## Grammatical Note — the Feminine Suffix "-ma"

### Consensus
Not applicable — only one commentary in this packet comments on this point.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
anon-trinle-char notes that the final "ma" in feminine forms such as *'dra ma* ("like...") and *byung ma* ("she who arose") in this verse is a feminine grammatical marker (*mo mtshon byed kyi bdag sgra*), comparable to its use in *mkha' 'gro ma* (ḍākinī), *glu ma* (songstress), and *chung ma* (wife) — and states explicitly that the same analysis applies to the final "ma" closing each of the remaining twenty homages — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-20`.

## Textual Variants and Attribution to Named Masters/Translators

### Consensus
Not applicable — no two commentaries here attest the same variant or attribution; each item below is independent.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed (the items below are complementary attributions, not conflicting claims about the same fact).

### Unique
- anon-utpala records that the translator Lo chen Nam bzang revised the third line of the verse to read "the lotus of the mouth of the protector of the three worlds," and explains the verse according to this easier-to-construe revision — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-10`.
- taranatha records an equivalent revision from a differently-named translator, Namkha Zangpo (*lo tsā ba nam mkha' bzang po*), to "she arose from the myriad pollen of the water-born [lotus] face of the protector of the three worlds," likewise preferred as easier to understand — `taranatha:c-1-10`.

- gendun-drub cites "Paṇchen Nyima Bepa" (likely the same or a closely related master under a variant name) for the summary epithet "Tārā, Utterly Heroic" — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-5`.

The source mapping flags this as ambiguous: the list does not itself confirm that its order tracks the root text's verse order, and its second name does not match Homage 2's epithet as used elsewhere in the same anthology. ## Deeper (Hidden / Ultimate-Meaning) Readings

### Consensus
Not applicable — this layer of reading is attested in substantial form in only one commentary for Homage 1.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
palden-sherab is the only commentary in this packet that reads every homage, including Homage 1, through an explicit fourfold hermeneutic — word-meaning (*tshig gi don*), general meaning (*spyi don*), hidden meaning (*sbas don*), and ultimate meaning (*mthar thug*) — stated as the method to be applied uniformly to all twenty-one Tārā verses — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-4-2`. For Homage 1 specifically: the *general meaning* layer supplies a Mahāyoga generation-stage visualization (see Iconography, above); the *hidden meaning* layer (Anuyoga completion-stage-with-marks) reads "the water-born face of the protector of the three worlds" as the vajra-body's three main channels (Avadhūtī, Rasanā, Lalanā) bearing the syllables OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ, with the verse's homage read as laying the ground of faith in this subtle-body basis — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-2` — and describes loosening the body's twenty-one channel-knots to traverse the ten bhūmis and five paths "like a lightning-flash," citing Longchenpa on the same channel-knot scheme — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-4`; the *ultimate meaning* layer (Atiyoga completion-stage-without-marks) reads the whole verse as pointing to primordial buddhahood as the three kāyas within self-arisen awareness — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-4-1`.

Separately, and far more briefly, taranatha offers a single non-literal reading of the same phrase without palden-sherab's larger fourfold apparatus: "the protector of the three worlds is the dharmakāya; what arose from it is the rūpakāya" — `taranatha:c-1-11`.
