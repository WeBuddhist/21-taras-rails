# The Benefits of the Praise — བསྟོད་པའི་ཕན་ཡོན། (^a-1–^a-7)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

The root text's closing section (heading `བསྟོད་པའི་ཕན་ཡོན། ^a-0`, 1-SOURCES/Text/སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པ།.md) runs six verses of benefits (^a-1–^a-6) followed by a one-line colophon (^a-7) stating that the praise was spoken by the Bhagavatī Buddha Vairocana to the Bhagavatī Tārā.

## Questions asked

1. How does each commentary structurally frame the closing section — does it recap the opening line "this praise by the root mantra and the twenty-one homages," and does any commentary organize the whole section under an explicit multi-part outline?
2. What, according to each commentary, is "the root mantra" referred to in the opening line — the peaceful and wrathful mantras taught earlier in the text, or a separate mantra?
3. What manner, motivation, and timing of recitation do the commentaries prescribe (devotion, mental clarity, rising at dusk and dawn, recollecting wrathful/peaceful forms)?
4. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to mere recollection in terms of fearlessness or protection from fear, and do any specify a number of fears (eight, sixteen)?
5. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to recitation in terms of purifying sin and averting or destroying lower rebirth?
6. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to recitation in terms of empowerment by countless buddhas/victors and eventual attainment of buddhahood, and do they render the count of victors consistently?
7. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to recitation in terms of protection from poison (stationary/moving, eaten/drunk)?
8. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to recitation in terms of protection from spirits, epidemic disease, and poison-affliction, and do they state that the benefit extends to beings other than the reciter?
9. How does each commentary interpret the closing verse's numerals "two, three, seven" — as a count of recitations, sessions, or time-periods — and do their readings agree?
10. What benefit(s) do commentaries attribute to the numerically-specified recitation in terms of fulfilling desires (son, wealth) and destroying obstacles?
11. Does any commentary give an allegorical or doctrinal reading of "poison" as an obstruction to buddhahood (e.g. wrong view, self-grasping, the obscurations) rather than a literal toxin?
12. Does any commentary single out faith as the principal cause of the benefits described, and if so, does it cite scripture in support?
13. Does any commentary gloss the individual syllables of the root mantra (OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ) doctrinally?
14. What does each commentary say about the colophon of the root text itself — who spoke the praise, and through what transmission?
15. Does any other commentary corroborate gendun-drub's and karma-maitri's allegorical identification of the obstructing "poison," and do their specific terms (wrong view, self-grasping view, the three obscurations) agree?
16. Does any other commentary corroborate pema-namgyal's report that the poison-clearing benefit presupposes a hundred-thousand or more prior recitations already accomplished?
17. Does any other commentary corroborate taranatha's list of additional siddhis (pills, eye-medicine, sword, yakṣiṇī, treasure) attained through recitation?

## Structural framing: the root mantra and the twenty-one homages

### Consensus
Several commentaries open their treatment of this section by recapitulating the root verse's own opening line, "this praise by the root mantra, and the twenty-one homages" (རྩ་བའི་སྔགས་ཀྱིས་བསྟོད་པ་འདི་དང་། ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་ནི་ཉི་ཤུ་རྩ་གཅིག), before proceeding to the practice and benefits proper:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-101`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-102`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-23-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-25-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-25-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-25-4`, `taranatha:c-22-0-2`, `taranatha:c-22-0-3`, `taranatha:c-22-0-4`, `taranatha:c-22-0-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-6-6` (6 commentaries)

Two commentaries additionally organize the *entire* closing section under a near-identical four-part outline, using closely matching Tibetan headings: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-1-1` divides it into "the special quality of the intention, the special quality of the time, the actual teaching of the benefits, and the benefits taught in condensed numerical form" (བསམ་པའི་ཁྱད་པར། དུས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར། ཕན་ཡོན་དངོས་བཤད་པ། གྲངས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་ཕན་ཡོན་བསྡུས་ཏེ་བསྟན་པ); `tenga-tulku`'s own four section-openers — "First, teaching the intention" (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-1`, བསམ་པ་བསྟན་པ), "Second, in terms of the time" (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-1`, དུས་ཀྱི་དབང་དུ་ན), "Third, actually teaching the benefits" (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-1`, ཕན་ཡོན་དངོས་བསྟན་པ), "Fourth, concluding by way of the numbers" (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-1`, གྲངས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་མཇུག་བསྡུ་བ) — match this division part-for-part, down to the wording of each part's name (e.g. ཕན་ཡོན་དངོས་བཤད་པ vs ཕན་ཡོན་དངོས་བསྟན་པ; གྲངས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས...ཕན་ཡོན་བསྡུས... vs གྲངས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་མཇུག་བསྡུ་བ). This is a genuine, specific structural correspondence between two commentaries, not observed in any other commentary in the packet.

— four-part outline attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-1` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
`gendun-gyatso:c-2-1` reports a live disagreement over the identity of "the root mantra" named in the opening line:
- **Position 1** (Jetsün Drakpa Gyaltsen's reading, endorsed by gendun-gyatso as likely correct): identifies "the root mantra" with the peaceful and wrathful mantras taught earlier in the text.
- **Position 2** ("some say," anonymous): a distinct four-line Tārā root mantra is meant.

`sungrab-tulku:c-25-3` independently supports Position 1 without engaging Position 2: it explicitly identifies "the root mantra" as Tārā's two name-mantras — the peaceful oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā and the wrathful oṃ nama tāre namo hare hūṃ hara svāhā — calling the verse "praise by way of the power and activity of the two name-mantras."

`taranatha`'s own etymological gloss of the root mantra (cited in full under "Etymology of the root-mantra syllables," below) works exclusively through the ten syllables oṃ-tā-re-tut-tā-re-tu-re-svā-hā — matching the peaceful mantra's own syllable count — without addressing the wrathful mantra or the two-position question directly. This is consistent with (a version of) Position 1's premise that the root mantra is drawn from the mantras taught earlier in the text, but taranatha's claims do not themselves state the two/four-line question either way, so this is noted as a data point rather than counted as a third position.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-0-1` gives its own six-part division of "the actual explanation of the benefits" — pacifying the causes/results of the lower realms; blessing by the buddhas; attaining great temporary and ultimate results; protection from poison, spirits, and epidemic; benefit extending beyond oneself to others; and a concluding statement on six-times-daily recitation — a different, more granular structure than gendun-drub's/tenga-tulku's shared four-part scheme, not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

## Manner, motivation, and time of recitation

### Consensus
A wide consensus holds that the praise is to be recited with genuine devotion/reverence and a clear or one-pointed mind, rising at dusk and dawn:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-1`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-1`, `taranatha:c-22-1-11`, `taranatha:c-22-1-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (9 commentaries)

Of these, several specify that the practitioner recollects Tārā's *wrathful* form at dusk and her *peaceful* form at dawn: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3` (5 commentaries).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement — commentaries that omit the wrathful/peaceful specification simply give the timing without that detail; this is not a conflicting account.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-3-1` gives a distinct rationale for the two times, not paralleled elsewhere: dusk is glossed as "the time when demons cling to dark night and cause harm," and dawn as "the time when the gods grant prophecies," rather than being glossed via Tārā's own wrathful/peaceful forms.

`taranatha:c-22-1-11` additionally analyzes the verse grammatically into object (the goddess), action (devotion), and agent ("whoever is intelligent") — a structural gloss not given by any other commentary in the packet.

## Fearlessness and protection from fear

### Consensus
Mere recollection of the goddess (or of the praise) is said to grant fearlessness / protection from fear:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-5`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `taranatha:c-22-1-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (10 commentaries)

Three of these additionally frame the fearlessness in terms of a specific — but in this packet unenumerated — set of fears, "the eight and sixteen fears": `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-5`, `taranatha:c-22-1-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3`. None of the three lists the sixteen fears individually within this packet's claims.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the eight/sixteen-fears mentions above.

## Purification of sin and lower rebirth

### Consensus
Recitation is said to pacify sin (the cause of lower rebirth) and to destroy or avert lower rebirth itself (its result):

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-6`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-7`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `taranatha:c-22-1-16`, `taranatha:c-22-1-17`, `taranatha:c-22-1-18`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-9`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (10 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-22-1-18` additionally states the converse: an excellent higher rebirth is positively accomplished, so that "the benefits of both this life and the next, even within saṃsāra, are taught" — not contradicted by, but not repeated in, any other claim in the packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-17` specifies that even a person whose prior sinful karma makes a lower rebirth *certain* is freed from it by the splendor of reciting the praise — a stronger formulation (overriding otherwise-fixed karma) not found in any other commentary's claims in this packet.

## Empowerment and attainment up to buddhahood

### Consensus
Recitation is said to bring swift empowerment/blessing by countless buddhas or victors (རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་, literally "victors, ten-million-fold sevenfold"), a state superior to one's present condition already in this life, and ultimately the attainment of buddhahood:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-3-1`, `anon-utpala:c-1-3-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-8`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-8`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-9`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-10`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-3-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `taranatha:c-22-1-18`, `taranatha:c-22-1-19`, `taranatha:c-22-1-20`, `taranatha:c-22-1-21`, `taranatha:c-22-1-22`, `taranatha:c-22-1-23`, `taranatha:c-22-1-24`, `taranatha:c-22-1-25`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

The Tibetan numeral for the victors is uniform across every attestation in the packet — རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་ (rgyal ba bye ba phrag bdun), "victors, seven times ten-million," i.e. seventy million — but this page's own raw-extraction English glosses render the same phrase inconsistently: "seventy million" (`anon-utpala:c-1-3-1`, English gloss), "seven times ten-million" (`gendun-gyatso:c-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-8`), and "seven hundred million" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5`, English glosses). Because the Tibetan itself does not vary, this is logged as a translation inconsistency internal to the individual raw extractions' own English glosses, not as an attested disagreement among the commentaries — it is not treated as a ⚑ divergence.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed in the Tibetan content itself (see the numeral note above).

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-21`–`taranatha:c-22-1-23` elaborate a chain not paralleled elsewhere: the empowerment (received via light-rays or a stream of nectar, actually or in a dream) is specifically identified as "the water-empowerment for ripening disciples," which purifies obscurations and gradually yields the siddhi of mahāmudrā.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3` elaborates the "superior state" with a comparison not found elsewhere: temporarily, like a wheel-turning king (in terms of qualities) or like Indra/Brahmā (in terms of merit); ultimately, the eleventh ground, Samantaprabhā ("All-Illuminating").

## Protection from poison

### Consensus
Even highly potent poison — whether "stationary" (e.g. of scorpions, snakes) or "moving," and whether already eaten or drunk — is said to be dispelled by mere recollection of the goddess, the praise, or her mantra:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-10`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-11`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-12`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-4-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-26`, `taranatha:c-22-1-27`, `taranatha:c-22-1-28`, `taranatha:c-22-1-29`, `taranatha:c-22-1-30`, `taranatha:c-22-1-31`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-9`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (10 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-3-3` gives the richest and most distinctive account of poison-types (snake-venom, rākṣasa-poison, elephant-must-poison, poison of nāgas/rabid dogs/snakes, black-donkey poison, compounded poisons such as goat-liver or rotten dog-corpse, and "transformed" poisons such as frozen goat-meat) and — uniquely — conditions the benefit on the reciter having *already* well accomplished "a hundred-thousand or more" prior recitations of praise to Tārā; only after that prior accomplishment does later mere recollection of the mantra clear and even "transform poison into nectar." No other commentary's claims in this packet state this prerequisite.

`taranatha:c-22-1-28` records a textual-critical note: Namkha Zangpo's emendation of "abiding" (a synonym for "stationary") to "whichever, stationary or moving" is said to be "easier to understand" — a variant-reading note not paralleled elsewhere.

## Protection from spirits, epidemics, and poison-affliction; extension to others

### Consensus
The mass of suffering afflicted by spirits, epidemic disease, and poison is said to be eliminated, and — in a large majority of attestations — the same benefit is said to extend to sentient beings other than the reciter:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-12`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-13`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-13`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-14`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-5-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-33`, `taranatha:c-22-1-34`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-10`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (10 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-34` specifies a distinctive mechanism for the "extends to others" benefit: the mantra-master removes others' suffering "merely by mental intention, or merely by performing the peaceful ritual action" — a specific means of transfer not stated by any other commentary in the packet.

## The meaning of "two, three, seven"

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this is a genuine multi-way divergence over what the closing verse's numerals "two, three, seven" (གཉིས་གསུམ་བདུན) refer to.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least five distinct readings are attested, several recorded by commentaries as internally contested traditions in their own right:

1. **Reciting the whole praise two / three / seven times outright**, likened by "some poets" to the well-known four-maṇḍala offering pattern, to obtain respectively a son, wealth, or all desires — reported (not necessarily endorsed) by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2` (⚑, Position 1 of three it records) as an unnamed-poets' reading.
2. **"Two" = practitioner-type (sharp- vs. dull-faculty), "three" = a session/time distinction, "seven" = seven recitations per session** — attributed to Spang Lo (PER-1) by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2` (⚑, Position 2) and continued by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-3`, which glosses "seven" as three sessions of seven (twenty-one total) granting all desires including fearlessness.
3. **"Two" = day and night, "three" = three sessions within each (six sessions total), "seven" = seven recitations per session (forty-two per day-night cycle)** — attributed to Rinpoché Bu ston (PER-4) by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2` (⚑, Position 3).
4. **"Two" = day/night, "three" = three times by day and three by night, recited in cycles of "seven, seven times over" (i.e. forty-nine)** — `gendun-gyatso:c-2-6`.
5. **"Two" = day and night; "three" = each of the three periods within them; "seven" = the number of recitations per period (seven per period across all periods of day and night)**, with a second reading also noted — "twice at dawn, thrice at midday, seven times at dusk," reached by offering a maṇḍala — both said to "come to the same result" since both derive from pith instructions on the tantra's meaning: `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-7`.

Two further commentaries give readings compatible with, but not verbally identical to, positions above without explicitly citing a named source:
- `karma-maitri:c-1-2-15`: recited three times by day and three by night, in cycles of "seven, seven times over" — closely parallel to gendun-gyatso's Position 4 (49 recitations), though phrased independently.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-6-1`: day and night each divided into three sessions, the praise recited seven times per session — closely parallel to gendun-drub's Position 3 (Bu ston) and gendun-gyatso's/karma-maitri's forty-nine-recitation reading, though again phrased independently and without naming Bu ston.

`taranatha:c-22-1-36` gives its own reading — "two" = recited by day and by night, "three" = either day or night divided into three sessions, and the praise recited seven times in each — attributed generally to "what arises from the guru's oral instruction" rather than to a named exegete; broadly similar in structure to gendun-drub's Position 3 / palden-sherab's reading, but not identical in its own wording and not explicitly cross-referenced to any of them.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-2`–`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-3` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-15` quote or paraphrase the verse's numerals without committing to a specific numerical scheme of their own.

`taranatha:c-22-1-15` separately notes that the benefits described "apply throughout" a six-session daily recitation, without tying this explicitly to the two/three/seven numerals.

### Unique
Each of gendun-drub's three attributed positions, konchok-thabkhe's two-reading reconciliation, and gendun-gyatso's forty-nine-recitation reading are each cited by only their own commentary in this packet; see the Divergences list above for full attributions.

## Desires fulfilled and obstacles destroyed

### Consensus
Reciting the praise (per the count discussed above) is said to fulfil desires — a son for one who desires a son, wealth for one who desires wealth, "all desires" generally — and to destroy or prevent obstacles:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-16`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-17`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-18`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-19`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-6`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-16`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-17`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-18`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-19`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-20`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-6-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-4`, `taranatha:c-22-1-35`, `taranatha:c-22-1-36` (routing note: gloss of the numerals, cited fully under the previous facet), `taranatha:c-22-1-37`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (9 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-37` distinguishes the "son" benefit by station: for a householder, lineage-continuation; for a renunciant, dharma-sons or disciples — a distinction not drawn by any other commentary's claims in this packet.

`taranatha:c-22-1-38` adds a substantially longer list of siddhis obtained — "the peaceful and other [four] activities," siddhi-pills, eye-medicine, the sword, the yakṣiṇī-siddhi, and great treasure — none of which is paralleled in any other commentary's claims in this packet.

## Doctrinal: allegorical readings of "poison" as an obstruction to buddhahood

### Consensus
None — each commentary that reads "poison" allegorically (rather than as a literal toxin, per "Protection from poison," above) gives its own distinct term for what the poison represents.

### ⚑ Divergences
Three commentaries independently offer an allegorical reading of the "poison" that obstructs attainment of buddhahood, using three different specific terms:
- **Wrong view** (ལོག་ལྟ) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-2`: "the poison obstructing progress up to the state of Buddhahood is the great poison of wrong view."
- **Self-grasping view** (བདག་ཏུ་ལྟ་བ), continuing into a partly literal list (thieves, flesh-eating spirits) — `karma-maitri:c-1-2-11`: "the very fierce poison that obstructs that attainment is self-grasping view, and thieves, flesh-eating spirits and so on."
- **The three obscurations** (སྒྲིབ་པ་གསུམ) — `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-5`: "the poison of exceeding fierceness... is the obscuration that obstructs [buddhahood] at the time of accomplishing it — the three obscurations... one becomes free even of the three obscurations."

Self-grasping view is, in general Buddhist doctrine, a species of wrong view, so karma-maitri's and gendun-drub's readings are not incompatible in substance — but their claims use different specific terms and neither claim cross-references the other, so they are recorded as related-but-distinct positions rather than merged into one consensus statement. Tenga-tulku's "three obscurations" is a still broader category (encompassing afflictive, cognitive, and other obscurations depending on the framework) and is not equated with either of the other two terms by any claim in the packet.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-6` extends its own scheme with a further allegorical identification: the "poison of the unmoving" (i.e. stationary poison, per the root verse's own contrast with "moving" poison) is glossed as delusion and hatred toward the Dharma and its teachers. No other commentary's claims in the packet address this second half of gendun-drub's own scheme.

### Unique
None beyond the three positions above.

## Doctrinal: faith as the principal cause of accomplishment

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab` gives an extended, five-claim doctrinal excursus not paralleled by any other commentary in the packet: the principal cause that brings about Tārā's blessing and accomplishment is heartfelt faith and reverence, joined with diligence, mindfulness, and samādhi on her body/speech/mind qualities (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-2`); this is supported by citations from the Prajñāpāramitā ("faith is the door to the appearance of dharma"), the *Ratnolka Sūtra* ("faith is the root that gathers virtuous wealth"), and a saying attributed to "Orgyen the Second Buddha" (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-3`); by a citation from the Vinaya on the Buddha never neglecting the disciples he is to tame across time (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-4`); and by a citation attributed to Palden Chökyi Drakpa — "for a cause that lacks nothing, by what could its result be turned back?" — offered as validating that the benefits described below (protection from the eight and sixteen fears, complete fearlessness) are logically assured once the full complement of causes (faith, diligence, mindfulness, samādhi, wisdom) is present (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-5`).

## Etymology of the root-mantra syllables

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha` alone, in this packet, glosses the ten syllables of the root mantra oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā doctrinally, syllable by syllable: oṃ as the supreme Brahmā, the dharmadhātu, the dharmakāya, and the jewel holding all qualities (`taranatha:c-22-1-1`, `taranatha:c-22-1-2`, `taranatha:c-22-1-3`); tāre as the wisdom arisen from the dharmakāya (`taranatha:c-22-1-4`); tuttāre as the body arisen from the primordial dharma (`taranatha:c-22-1-5`); ture as the great compassion arisen from the ineffable dharma, i.e. emanation and enlightened activity (`taranatha:c-22-1-6`); svā as self-blessing (`taranatha:c-22-1-7`); and hā/e ma ho glossed further (`taranatha:c-22-1-8`), concluding that these signify the illusion-like body, buddha-fields, and boundless enlightened activity (`taranatha:c-22-1-9`), by way of which meanings the praise is offered through the root mantra (`taranatha:c-22-1-10`). No other commentary's claims in the packet gloss the mantra syllable-by-syllable in this way.

## Colophon: attribution of the praise

### Consensus
The root text's own closing colophon (^a-7) — that the praise was spoken to the Bhagavatī Tārā by the perfectly complete Buddha, the great Vairocana — is independently attested and, in several cases, further elaborated:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-20`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-9`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-5`, `taranatha:c-22-1-39` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement — the additional transmission details below are independent elaborations, not conflicting accounts of who spoke the praise.

### Unique
`drakpa-gyaltsen` alone in this packet gives a full transmission lineage for its own textual tradition: transmitted through Master Ārya Nāgārjuna (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-21`), translated by the translator Nyen (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-22`), and redacted/finalized by the great venerable Drakpa Gyaltsen himself (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-23`).

`konchok-thabkhe:c-3-9` uniquely attributes the tantra to dual authorship — spoken by Vairocana *and* by "our own Teacher" [Śākyamuni] — citing the *Mañjuśrī-nāma-saṃgīti* ("taught by past buddhas, future buddhas will also teach it, and the perfect buddhas of the present teach it again and again") in support of the general principle that a tantra may have multiple legitimate speakers.

`taranatha:c-22-1-39`–`taranatha:c-22-1-41` uniquely engages in textual criticism of the attribution: the praise is said to derive from the *Tārā Tantra-King in Seven Hundred Verses*; a text currently circulating locally under the name "Tantra of Various Activities" is explicitly *not* taranatha's source for this praise, since scholars hold it to be a later-authored (non-canonical) tantra; nonetheless, taranatha allows that the praise could still be a portion of an extended tantra or a condensed tantra without contradiction.
