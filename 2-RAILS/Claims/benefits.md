## Structure of the benefits teaching

### Consensus
Several commentaries frame ཕན་ཡོན (the benefits) as unfolding in four parts: the special quality of intention (བསམ་པའི་ཁྱད་པར), the special quality of time (དུས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར), the actual benefits explained at length (ཕན་ཡོན་དངོས), and the benefits condensed by way of number (གྲངས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་ཕན་ཡོན་བསྡུས་པ).
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-0-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
palden-sherab instead frames the benefits teaching as splitting into only **two** parts — the principal cause from which the benefits arise (faith/diligence/recollection) and the actual explanation of the benefits (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-0-1`) — with the latter further divided into **six** sub-benefits: pacifying lower-realm causes/results, buddhas' blessing, great temporary/ultimate results, protection from poison/spirits/epidemic, benefit extending to others, and a concluding six-session recitation statement (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-0-1`). konchok-thabkhe's benefits node is not given an explicit four-part or two-part label by the commentary's own outline, though its content moves through the same broad stages — motivation, time, brief statement, future-life benefits, present-life benefits, numeric benefits (`konchok-thabkhe:c-3-1` through `c-3-8`).

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Who benefits and under what conditions

### Consensus
Across the corpus, the benefits are said to accrue to "whichever wise/intelligent one" (བློ་ལྡན་གང་གིས) who is "genuinely endowed with devotion/reverence" (གུས་ཡང་དག་ལྡན་པ) toward the goddess and recites the praise with faith — e.g. drakpa-gyaltsen: *ལྷ་མོ་ལ་གུས་ཡང་དག་ལྡན་པའི། ། ... ལུས་ངག་ཡིད་གསུམ་ལྷ་མོ་ལ།* ("Genuinely devoted to the goddess — with body, speech, and mind devoted to the goddess").
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-9`, `taranatha:c-22-1-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-1-3` (10 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
gendun-gyatso records an internal divergence on the identity of "the root mantra" (རྩ་བའི་སྔགས) invoked as the object of this practice: Jetsün Drakpa Gyaltsen's position, endorsed by the commentary's own author, identifies it with the peaceful and wrathful mantras taught earlier in the text; an anonymous "some say" position holds that a distinct four-line Tārā root mantra is meant (`gendun-gyatso:c-2-1`).

### Unique
- palden-sherab: the "principal cause" is faith itself, supported by citations from the Prajñāpāramitā, the Ratnolka Sūtra, Guru Rinpoche/Orgyen the Second Buddha, and the Vinaya's simile of the ocean's constancy (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-4`); the benefits are "validly established" because the complete assembly of causes — faith, diligence, mindfulness, samādhi, wisdom — is present, citing Palden Chökyi Drakpa (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-5`).
- sungrab-tulku: identifies "Nyurma" (the Swift One) with Mother Prajñāpāramitā herself, empowered by the buddhas of the three times — the antecedent for the benefits that follow (`sungrab-tulku:c-2-12`).
- taranatha: specifies the manner of practice as clear-minded and diligent, preceded by prostration and offering (`taranatha:c-22-1-12`), and states the benefits apply to whoever recites "in the six sessions" (`taranatha:c-22-1-15`) — see the six-session numeric reading below.

## Recitation practice — timing and manner

### Consensus
The praise is to be recited rising at dusk (སྲོད) and dawn (ཐོ་རངས), with the wrathful form of the deity recollected/visualized at dusk and the peaceful form at dawn — e.g. gendun-drub: *སྲོད་དང་ཐོ་རངས་ལངས་པར་བྱས་ནས། དྲན་པ ... སྤྱིར་སྐུ་དང་། གསུང་སྔགས་དང་། བསྟོད་པ་དང་། ཁྱད་པར་སྲོད་ཀྱི་དུས་སུ་ཁྲོ་མོའི་ངང་། ཐོ་རངས་དུས་སུ་ཞི་བའི་སྐུ་སོགས་དྲན་པ* ("rising from bed at dusk and at dawn ... specifically her wrathful form at dusk and her peaceful form at dawn").
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-7`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-8`, `taranatha:c-22-1-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the basic dusk/dawn, wrathful/peaceful pairing itself — commentaries diverge only on how many total daily sessions this expands to once combined with the recitation-count instruction (see next facet).

### Unique
pema-namgyal gives a doctrinal reason for the two times: dusk is "the time when demons cling to dark night and cause harm," and dawn is "the time when the gods grant prophecies" (`pema-namgyal:c-3-1`).

## Recitation count and the worldly benefits it yields

### Consensus
Reciting the praise a specified number of times fulfills worldly wishes — a son for one who desires a son, wealth for one who desires wealth, and, in general, all that is desired — and destroys obstacles/obstructors: *བུ་འདོད་པས་ནི་བུ་ཐོབ་འགྱུར་ཞིང་། ནོར་འདོད་པས་ནི་ནོར་རྣམས་ཉིད་ཐོབ། འདོད་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར་ཏེ། བགེགས་རྣམས་མེད་ཅིང་སོ་སོར་འཇོམས་འགྱུར* ("one who desires a son obtains a son ... all desires are obtained ... obstacles are removed and individually destroyed").
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-15`–`c-2-19`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-1`–`c-2-2-3-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-6`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-15`–`c-1-2-20`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-6-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-13`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-14`, `taranatha:c-22-1-35`–`c-22-1-37`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
The exact numerological reading of "two, three, seven" (གཉིས་གསུམ་བདུན) varies considerably across the corpus:

- **Position 1 — three alternative counts, each tied to a different wish:** recited twice → a son; thrice → wealth; seven times → all desires — attributed by gendun-drub to unnamed poets, "likened to the well-known four-maṇḍala offering pattern" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2`, Position 1).
- **Position 2 — numbers as practitioner-type and time distinctions, not raw counts:** "two" = practitioner-type (sharp-facultied dharma-follower vs. dull-facultied faith-follower); "three" = the dawn-to-dusk time-span; "seven" = recite seven times in each of the three periods of day and night (three sevens = twenty-one) — attributed to Spang Lo (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2` Position 2, continued at `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-3`).
- **Position 3 — six sessions, forty-two recitations per day:** "two" = day and night; "three" = three sessions within each (six sessions total); "seven" = seven recitations per session — attributed to Rinpoché Bu ston (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-4-2` Position 3). A closely related six-session/seven-per-session reading also appears, without naming Bu ston, in `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-6-1` (three day sessions, three night sessions, seven recitations each) and `taranatha:c-22-1-36` ("two" = day and night; "three" = each day's or night's three sessions; seven recitations per session, "according to the guru's oral instruction"). taranatha's separate note that the benefits apply to "whoever ... recites in the six sessions" (`taranatha:c-22-1-15`) presupposes this same six-session frame.
- **Position 4 — graduated dawn/midday/dusk count with maṇḍala offering:** "twice at dawn, thrice at midday, seven times at dusk," each accompanied by a maṇḍala offering — recorded by konchok-thabkhe as a second reading alongside one matching Position 3, with the explicit note that "either method comes to the same result" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-3-7`).
- **Position 5 — forty-nine recitations total:** "two" = day and night; "three" = three times by day and three by night; the whole praise is then recited "seven-seven" (7×7 = 49) times — `gendun-gyatso:c-2-6` and, in near-identical wording, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-15`.

### Unique
- taranatha: repeated recitation and accomplishment additionally yield "all kinds of siddhis, such as the peaceful and other [four] activities, siddhi-pills, eye-medicine, the sword, the yakṣiṇī [siddhi], and great treasure" (`taranatha:c-22-1-38`).
- gendun-gyatso and karma-maitri both note that obstructing spirits/obstacles either do not arise at all or, if they do, are destroyed by the power of the Venerable Lady herself (`gendun-gyatso:c-2-7`; `karma-maitri:c-1-2-19`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-20`).

## Fearlessness — general protection from fear

### Consensus
Merely by recollection (དྲན་པ་ཙམ་གྱིས) of the deity, the reciter is granted complete fearlessness (མི་འཇིགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད); several commentaries specify this as protection from the traditional eight and sixteen great fears (འཇིགས་པ་བརྒྱད་དང་བཅུ་དྲུག): drakpa-gyaltsen — *དྲན་པས་མི་འཇིགས་ཐམས་ཅད་རབ་སྟེར ... ལྷའི་སྐུ་དྲན་པ་ཙམ་གྱིས་མི་འཇིགས་པ་སྦྱིན་པའོ* ("by recollection she grants all fearlessness ... by mere recollection of the divine body, fearlessness is granted").
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-5`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-7`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-9`, `taranatha:c-22-1-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
sungrab-tulku, in its separate "condensed praise" appendix, derives the same fearlessness benefit directly from the mantra syllable TUTTĀRE, glossed as "anguish" — Tārā's compassionate anguish for beings, likened to a mother's love for her only child, by which she "dispels the eight fears and sixteen fears and so forth" (`sungrab-tulku:c-26-7`).

## Purification of misdeeds and freedom from lower rebirth

### Consensus
Reciting/recollecting the praise pacifies all misdeeds/sin (སྡིག་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་བ) and destroys all lower rebirths and their resultant suffering (ངན་འགྲོ་ཐམས་ཅད་འཇོམས་པ).
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-6`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-7`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-7`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-9`, `taranatha:c-22-1-16`–`c-22-1-18`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-2-9`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
sungrab-tulku's condensed-praise appendix derives this benefit from a mantra syllable — SVĀHĀ, glossed as "peace," which "pacifies all misdeeds and increases every accumulation of virtue" (`sungrab-tulku:c-26-11`).

## Empowerment by countless buddhas, and progress toward buddhahood

### Consensus
Seventy million (བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན) victors/buddhas will swiftly grant empowerment, and the reciter attains a state greater than the present one, ultimately reaching the state of buddhahood: *རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། མྱུར་དུ་དབང་ནི་བསྐུར་བར་འགྱུར་ལ། འདི་ལས་ཆེ་བ་ཉིད་ནི་འཐོབ་ཅིང་། སངས་རྒྱས་གོ་འཕང་མཐར་ཐུག་དེར་འགྲོ*.
— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-3-1`–`c-1-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-6`–`c-2-8`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-8`–`c-1-2-10`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-3-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`, `taranatha:c-22-1-19`–`c-22-1-25`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (12 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
The packet's own English glosses render the identical Tibetan numeral phrase བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན (literally "seven ten-millions" = seventy million) inconsistently — most commentaries gloss it "seventy million" (e.g. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-3-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10`), while `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` gloss the same phrase "seven hundred million." No commentary itself treats this as a live dispute; it is recorded here as an observed inconsistency in the corpus's numeral glosses, not a doctrinal disagreement.

### Unique
- taranatha gives by far the most granular staged account: seeing the emanation-buddhas' faces (waking or in dream) → empowerment by light-rays or a nectar-stream → the water-empowerment of disciple-ripening → purification of obscurations and attainment of samādhis/dhāraṇīs → gradual attainment of mahāmudrā siddhi → traversing the noble bhūmis → complete buddhahood (`taranatha:c-22-1-19`–`c-22-1-25`).
- tenga-tulku specifies the final attainment as "the eleventh ground of Samantaprabha (All-Illuminating)," with graded temporary results — wheel-turning king in terms of great qualities, Indra or Brahmā in terms of merit (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`).
- sungrab-tulku's condensed-praise appendix derives the same benefit from the mantra syllable TURE, glossed as bestowing "wealth, resources, longevity, merit, and power" temporarily and "liberation and the rank of omniscience" ultimately (`sungrab-tulku:c-26-9`).
- palden-sherab enumerates the temporary happiness in unusually specific terms: "long life, merit, entourage, resources, power, fame, scriptural transmission, and the qualities of realization" (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-3-1`).

## Protection from poison

### Consensus
By recollecting the deity/mantra, poison — whether from a stationary source or a moving creature, and whether already eaten or drunk — is thoroughly cleared and its harm removed: *དེ་ཡི་དུག་ནི་དྲག་པོ་ཆེན་པོ། བརྟན་གནས་པའམ་གཞན་ཡང་འགྲོ་བ། ཟོས་པ་དང་ནི་འཐུངས་བ་ཉིད་ཀྱང་། དྲན་པས་རབ་ཏུ་སེལ་བ་ཉིད་ཐོབ*.
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-9`–`c-2-11`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-12`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-4-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-27`, `taranatha:c-22-1-29`–`c-22-1-31`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-9`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (9 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Three commentaries additionally give an **allegorical, doctrinal** reading of "poison" alongside — not instead of — the literal reading:
- gendun-drub reads the "unmoving poison" specifically as denigrating the nature of reality through delusion and hatred toward the Dharma and its teachers (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-6`), while separately reading "moving poison" literally as being bitten by rabid dogs or snakes, or eating/drinking poisoned food (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-3`).
- karma-maitri glosses the "very fierce poison" obstructing attainment as self-grasping view together with thieves and flesh-eating spirits (`karma-maitri:c-1-2-11`), before separately noting that the poison of moving creatures such as snakes is cleared by mere recollection (`karma-maitri:c-1-2-12`).
- tenga-tulku identifies the poison obstructing buddhahood as "the three obscurations" that "destroy all the happiness of the state of liberation," removed by one-pointed supplication (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-5`), while elsewhere in the same passage giving the literal reading — fang-poison, flesh-poison, hair-poison, and the poison of snakes and other moving creatures (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-7`).

The remaining commentaries that address poison (drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-gyatso, konchok-thabkhe, palden-sherab, pema-namgyal, taranatha, tsultrim-namdak) read it literally throughout, without an allegorical layer.

### Unique
- pema-namgyal gives the most extensive taxonomy of poison-types in the corpus — virulent snake-venom, rākṣasa-poison, elephant-must poison, naturally-arising elemental poison, harm from nāgas/rabid dogs/snakes, innately-arisen animal poison (black donkey), compounded poison (goat-liver, rotten dog-corpse), and transformed poison (frozen goat-meat) — and specifies that prior accomplishment of a hundred-thousand recitations of the praise is what allows later mere recollection to clear and even transform such poison into nectar (`pema-namgyal:c-3-3`).
- tenga-tulku adds that the benefit here is not only the removal of poison-suffering but also a positive attainment: merely recollecting the Venerable Lady's blessing grants "special, excellent bliss" (བདེ་བ་ཁྱད་པར་ཅན) in addition to dispelling that suffering (`tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-10`).

## Protection from spirits (gdon) and epidemic disease (rims)

### Consensus
The mass of suffering caused by evil spirits (གདོན), epidemic disease (རིམས), and poison together — frequently listed as a single compound, གདོན་དང་རིམས་དང་དུག — is entirely eliminated.
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-12`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-13`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-13`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-4-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-33`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-12`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (10 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond what is recorded under Protection from poison above — the source verse frequently combines poison, spirit-harm, and epidemic disease in a single clause, so several claims are cited under both facets.

## Benefit extends to others, not only to the reciter

### Consensus
The stated benefits are not confined to the reciter — they are explicitly said to extend to other sentient beings as well: *སེམས་ཅན་གཞན་པ་རྣམས་ལ་ཡང་ངོ*.
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-14`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-5-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-34`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-14` (9 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
taranatha specifies the mechanism most explicitly: the "vidyā-mantra-master," merely by mental intention or by performing the peaceful ritual action, removes suffering for other beings (`taranatha:c-22-1-34`).

## Origin and attribution of the benefits teaching

### Consensus
The praise, and with it its benefits, is attributed to having been spoken by the buddha Vairocana (རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད), often specified as drawn from a root tantra.
— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-20`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-5`, `taranatha:c-22-1-39`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-6` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
konchok-thabkhe records that the tantra was taught by **both** Vairocana and "our own Teacher" [Śākyamuni], citing the precedent that many tantras have multiple authorship across the three times, and quoting the Mañjuśrī-nāma-saṃgīti: "taught by past buddhas, future buddhas will also teach it, and the perfect buddhas of the present teach it again and again" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-3-9` — flagged ambiguous by the mapping pass as possibly colophon-adjacent rather than strict benefits content; retained here as the corpus's clearest statement on the benefits-teaching's origin/authorship).

### Unique
- drakpa-gyaltsen additionally records a lineage-transmission chain: from Vairocana through Master Ārya Nāgārjuna (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-21`).
- taranatha names the source as the "Tārā Tantra-King in Seven Hundred Verses" (རྟོག་པ་བདུན་བརྒྱ་པ), explicitly distinguishes it from a text "renowned as the Tantra of Various Activities" currently circulating in the region — which scholars hold to be an authored, non-canonical work — while allowing it might still be a portion of an extended or condensed tantra (`taranatha:c-22-1-39`–`c-22-1-41`).
- sungrab-tulku records an entirely separate origin account for its "condensed praise" appendix (a different, one-verse text): spoken by Tārā herself in response to Jowoje Palden Atiśa's prayer, with reciting this single verse once said to equal reciting the mantra of Tārā once (`sungrab-tulku:c-26-12`).

## Named hazards overcome through prayer to Tārā (narrative tradition)

This facet is attested almost exclusively by tsultrim-namdak's dedicated compilation of protection-narratives, "Drum-Sound of Faith" (Work 4 of its nine-work anthology) — the corpus's only source of attributed, place-and-person-specific stories rather than compressed verse-gloss statements. Its own opening states the purpose: to compile "credible accounts of protection from fears through prayer to the Noble One's feet ... drawn from the vajra-words of past sages and from actual occurrences," so as to "increase our own faith" (`tsultrim-namdak:c-4-0-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-4-0-2`).

### ⚑ Divergences
None of the individual episodes above is disputed or contradicted by any other commentary. The divergence, if any, is one of genre: every other commentary that addresses "hazard/danger" does so only in the compressed, unattributed verse-gloss form recorded under Fearlessness / Protection from poison / Protection from spirits and epidemic disease above, never as a named, place-and-person-specific story.

### Unique
tsultrim-namdak also records, in a separate part of its anthology (a four-maṇḍala sādhana liturgy, not the dedicated benefits compilation), a formal enumeration of "the sixteen fears of this life" as a personal aspiration-prayer line: fire, water, lions, elephants, snakes, flesh-eating demons, chains/imprisonment, thieves, leprosy, famine, enemies, thunderbolts, royal punishment, separation from friends, loss of one's aims, and spirit-harm — plus evil dreams and ill omens, and ultimately all coarse and subtle saṃsāric suffering (`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-4-3-1-1`). This is liturgical aspiration embedded in a different composition within the same anthology, not itself a benefits-teaching statement, but it is the corpus's fullest single enumeration of the traditional "sixteen fears" that other commentaries name only piecemeal (see Fearlessness above).

## Other general benefit statements

### Unique
No consensus on this facet — each of the following is a single-commentary statement not paralleled elsewhere in the corpus on this specific point:
- gendun-gyatso closes its benefits section with a dedication verse likening recitation to gathering nectar from a garland of petals, praying that through Tārā's kindness all beings be led to the expanse of the dharmakāya (`gendun-gyatso:c-2-9`).
- sangye-nyentrul's colophon carries a bare general dedication: "May this become a cause of benefit for all!" (`sangye-nyentrul:c-24-3`).
- tenzin-dhonzang, while glossing the opening invocation rather than within a dedicated benefits section (this commentary's transmission lacks one entirely — see Coverage), states that merely recollecting Tārā's form as seen, her speech as heard, and her mind's omniscient wisdom purifies sins and obscurations, frees one from lower rebirth, grants a good rebirth, and awakens the Mahāyāna potential — "reciting Tārā's name has boundless benefits" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-98` — flagged ambiguous by the mapping pass owing to its location outside the commentary's own missing benefits node; retained here as a genuine general-benefit statement).
- taranatha closes with a dedication verse: "By whatever virtue is obtained from this thorough explanation of the tantra of praise to her who is the mother of the victors of the three times, may [beings] accomplish the state of the Noble Lady" (`taranatha:c-22-1-43`).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these four statements do not conflict, they simply do not overlap.
