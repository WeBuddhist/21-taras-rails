# Māra — བདུད། (the tempter-deity and the fourfold māras)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
> Unlike a spine-slot topic page, this packet was not assembled by `assemble_packet.py`/spine-map — Māra is a cross-cutting keyword subject, not a registered spine slot — but by a parallel deterministic script, `assemble_keyword_packet.py`, which routes claims via the keyword-extraction pipeline's Signal-A per-commentary claim-tagging (`0-INBOX/AI_translation/keyword-extraction/output/signal_a/<id>.json`, recording which claim IDs are "about" this term per commentary); it carries the same guarantee as `assemble_packet.py` — every claim block below is copied character-for-character from the commentary's own raw claims file.

Māra (བདུད།) surfaces at several distinct points across the Twenty-One Homages rather than at one spine slot — hence `spine: global`. The packet's 56 claims cluster mainly around two verses: Homage 8, "Ture, greatly terrifying" (ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུ་རེ་འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ...), whose second line names "Māra's heroes" (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ) as what she destroys; and Homage 10, the "TUTTĀRA" laughter verse, whose laughter subjugates Māra together with the world. A smaller set of claims treats etymology, a different "victorious without limit" verse, esoteric/completion-stage readings, and one closing-section recitation benefit.

## Questions asked

3. Do commentaries quote the Ture verse (Homage 8) verbatim, and does its wording remain consistent across quotations, or are there textual variants?
4. How do commentaries gloss "Māra's heroes/champions" (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ) in the Ture verse — as the four māras (བདུད་བཞི), and if so, which four do they name, in what order, and how fully?
5. Do the commentaries that name all four māras converge on the same standard set (afflictions, aggregates, lord of death, god's son), or do any enumerations diverge?
6. Does any commentary give a variant or non-standard enumeration of the māras destroyed, and does it conflict with that same commentary's own claims elsewhere in the packet?
7. Which specific being do commentaries identify as the Māra subjugated by the laughing mantra TUTTĀRA/TĀRA/tāhatture in verse 10, and do they name him consistently as lord of the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods?
8. Beyond Māra himself, what other beings or worlds do commentaries say are brought under Tārā's power by the same verse's laughter?
9. Do any commentaries offer a completion-stage or "hidden meaning" (tantric) reading in which "Māra" denotes an inner state — grasping, craving, or conceptual thought — rather than (or alongside) an external being, and do their specific inner-yogic mappings agree with one another?
10. Does any commentary explain Tārā's wrathful manifestation itself as generated specifically in order to destroy Māra?
11. Outside the Ture and TUTTĀRA verses, do commentaries describe Tārā's general victory over Māra among "limitless opponents," and on which other verse do they base this?
12. Does any commentary attribute a māra-destroying benefit to the act of reciting the praise itself, distinct from the doctrinal or iconographic content of the verses on Māra?
13. Does any other commentary corroborate drakpa-gyaltsen's claim that only the affliction-māra is named directly by the verse's own grammar, with the remaining three destroyed only "incidentally"?

## Structural framing: this section of the praise as "the quality of overcoming Māra"

### Consensus
Two commentaries explicitly frame the Ture-verse homage as being, in its entirety, about the quality/activity of overcoming Māra:

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-1` (2 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-1` places it as the first of seven homages in the wrathful-form section of the praise, glossed as "the quality of overcoming the Māras and the two obscurations." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-1` gives a simple structural heading: "Eighth, homage to Vanquisher of Māras and Enemies."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Etymology: epithets glossed via māra-destruction

### Consensus
Two epithets are etymologized by more than one commentary via Tārā's māra-destroying activity.

"Heroine" (དཔའ་མོ, dpa' mo) is glossed as deriving in part from her destroying the host of afflictions/māras (བདུད་སྡེ་ / ཉོན་མོངས་བདུད་སྡེ་):

— attested: `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-3` (3 commentaries)

Two of the three (`karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-3`) give this as a pure māra-destruction etymology. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4` gives a compound etymology instead — "since her activity is swifter than other buddhas' *and* she destroys the host of māras, she is 'Heroine'" — pairing swiftness with māra-destruction rather than resting on māra-destruction alone.

The epithet/utterance "Ture" is connected specifically to Māra by two commentaries, though via two different specific glosses: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-49` gives a wordplay etymology — "by Ture's terror she dispels all Māras; because she terrifies Māra, she is 'greatly terrifying'" — while `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-1` explains that merely proclaiming the fear-dispelling utterance "ture" protects beings "from the great terror of māras and so on":

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-49`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-1` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-6` etymologizes a third epithet, "the Glorious One" (དཔལ་མོ, dpal mo), the same way: "because she utterly destroys the host of māras without remainder, she is 'the Glorious One.'" No other commentary's claims in this packet etymologize this particular epithet via māra-destruction.

## The Ture verse (Homage 8) quoted verbatim

### Consensus
The Ture verse is quoted at length, essentially verbatim, by six commentaries. `yama-sonam:c-3-8-3`'s quotation (introduced "From the tantra") reads:

**བོད་ཡིག:** རྒྱུད་ལས། ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུ་རེ་འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ། ། བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་མ། ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཞལ་ནི་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ལྡན་མཛད། ། དགྲ་བོ་ཐམས་ཅད་མ་ལུས་གསོད་མ།
**English:** "From the tantra: 'Homage to Ture, great fear, destroyer of Māra's heroes; whose lotus-face bears a wrathful frown, who slays all enemies without exception.'"

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-8-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-27` (6 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-2` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-3` each quote only part of the four-line verse (the first two lines and the last three lines respectively), consistent with — not conflicting with — the fuller quotations given by the other three (`yama-sonam:c-3-8-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-2`). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-27` reads the second syllable of the first line as ཆེན་པོས (instrumental) rather than ཆེན་མོ (nominative, as in every other quotation in this packet), and its own quotation is truncated mid-word (ending at དགྲ་); this is recorded as its own, fourth, textual variant — internal to this one quotation, not a doctrinal disagreement, so it is not treated as a ⚑ divergence.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed (see the textual-variant note above).

### Unique
None beyond the variant reading noted above.

## Doctrinal identification: "Māra's heroes" as the four māras (བདུད་བཞི)

### Consensus
A broad consensus (14 of the 16 commentaries in this packet) glosses the Ture verse's "Māra's heroes/champions" (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ) as denoting the four māras (བདུད་བཞི) — the standard fourfold Buddhist classification of obstructing forces:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-50`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-51`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-28`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` (14 commentaries)

Within this list, drakpa-gyaltsen's own reading is built from two claims read together, not one: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-50` glosses "heroes" as naming only the affliction-māra directly in the verse's own grammar ("'heroes' means the Māra of the afflictions"), and the immediately following `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-51` adds that "by defeating that [affliction-Māra], the three [Māras] are incidentally destroyed" — together, not individually, arriving at the same "all four" reading given directly by the other thirteen.

Two further claims in this list use a compressed Tibetan phrase that is genuinely ambiguous between "the four māras, [chief of which is] the affliction[-māra]" and "the four kinds of affliction-māra": `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5` (ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་བདུད་བཞི, English gloss "the four afflictive māras") and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4` (ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་བདུད་བཞི, English gloss "the four māras of the afflictions"). Both are counted above because either reading affirms a set of four māras destroyed by Tārā; neither claim's own wording resolves which of the two readings is intended, and this page does not resolve it for them.

Of the fourteen, six enumerate the standard set by name in full — the affliction-māra (ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་བདུད་), the aggregate-māra (ཕུང་པོའི་བདུད་), the māra of the lord of death (འཆི་བདག་གི་བདུད་), and the deva-putra/god's-son māra (ལྷའི་བུའི་བདུད་) — though in varying order and wording:

— full four-name enumeration attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` (6 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11`, in its iconographic gloss of the same verse, gives a different enumeration of the "hardest-to-tame among the māras": afflictions, birth, aging, sickness, and death (per its own English gloss). This five-item list replaces the aggregate-māra and deva-putra-māra of the standard set with birth, aging, and sickness, and does not match the fourfold enumeration `tsultrim-namdak` itself gives elsewhere in this same packet at `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` ("the māra of afflictions, of the aggregates, of the lord of death, and of the god's son"). This is recorded as an internal tension within tsultrim-namdak's own commentary as much as a divergence from the rest of the packet.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-8-4` glosses "Māra's heroes" differently again, as "the generals" (དམག་དཔོན) — a military-command reading, not an enumeration of named māras at all: "she destroys the heroes of Māra, that is, the generals."

## The named cosmological Māra: Paranirmitavaśavartin, subjugated by TUTTĀRA's laughter (Homage 10)

### Consensus
Eleven commentaries, treating the "TUTTĀRA"/laughter homage (verse 10 of the Twenty-One Homages), agree that the mantric sound of Tārā's laughter subjugates Māra together with other beings or worlds:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`, `taranatha:c-10-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-10` (11 commentaries)

Of these, seven go further and explicitly name the specific being subjugated — not a doctrinal abstraction but a named cosmological figure, the lord of the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods (the sixth and highest heaven of the desire realm) — variously transliterated/translated as "Māra, lord of the Paranirmitavaśavartin [devas]" (གཞན་འཕྲུལ་དབང་བྱེད་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་བདུད་), "Māra Dgaʼ-rab-dbang-phyug" (བདུད་དགའ་རབ་དབང་ཕྱུག), or (one English gloss) "Kāmadeva Rabga Wangchuk":

— explicit naming attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-10` (7 commentaries)

The remaining four in the broader list describe the target only generically — as "māras" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`), "the māras and the tīrthika world" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3`), or "the Māra who subjugates other worlds" (`taranatha:c-10-6`, a descriptive periphrasis rather than the specific title) — without using the specific Paranirmitavaśavartin title.

Several of the eleven additionally report that the same laughter brings further targets under Tārā's power beyond Māra himself: the tīrthika world (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3`); kings, ministers, renunciants, and householders (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`), with `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2` corroborating two of the four — ministers and householders, but not kings or renunciants by name; the eight great worldly gods (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`); or "all three worlds" generally (`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the variation is in specificity (named being vs. generic "māras") and scope (Māra alone vs. Māra-plus-world), not in doctrine.

### Unique
None beyond the notes above.

## Esoteric/completion-stage readings: "Māra" as inner grasping or craving

### Consensus
At least three commentaries move beyond the literal reading of "Māra" in this section of the praise, offering a doctrinal or tantric ("hidden meaning") interpretation in which the māra(s) destroyed denote an inner mental state — ordinary craving, grasping, or conceptual thought — dissolved through yogic or contemplative means, rather than (or alongside) an external being:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-4-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-2`, `taranatha:c-13-11` (3 commentaries)

Each of the three gives its own distinct elaboration; none restates another's, so no single shared position beyond this general pattern is stated.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the three do not conflict; they give unrelated, independently elaborated esoteric readings (see Unique).

### Unique
`palden-sherab` gives four separate completion-stage ("hidden meaning") readings across this section, each mapping a different element of the outer description onto inner tantric physiology: the crown-ornament's joy as co-emergent bliss stabilized at the crown, the eightfold laughter as the eight drops of the four cakras' forward-and-reverse movement, and TUTTĀRA as tummo's fire, which "burns" one's own innate conceptual attachment ("Māra") and the ordinary, contaminated craving-sensation of body/speech/mind ("the world") into uncontaminated great-bliss wisdom (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1`); the dissolving of the life-force wind into the central channel as "destroying Māra's hero," and the piercing of the vital points of drop/wind/mind as slaying the conceptual "enemy" of pleasurable sensation, culminating in meeting Tārā's own face as great bliss inseparable from wind-mind (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-1`); in the completion-stage-without-marks, the transferring breath and all conceptual thought as themselves the wind of ultimate wisdom (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-4-1`); and, in a separate passage, the karmic winds being overcome in the central channel so that the drop of bliss-emptiness co-emergent wisdom stabilizes, "thereby destroying the enemy-host of ordinary grasping and the afflictions" (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-2`).

`taranatha:c-13-11` gives a single, shorter esoteric gloss on the word "joy" in the same laughter-verse's context: the winds becoming the drop of bliss, "thereby destroying the enemy hosts of Māra and the afflictions" — structurally similar to palden-sherab's general pattern (winds/drops overcoming Māra-as-affliction) but independently phrased, with no channel/cakra detail, and not cross-referenced to palden-sherab's own account.

`sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-2`, in a different, ritual/generation-stage context (visualizing a protective vajra-fence around the maṇḍala), pairs the destruction of "Māra's forces" with the destruction of "the confused cognition grasping at self and other, both the form-possessing and the formless aspects" — an outer/inner pairing structurally parallel to palden-sherab's and taranatha's completion-stage equations, but framed as generation-stage ritual protection rather than completion-stage inner yoga.

## Tārā's wrathful manifestation as generated specifically to destroy Māra

### Consensus
Two commentaries make the same specific doctrinal point: that Tārā's wrathful physical form is itself generated or manifested for the purpose of destroying Māra, rather than the destruction being merely an activity a pre-existing wrathful form happens to perform:

— attested: `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-28` (2 commentaries)

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-7` states this as an identification: "in the wrathful manner that destroys the host of māras, she manifests as Tārā herself." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-28` gives the fuller causal sequence: from the peaceful state of her dharmatā-mind, she manifests in a form of great terror specifically in order to destroy "Māra's heroes."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## General victory over Māra "among limitless opponents" (a separate verse)

### Consensus
Three commentaries, commenting on a different verse from the Ture/TUTTĀRA homages discussed above (the verse describing Tārā as acting "victorious without limit," མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ་སྤྱོད་མ), agree that Māra is named among the "limitless" opponents over whom she is victorious:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-24`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-3` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-2` frames this victory as accomplished specifically "endowed with the ten perfections" — a causal/practice-based framing not given by the other two.

### Consensus
None — this facet is attested by a single commentary only.

None observed.

`taranatha:c-22-1-35`, in the closing benefits section of the praise (treated in full at `2-RAILS/Claims/benefits.md`), states that "by all taking up the final line, obstacles are eliminated and māras and so on are individually destroyed" — a recitation-benefit claim distinct from the doctrinal/iconographic content of the other facets on this page.
