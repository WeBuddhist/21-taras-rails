## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but not all mark it the same way. Ten give an explicit global ordinal, "the fifteenth" (བཅོ་ལྔ་པ/བཅོ་ལྔ་བ) or its numeral: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-15-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1` (via its node's own ༡༥ marker), `sungrab-tulku:c-18-1` (marked "༼༡༥༽"), `taranatha:c-15-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-1`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-18` (its own claim title labels its content "Verse 15"). Four instead nest this verse locally as "the second" (གཉིས་པ) within a "praise by way of the dharmakāya (form)" (ཆོས་སྐུའི་(རྣམ་པའི་)སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-1` all open with the near-identical clause "གཉིས་པ་ཆོས་སྐུའི་(རྣམ་པའི་)སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི"; `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-1` shares the same "ཆོས་སྐུའི་རྣམ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི" phrasing and the same node position (1.2.2.2) but its own quoted claim text does not repeat the word "གཉིས་པ." `sungrab-tulku:c-18-1` gives both markers together: the global numeral "(15)" and the "ཆོས་སྐུའི་རྣམ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ" classification. The remaining commentary, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, supplies no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — its content was routed to this slot by claim-content match (its first claim opens by quoting this verse's own first line).

Nine commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines verbatim (as one block or split across adjacent claims):

## Name and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name for this homage's Tārā gives one of several distinct traditions; this facet is a set of divergent (though sometimes overlapping) namings, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least three distinct naming traditions are attested, plus one purely functional classification:

1. **"[Lady/Tārā of] Great Peace"** (ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-1` ("the fifteenth homage: to the Venerable Lady Great Peace," རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ), `sungrab-tulku:c-18-3` ("bowing... to the object of homage, venerable Tārā, the Great Peaceful One," རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-1` (heading: "Fifteenth, homage to Great Peace," ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ). Three commentaries; `anon-trinle-char` and `sungrab-tulku` share the exact phrase ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ, while `tenzin-dhonzang`'s heading uses the same two words without the honorific རྗེ་བཙུན་མ.
2. **"[Ārya Tārā] the Utterly Peaceful, Who Pacifies Misdeeds and Obscurations"** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-1` ("Ārya Tārā the Utterly Peaceful, Who Pacifies Misdeeds and Obscurations," སྒྲོལ་མ་རབ་ཞི་མ), corroborated in substance (though not in exact wording) by `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-1-1` ("this 'Tārā Who Pacifies Misdeeds and Obscurations,'" སྡིག་སྒྲིབ་ཞི་བར་མཛད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ), and by `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-18` ("Sgrol-ma rab-tu-zhi-ma," སྒྲོལ་མ་རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་མ — "the Utterly Pacifying One" — sharing the element རབ་(ཏུ་)ཞི་མ with palden-sherab's སྒྲོལ་མ་རབ་ཞི་མ). Three commentaries converge on a "pacifies/utterly-peaceful" naming built on the root ཞི་, distinct from group 1's ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ.
3. **"Tārā of Virtuous, Peaceful Excellence"** (དགེ་ལེགས་ཞི་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-6` states in its own voice, "She is called here 'Tārā of Virtuous, Peaceful Excellence'" (འདི་ལ་དགེ་ལེགས་ཞི་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་འབྱུང་ངོ་). This is independently corroborated by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-12`, which reports (re-read and confirmed against gendun-drub's own claim): "As for her name, Rje Dge 'dun grub calls her 'Tārā, Virtuous and Peaceful'" — quoting the identical phrase དགེ་ལེགས་ཞི་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ. The two claims name and quote each other's tradition character-for-character on this specific epithet, a rare case in the packet of one commentary's report about a named individual being directly checkable against that individual's own text.

The remaining classification is not a proper name but a functional label: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-1` titles the verse "homage by way of destroying misdeeds" (སྡིག་པ་འཇོམས་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ), and `sungrab-tulku:c-18-1` (alongside its "(15)" numeral) and the four commentaries in the "Structural placement" facet above classify it as "praise by way of the dharmakāya (form)."

The remaining commentaries (`anon-utpala`, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha`, `tenga-tulku`) supply no proper name for this homage's Tārā within the claims in this packet.

### Unique
`sangye-nyentrul:c-16-1-1`'s and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-1`'s naming both additionally specify a white body colour attached to the name (see "Iconography," below) — noted here only as a naming detail, not repeated as a separate attestation.

## Etymology of "Blissful, Virtuous, Peaceful"

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is the packet's central divergence. One commentary's own claim itself names the disagreement explicitly as a live dispute between two readings (see Position 2 under Divergences, below, for the citation).

### ⚑ Divergences
Three distinct readings of "Blissful, Virtuous, Peaceful" (and, closely tied to it, the verse's "domain of nirvāṇa's peace") are attested:

**Position 1 — her own inherent qualities or freedoms** (bliss/virtue/peace explained as what she herself is or has been freed from, not as something bestowed): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-3` ("free of suffering, she is 'blissful'"), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-4` ("cause does not accumulate non-virtue, 'virtuous'"), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-5` ("free of afflictions, 'peaceful'"), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-78` ("possesses uncontaminated bliss," ཟག་མེད་ཀྱི་བདེ་བ་དང་ལྡན་མ), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-79` ("free from the afflictions to be abandoned"), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-80` ("acts from within the pacified-suffering state"), `gendun-gyatso:c-1-15-1` (uncontaminated bliss for self and other; abandoned afflictions; free of stains), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-5` (nature free of outflows; abandoned afflictions; free from harsh contact), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2` (result free of suffering; cause abandoned non-virtue; free of afflictions — near-identical to `anon-utpala`'s three claims, see note below), `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1` (result exhausted; cause abandoned; destroyed by wisdom of the path), `sungrab-tulku:c-18-4` (result exhausted; cause of two afflictions abandoned; accomplished by wisdom realizing emptiness), `taranatha:c-15-3` ("Bhagavatī herself is always blissful, virtuous, and of a peaceful nature"), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-3` (result free of suffering via path-of-seeing abandonments; cause of virtue manifest via path-of-meditation abandonments; stains of habitual tendencies pacified), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-3` (offers several alternate own-quality glosses: freed from lower rebirth, or free of illness/suffering, or possessing undefiled bliss = blissful; afflictions-with-imprints abandoned, or liberation attained = virtuous; not even the subtlest cognitive-obscuration stain = peaceful), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-51` (absence of suffering = blissful; exhaustion of karmic/afflictive causes = virtuous; abandonment of both obscurations = peaceful), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-18` (result exhausted = blissful; causes not accumulated = virtuous; afflictions abandoned = peaceful). Eighteen citations across eleven commentaries.

`anon-utpala`'s and `lobsang-dawa`'s wording is close to verbatim for two of the three clauses — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-3` "འབྲས་བུ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་མེད་པས་བདེ་མ།" against `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2`'s "འབྲས་བུ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་མེད་པས་བདེ་མ་དང" (identical save the connective particle), and `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-5` "སྤང་བྱ་ཉོན་མོངས་མེད་པས་ཞི་མ།" against `lobsang-dawa`'s "ཉོན་མོངས་མེད་པས་ཞི་མ" (identical, `lobsang-dawa` drops སྤང་བྱ); the "virtuous" clause differs by one verb (`anon-utpala`'s མི་གསོག་པས், "does not accumulate," vs `lobsang-dawa`'s སྤངས་པས், "has abandoned").

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1` gives a structurally distinct variant within the same "her own qualities" family: it maps the four terms onto the four truths (result/absence-of-suffering = "happy"; cause/abandoned-non-virtue = "virtuous"; path/exhausted-by-wisdom = "peaceful"; cessation-dharmakāya = "nirvāṇa's peace"), adding that in extensive division her domain of activity is "the twenty-seven activities taught in the Abhisamayālaṃkāra." Because this reading is built on an explicit four-truths framework not shared by the plainer own-quality glosses above, it is recorded here as a related but distinct sub-variant rather than folded into the main Position-1 attestation list.

**Position 2 — what she bestows on others** (the same three terms explained not as her own state but as what she grants beings — higher rebirth, liberation, buddhahood): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-4` ("because...the venerable lady bestows the higher rebirth of gods and humans, she is 'Bliss'; because she grants the state of liberation...'Virtue'; because she grants the state of omniscience...'Peace'"), supported by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-5` (naming this as the tantra's own intended reading, against other commentaries' alternate mappings) and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-6` (citing a verse from Nyima Sbaspa: "Goddess, by Bliss you grant higher rebirth; by Virtue you grant liberation; likewise, by Peace, complete buddhahood," ལྷ་མོ་བདེ་མས་མཐོ་རིས་སྦྱིན། དགེ་མས་ཐར་པ་སྦྱིན་པ་སྟེ། དེ་བཞིན་ཞི་མས་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས།); `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-2` independently adopts the same reading, citing a near-identical verse attributed instead to "a mahāsiddha" (གྲུབ་ཆེན): "ལྷ་མོ་བདེ་ལས་མཐོ་རིས་སྦྱིན། དགེ་མས་ཐར་བ་སྦྱིན་པ་སྟེ། དེ་བཞིན་ཞི་མས་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས། སྦྱིན་མཛད་སྡིག་འཇོམས་བསྒོམ་པར་བྱ།" — the third line is character-identical to `anon-trinle-char`'s citation, the first two lines differ by a single particle or spelling each (བདེ་མས/བདེ་ལས; ཐར་པ/ཐར་བ), and `konchok-thabkhe`'s citation adds a fourth line absent from `anon-trinle-char`'s. Whether "Nyima Sbaspa" and "a mahāsiddha" name the same author cannot be determined from the packet — the two claims are recorded as two independent attributions of a near-identical verse, not assumed to be the same citation. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-42` gives a third, independent witness to Position 2 in its own prose (not via a quoted verse): "she who establishes beings in higher rebirth is 'Bliss,' she who establishes them in liberation is 'Virtue,' she who establishes them in omniscience is 'Peace.'" Three commentaries.

**Position 3 — the four paths** (accumulation, preparation, seeing, no-more-learning): reported only within `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-3`'s own ⚑-flagged internal divergence (see below) as the view of "other, unnamed scholars" (མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག), mapping bliss/virtue/peace/nirvāṇa onto states attained progressively across the four paths, culminating in the actualized dharmakāya. Not attested independently by any other commentary in the packet.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-3` is itself a divergence-report worth flagging separately: its own text records a two-way disagreement between "Rje btsun Grags pa" (Position 1) and "other, unnamed scholars" (Position 3, the four-path mapping) — i.e., one commentary in this corpus explicitly discusses the same style of interpretive split this topic page is synthesising, though its two named positions (1 and 3) are not identical to this page's Position 1/Position 2 axis. Re-reading `gendun-drub`'s quoted "Position 1" against `drakpa-gyaltsen`'s own claims confirms a close match: `gendun-drub`'s citation "བདེ་མ་ནི། ཟག་མེད་ཀྱི་བདེ་བ་དང་ལྡན་མ།" is character-identical to `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-78`'s own wording; the "virtuous" and "peaceful" clauses are close paraphrases of `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-79` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-80` but not verbatim matches (e.g. `gendun-drub`'s སྤང་བྱ་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་བྲལ་བས vs `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-79`'s བླང་བྱ་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་བྲལ་བས, a spang-bya/blang-bya variant).

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-7` elaborates uniquely on the "domain" clause: nirvāṇic peace, subtle and coarse, has become the goddess's own enjoyment (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ལམ་ལོངས་སྤྱོད), praising her both for being free of suffering herself and for having the power to establish others in that same state — a synthesis-level bridge between Position 1 and Position 2 not made explicit by any other single claim in the packet.

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-6` and `taranatha:c-15-4` both separately gloss the domain-of-nirvāṇa clause as "resting/abiding in the (supreme, non-abiding) peace of nirvāṇa," consistent with but not verbatim identical to each other. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-6` ("because she constantly abides in meditative equipoise in the expanse of supreme peace, the nirvāṇa beyond the two obscurations, that [expanse] itself becomes her domain") and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-5` ("she has passed beyond the sorrow of the two obscurations, and abides in equipoise on suchness...her very sphere of practice") give two further, independent domain-of-nirvāṇa glosses in the same family — consistent in substance with the karma-maitri/taranatha pair above but not verbatim identical to it or to each other.

Two commentaries share a further "benefit" elaboration, appended after the etymology proper, describing what she generates for beings rather than what the three terms mean: `taranatha:c-15-5` ("for beings, she is the Mother who generates happiness in this life and virtue for the sake of the next," དེའི་ཕྱིར་འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་ལ། ཚེ་འདིའི་བདེ་བ་དང་། ཕ་རོལ་གི་དོན་དུ་དགེ་བ་བསྐྱེད་པར་མཛད་པ་མ), `taranatha:c-15-6` ("shows the path of peace to the fortunate"), `taranatha:c-15-7` ("establishes beings in the domain of non-abiding nirvāṇa"); and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-52`, whose opening clause is near-verbatim identical to `taranatha:c-15-5` — "དེའི་ཕྱིར་འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་ལ་ཚེ་འདིའི་བདེ་བ་དང་། ཕ་རོལ་གྱི་དོན་དུ་དགེ་བ་བསྐྱེད་པར་མཛད་པ" — differing only in the case particle after ཕ་རོལ (གི vs གྱི), and continuing with a "shows the peaceful path" clause that echoes `taranatha:c-15-6` in substance though not in exact wording. This is a genuine close corroboration between `taranatha` and `tsultrim-namdak`, but it is a benefit-oriented coda distinct from either Position 1 or Position 2's etymological claim about what "Bliss/Virtue/Peace" itself *means* — it says what she *does*, not why she is *called* these names — and is recorded here rather than folded into either Position's attestation list (Rule 9).

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-4` cites an unrelated canonical sutra verse ("Commit no evil whatsoever; perfectly practice virtue; thoroughly tame one's own mind — this is the teaching of the buddhas") as a loose ethical parallel to the bliss/virtue/peace theme; no other claim in the packet cites this verse, and it does not itself gloss the homage's own etymology.

## The mantra: OṂ and SVĀHĀ destroy great sin

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries state that this homage's mantra is bookended by OṂ (at the beginning) and SVĀHĀ (at the end) and that reciting it destroys great sin, misdeeds, or their suffering-result (however each commentary specifically enumerates what is destroyed — see "Unique," below, for the enumerations):

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-7`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-82`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-15-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-43`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-18-5`, `taranatha:c-15-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-53`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-18` (16 commentaries; `anon-utpala`'s and `gendun-drub`'s attestations each span two or three adjacent claims that together state the mantra's text, its OṂ/SVĀHĀ structure, and its sin-destroying effect)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement — commentaries vary only in how fully they enumerate "great sin" (see Unique), not in whether the mantra destroys it.

### Unique
Several commentaries give a specific enumeration of what the mantra destroys, not shared verbatim by any other single claim: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-9` ("the reciter's five heinous acts and the abandonment of Dharma...attachment, aversion...heat, cold"); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-2` ("the ten root vidyā-mantras...or [the mantra] endowed with the eighteen activity-endings...destroys even the great, painful result of the five heinous crimes"); `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-43` ("the ten unwholesome deeds and the great five crimes without interval...bodily suffering from illness, spirit-affliction...mental terror such as madness, derangement, fainting"); `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-7` ("the ten non-virtues...the five acts of immediate retribution, sins that transgress the three vows, sins accumulated by body, speech and mind, and the great sin of abandoning the Dharma; furthermore...desire and hatred...and the great sufferings that are the fruit of the three lower realms"); `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-18` ("the five heinous transgressions, the ten non-virtues, the three poisons, and their resultant suffering of the lower realms").

`taranatha` gives three additional word-gloss claims on the mantra not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: `taranatha:c-15-9` (SVĀHĀ as the mantra-ending marking "peaceful" [pacifying] activity, one of a set of activity-classifying endings), `taranatha:c-15-10` (cross-referencing TRAD for repelling and PHAT for slaying, "as stated above" in its own commentary), and `taranatha:c-15-11` (noting that endings for other activities are "indicated in passing" by the same logic).

`taranatha:c-15-17` adds a doctrinal gloss not shared elsewhere: OṂ and SVĀHĀ together symbolize the "indestructible speech-sound" (གསུང་གཞོམ་མེད་ཀྱི་སྒྲ), arising infinitely. `taranatha:c-15-18` closes with "pacifying sin...accomplishes the welfare of beings — this applies at the level of the result," an explicit statement (in `taranatha`'s own voice) that this is where its word-meaning exposition is "applied to the result" — part of an internal four-part structure (word-meaning, benefit, mantra-gloss, ultimate meaning/result) that the page notes, purely as an observation about how `taranatha`'s own extraction is organized, resembles the shape of `palden-sherab`'s four-level exegesis in the "Hidden / completion-stage meaning" facet below — a structural parallel the page draws, not a claim either source makes about the other.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-82` frames the mantra's effect distinctively as an expression of dharmakāya-nature acting on *others*: "by her own nature as dharmakāya, she destroys the afflictions to be abandoned in others" — a phrasing not repeated by any other claim in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-8` describes a specific visualization practice paired with mantra recitation: sins visualized as exiting the body in the form of soot and thick black smoke while reciting the mantra and accumulating a count. No other claim in the packet describes this specific visualization (contrast the nectar-ablution visualization in "Generation-stage ablution practice," below, which is a different purification method).

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give the identical mapping of the four terms (plus "destroyer of great sins") onto the five wisdoms of buddhahood:

— attested: `taranatha:c-15-12`, `taranatha:c-15-13`, `taranatha:c-15-14`, `taranatha:c-15-15`, `taranatha:c-15-16`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1` (2 commentaries)

Re-read side by side, the five equations are character-identical in the key terms: "blissful" = discerning wisdom (སོར་རྟོག་ཡེ་ཤེས་, `taranatha:c-15-12` / `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1`), "virtuous" = mirror-like wisdom (མེ་ལོང་ཡེ་ཤེས་, `taranatha:c-15-13`), "peaceful" = wisdom of equality (མཉམ་ཉིད་ཡེ་ཤེས་, `taranatha:c-15-14`), "peace of nirvāṇa" = dharmadhātu wisdom (ཆོས་དབྱིངས་ཡེ་ཤེས་, `taranatha:c-15-15`), and "destroying great sins" = all-accomplishing wisdom (བྱ་གྲུབ་ཡེ་ཤེས་, `taranatha:c-15-16`). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1` embeds the same five equations within a broader completion-stage-without-marks frame — five wisdoms as the "self-radiance" of self-aware Tārā, self-pacifying the obscuring misdeeds of self-grasping, truth-grasping, characteristic-grasping, and dualistic grasping — elements not present in `taranatha`'s parallel claims, so the shared attestation above is limited strictly to the five term-to-wisdom equations, not to `palden-sherab`'s surrounding frame (Rule 9).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement between the readings below — each is an independent, non-overlapping elaboration rather than an incompatible reading of the same point.

### Unique
`palden-sherab` gives a four-level exegesis of this verse not paralleled by any other commentary in the packet: word-meaning as the four truths (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1`, cited above under "Etymology"), benefit of mantra recitation (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-2`, cited under "The mantra"), completion-stage-with-marks as the four joys arising from striking the vital point of the four channel-wheels (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-3-1`: "'happy,' 'virtuous,' 'peaceful,' and 'nirvāṇa's peace' are [attained when]...the four [processes] of dropping, holding, reversing, and arraying the drop in the four channel-wheels strike the vital point, [giving rise to] joy, supreme joy, joy-free-of-joy, and co-emergent joy...whose wisdom-essence is co-emergent great-bliss wisdom"), and completion-stage-without-marks as the five wisdoms (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1`, cited in Consensus above).

`tenga-tulku` reads the verse's two halves as a body/speech/mind (three-kāya) exposition, not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-3` frames the bliss/virtue/peace etymology (abandonments on the paths of seeing and meditation, cited above under "Etymology") as "the qualities of her mind, the dharmakāya" (ཐུགས་ཆོས་སྐུའི་ཡོན་ཏན); `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-4` frames the mantra's protection from the eight/sixteen fears (cited above under "The mantra"/"Fears") as "the qualities of her speech, the sambhogakāya" (གསུང་ལོངས་སྐུའི་ཡོན་ཏན), and the destruction of sin through confession before her marked body as "the quality of her body, the nirmāṇakāya" (སྐུ་ཡི་ཡོན་ཏན), concluding "thus she is praised by way of the qualities of body, speech, and mind." No other claim in the packet maps this verse onto the three kāyas.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58` is a ⚑ ambiguous-fit claim, carried through per Rule 6 rather than silently absorbed or dropped: a "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) passage reading iconographic elements from *several* homages together as the four empowerments and the two stages. It names elements from `^1-12` (Amitābha in the locks, crescent-moon crown), `^1-15` (this verse's own Bliss/Virtue/Peace/Pacified terms, read as the wisdom-empowerments), `^1-10` (garland of light as crown-ornament), and `^1-13`/`^1-16` (encircled, delighting, right-extended-left-bent) in one doctrinal synthesis spanning multiple verses. It does not belong to this slot alone, and its contribution here — reading Bliss/Virtue/Peace/Pacified as the "four wisdom-empowerments" and, taken together with "these same three," as "the fourth empowerment endowed with the three liberations" — is recorded as a single, unparalleled witness rather than folded into the five-wisdoms Consensus above (a different empowerment/wisdom scheme, not the same five-wisdoms mapping `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` share).
