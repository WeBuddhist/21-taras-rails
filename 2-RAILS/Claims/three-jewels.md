# དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། — The Three Jewels

## Questions asked

1. What root-verse text names the Three Jewels (dkon mchog gsum), and which commentaries quote it directly?
2. What is the hand-gesture (mudrā) that symbolizes the Three Jewels, and how do commentaries describe its finger positions?
3. Do commentaries agree on which fingers hold the utpala/lotus stem and which three are raised in the Three-Jewels mudrā?
4. Does any commentary describe this same mudrā recurring across visualizations of Tārā's other emanations beyond the ninth homage?
5. What deeper or "hidden" (sbas don) meanings do commentaries assign to "Three Jewels" beyond the plain iconographic gloss?
6. Do any commentaries independently converge on identifying the hidden-meaning "Three Jewels" with the three tantric substances (seminal fluid, essence, wind)?
7. How is Tārā herself characterized in relation to the Three Jewels — as their embodiment, as their worshipper, or otherwise — outside the verse-9 mudrā description?
8. Does any commentary treat the Three-Jewels mudrā as identical to Tārā's own samaya-mudrā?
9. What role do the Three Jewels play in refuge and offering ritual within a sādhana built around this praise?

## Root verse — the textual citation of the Three-Jewels homage

### Consensus
Seven commentaries quote the full four-line root-verse as the textual basis for the ninth homage, before glossing it: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི། སོར་མོས་ཐུགས་ཀར་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ། མ་ལུས་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ(ས)་བརྒྱན་པའི། རང་གི་འོད་ཀྱི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་འཁྲུགས་མ" ("Homage to her whose heart is adorned by the fingers of the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels; whose own mass of light — adorned by a wheel in every direction — is agitated").
— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-9-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32` (7 attestations, 7 commentaries)

Two further claims are routed to this same node by Signal-A tagging but do not themselves support the full four-line quotation above, so they are kept out of the attestation count rather than padding it (Rule 9):
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-2` contains only the first two lines — the mudrā/heart-adornment content — and nothing of the "wheel of every direction"/"mass of light agitated" lines.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12` shares the same opening two lines, then continues into a substantially different textual tradition: Tārā of the Khadira/Śiṃśapā forest, blue-green in body colour, whose light pacifies or agitates the eight/sixteen fears — not the "wheel of every direction" continuation quoted above. This is a real in-corpus textual variant on this homage's third and fourth lines, not a copying or extraction error, and it is not reconciled by any other claim in the packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
No claim conflicts with the Consensus attestation's own wording. Three further notes on the nine claims that touch this node, recorded without further interpretation rather than as a competing position:
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12`'s divergent third/fourth lines (above) are a genuine textual variant left unreconciled — flagged here rather than folded silently into the Consensus.
- gendun-drub's root-verse claim (cited above) is itself titled in the raw claims file "Verse 10 quoted," while the majority of commentaries that number this homage (e.g. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-1`) call it the ninth (དགུ་པ). The quoted verse text does not differ; only the ordinal label does, and the source of the discrepancy is not stated in either raw claims file.
- yama-sonam's root-verse claim (cited above) frames its citation as being "from the tantra" (རྒྱུད་ལས), whereas every other root-verse citation in this facet (dharmabhadra, gendun-drub, palden-sherab, sungrab-tulku, tenga-tulku, tenzin-dhonzang, tsultrim-namdak) presents the same lines without any such attribution, simply as the praise's own verse. This packet does not contain enough context to say whether yama-sonam treats the praise itself as a tantra or is citing a distinct tantric source that happens to share this verse.

### Unique
None beyond the two partial/variant claims noted above.

## Iconography — the mudrā's hand, fingers, and meaning

### Consensus
Commentaries that gloss the mudrā converge on two related points: (a) the raised triad of index, middle, and little fingers on Tārā's left hand is identified as the sign symbolizing the Three Jewels (Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha); and, where the same claim also describes the rest of the hand, (b) the thumb and ring finger join and hold the stem of an utpala (blue lotus) at or adorning her heart.

**Attested — both (a) raised-fingers-meaning and (b) thumb/ring-finger utpala-hold in the same claim** (10 attestations, 10 commentaries): `yama-sonam:c-3-9-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33`

**Attested — (a) raised-fingers-meaning only, no utpala-hold detail in this particular claim** (3 attestations): `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56`

**Attested — (b) utpala-hold-at-heart only, no explicit raised-fingers/Three-Jewels gloss in this particular claim** (1 attestation): `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55` (drakpa-gyaltsen states the Three-Jewels meaning of the three raised fingers separately, in `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56` above)

### ⚑ Divergences
`sungrab-tulku:c-12-4` assigns different fingers to the same gesture than every other commentary above: "Venerable Tārā's left thumb and middle finger (མཐེ་མོ་དང་གུང་མོ) hold the stem of an utpala flower, while her index, ring finger, and little finger (མཛུབ་མོ་དང་སྲིན་ལག་མཐེའུ་ཆུང) are raised together." Every other commentary in this facet pairs thumb + ring finger as the pair holding the stem, with index + middle + little as the raised triad; `sungrab-tulku` swaps the ring finger and middle finger between the two roles. No other commentary in the packet shares this assignment.

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## Recurrence of the mudrā across Tārā's twenty-one emanations

### Consensus
None — this facet is attested by a single commentary only.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed (no other commentary addresses whether the mudrā recurs beyond the ninth homage, so there is nothing to diverge from).

### Unique
`palden-sherab` describes the same left-hand Three-Jewels mudrā — thumb and finger holding an utpala stem at ear-level, its pollen-centre bearing a further emblem — as a recurring standard element of the generation-stage (bskyed rim) visualization for at least eighteen of Tārā's individual emanation-forms elaborated later in the text, each paired with the right hand's supreme-giving mudrā and each holding a distinct emblem atop the utpala (a life-vase, a drawn bow, a vajra-dagger, a blazing sword, a stūpa, a crossed vajra, a white parasol, a nectar-casket, a golden fish, a victory-banner, a pestle, and others).
— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-3` (Swift Heroine), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` (Uṣṇīṣavijayā), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-2-1` (Kurukullā), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-2-1` (Great Terrifying One), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-7-2-2` (Tummo the Invincible), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-2-1` (Unconquered by Others), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-1` (Tārā of the Śiṃśapā Forest), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-2-1` (Victorious over the Three Worlds), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-2-1` (Benefit-Accomplishing), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-2` (Victorious in Battle), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1` (Wrathful-Browed), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-2-1` (Utterly-Peaceful), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-2-1` (Destroyer of Sorcery), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-2-1` (Immeasurably Subduing), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-1` (Great Peacock), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-1` (Invincible-Uṣṇīṣa-White-Parasol), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-2-2` (Forest-Dwelling), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-2-1` (Radiant Goddess) — 18 attestations, one commentary. No other commentary in this packet extends the Three-Jewels mudrā's iconography beyond the ninth homage itself, so this breadth of attestation cannot be cross-checked against the rest of the corpus; recorded here transparently as a single-source finding (per Rule 5/6 of the consolidation skill).

## Doctrinal / hidden-meaning interpretations

### Consensus
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` and `taranatha:c-9-9` independently converge, in near-identical wording, on a completion-stage ("hidden meaning," sbas don) gloss: "symbolizing the Three Jewels" refers not to the outer gesture but to three tantric substances — seminal fluid, [female] essence-drop, and wind (ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ) — with the mudrā-fingers representing their union as one. Both claims share the identical phrase "དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ནི་ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ་སྟེ" ("symbolizing the Three Jewels is the three of seminal fluid, essence, and wind").
— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1`, `taranatha:c-9-9` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as directly conflicting positions — the further hidden-meaning readings below are additional, distinct layers rather than answers that contradict the substance-reading above or one another.

### Unique
- `yama-sonam:c-3-9-6` — assigns the mudrā's "cause" (rgyu) to the two jewels of Buddha and Dharma, and its "activity" (bya ba) to showing the meaning of the Saṅgha "carrying [the teaching] onward" (rjes su bskyang ba) — a cause/activity split not found in any other commentary in the packet. (Note: this claim block also contains an unrelated gloss of "wheel of all directions" describing Tārā's radiating light; only the cause/activity portion is cited here as bearing on the Three Jewels.)
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` — maps Tārā's own body to the Saṅgha, her speech to the sacred Dharma, and her mind to the Buddha ("her body is the Sangha, her speech the sacred Dharma, her mind the Buddha — the union of all Three Jewels"), alongside parallel three-kāya and three-root mappings — a body/speech/mind-to-Jewel correspondence not found elsewhere in the packet. (The same claim's plain gesture-description is cited separately in the Iconography facet above.)
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-4-1` — offers a further, "ultimate" (mthar thug) completion-stage-without-marks reading beyond its own `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1`: the three doors of body, speech, and mind — primordially appearance, sound, and awareness, the essence of the three vajras — "awaken as the essence of 'symbolizing the Three Jewels,'" destroying the twenty peaks of the innate transitory-collection view and becoming buddha within Samantabhadrī's expanse. This builds on but is a distinct claim from `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1`'s substance-reading, and is not paralleled by `taranatha:c-9-9` or any other commentary.

## Tārā's relationship to the Three Jewels — attributions and epithets

### Consensus
None — the seven claims below approach Tārā's relationship to the Three Jewels from seven distinct angles (an author's own opening homage, a structural heading, an etymological gloss, an epithet, two external praise-citations, and an identity-claim about the mudrā itself), and no single formulation is shared by two or more commentaries.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries characterize Tārā's relationship to the Three Jewels in opposite directions:
- `sungrab-tulku:c-12-3` glosses "homage" (phyag 'tshal) as bowing to "venerable Tārā, the Worshipper [mchod-ma] of the Three Jewels" (རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཆོད་མ) — positioning her as a devotee who venerates the Three Jewels, distinct from them.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-3` states the opposite direction: one "must regard the guru, the Venerable Mother herself, as the embodiment of the union of the Three Jewels" (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་པོ་འདུས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་དུ་བསམས་དགོས) — she is not a worshipper of the Three Jewels from outside them but is their very embodiment. (Compare `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` in the Doctrinal facet above, which extends this into a body/speech/mind correspondence.)

These are two different construals of the same question — is Tārā a devotee of the Three Jewels, or their embodiment? — drawn from two different commentaries, and are recorded here as opposing positions rather than merged.

### Unique
- `gendun-drub:c-0-2` — the commentary's own opening homage, "Homage to the noble Three Jewels" (འཕགས་པ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ), a dedicatory line preceding the commentary proper, addressed to the Three Jewels in general rather than to Tārā specifically.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-1` — the section heading itself names this homage "homage to She Who Signifies the Three Jewels" (དགུ་པ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ལ་བསྟོད་པ).
- `yama-sonam:c-3-9-7` — cites master Nyima Sbaspa's praise: "Well born from Buddha and Dharma; great, loving one who nurtures the entire Saṅgha" (སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས་ལས་ལེགས་སྐྱེས་ཤིང་། དགེ་འདུན་ཀུན་སྐྱོང་བརྩེ་ཆེན་མ) — an external attribution splitting Tārā's connection to the Three Jewels into being "born from" Buddha and Dharma while herself "nurturing" the Saṅgha (paralleling, without being identical to, this same commentary's own cause/activity gloss cited in the Doctrinal facet above).
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4` — cites Gendün Drup's praise to White Tārā, in which the left-hand utpala-mudrā of refuge-giving is described as symbolizing "the buddhas of the three times," with the mudrā's own nature further explained as showing "all Three Jewels gathered as one" (དཀོན་མཆོག་མ་ལུས་པ་འདུས་པའི་ངོ་བོར) — an attributed reading assigning the Three-Jewels meaning and the "buddhas of the three times" meaning to different elements of the same gesture.
- `taranatha:c-9-3` — states plainly that "the utpala-mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels is Tārā's samaya-mudrā" (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཨུཏྤ་ལའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ནི་སྒྲོལ་མའི་དམ་ཚིག་གི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) — identifying the Three-Jewels mudrā itself as Tārā's own samaya (commitment) mudrā, a specific identification not made by any other commentary in the packet.

## Refuge and ritual offering to the Three Jewels within the sādhana

### Consensus
None — this facet is attested by a single commentary's sādhana instructions only.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed (no other commentary in the packet provides a comparable sādhana sequence to compare against).

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak` alone traces a coherent ritual arc in which the Three Jewels recur as the practice unfolds:
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-1-0-1` — among the preliminary qualifications for the practice, the practitioner must have already "gone for refuge to the Three Jewels" (མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་སོང་), alongside receiving empowerment and training in renunciation and bodhicitta.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-1-3-2` — among the material preparations, notes that the "four-maṇḍala rite" is elsewhere explained as offering one maṇḍala to the Three Jewels in general and three to Tārā, though this tradition condenses it to a single mudrā-offering.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-2` — the refuge-taking itself is performed with a two-line verse: "Namo. To the Venerable Lady, the very embodiment of all the Jewels, I and all beings go for refuge" (དཀོན་མཆོག་ཀུན་དངོས་རྗེ་བཙུན་མར།...སྐྱབས་སུ་མཆི), taken with reverence of body, speech, and mind.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-3` — glosses the object of refuge in two tiers: the "common" (thun mong) object is the Three Jewels — Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha — and "above" that, in the secret-mantra vajrayāna, refuge is taken in Tārā as the embodiment uniting the Three Roots (guru, yidam, ḍākinī); refuge is to be taken until buddhahood is attained.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-4` — states the need and benefit of refuge: it is "the entry-gate of the Buddhist path," "the root of every path to liberation," and having taken it, one is "protected and guarded by the Three Jewels."
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-1` — at the start of the four-maṇḍala offering proper, "the general offering to the Three Jewels" (དཀོན་མཆོག་སྤྱི་ལ་འབུལ་བ) is made first, to the same field of refuge invoked earlier.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-5-1` — describes a general maṇḍala offering directed to "the Three Jewels, the precious Three Roots, and the ocean-like assembly of dharma-protectors and wealth-gods," with one's own and all beings' bodies, resources, and the universe emanated as an offering-cloud.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-6-1` — a prayer before the field of merit addresses "Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha; guru, yidam, ḍākinī; dharma-protectors, wealth-gods, treasure-lords" together as arising from "the essence of a single wisdom," requesting pacification of illness and increase of life, merit, and accomplishment.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-3` — in the self-generation sequence, immediately upon manifesting as Tārā, the practitioner invites "the Noble One's twenty-one emanations, together with an ocean-cloud of the Three Jewels" (དཀོན་མཆོག་རྒྱ་མཚོ་སྤྲིན་འཕྲོ་བ) into the sky before them.

No other commentary in this packet provides a comparable sādhana structure for this praise, so this sequence cannot be cross-checked against the rest of the corpus; recorded here transparently as a single-source finding.
