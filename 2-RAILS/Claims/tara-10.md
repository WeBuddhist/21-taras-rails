# Tārā 10 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བ་བརྗིད་པའི། / Homage to She Who Draws the Māras and the World under Power

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Where does each commentary place this verse structurally (a global ordinal "tenth", a local "third" within a crown-ornament-and-laughter sub-group, or no explicit ordinal), and how is the root verse itself quoted — in full, split across claims, or only partially?
2. What name, epithet, or classificatory label does each commentary give this homage or this specific form of Tārā?
3. How do commentaries describe the crown-ornament, her great joy, and the radiating light that opens the verse — and is this radiance explained as fulfilling the wishes of the faithful?
4. What is this Tārā's body colour, number of arms, and hand-implements, where a commentary specifies them?
5. What mantra-sound of laughter does the root verse's "TUTTĀRA" name, and which specific Māra (or other being) is subjugated by it?
6. Does any commentary state that the subjugated beings are thereby established in liberation and omniscience, or in some other resultant state?
7. Do any commentaries give a completion-stage or other "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) reading of the crown-joy, laughter, and TUTTĀRA, and if so, do their readings agree?
8. Does any commentary report benefits from reciting or recollecting this verse's Tārā, or a divergent liturgical application of it?
9. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's report of two further naming traditions for this homage (Je Conepa's "Drawing under Power the Māras and the World," and "others'" shortened "Drawing under Power the Māras")?
10. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's and Nyima Sbaspa's four-armed, crown/sword/Aśoka-branch iconography, against palden-sherab's, sangye-nyentrul's, and tsultrim-namdak's two-armed, victory-banner iconography?
11. Does any other commentary corroborate gendun-drub's "Lamp Who Dispels Sorrow" epithet, and does its own wording actually support anon-trinle-char's report that Gedun Drub follows Nyima Sbaspa's "Tārā, Dispeller of Sorrow" naming, despite the lamp/Tārā noun discrepancy?
12. Does any other commentary corroborate gendun-drub's variant mantra reading "TĀRA" (dropping the initial "tut-") and its variant target, "the eight great worldly gods," against the "TUTTĀRA...Paranirmitavaśavartin" reading shared by the rest of the corpus?
13. Does any other commentary corroborate konchok-thabkhe's report that the tīrthika world specifically, not only māras, is subjugated by this verse?
14. Does any other commentary corroborate taranatha's and palden-sherab's shared hidden-meaning scheme (crown-joy as the innate wisdom/co-emergent bliss relied upon by the mother channel at the crown; TUTTĀRA as tummo's fire; Māra as attachment/conceptuality; the world as ordinary body-speech-mind craving)?
15. Does any other commentary corroborate konchok-thabkhe's report of two named traditions — "the great Jowo" applying this line to a tīrthika-subduing liturgical rite, and "a mahāsiddha" reading it via the four joys of completion-stage samādhi?
16. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's extensive benefits-of-recitation material (the five liberations, the Jowo/Medicine-Buddha analogy, the Hundred Deities of Tushita citation, and the Legdrima citation on protection from the eight great fears) for this specific verse?
17. Does any other commentary corroborate palden-sherab's, sangye-nyentrul's, and tsultrim-namdak's shared naming of this Tārā as "Tārā Who Subjugates the World, Victorious over the Three Worlds"?
18. Does any other commentary corroborate the near-verbatim, three-way convergence (taranatha, palden-sherab, tsultrim-namdak) that subjugated beings are established in "liberation and omniscience" (ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ), against sangye-nyentrul's different phrase "your own state"?

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but mark it differently. Nine give an explicit global ordinal, "the tenth" (བཅུ་པ/བཅུ་བ) or its numeral: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-1` (marked "༼༡༠༽"), `taranatha:c-10-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-1` (via its node's own ༡༠ marker, not stated within the claim body itself). Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the third" (གསུམ་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the third member of a "praise by way of the crown-ornament and the sound of laughter" (དབུ་རྒྱན་དང་བཞད་སྒྲའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-1` (character-identical to `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-1`), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-1` (same classification but wording differs — བཞད་པའི rather than བཞད་སྒྲའི, བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི rather than བསྟོད་པ་ནི), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-1` (bare "the third," གསུམ་པ་ནི, without the classificatory phrase). `sungrab-tulku:c-13-1` also uses the "crown-ornament and sound of laughter" phrasing (དབུ་རྒྱན་དང་བཞད་སྒྲའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) but pairs it with the global numeral "(10)" rather than "the third" — a hybrid marking, as with `sungrab-tulku` at this same slot-type elsewhere in the corpus. The remaining three (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed here by claim-content match.

Seven commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines as one continuous block: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2` (marked internally with "…" between padas, as given in the packet), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-35` (7 commentaries). These quotations are close but not verbatim-identical to the root text (which itself reads "ཏུ་ཏྟྭ་ར་ཡིས" at line 3) or to one another: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-35` spell the mantra "ཏུཏྟཱ་ར" (TUTTĀRA); `sungrab-tulku:c-13-2` alone spells it "ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ" (TUTTĀRE). Line 2 also varies: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-2` drops the བ in "ཕྲེང་བ་སྤེལ་མ" (reading "ཕྲེང་སྤེལ་མ"), and `sungrab-tulku:c-13-2` adds an extra འ ("འཕྲེང་བ" for root "ཕྲེང་བ"); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-35` drops the ཏུ in line 1 ("རབ་དགའ་བ" for root "རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བ").

`drakpa-gyaltsen` gives the same four lines split across three separate claims rather than one block: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-59` (lines 1–2), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-62` (line 3), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-63` (line 4). `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-2` quotes only lines 1–2 verbatim; the remaining two lines appear in that commentary only as a paraphrastic gloss (see "Mantra of laughter and who is subjugated," below — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`), not as a verbatim continuation of the quotation. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13`, a separate extraction from the same commentary, gives a partial quotation interrupted with "…" and blended with paraphrase, not a clean four-line block.

The remaining seven commentaries give only a partial, paraphrastic, or word-by-word-glossed opening in this packet rather than a clean four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2` (mixes verse fragments with inline gloss), `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-1`, `taranatha:c-10-1` (which explicitly abbreviates with "སོགས", "etc."), and `pema-namgyal` (which does not quote the verse at all in this packet, being routed here by claim-content match).

None observed — the ordinal-marker and quotation differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad and different extraction granularity, not disagreement about which verse this is.

None beyond the structural notes above.

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name for this homage gives a name from one of several distinct traditions; this facet is a set of divergent traditions, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least three distinct naming traditions are attested, plus one purely classificatory label:

1. **"She Who Draws the Māras and the World under Power"** (བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་སྡུད་མ / དབང་དུ་མཛད་མ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-1` (section heading), corroborated within the same commentary by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-5` (etymological explanation: "through these two causes she brings all māras and the world under power, and so she is renowned by the name...") and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-8` (attributed there to "Rje Co ne pa"). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-9` additionally reports that "others" give a shortened form, "Drawing under Power the Māras" (བདུད་དབང་སྡུད་མ), dropping "and the World." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-1` gives a related but distinct short form, "She Who Draws the World Under Her Power" (འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་སྡུད་མ) — the opposite omission, dropping "māras" rather than "world." `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-1` gives the same underlying content as a classificatory label rather than a proper name: "homage by way of subduing māras and the world" (བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་དུ་མཛད་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ). `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, within its own word-gloss of "homage," separately calls her "the Subjugator of Māras" (བདུད་དབང་དུ་སྡུད་མར), omitting "world" — a fourth short variant, distinct in wording from `anon-trinle-char`'s reported "others'" variant.
2. **"Tārā Who Subjugates the World, Victorious over the Three Worlds"** (འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་དུ་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ལས་རྒྱལ་མ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-1-1` (character-identical to `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-1`), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` (near-identical: "འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་དུ་བྱེད་པ་སྒྲོལ་མ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རྒྱལ", dropping ལས་ and the article on སྒྲོལ་མ). Three commentaries.
3. **"Tārā, Dispeller of Sorrow"** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-7` reports that Nyima Sbaspa calls her "Tārā, Dispeller of Sorrow" (མྱ་ངན་སེལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) and that "Rje Dge 'dun grub also follows this [naming]." `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-5`'s own text does carry a "Dispeller of Sorrow" epithet at this verse, but the noun it uses is "སྒྲོན་མ" (lamp/torch) rather than "སྒྲོལ་མ" (Tārā) — as the packet itself flags, "a distinct epithet form, not a verbatim occurrence of her name." This partially corroborates `anon-trinle-char`'s report (the "dispeller of sorrow" theme and the specific attribution to Gedun Drub both match) while leaving the specific head-noun claim ("Tārā," not "lamp") unconfirmed by `gendun-drub`'s own wording.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-3` etymologically glosses "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself — dispelling grasping at true existence and the subtle stains of the three tantric stages of appearance/increase/attainment, cutting the net of doubt obstructing direct realization of the two truths; "འཚལ" is also read as requesting the blessing that such realization arise in one's own mindstream. No other claim in the packet parallels this etymology.

## Crown-ornament, joy, and radiating light

### Consensus
Sixteen of sixteen commentaries describe her jewelled crown-ornament as radiating or spreading a garland of light: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-4`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-10`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-60`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-61`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-32`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, `taranatha:c-10-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-35` (16 commentaries — every commentary in the packet touches this theme in some form).

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-4`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-10`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-60`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-61`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-32`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, `taranatha:c-10-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-35` (16 commentaries)

A narrower group of six explicitly frames this radiance as caused by, or coincident with, *fulfilling the wishes/hopes of the faithful* (a repeated verb-pattern རེ་བ་བསྐོང་བས/སྐོང་བས → དགའ་བ་བསྐྱེད, "by fulfilling [their] hopes, generates joy"): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-59`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-4`. Of these, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-4` share the closest phrasing (རེ་བ་བསྐོང་བས/སྐོང་བས་...་དགའ་བ་བསྐྱེད).

— wish-fulfillment attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-59`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-4` (6 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-2` frames the joy as her own mindstream "filled with utter joy" (ཐུགས་རྒྱུད་རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བས་གང་བ) rather than as joy caused by fulfilling others' wishes — a distinct framing not paralleled by the wish-fulfillment group above.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-32`, and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3` each describe her generating joy or delight *in* beings, or possessing joy *in bestowing* benefit on them, without using the specific "fulfilling wishes/hopes" phrase shared by the six above — related in theme, but not counted toward that narrower consensus per Rule 9.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-4` additionally frames the crown's radiance as "the miracle of physical, magical display" (སྐུ་རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་ཆོ་འཕྲུལ), one of "the three miracles" — a doctrinal classification not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

## Body colour, arms, and hand-implements

### Consensus
None — this facet is a two-position divergence, with only some commentaries specifying a body colour or arm-count at all.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Red, one face, four arms** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-10` ("her body colour is red like coral; she abides in dancing posture upon a seat of moon and red lotus; her first two hands are bound in the mudrā of great joy at the crown of her head"), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-11` ("her second right hand holds the sword-mudrā, her second left hand grasps a branch of the sorrowless [Aśoka] tree"), supported by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own sādhana at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-12` ("Upon lotus and moon, coral-coloured; one face, four arms; the first two bound at the crown, brandishing a sword; holding a branch of the aśoka"). One commentary (`anon-trinle-char`, internally consistent across three of its own claims plus a supporting citation).

**Red, one face, two arms, right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left holding a victory banner** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-2-1` ("red-coloured, both legs in archer's posture, one face, two hands, right in supreme-giving, left...holding at ear-level, atop an utpala's pollen-centre, a victory-banner"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-2-2` (light-rays radiate from her body and the banner), `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-1-1` ("red body"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-1-2` ("one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving mudra, left holding an utpala atop which a victory banner"). Two commentaries (`palden-sherab`, `sangye-nyentrul`) closely agree, including the shared detail of the banner set atop an utpala.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` partially corroborates this second position — "body red...hand holds a victory-banner" (སྐུ་མདོག་དམར་མོ་ཕྱག་ན་རྒྱལ་མཚན་འཛིན་པ) — but does not state an explicit arm-count, does not name a right-hand supreme-giving mudrā, and does not place the banner atop an utpala as `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul` both do. It is recorded as a partial, not full, attestation of that position.

No other commentary in the packet states an explicit body colour or arm-count for this Tārā; the remaining eleven describe only the crown-ornament and mantra (see facets above) without addressing overall body colour or number of arms.

None beyond the two positions above.

## Mantra of laughter and who is subjugated

### Consensus
Eight commentaries state, in a single claim or an adjoining pair, that her mantra-laughter (TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE, or a longer mantra form) subjugates *both* a specifically named Māra — lord of the Paranirmitavaśavartin gods, called dGa'-rab-dbang-phyug (Kāmadeva) or Dbang-phyug (Īśvara) — *and* the world or worldly beings at large:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-5` + `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-9` + `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-36` + `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-37` (8 commentaries)

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-9`–`c-4-10-10` gives the fullest mantra form of the group, the entire ten-syllable vidyā-mantra "OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ," rather than the bare laughter-syllable "TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE" cited by the others. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2` both additionally characterize the mantra as "eightfold laughter" (བཞད་པ་བརྒྱད་ཅན / བཞད་པ་བརྒྱད་ལྡན); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-2-2` gives the specific eight syllables "HA HĀ, HI HĪ, HE HAI, HO HAU," while `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2` gives a close but not identical set, "HA HĀ, HI HI, HE HE, HO HO" (simple repetition rather than diphthongs); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-36` also uses "eightfold laughter" (བཞད་པ་བརྒྱད་ལྡན) without giving the syllables. Three commentaries share the "eightfold laughter" framing, two of them with (differing) syllable lists.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-5` independently supports the same "Māra and world both subjugated" pattern with different, less specific naming — "undaunted by Māra's power...she proclaims the great sound of the vidyā-mantra syllable 'tuttāre'...this is the greatness by which she draws under power all classes of māras, chiefly the Lord of Desire (འདོད་པའི་བདག་པོ་དགའ་རབ་དབང་ཕྱུག) and the like" — naming the same figure (dGa'-rab-dbang-phyug) as the consensus group above, though as one among "all classes of māras" rather than as the sole named target. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-63` ("by that mantra's sound of laughter, she brings Māra and the world under her power") and `taranatha:c-10-6`–`c-10-7` ("even the Māra who subjugates other worlds is subjugated by you...all other worlds are also drawn under her power") support the same Māra-and-world pattern using only the generic term "Māra," without the specific Paranirmitavaśavartin/Kāmadeva naming. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4` gives a related but distinct scope — "she terrifies all hosts of māras and enemies, and overpowers...all three worlds" (generic plural māras, and "three worlds" rather than "the world").

### ⚑ Divergences
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4` gives a genuinely different mantra-reading and target from every other claim in the packet: it reads the root text's mantra syllable as "TĀRA" (dropping the initial "tut-": "སྔགས་ཀྱི་བཞད་སྒྲ་རབ་ཏུ་བཞད་པ་ཏཱ་ར་ཡིས"), and names the subjugated party as "the māras and the eight great worldly gods" ('jig rten gyi lha chen brgyad) rather than the Paranirmitavaśavartin-lord named by the consensus group. No other claim in the packet corroborates either the shortened mantra-form or the "eight great worldly gods" target.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3` names a different additional target not found elsewhere: "she subdues the māras and the tīrthika world" (བདུད་དང་མུ་སྟེགས་པའི་འཇིག་རྟེན), adding "tīrthikas" (non-Buddhist religious opponents) to the standard māras-and-world formula. No other commentary in the packet names tīrthikas at this verse.

### Unique
`sangye-nyentrul:c-11-1-3` adds a practice-instruction not paralleled elsewhere: "one meditates that the desire-realm gods are subjugated, and, through the sound of the eightfold-laughter mantra, the Paranirmitavaśavartin host is subjugated — this is the visualization sequence" (དམིགས་རིམ).

## Outcome of subjugation: liberation and omniscience

### Consensus
Three commentaries state, in near-identical phrasing, that the subjugated beings are thereby established in "liberation and omniscience" (ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ):

— attested: `taranatha:c-10-8` ("ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ལ་འགོད་དོ"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2` ("ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ལ་འགོད་པར་མཛད་མ"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-37` ("ཐར་པ་དང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པའི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་བཀོད་པར་མཛད་མ") (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement.

### Unique
`sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2` gives a related but distinct outcome-phrase — subjugated beings are established "in your own state" (ཉིད་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང), not explicitly "liberation and omniscience" — and is therefore not counted toward the consensus above per Rule 9.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give closely parallel completion-stage ("hidden meaning," སྦས་དོན) readings of this verse's crown-joy, laughter, TUTTĀRA, and Māra/world, though `palden-sherab`'s reading is delivered as one single bundled claim covering all four elements while `taranatha`'s is split across four adjacent claims:

— attested: `taranatha:c-10-9`, `taranatha:c-10-10`, `taranatha:c-10-11`, `taranatha:c-10-12`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` (2 commentaries)

Re-reading the two side by side clause by clause: `taranatha:c-10-9` ("great joy...adorning the head...is the innate [wisdom] relied upon by the mother [channel] at the crown," སྤྱི་བོར་མས་བརྟེན་པའི་ལྷན་སྐྱེས) and the opening of `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` ("her crown adorned by the majestic light-rays of great joy...is the co-emergent bliss stabilized at the crown," སྤྱི་བོར་མས་བརྟན་པའི་ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་བདེ་བ) share the near-identical phrase སྤྱི་བོར་མས་བརྟེན/བརྟན་པའི་ལྷན་སྐྱེས ("relied upon/stabilized by the mother [channel] at the crown, innate/co-emergent"). `taranatha:c-10-11` ("TUTTĀRA is 'mother of burning,' that is, by the fire of tummo burning [Māra]," གདུང་བ་མ་སྟེ་གཏུམ་མོའི་མེས) and the TUTTĀRA-clause within `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` ("TUTTĀRA is 'burning heat,' the extremely sharp mass of tummo's fire that burns," གདུང་བ་སྟེ་གཏུམ་མོའི་མེ་དཔུང་...གདུང་བའོ) likewise closely match. `taranatha:c-10-12` ("Māra — attachment and conceptuality — and the world's three [doors], body, speech, and mind, are blessed and subjugated by the wisdom of great bliss") closely parallels the closing of `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` ("Māra is one's own innate conceptual attachment; the world is [the] ordinary, contaminated craving-sensation of body, speech, and mind — bringing these under [her] power within the expanse of uncontaminated great-bliss wisdom"). The two readings diverge specifically on "laughing, greatly laughing": `taranatha:c-10-10` reads it as "the fire of tummo accompanied by sound," while `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` instead reads it as "eightfold laughter" — the drop's eight-stage forward-and-reverse movement through the four cakras — reserving the tummo-fire reading specifically for the next word, TUTTĀRA, as noted above. This is `palden-sherab`'s own elaboration, not shared by `taranatha` (per Rule 13, this difference is recorded as a divergence in how the two commentaries gloss this one pada, not flattened into the consensus).

### ⚑ Divergences
On "laughing, greatly laughing" specifically: `taranatha:c-10-10` reads it as tummo's fire itself; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` reads it instead as the eight-stage drop-movement through the four cakras ("eightfold laughter"), reserving fire-imagery for TUTTĀRA alone. No third claim in the packet addresses this specific pada at the hidden-meaning level.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-10-13` adds a closing note not paralleled in `palden-sherab`: "this is the branch of recollection" (རྗེས་དྲན་གྱི་ཡན་ལག).

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-4-1` extends the exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks": as the channel of self-arisen awareness-wisdom expands, all deluded subject-object appearance is overpowered and the appearance-existence of the three worlds dissolves into the single drop of self-awareness — hence "victorious over the three worlds." Not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the corpus.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-4` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-5` give a doctrinally distinct completion-stage reading, attributed to two named sources within konchok-thabkhe's own text: "the great Jowo" applies this line's wording to a liturgical rite for subduing tīrthikas, while "a mahāsiddha"'s verse is read as describing the culmination of the four joys of stainless samādhi, co-emergent great bliss transforming the aggregates/elements/sense-fields into deity-nature (`c-2-10-4`); `c-2-10-5` further reads the crown-ornament's light, combined with the five-buddha-families symbolism, as teaching the union of body and mind, applied to the ritual of the four empowerments. This "four joys / four empowerments" framing does not verbally overlap with `taranatha`'s or `palden-sherab`'s "co-emergent bliss at the crown / tummo-fire / eightfold-drop-movement" scheme, and is not treated as corroborating it.

## Benefits of recitation and recollection

### Consensus
None — this material is attested by a single commentary in this packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang` gives an extended, unparalleled account of the benefits of reciting or recollecting this verse's Tārā: fulfilling the hopes of the faithful generates joy in those who see her, and merely seeing her form, hearing her speech, or recollecting her wisdom-mind purifies sins and obscurations, frees one from lower rebirth, and awakens the Mahāyāna potential — described as the benefits of "liberation through hearing," "seeing," "knowing," "remembering," and "experiencing" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-4`). This is supported by a citation from Jowo (Atiśa) drawing an analogy with the Medicine Buddha, whose name alone dispels the suffering of lower rebirths (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-5`), stated to hold "in every respect" for Tārā as well (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-6`), and by a citation from the "Hundred Deities of Tushita" on the meaningfulness of seeing/hearing/recollecting her (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-7`). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-10`, in the same claim that glosses her subjugation of Māra Paranirmitavaśavartin (see "Mantra of laughter," above), further identifies "magnetizing others' minds" as her chief enlightened activity and cites the "Legdrima" on protection "from the eight great terrifying sufferings and harm from elemental spirits...the terrors of the afflictive and cognitive obscurations." No other commentary in the packet addresses recitation benefits or liturgical protection at this specific verse.
