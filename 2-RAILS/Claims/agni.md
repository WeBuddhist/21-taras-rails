## Questions asked

1. In what contexts do the commentaries name Agni among the great worldly gods who worship Tārā, and which homage-verse of the root praise do these glosses attach to?
2. Do commentaries further identify Agni's directional-guardian role (southeast), or place him among a wider set of ten directional guardians summoned into Tārā's service, distinct from the bare worshipper-list mention?
3. Does more than one commentary independently gloss Agni with the epithet "sage" (drang srong), and do any of these elevate it to "chief of sages" / "great among sages"?
4. What tantric "hidden meaning" (sbas don) identification do commentaries give for Agni, and does it converge with the elemental scheme used for the other worshipper-gods?
5. Does any external tantric citation (e.g. Cakrasaṃvara mandala material) independently corroborate Agni's directional-guardian identity?
6. Does any commentary attribute a role to Agni beyond the worshipper/guardian framing, and if so is that role substantiated within the claim's own content, or only a trailing/incomplete list-mention?
7. Which of the sixteen commentaries are silent on Agni, if any?

## Agni among the great worldly gods who worship Tārā

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries — every commentary in the packet except `taranatha` — name Agni (མེ་ལྷ) among the recurring roster of great worldly gods, alongside Indra, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Viśveśvara/Sarveśvara, who worship, praise, or rely upon Tārā. The wording is stable across commentaries, closely following or quoting the same homage-verse, e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ། ། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་མཆོད་མ། ། འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། ། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།" — "Homage to her who is worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Sarveśvara, and praised from in front by bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and hosts of yakṣas."

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-35`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` (15 commentaries)

Most of these claims go on to specify Agni's directional identity as guardian of the **southeast**: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་མེ་ལྷ" — "the southeast guardian Agni"), `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་མེ་ལྷ" — "Agni of the southeast"), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2` ("ཤར་ལྷོ་མེ་ལྷ"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་མེ་ལྷ"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ" — "the sage Agni of the southeast"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ"), `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5` ("ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ"), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3` ("ཤར་ལྷོར་མེ་ལྷ"). This directional identification is consistent across every commentary that states one — no commentary places Agni in a different direction within this facet.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` gives the fivefold worshipper-verse a wrathful colouring not found in the other fourteen claims: in its quotation, the great worldly gods (including Agni) "tremble" (འདར་) as they reverently worship the wrathful emanation Gdon-rigs-rnams-'joms-ma, rather than simply paying calm homage. This is one claim's own framing of the same underlying worshipper-list fact, not a separate consensus.

## Agni among the ten directional guardians summoned into Tārā's service

### Consensus
Two commentaries — `tenga-tulku` and `tenzin-dhonzang` — go beyond the worshipper framing above and place Agni within a fuller list of ten directional guardians (ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ / ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ) who are not merely worshippers but are summoned by Tārā to accomplish activity entrusted to them:

— attested: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` (2 commentaries)

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`: "ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ་ནི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ་པོ་སྟེ་སྟེང་དུ་ཚངས་པ། ཤར་དུ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན། ཤར་ལྷོར་མེ་ལྷ...དེའི་འཁོར་དང་བཅས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རྗེ་བཙུན་མའི་ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཏཱཾ་གྱི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་བཀུག་པར་མཛད་ཅིང་། ཅི་བཅོལ་བའི་ལས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲུབ་པ..." — "The 'earth-protectors' are the ten directional guardians: Brahmā above, Indra in the east, Agni in the southeast...these hosts, together with their retinues, are all summoned by the light rays of the syllable TĀṂ at the Venerable Lady's heart, and accomplish whatever activity is entrusted to them." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` gives the same "summoned into servitude" framing: "ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བའི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ་སྟེ་མེ་ལྷ་དང་...དེ་དག་གི་འཁོར་གྱི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་འཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པའི་བྲན་དུ་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་པའི་མ" — "She is Tārā, the mother able to summon into servitude, for accomplishing enlightened activity, the ten guardians of the earth — the fire-god [and others]." Both claims name Agni first (or near-first) among the ten and both use the same "summoned to serve/accomplish activity" framing, independently of each other's wording.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6` cites an external tantric source — notes on the Cakrasaṃvara subtle-drop body-maṇḍala from "The Entry-Point to the Ocean of Great Bliss" — that independently lists eight directional guardians including Agni in the southeast: "ཤར་ལྷོར་མེ་ལྷ" ("of the southeast — the fire-god"), alongside Indra (east), Yama (south), the water-god (west), Vaiśravaṇa (north), a rākṣasa-lord (southwest), the wind-god (northwest), and Īśāna (northeast). This citation is offered as an external doctrinal parallel for the directional-guardian scheme generally, not as a statement about Tārā's own worship; no other commentary in the packet cites this specific Cakrasaṃvara source for Agni.

## Agni's epithet as "sage" / "chief of sages" (དྲང་སྲོང)

### Consensus
Six commentaries independently attach the epithet "sage" (དྲང་སྲོང) to Agni — three using it as a bare descriptor, three elevating it to "chief of sages" or "great among sages":

**Bare "sage" descriptor** — attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5` (3 commentaries)
All three use the identical collocation "དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ" ("the sage Agni"), each within their own gloss of the southeast directional guardian: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1` "ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ་དང་།"; `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1` "ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ།"; `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5` "ཤར་ལྷོའི་དྲང་སྲོང་མེ་ལྷ་དང་།" — these three claims are also cited above (facet 1) for the bare worshipper-list content; here they are additionally cited for their shared use of the "sage" epithet, per their own distinct wording.

**Elevated to "chief of sages" / "great among sages"** — attested: `taranatha:c-6-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20` (3 commentaries)
`taranatha:c-6-4` states plainly: "མེ་ལྷ་ནི་དྲང་སྲོང་གི་གཙོ་བོའོ།" — "Agni is the chief of the sages." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20` gives the identical identification in near-mirrored word order: "དྲང་སྲོང་གི་གཙོ་བོ་མེ་ལྷ..." — "Agni, chief among sages..." `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24` gives a comparative form of the same idea: "དྲང་སྲོང་ན་ཆེ་བའི་མེ་ལྷ" — "Agni, great among sages" (this claim is also cited above in facet 1 for the bare worshipper-list content).

None observed.

## Hidden-meaning (tantric) identification of Agni with the fire element

### Consensus
Two commentaries — `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` — read Agni's presence in the worshipper-verse as also carrying a tantric "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན), converging independently on the same elemental correspondence already noted for the other four worshipper-gods in `brahma.md`:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-15`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ལྔ་ནི་འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་སྟེ། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས། མེ་ལྷ་ནི་མེ། ཚངས་པ་ནི་ཆུ། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནམ་མཁའ་འོ།" — "The hidden meaning: Indra and the other four are the five elements — Indra is earth, **Agni is fire**, Brahmā is water, Vāyu is wind, and Viśveśvara is space." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` gives the identical mapping within its own completion-stage-with-marks exegesis: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས་དང་། མེ་ལྷ་མེ་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཆུ་དང་། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་དང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ནམ་མཁའ་ཡིན་པས་ན་..." — "Indra is earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, Vāyu is wind, and Viśveśvara is space — thus the elemental winds dissolving into the drop is 'worship.'" Agni-as-fire is the one identification in this fivefold elemental scheme where the deity's own name and the assigned element already coincide in ordinary usage (མེ་ལྷ, "the fire-god," mapped to མེ, "fire") — both commentaries state it as a matter of course, without further comment on this coincidence.

None observed.

## Claims reviewed, not separately cited

- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-3` — this claim's own content is primarily the narrative of Indra and Brahmā requesting the Buddha to turn the Dharma-wheel; it ends mid-list with "...these are the requesters Indra and Brahmā; also the fire-god, and" (a sentence trailing directly into the next claim, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, which is a generic statement about worship by "the wind-god and the great mundane gods of manifold forms," not a completion of any assertion about Agni specifically). Because the claim's own content makes no substantive statement about Agni beyond naming him mid-sentence, it is not cited above per Rule 9 (full-statement support) — citing it for "Agni requesting the Dharma-wheel" would misattribute to Agni a role this claim only assigns to Indra and Brahmā by name.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-4` — reproduces the root praise's full twenty-one homage-verses as continuous recitation text, including the fivefold worshipper-verse already cited above via `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`/`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9`; this claim adds no content about Agni beyond what those two claims already attest, so it is logged here rather than cited independently.
