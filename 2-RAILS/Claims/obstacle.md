# བགེགས། — Obstacles / Obstructing Spirits (vighna)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> Unlike a registered spine-slot topic page, this packet was not assembled by
> `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map, nor by the keyword-routing script used for
> `gdon.md` and `mara.md` (`assemble_keyword_packet.py`) — no deterministic packet
> exists yet for this term. Per the task's explicit instruction, the gather step was
> done directly: `rg -n 'བགེགས'` was run across all sixteen `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/`
> files, and every hit was read in its full claim-block context and triaged by hand.
> This page therefore carries the same citation guarantee as the other two — every
> quotation below is copied character-for-character from the commentary's own raw
> claims file — but the gather step is manual rather than scripted, and is reported
> here as such rather than silently presented as deterministic.

The term བགེགས (*bgegs*, "obstacle" / "obstructing spirit," Skt. *vighna*) surfaces
41 times across 14 of the corpus's 16 raw claims files. The great majority of these
occurrences are **not** independent content: they are either (a) the closing
benefits-verse's stock phrase "བགེགས་རྣམས་མེད་ཅིང་སོ་སོར་འཇོམས་གྱུར" ("obstacles are
removed, each and every one"), already fully treated by `2-RAILS/Claims/benefits.md`
under its "Desires fulfilled and obstacles destroyed" and "The meaning of two,
three, seven" facets; or (b) a loose, generic pairing of "བགེགས" with "དགྲ" (enemy),
"གདོན" (dön-spirit), or "བདུད" (māra) as a stock two- or three-word idiom for
"dangers/hindrances in general," with no distinctive content of its own, scattered
across many different verses. These are logged in "Claims reviewed, not separately
cited," below, with `benefits.md`, `gdon.md`, or `mara.md` named where they are the
more specific home for that content.

Two facets, however, are **not** subsumed by those three pages and are consolidated
here in full: (1) a root verse (called "the fourteenth" by several commentaries'
own numbering) whose entire epithet, in at least two commentaries, names Tārā as
destroyer of a specific class — "dam-sri-bgegs," paired in one commentary with "the
nine gongpo brothers" and "the eight classes" — distinct from both gdon (verse 21)
and māra (verses 8/10); and (2) a recurring technical gloss, given independently by
four commentaries, identifying "'byung po" (bhūta) — one of the four classes said to
worship Tārā alongside vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas — specifically as "the class
of bgegs." A third, thinner facet gathers a few genuinely unique ritual/doctrinal
mentions that do not fit either of the above or the three existing topic pages.

## Questions asked

1. Does any root verse (beyond gdon.md's verse 21 and mara.md's verses 8/10) center its entire epithet on a named class of བགེགས (obstructing spirits), distinct from gdon and māra?
2. How consistently do commentaries name the malevolent beings destroyed in the "seven subterranean levels" verse — as dam-sri-bgegs, as nāga/asura/rākṣasa/preta, or only generically ("the malicious ones")?
3. Does any commentary pair "the nine gongpo brothers" (འགོང་པོ་སྤུན་དགུ) and "the eight classes" (སྡེ་བརྒྱད) with dam-sri-bgegs in this same verse, and is that pairing corroborated elsewhere?
4. In the verse praising Tārā as worshipped by bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas, how do commentaries gloss "bhūta" ('byung po) — as a class of bgegs, as a named deity (Gaṇapati), or only generically ("tshogs bdag and the like")?
5. Outside these two verses, where else does བགེགས appear across the corpus, and is any of that content distinct from what benefits.md, gdon.md, and mara.md already cover?
6. Does any commentary describe a ritual practice (e.g. a ransom/glud offering) specifically aimed at appeasing or destroying bgegs, distinct from the recitation-benefit and mantra-power material already covered?
7. Does any commentary give a doctrinal/completion-stage reading in which bgegs denotes an inner mental state, and is that reading corroborated elsewhere in the corpus?

## The "fourteenth" verse: destroyer of dam-sri-bgegs across the seven subterranean levels

### Consensus
None as a single shared statement about *who* is destroyed — see ⚑ Divergences. All
commentaries that address this root verse (its own epithet: "she who strikes the
surface of the earth with her palm and stamps with her foot, and — wrathful-browed,
with the syllable HŪM — crushes the seven levels") agree only on the verse's basic
action: light or fire from the syllable HŪM, emitted from her hands/feet or heart,
destroys malevolent beings dwelling across seven named subterranean strata (variously
enumerated: the base, the surpassing base, the baseless base, the very base, the base
of essence, the excellent base, the pure base).

### ⚑ Divergences
Commentaries diverge sharply on how they name the beings destroyed by this verse.

**Position A — generic cosmological classes (nāga, asura, rākṣasa, preta), no
mention of bgegs.** The majority position: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77` ("scattering
into fragments the nāgas, asuras, and so on"), `taranatha:c-14-6` ("crushes and
scatters ... all the malicious nāgas, asuras, rākṣasas, and pretas"),
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41` ("nāgas, asuras, rākṣasas, pretas, serpent-spirits, and the
functionaries of hell"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48` ("the strongholds of the
nāgas, asuras, rākṣasas, and pretas" — corroborating; this claim's own text does not
use the word བགེགས), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4` ("the malicious nagas, asuras, Yama
and the like"), and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` (names no specific classes at
all — "all the malevolent beings," གདུག་པ་ཅན་ཀུན).

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77`, `taranatha:c-14-6`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4`,
`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` (6 commentaries)

**Position B — "dam sri" specifically, without the word བགེགས.**
`sungrab-tulku:c-17-5`: "the malicious *dam sri* demons abiding on these seven
subterranean levels have their hearts and heads crushed."

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5` (1 commentary)

**Position C — "dam-sri-bgegs" / "bgegs-rigs," explicitly naming the class as
བགེགས.** Two commentaries, independently:

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-1` — the verse's own identifying heading, quoted
verbatim in the raw file: "བཅུ་བཞི་པ་དམ་སྲི་བགེགས་རིགས་འགོང་པོ་སྤུན་དགུ་དང་སྡེ་བརྒྱད་
གནོད་པ་འཇོམས་པའི་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་མ" — "the fourteenth, 'Wrathful-Browed
Ārya Tārā, Destroyer of the Harm of Damsi Obstructors (dam sri bgegs rigs), the
Nine Gongpo Brothers, and the Eight Classes.'"

`sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2` ("crushing the hearts and heads of the malicious dam
sri spirits"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1` ("who destroys the harm of malicious dam
sri spirits and obstructing classes [དམ་སྲི་བགེགས་རིགས་ཀྱི་གནོད་པ]"), and
`sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-3` ("the malicious dam sri spirits are destroyed and
dissolved into the expanse of dharmatā") — all describing the same verse, though
only `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1`'s own quoted phrase uses the compound "དམ་སྲི་བགེགས་རིགས"
verbatim; `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-3` name "dam sri"
alone within their own quoted Tibetan (matching Position B's vocabulary), with
"bgegs" appearing in this raw file's English gloss of `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1`
rather than as a second, independent Tibetan term.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2`,
`sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-3` (2 commentaries)

The remaining six commentaries in this packet — `gendun-drub`, `karma-maitri`,
`konchok-thabkhe`, `gendun-gyatso`, `tenga-tulku`, and `anon-trinle-char` — do not
address this verse's seven-levels content at all, under any name; see Coverage,
below.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-1` alone, in this packet, pairs "dam-sri-bgegs" with two
further named classes in the *same* verse-title — "the Nine Gongpo Brothers"
(འགོང་པོ་སྤུན་དགུ) and "the Eight Classes" (སྡེ་བརྒྱད, *sde brgyad*, the standard
eightfold class of worldly spirits) — not paralleled by `sangye-nyentrul`'s or
`sungrab-tulku`'s claims on the same verse.

## བགེགས as a technical gloss for "'byung po" (bhūta) among Tārā's worshippers

### Consensus
In the verse describing Tārā as praised by "bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and hosts of
yakṣas" (each assigned a direction and, in several commentaries, a named class-leader),
four commentaries independently gloss "'byung po" (bhūta) specifically as the class of
"bgegs" (obstructing spirits), headed by a "lord of hosts" (ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ):

— attested: `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2` ("the obstructing-spirit elementals," བགེགས་སོགས་
འབྱུང་པོ, northeast), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-2` ("'byung po' are the lords of the
retinues of the north-east, obstructing and misleading spirits," འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་...
བགེགས་དང་ལོག་འདྲེན), `taranatha:c-6-9` ("bhūtas are the 'lord of hosts' and others —
obstructors who lead [beings] astray," འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་བགེགས་དང་
ལོགས་པར་འདྲེན་པ), and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21` ("the class of obstructing
spirits headed by the lord of the bhūta hosts," འབྱུང་པོ་ཞེས་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་གཙོས་པའི་
བགེགས་ཀྱི་རིགས) (4 commentaries).

The wording is close enough across all four (bhūta = lord-of-hosts-headed bgegs-class)
to note as a genuinely shared technical gloss, not four independent coincidences.

### ⚑ Divergences
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-40`, glossing the identical line ("praised in front by bhūtas,
vetālas, gandharvas, and hosts of yakṣas"), does not use the word bgegs at all: "'byung
po' means [beings] such as Gaṇapati" (འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་ཚོགས་བདག་ལ་སོགས་པའོ) — identifying
the class by a named deity exemplar (Gaṇapati, ཚོགས་བདག) rather than by the generic
term "obstructing spirits."

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## Ritual and doctrinal mentions distinct from benefits.md, gdon.md, and mara.md

### Consensus
None — every claim below is attested by only one commentary in this packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are independent, non-overlapping mentions.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-6` describes a ransom (*glud*) practice not paralleled
elsewhere in the packet: one's own downfalls and obscurations are cast as a ransom
into the mouth of the lord of death together with an effigy-ransom for the guru and
for all beings' life-force; the claim states explicitly that "if there exist harmful
obstructing spirits [གདོན་བགེགས་ཀྱི་རིགས], they too are satisfied by it" (i.e.
propitiated/pacified by the ransom-offering) alongside settling outstanding karmic
debts.

`tenzin-dhonzang` alone in this packet uses a recurring formulaic attribution —
"this deity's chief enlightened activity is [dispelling/guarding against/pacifying]
obstacles [བགེགས]" — applied at four separate points in its own exposition, at
different verses: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-7` (destroying "the harm of obstructing
spirits and elemental beings of the upper, lower and middle realms"),
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-12` ("guarding against obstacles and hindrances"), and
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-5` ("pacifying obstacles"). No other commentary in the
packet uses this repeating "chief activity" framing for bgegs.

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-12`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-5` (1 commentary)

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-55`, in a doctrinal gloss of a different verse (16, "the
enemy's body"), reads "outer enemies" as "māras, obstructors [བགེགས], and the class of
döns" collectively, contrasted with "inner enemies" (self-grasping and grasping at
"mine"). This is a generic outer/inner enemy list rather than a distinct named class,
and is not the same verse or vocabulary as `mara.md`'s completion-stage facet (which
covers verses 8, 10, and 13 for other commentaries) — logged here as its own, thin,
data point rather than folded into that page.
