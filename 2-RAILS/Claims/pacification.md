## Questions asked

1. Where does the term ཞི་བ ("peace") occur in the root verse of the Twenty-One Homages, and how consistently is that verse quoted across commentaries?
2. How do commentaries gloss ཞི་བ as one of the six perfections named in that verse — is it consistently equated with wisdom (ཤེས་རབ), or read differently?
3. What effects does ཞི་བ / pacification produce when commentaries describe it as a benefit of recitation, mantra, or contact with Tārā?
4. Does any commentary place "peaceful" within a ritual-activity classification — a twofold peaceful/wrathful (ཞི་ཁྲོ) pairing, or a fourfold "four activities" (ལས་བཞི) scheme of pacifying/increasing/controlling/wrathful?
5. Is any one of the Twenty-One Homages identified or titled by an epithet built on ཞི་བ, and what does that epithet's exegesis contain?
6. Do any commentaries give a tantric completion-stage or view-based reading of ཞི་བ, distinct from its exoteric senses?
7. Is ཞི་བ used anywhere in the corpus in the general sense of nirvāṇa/ultimate liberation, apart from the paramita-list or epithet senses?
8. Does any commentary connect ཞི་བ or pacification to the twenty-seven enlightened activities enumerated in the Abhisamayālaṃkāra?
9. Tenga-tulku is the only commentary in the packet to name an explicit fourfold "four activities" (ལས་བཞི) scheme with Peaceful Tārā as one of the four — do any of the other fourteen consulted commentaries corroborate or even mention a fourfold tantric-activity classification for "peace"?
10. Konchok-thabkhe parses "austerity+peace" (དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ) as a single compound meaning ethical discipline, with wisdom identified with Tārā herself rather than with the word "peace" — does any other commentary read the compound this way?
11. Tenga-tulku alone glosses ཞི་བ as freedom from fault (stainlessness) across all six perfections, rather than as any one specific perfection — is this reading attested elsewhere in the packet?
12. Palden Sherab alone offers completion-stage "hidden meaning" readings of ཞི་བ (tummo's pacified torment, dharmadhātu, self-liberation) — does any other commentary in the packet read ཞི་བ this way?
13. Anon-yama-sonam's own commentary reports an exegetical split among earlier, unnamed commentaries over whether "peace" or "domain" (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ) carries the wisdom-sense — can either side of that reported split be matched to a specific named commentary elsewhere in this packet?

## Root-verse attestation

### Consensus
Four commentaries quote, in the course of their own exegesis, the same root verse enumerating Tārā's domain of practice (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ) as six items — generosity, diligence, austerity, peace, patience, and meditative concentration — with "peace" (ཞི་བ) named as the fourth:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2` (4 commentaries)

dharmabhadra's citation reads: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་གསེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི།། པདྨས་ཕྱག་ནི་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ།། སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ།། བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།།" (`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`). The substance is identical across all four attestations; two spell the opening syllable གསེར་སྔོ (dharmabhadra, palden-sherab: "golden-blue") against the other two's སེར་སྔོ (yama-sonam, tenzin-dhonzang: "yellow-blue") — a minor orthographic variant across transmissions, not a doctrinal divergence.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None.

## Etymology: "peace" as one of the six perfections

### Consensus
The majority reading equates "peace" (ཞི་བ) with the sixth of the standard six pāramitās, wisdom (ཤེས་རབ) — the verse's term "peace" is read as this text's own word for the perfection ordinarily called "wisdom":

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `taranatha:c-3-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2` (7 commentaries)

The stated rationale varies even within this shared equation: dharmabhadra glosses it as "the wisdom that pacifies the darkness of delusion" (རྨོངས་པའི་མུན་པ་ཞི་བའི་ཤེས་རབ), while yama-sonam instead reasons that "'peace' [means] wisdom" because peace "becomes the root of attaining the peace of nirvana" (ཞི་བ་མྱང་འདས་ཐོབ་པའི་རྩ་བར་གྱུར་པས་ན་ཞི་བའམ་ཤེས་རབ). taranatha, lobsang-dawa, sangye-nyentrul, pema-namgyal, and palden-sherab state the equation in passing (e.g. taranatha: "ཞི་བ་སྟེ་ཤེས་རབ་") without separately arguing for it.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position 1 — "peace" glossed as the general pacification of afflictions/discordant factors, without being equated with wisdom specifically:**
gendun-drub states plainly: "ཞི་བ་ཞེས་པ། མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་སེར་སྣ། ལེ་ལོ། འཆལ་ཚུལ། ཁོང་ཁྲོ། རྣམ་གཡེང་། ལོག་པའི་ལྟ་བ་སོགས་ཞི་བ་ནི། སྒྲོན་མའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཏེ" — "'Peaceful' [means]: pacifying the discordant factors — miserliness, laziness, poor discipline, anger, distraction, wrong views, and so on — is the domain of the Torch-Bearer [Tārā]" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-5`). drakpa-gyaltsen gives the same reading in miniature: "ཞི་བ་ ནི་ 8 ཉོན་མོངས་པ།" — "'Peace' is [the pacification of] afflictions" (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-18`). gendun-gyatso similarly states that Tārā "དེ་ཡངཉོན་མོངས་པ་ཞི་བར་བྱེད་པའོ།" — "moreover, is the one who pacifies the afflictions" (`gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`), without naming wisdom.

Position attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-18`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2` (3 commentaries)

**Position 2 — "austerity+peace" read as a single compound meaning ethical discipline, with wisdom identified with Tārā herself rather than with the word "peace":**
konchok-thabkhe identifies Tārā as the perfection of wisdom itself, with the *field* of her conduct built from five items rather than six, pairing austerity and peace into one: "སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་།དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་སྟེ་ཐབས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ལྔས་...བསྟེན་པའི་གནས་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་མ་ཉིད་ལའོ།" — "generosity, diligence, the *peace of austerity* — that is, discipline, patience, and meditative concentration...the perfection of wisdom herself, the basis relied upon" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`). This is a structurally different parse of the verse from every attestation above: "austerity" and "peace" are not two separate items each mapping to a distinct perfection, but one construct meaning discipline.

**Position 3 — "peace" glossed as freedom from fault (stainlessness) across all six perfections, rather than as any one specific perfection:**
tenga-tulku reads the same word as describing Tārā's freedom from the characteristic fault of each of the six perfections in turn, rather than naming a sixth perfection: "8 ཞི་བ་ཞེས་པས་དངོས་བསྟན་གྱི་དབང་དུ་བྱས་ན། ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་སྐྱོན་གྱི་རྣམ་པས་མ་གོས་པ་སྟེ...ཤེས་རབ་ལ་ཤེས་འཆལ་གྱི་སྐྱོན་གྱིས་མ་གོས་པར།" — "taking 'peace' to refer directly [to this]: unstained by any fault regarding the six perfections...unstained by faulty knowledge in wisdom" (`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`).

**Position 4 — a self-reported exegetical split, documented within a single claim:**
konchok-thabkhe's own commentary records a disagreement it attributes to earlier, unnamed predecessors, and states which side it itself follows:
- earlier commentaries (unnamed): "འགྲེལ་བ་སྔ་མ་རྣམས་ལས་འགའ་ཞིག་གིས་ཞི་བ་དང་། འགའ་ཞིག་གིས་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཞེས་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་སྦྱར་ཀྱང་" — "some earlier commentaries connect 'peace,' and others 'field of conduct,' with the perfection of wisdom" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-4`);
- this commentary, following Nyima Sepa: "འདིར་གྲུབ་ཆེན་གྱིས ཤེས་རབ་བདག་ཉིད་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ལྔ། ། ཡོངས་རྫོགས་འཕགས་མ་ཆོ་ག་བཞིན།" — identifying wisdom itself with the completeness of the five perfections (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-4`).

Because both sides of this reported split come from the same claim ID, both are cited to `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-4` rather than split across two attestations.

A related but non-adjudicating observation: yama-sonam's own commentary independently notes that the tradition it draws on is divided on this same question, without itself resolving it or naming which commentaries hold which view: "བཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅེས་གསུངས་པ་ནི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཀྱིས་ཉོན་མོངས་ཞི་བའི་དོན་དུ་འགྲེལ་པ་དུ་མས་བཀྲལ་ཞིང་། ཞི་བའི་དོན་ལ་འགའ་ཞིག་གིས་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ལ་སྦྱར་བ་དང་། གཞན་དག་ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུའི་མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་ཞི་བའི་དོན་དུ་བྱས་ནས་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་གྱིས་ཤེས་རབ་བསྟན་པར་གསུངས་སོ།" — "'austerity' is explained by many commentaries as meaning that afflictions are pacified by discipline; on the meaning of 'peace,' some apply it to the perfection of wisdom, while others take it to mean the pacifying of what is discordant with the ten perfections, explaining that 'domain' thereby teaches wisdom" (`yama-sonam:c-3-3-8`). Because the commentaries this claim describes are not named, this cannot be matched to a specific position above (see Question 13) and is logged here as its own independent testimony rather than folded into either side.

### Unique
None beyond the divergence positions above.

## Pacification as effect: benefits of recitation, mantra, and contact

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently state, in near-identical terms, that reciting this homage-praise with faith utterly pacifies the misdeeds/causes of lower rebirth and destroys their resultant suffering:

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-1-1` (2 commentaries)

gendun-gyatso: "ངན་འགྲོའི་རྒྱུ་སྡའི་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་བ་སྟེ་ཟད་པར་འགྱུར་ཞིང་། འབྲས་བུ་ངན་འགྲོའི་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཐམས་ཅད་འཇོམས་པ་ཉིད་མཛད་དོ།།" — "all causes of lower rebirth are utterly pacified, that is, exhausted; and [Tārā] destroys entirely the resultant suffering of the lower realms" (`gendun-gyatso:c-2-3`). palden-sherab: "སྡིག་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རབ་ཞི་བྱེད། ངན་འགྲོ་ཐམས་ཅད་འཇོམས་པ་ཉིད་དོ།" — "[this praise] utterly pacifies all misdeeds, and destroys all lower rebirths" (`palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-1-1`).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the remaining commentaries below describe different specific things being pacified (illness, poison, obstacles, conceptual thought) rather than disputing the lower-rebirth benefit above.

### Unique
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-21-2` — the light of three syllables placed at three places on her body is said to have "the power to pacify afflictions" (ཉོན་མོངས་ཞི་བའི་མཐུ).

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-5` — glossing "austerity" (not "peace" itself) as moral discipline, by which "afflictions are pacified" (ཉོན་མོངས་པ་ཞི་བར་བྱེད་པ).

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-6-2` — meditating on OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ at the three places (body, speech, mind) is said to pacify harm to those three doors (སྒོ་གསུམ་ཉེར་འཚེ་ཞི་བའི་མཐུ).

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-2` — her light-rays are said to pacify, "without exception," all outer, inner, and secret obstacles to the three doors.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49` — the mantra OṂ TARA TARA PHAṬ (or OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE PHAṬ SVĀHĀ) is said to pacify pre-existing poison-afflictions such as rabies and snakebite, and to prevent their future occurrence.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-1-13` — the mere touch of her body is said to pacify "all subtle and coarse conceptual thought" (ཕྲ་རགས་རྣམ་རྟོག་ཀུན...ཞི་ནས) into the state of bliss-emptiness.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-10` — a closing dedication prays that, by the power of this praise, illness, spirit-harm, poverty, and conflict be pacified wherever the practitioner dwells (ནད་གདོན་དབུལ་ཕོངས་འཐབ་རྩོད་ཞི་བ).

## "Peaceful" as a ritual-activity category

### Consensus
Three commentaries situate "peaceful" within a twofold ཞི་ཁྲོ (peaceful/wrathful) structural pairing — dividing the homages, their root mantras, or recitation practice into "peaceful" and "wrathful" halves:

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-11` (3 commentaries)

gendun-drub divides "praise by bodily forms" into "praise by peaceful forms and praise by wrathful forms" (ཞི་བའི་རྣམ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་དང་། ཁྲོ་མོའི་རྣམ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-1`) and, separately, opens its treatment of "praise by activity" with "the activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras" (ཞི་ཁྲོའི་སྔགས་ཀྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས) (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-1`). karma-maitri prescribes reciting "fiercely in the evening and peacefully at dawn" (སྲོད་ལ་དྲག་པོ་དང་ཐོ་རངས་ཞི་བ) (`karma-maitri:c-1-2-4`). tenzin-dhonzang states that the twenty-one verses of praise arise from two root mantras, "the root mantra of peaceful Tārā" and "the root mantra of wrathful Tārā" (སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞི་བའི་རྩ་བའི་སྔགས...སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྲོ་མོའི་རྩ་བའི་སྔགས) (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-11`).

### ⚑ Divergences
tenga-tulku is the only commentary in the packet to name an explicit **fourfold** activity scheme rather than the twofold ཞི་ཁྲོ pairing above: "༡ ཞི་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཀར་མོ། ལས་བཞིའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཕྱག་མཚན་མི་འདྲ་བས།" — "1: Peaceful Tārā, white. The Tārās of the *four activities* [ལས་བཞི] have differing hand-emblems: right hand in the refuge-granting mudrā, left holding an utpala; for the rest, the right hand is always the supreme-granting mudrā" (`tenga-tulku:c-z-2`). No other commentary consulted in this packet names a fourfold "four activities" (ལས་བཞི) scheme (pacifying/increasing/controlling/wrathful) for "peaceful" — see Question 9. This single attestation is the corpus's only direct link between ཞི་བ and the specific doctrinal category named in this topic's brief, and is the reason this topic is flagged as thinly attested.

### Unique
`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-6-8` — a nuanced middle case, neither clearly twofold nor fourfold: "ཞབས་ཞི་བའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་ཡིན་ཀྱང་མེ་འབར་བའི་དབུས་ན་བཞུགས་པ་ཅུང་ཟད་ཁྲོ་ཚུལ་ཙམ་མོ།" — "although the posture of her feet is that of a peaceful [deity], her abiding in the midst of blazing fire is merely a slight wrathful manner" — an iconographic observation that a single figure's appearance can blend "peaceful" and "wrathful" register rather than falling cleanly into either category.

## Epithet: the homage named for "Peace"

### Consensus
Three commentaries each identify one homage among the Twenty-One by an epithet built on ཞི་བ, glossing it as the pacification of misdeeds and/or obscurations. Each numbers this homage differently within its own outline (per Rule 1, node numbering is never assumed uniform across commentaries):

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-1-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3` (3 commentaries)

palden-sherab (his fifteenth): "བཅོ་ལྔ་པ་སྡིག་སྒྲིབ་ཞི་བར་མཛད་པའི་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་རབ་ཞི་མ་ནི།" — "the fifteenth, 'Ārya Tārā the Utterly Peaceful, Who Pacifies Misdeeds and Obscurations'" (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-1`). sangye-nyentrul (his sixteenth) glosses the same class of epithet as "Peaceful, Virtuous, Pacified" (`sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1`) and separately names "this 'Tārā Who Pacifies Misdeeds and Obscurations'" as white-bodied (`sangye-nyentrul:c-16-1-1`). tenzin-dhonzang gives a plain section heading, "homage to Great Peace" (ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ), which appears at two different points in his own outline — once numbered "fifteenth" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-1`) and once "second" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-1`) — plus a word-gloss of the homage's opening "ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ" describing Tārā's radiant face (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3`). This double heading is reported as-is rather than resolved; it may reflect two different levels of tenzin-dhonzang's own outline rather than a contradiction.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-6` — a related but distinct epithet, not tied to a specific homage node: "འདི་ལ་དགེ་ལེགས་ཞི་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་འབྱུང་ངོ་།" — "she is called here 'Tārā of Virtuous, Peaceful Excellence.'"

## Tantric completion-stage readings of "peace"

### Consensus
None — every claim on this facet comes from a single commentary; see Unique below.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-3-1` — in a "hidden meaning, completion-stage-with-marks" reading of an earlier homage, tummo-fire is described as causing bodhicitta to descend and melt, "pacifying the fire's torment" (མེའི་གདུང་བ་ཞི་བ), and dissolving the knots of the afflicted channels.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-3-2` — in the same interpretive register applied to homage 21, "peace" is read as "the clear, non-grasping...primordial-purity empty great clear light" (ཞི་བ་ནི་གསལ་ལ་འཛིན་པ་མེད་པ...འོད་གསལ་ཆེན་པོ) — a Dzogchen/clear-light gloss, not a moral or paramita sense.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2` — continuing the same passage, "peaceful, endowed with power" (ཞི་བ་མཐུ་དང་ལྡན་མ) is glossed as the self-liberation of the deluded appearance of the two obscurations, "like ice melting into water."

No other commentary consulted in this packet offers a comparable completion-stage or view-based reading of ཞི་བ — see Question 12.

## "Peace" as nirvāṇa / ultimate liberation

### Consensus
Apart from the paramita-list sense (see "Etymology," above) and the epithet sense (see "Epithet," above), two commentaries also use ཞི་བ in the general Mahāyāna sense of nirvāṇa or liberation from saṃsāric suffering:

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-52` (2 commentaries)

konchok-thabkhe states she is "supreme in conduct, victorious over the boundless directions of both saṃsāra and peace [nirvāṇa]" (སྲིད་པ་དང་ཞི་བའི་ཕྱོགས་མཐའ་ཡས་པ) (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-3`). tsultrim-namdak states that she "shows the peaceful path of sacred Dharma" and "ultimately establishes [beings] in" nirvāṇa, having "manifestly realized the great peace of dharmatā" (ཆོས་ཉིད་ཞི་བ་ཆེན་པོ...ཞི་བ་དམ་པའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་ལམ) (`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-52`).

## "Peace" within the Abhisamayālaṃkāra's twenty-seven activities

### Consensus

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-1-3-1` — describes Tārā's overall manner of liberating beings as proceeding through "twenty-seven activities, ranging from establishing beings in the activity of pacification [ཞི་བའི་ལས] up to establishing them in nirvāṇa" — naming "the activity of pacification" as the first of the twenty-seven.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1` — in extensive word-gloss of the "Utterly Peaceful" homage (see "Epithet," above), breaks its four component terms into the four truths (happy=result, virtuous=cause, peaceful=path, nirvāṇa's-peace=cessation/dharmakāya), then states that "in extensive division, the Jetsünma's very domain of activity is the twenty-seven activities taught in the Abhisamayālaṃkāra."
