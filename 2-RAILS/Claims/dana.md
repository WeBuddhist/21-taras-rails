# Dāna — སྦྱིན་པ། (Generosity, the First Pāramitā)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather method note.** No Signal-A packet-assembly script exists for this
> topic (it is not one of the 21 registered Tārā-verse spine slots). Per this
> topic's own task instructions, the packet was gathered manually: `rg -n
> "སྦྱིན་པ"` against all sixteen `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files,
> followed by `Read` of each hit's full claim block (heading through `Cite:`
> line), copied character-for-character — never retyped. Every one of the
> sixteen commentaries returned at least one genuine hit on dāna as a
> doctrinal/pāramitā category; none is silent on this topic.
>
> **Scope note — three unrelated senses of སྦྱིན་པ in this corpus.** The
> search surfaced 51 raw hits, of which 33 concern dāna the pāramitā (this
> page's subject). The remaining 18 use སྦྱིན་པ in one of two unrelated senses
> and are excluded, listed under "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below
> rather than silently dropped: (a) མཆོག་སྦྱིན(་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) — "supreme-giving"
> or *varada* mudrā, an iconographic hand-gesture name, no pāramitā content;
> (b) the generic verb སྦྱིན་པ/སྦྱིན་པར་མཛད་པ ("to grant/bestow"), used of
> Tārā's own activity — granting fearlessness, blessing, cooling, siddhis,
> wishes, torma-offerings — with no doctrinal-category content. Sibling
> pāramitā topics (`vīrya`, `kṣānti`, `dhyāna`) and the pāramitā category page
> itself (`2-RAILS/Claims/paramita.md`, not yet consolidated at time of
> writing) are expected to cite some of the same list-claims independently;
> this page does not defer to them and cites what it needs directly, per this
> batch's own instructions.

## Questions asked

1. How does each commentary treat the root verse's mention of སྦྱིན་པ (dāna) as part of Tārā's own domain of practice (spyod yul), and how many commentaries quote the verse verbatim versus paraphrase it?
2. How many pāramitās does each commentary count in this list, and how does dāna's position within that count vary — six (standard), ten (extended), five-plus-basis, or subsumed into a larger set such as the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment?
3. Does any commentary give an explicit lexical/definitional gloss of the term སྦྱིན་པ itself, or classify it into subtypes (e.g. material giving, protection from fear, Dharma-giving)?
4. Which commentaries treat dāna specifically as the antidote (གཉེན་པོ) to miserliness (སེར་སྣ), and do they cite a canonical source for this pairing?
5. Do any commentaries offer a tantric/completion-stage or ultimate (དོན་དམ) reading of dāna beyond the ordinary sense of material generosity?
6. Do any commentaries cite external canonical or exegetical sources (sūtra, Abhidharmakośa, Lamrim literature) specifically on dāna's function or benefit, independent of the root verse?
7. Is any commentary silent on སྦྱིན་པ as a doctrinal/pāramitā category entirely, as opposed to using སྦྱིན་པ only in an unrelated iconographic or verbal sense?

## Root verse: dāna named first among the pāramitās that are Tārā's own "domain of practice" (spyod yul)

### Consensus

**Verbatim quotation.** Seven commentaries quote the same root verse line
character-for-character (minor orthographic variation only — ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི vs
ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱིས, པདྨས་ཕྱག་ན vs པདྨས་ཕྱག་ནི): "སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། །
བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" ("[whose] very domain of practice is
generosity, diligence, austerity, peace, patience, and meditative
concentration") — homage 3 of the twenty-one, addressed to the golden-blue,
lotus-handed Tārā.

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`,
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10` (7 commentaries)

**Paraphrase/gloss.** A further twelve claims, across eleven additional
commentaries, gloss the same list without quoting the verse verbatim, each
naming dāna first among the pāramitās constituting this Tārā's own domain of
practice:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-5`,
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`,
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`,
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `taranatha:c-3-4`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11`,
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2` (12 claims, 11 commentaries — palden-sherab
overlaps with the verbatim group above via a second, distinct claim)

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2` both draw a further
inference from the same list: because she practises all six pāramitās, she is
addressed as "Bodhicitta Lady" (byang chub sems ma) and, in pema-namgyal's
gloss, "Daughter of the Victors" (rgyal ba'i sras mo).

### ⚑ Divergences

Every commentary above agrees dāna is named first in this list. Several,
however, diverge on how many pāramitās the list ultimately comprises or how
it is classified:

- **Ten pāramitās** (extending the six named terms by further subdividing
  "peace" into wisdom, or by adding method/power/aspiration/wisdom as a
  second tier): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4` (elsewhere in the same
  commentary, the "ten perfections" listed explicitly as generosity,
  discipline, patience, diligence, meditative absorption, wisdom, method,
  power, aspiration, and wisdom, tied to the ten bodhisattva bhūmis),
  `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` (similarly ties a ten-fold list — generosity,
  ethics, patience, diligence, meditative stability, wisdom, skillful
  means, aspiration, power, wisdom — to attainment of the ten grounds). The
  paraphrase claim already cited above under "Root verse" Consensus,
  yama-sonam's claim on the same node (see that section), likewise
  states "she has made the ten perfections her domain" — the same
  ten-count position, not re-cited here to avoid a duplicate citation
  within one facet.
- **Five pāramitās, with wisdom held out as the basis rather than counted as
  a list member**: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3` identifies Tārā herself with the
  perfection of wisdom, and describes generosity, diligence, "the peace of
  austerity" (discipline), patience, and meditative concentration as the
  *field of conduct* of that wisdom — five pāramitās named, with wisdom
  standing apart as their basis rather than a sixth member of the same list.
- **Subsumed into the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment**:
  `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`, glossing a differently-numbered verse in the same
  commentary's appendix praise-cycle, names generosity, diligence,
  austerity/discipline, peace/wisdom, patience, and meditative concentration
  as among "the thirty-seven factors on the side of enlightenment"
  (byang phyogs kyi chos sum cu rtsa bdun) rather than as a discrete
  six-or-ten pāramitā set — the only commentary in the packet to fold the
  pāramitā list into this broader Abhidharma category.

No commentary states these framings as directly contradicting one another
(none says "not ten" or "not five"); they are recorded as divergent
classifications of the same underlying list rather than as a resolved
disagreement.

### Unique

None beyond the divergent classifications above.

## Definitional gloss and classification of dāna

### Unique

- **Tautological word-gloss** — `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-15` glosses the term
  directly: "སྦྱིན་པ་ ནི་ སྦྱིན་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའོ" ("'Generosity' is the
  perfection of generosity") — the only claim in the packet that glosses the
  term as such, independent of any broader list context.
- **Threefold classification** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-6` is the only claim
  in the packet to subdivide dāna itself: "སྦྱིན་པ་ལ་ཟང་ཟིང་གི་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་།
  མི་འཇིགས་པ་སྐྱབས་ཀྱི་སྦྱིན་པ། ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་གསུམ" ("generosity comprises
  three: the giving of material goods, the giving of protection from fear,
  and the giving of Dharma"), part of the same claim's parallel threefold/
  fivefold subdivisions of all six pāramitās.

### Consensus

None — only one commentary each addresses the tautological gloss and the
threefold classification; no second commentary independently states either.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

## Dāna as the antidote (གཉེན་པོ) to miserliness (སེར་སྣ)

### Consensus

Three commentaries independently identify dāna as the specific antidote to
the fault of miserliness, within an antidote-structured reading of all six
pāramitās:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` (3 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-6-5` states it most directly: "སེར་སྣའི་གཉེན་པོ་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་།"
("generosity [is] the antidote to miserliness"), continuing the same
antidote-pairing through all six pāramitās (diligence/laziness,
discipline/poor conduct, wisdom/delusion, patience/anger,
concentration/distraction). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` reaches the same
pairing from a different angle — reading "peace" (ཞི་བ) in the root verse as
Tārā's being unstained by fault with respect to each of the six, starting
"སྦྱིན་པའི་སྐབས་སུ་སེར་སྣའི་སྐྱོན་གྱིས་མ་གོས་པ" ("in the case of generosity, [she
is] unstained by the fault of miserliness"). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` gives
the fullest version, citing the *Abhidharmakośa* itself for the discipline
pairing while stating the generosity/miserliness pairing directly:
"སྦྱིན་པའི་དགྲ་སེར་སྣ" ("miserliness is the enemy of generosity").

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — all three commentaries agree on this specific pairing.

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above.

## Tantric and "ultimate" readings of dāna beyond ordinary generosity

### Unique

- **Hidden meaning: abandoning self-grasping** — `taranatha:c-3-7` reads
  dāna, within a "hidden meaning" (sbas don) layer of the same root verse, as
  "བདག་འཛིན་བཏང་བའི་སྦྱིན་པ" ("the generosity that abandons self-grasping"),
  paired with a non-ordinary diligence and patience in the same reading.
- **Completion-stage: freedom from self-grasping regarding bliss is dāna** —
  `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-2`, mapping the six pāramitās onto a
  completion-stage visualization, states "བདེ་བ་ལ་བདག་འཛིན་བྲལ་བའི་སྦྱིན་པ་སྟེ"
  ("freedom from self-grasping with respect to bliss is generosity").
- **Canonical sūtra quotation: non-grasping is dāna** —
  `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2` quotes the *Sūtra of the Questions of
  Brahmaviśeṣacintin* for the ultimate, non-referential form of each
  pāramitā: "མི་འཛིན་པ་ནི་སྦྱིན་པའོ" ("non-grasping is generosity"), continuing
  through the other five pāramitās in the same non-referential register.

### Consensus

None — taranatha's "abandoning self-grasping" and palden-sherab's two
distinct claims (a completion-stage gloss and a canonical sūtra quotation)
converge on a similar theme (non-grasping/non-self-clinging as dāna's
ultimate meaning) but are not the same statement and are not corroborating
citations of one another — each is recorded as its own unique claim per Rule
10, rather than pooled into a false consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — these are complementary, not competing, elaborations.

## Scriptural citations on dāna's function and benefit

### Unique

Both claims in this facet are unique to a single commentary; no other
commentary in the packet independently cites external canonical or Lamrim
literature specifically on dāna.

- **Extensive Prātimokṣa Sūtra, on merit** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-74` quotes:
  "སྦྱིན་པས་བསོད་ནམས་རབ་ཏུ་འཕེལ།" ("through generosity, merit greatly
  increases"), continuing through discipline (deterring enemies), virtue
  (abandoning sin), and exhaustion of afflictions (passing beyond sorrow).
- **Condensed Points of the Lamrim, on miserliness** —
  `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-7` quotes: "སྦྱིན་པ་འགྲོ་བའི་རེ་སྐོང་ཡིད་བཞིན་ནོར། །
  སེར་སྣའི་མདུད་པ་གཅོད་པའི་མཚོན་ཆའི་མཆོག" ("generosity is the wish-fulfilling
  jewel that fulfills beings' hopes, the supreme weapon that cuts the knot of
  miserliness"), continuing into a parallel verse on discipline.

### Consensus

None — both citations are unique to tenzin-dhonzang.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.
