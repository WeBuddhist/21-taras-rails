## Questions asked

1. Does each commentary name gandharva among the fourfold host (bhūta, vetāla, gandharva, yakṣa) who bow/praise Tārā, and in what wording?
2. How does each commentary identify or explain what a gandharva actually is — retinue of Indra, exemplified by a named chief, or an intermediate-state being?
3. drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, karma-maitri, gendun-gyatso, and tsultrim-namdak identify the gandharvas as headed/exemplified by "the five-crested one" (ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་པ / Pañcaśikha) — do other commentaries corroborate, contest, or name a different exemplar?
4. dharmabhadra, lobsang-dawa, sungrab-tulku, palden-sherab, and sangye-nyentrul identify gandharvas as the retinue of Indra (བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར) — is this corroborated elsewhere, and is it stated as compatible with the "five-crested one" reading?
5. taranatha names a different gandharva exemplar (rendered in its own English gloss as Viśvāvasu) — is this corroborated or contradicted by any other commentary's naming?
6. konchok-thabkhe reads "gandharva" via the Abhidharma as a synonym for an intermediate-state being, paired with Yama as its "lord" — does any other commentary corroborate or contest this reading?
7. Does tenga-tulku's description of gandharvas as "wandering the intermediate state" echo konchok-thabkhe's Abhidharma-based reading, and if so, how closely?
8. Does any commentary give a tantric or completion-stage symbolic equivalence for gandharva, and do multiple commentaries converge on the same equivalence?
9. Do palden-sherab's and taranatha's completion-stage equations of gandharva with wind (རླུང) also agree on the mappings of the other three classes in the same fourfold list (bhūta, vetāla, yakṣa)?
10. Does any commentary attribute a specific protective benefit to prostration with respect to gandharva-kings?
11. Does any commentary situate gandharva within a broader taxonomy of harmful spirits outside the fourfold-host verse?

## Textual attestation — gandharva among the fourfold host who praise Tārā

### Consensus
The verse describing Tārā's worship by the great mundane gods (Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Īśvara/Maheśvara) also names hosts of bhūta, vetāla, gandharva, and yakṣa as beings who bow and praise her "from in front":

འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། །གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།
("...praised in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas.")

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-11`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4` (12 commentaries)

Note: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3` fills this slot with the phrase "དྲི་ཟའི་བདག་པོ་གཤིན་རྗེ" ("Yama, lord of gandharvas") rather than a bare "gandharva" — the same structural position in the fourfold list, but a distinct textual/etymological reading; see the Identity facet below, where this same commentary's fuller etymology is given at `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-6`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on whether gandharva belongs in this fourfold list — only on what a gandharva *is* (see Identity facet).

### Unique

## Identity and etymology of gandharva

### Consensus
None. Every commentary that goes beyond bare textual attestation (facet above) to explain what a gandharva actually *is* gives one of several further identifications; the commentaries do not restate one another's specific identification verbatim, so no single shared position is recorded here — see the Positions below for where several commentaries do converge on the same broad reading.

### ⚑ Divergences

**Position 1 — gandharva as the retinue of Indra (བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར):**
- `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" ("the gandharvas, retinue of Indra").
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས" ("the gandharvas — the retinue of Indra").
- `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" ("the gandharvas, retinue of Indra").
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-2`: "དྲི་ཟ་ནི་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཤིང་རྟ་སོགས་རྣམས" ("'gandharvas' are the various chariot-[riding] retinue of Indra, lord of gods, of the east").
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`: "ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སུ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས" ("Indra's retinue of gandharvas [in the] east").

(5 commentaries.)

**Position 2 — gandharva headed/exemplified by "the five-crested one" (ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་པ, Pañcaśikha):**
- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-42`: "དྲི་ཟ་ནི་ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་པ་ལ་སོགས་པའོ།" ("'Gandharvas' means [beings] such as the five-crested ones.")
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "དྲི་ཟ་ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས" ("the five-crested gandharvas and others").
- `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-11`: "ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་སོགས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས" ("gandharvas, such as the 'five-crested ones'").
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`: "ཟུར་བུད་ལྔ་བ་ལ་སོགས་བའི་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས" ("the gandharvas headed by Zur-bu-lnga-ba [the five-crested one]").
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22`: "དྲི་ཟ་དྲི་ཟའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཟུར་ཕུད་ལྔ་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས" ("the gandharvas by the gandharva-king Pañcaśikha [the five-crested one] and others").

(5 commentaries.)

Positions 1 and 2 are not stated as contradicting one another by their own commentaries — a being-class serving as Indra's retinue could in principle be headed by a named chief such as Pañcaśikha (a figure who, in the wider Indic tradition Position 2's translators are drawing on, is himself counted as one of Indra's own court musicians) — but no commentary in this packet states this harmonization itself.

**Position 3 — a different named exemplar, Viśvāvasu (per its own English gloss), not paralleled elsewhere in the packet:**
- `taranatha:c-6-11`: "དྲི་ཟ་ནི་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཤིང་རྟ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས་དང་།" ("Gandharvas are Viśvāvasu and others.")

No other commentary in this packet names this particular figure as the gandharvas' exemplar; the claim's own naming stands apart from both the retinue-of-Indra framing (Position 1) and the five-crested-one framing (Position 2).

**Position 4 — konchok-thabkhe's reinterpretation: gandharva as a technical synonym for "intermediate-state being," paired with Yama as its lord, drawn from the Abhidharma:**
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`: root phrase reads "དྲི་ཟའི་བདག་པོ་གཤིན་རྗེ" ("Yama, lord of gandharvas"), not a bare "gandharva."
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-6`: "དྲི་ཟ་ཞེས་པ་བར་དོ་བའི་མིང་གི་རྣམ་གྲངས་སུ་ཆོས་མངོན་པ་ལས་གསུངས་པ་དང་། ཤི་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་གཤིན་པུ་རྗེ་ཞེས་པ་ཡང་བར་དོ་ལ་དབང་བསྒྱུར་བ་ལ་བྱེད་དགོས་པས་དྲི་ཟ་ཞེས་པ་གཤིན་རྗེ་ལ་སྦྱར་ནས་བཀོད་པ་ཡིན་པས་དཔྱད་བར་བྱའོ།" ("'gandharva' is stated in the Abhidharma as a synonym for 'intermediate-state being,' and since 'Lord of the Dead of all the deceased' [Yama] must also govern the intermediate state, 'gandharva' has here been paired with Yama — a point the author flags for further examination.")

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4` independently describes the gandharvas as "wandering the intermediate state" (སྲིད་པ་བར་དོར་འཁྱམས་པའི་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས), without citing the Abhidharma or pairing the term with Yama. This is recorded as a partial corroboration of konchok-thabkhe's specific association of gandharva with the intermediate state (see the facet below), not as full agreement with konchok-thabkhe's own etymological chain.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Corroboration check — gandharva and the intermediate state (bar do)

### Consensus
None — konchok-thabkhe's claims give the fuller, Abhidharma-cited chain; tenga-tulku's claim gives only the descriptive element, independently phrased, in a different doctrinal context (a passage on why spirit-classes attend Tārā, not an etymological aside).

### ⚑ Divergences
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-6` states, citing the Abhidharma, that "gandharva" is a technical synonym for "intermediate-state being," and on that basis pairs the term with Yama as the being who governs the intermediate state — an etymological/technical claim, flagged by the commentary's own author as needing further examination. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4` independently calls the gandharvas beings "wandering the intermediate state" (སྲིད་པ་བར་དོར་འཁྱམས་པའི་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས) in the course of describing them as harboring harmful intent toward others — a descriptive characterization that touches the same association (gandharva ↔ bar do) without konchok-thabkhe's Abhidharma citation or Yama-pairing. The page treats this as a partial, independent echo rather than full corroboration, since the two claims differ in how far they take the association and in the doctrinal work it is made to do.

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4` (2 commentaries)

None beyond the above.

## Tantric completion-stage symbolism: gandharva as wind (རླུང)

### Consensus
Two commentaries give an identical fourfold completion-stage ("hidden meaning") mapping of the same fourfold host onto inner elements, agreeing precisely on gandharva = wind:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`, `taranatha:c-6-17` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ་དང་། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་དང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" ("'bhūtas' are the channels, 'vetālas' are the drop, 'gandharvas' are the wind, 'yakṣas' are conceptual thought").
`taranatha:c-6-17`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" ("bhūta is the channel; vetāla is the drop; gandharva is the wind; yakṣa is conceptual thought").

Re-read together, the two claims give the same four equivalences in the same order (channel / drop / wind / thought) — a genuine, word-for-word corroboration, not merely two commentaries independently choosing "wind" for gandharva while differing on the rest.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Benefit: protection from the fear of gandharva-kings through prostration

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-25` states that prostrating to Tārā, before whom spirits, cannibal-demons, gandharva-kings (naming as exemplar "the horse-headed one," རྟ་མགོ་ཅན — a third distinct named exemplar, alongside Positions 2 and 3 above, not paralleled by any other claim in this packet), and yakṣas tremble and bow, frees one "from the fear of those spirits in this life and in all lives." No other commentary in this packet ties a specific gandharva-related protective benefit to the act of prostration.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.

## Gandharva within a broader taxonomy of harmful spirits (outside the fourfold-host verse)

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-6`, quoting the White Parasol (Sitatapātra) dhāraṇī rather than the root praise itself, lists gandharva-harm (དྲི་ཟའི་[གདོན]) as one of twenty-eight named classes of harmful spirit (alongside deva-, nāga-, asura-, garuḍa-, kinnara-, yakṣa-, rākṣasa-, and preta-harms, among others) from which the practitioner seeks protection. This is a distinct context from the fourfold-host verse's praise-scene: here gandharva appears as a potential source of harm within a separate cited text, not as a worshipper of Tārā. No other commentary's claims in this packet situate gandharva within this broader taxonomy.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.
