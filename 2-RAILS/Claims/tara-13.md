# Tārā 13 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར། / Homage to She Who Abides Amid the Blazing Garland of the Final Aeon's Fire

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but mark it in three different ways. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the thirteenth" (བཅུ་གསུམ་པ) or its numeral: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-1` (marked "༼༡༣༽"), `taranatha:c-13-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-1`. Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the sixth" (དྲུག་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the sixth member of a "praise by way of her wrathful manner of abiding" (དྲག་པོའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-1` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-1` share a character-identical opening clause, དྲུག་པ་དྲག་པོའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི། `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-1` uses related but non-identical wording (དྲུག་པ་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་དེ་ཉིད་ཁྲོ་མོར་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི།, substituting ཁྲོ་མོར་ for དྲག་པོའི་ and a different final verb); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-1` gives only the bare ordinal, དྲུག་པ་ནི། with no descriptive clause. `sungrab-tulku:c-16-1` shares the "wrathful manner of abiding" phrasing (དྲག་པོའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) with the local-ordinal group but pairs it with the global numeral "(13)" rather than "the sixth" — a hybrid of both marking conventions. The remaining four (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match (`sangye-nyentrul`'s node title carries the numeral ༡༣, but that numeral does not appear inside the claim text itself).

Nine commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines verbatim (continuously or, in `drakpa-gyaltsen`'s extraction, split across three adjacent claims):

— full verse quoted attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-72`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-73`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-74`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-44` (9 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-2`'s own claim title labels its quotation "Verse 14" even though the spine map routes it here as verse 13's content — an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's own extraction, not evidence gendun-drub disagrees about which verse this is (the same quirk appeared for gendun-drub's tara-09 page).

The remaining seven commentaries give only a paraphrase or partial quotation in this packet: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1`, `karma-maitri` (its claims gloss individual phrases rather than quoting the verse as a block), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2` (quotes a mahāsiddha's and the Jowo's *own* verses, then paraphrases the root verse), `lobsang-dawa` (its "Verse 29"/"Verse 30" citations reword the line, e.g. inserting ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་ not present in the root), `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-1` (free paraphrase), `taranatha:c-13-2` (explicitly abbreviates with "སོགས", "etc."), and `pema-namgyal` (does not quote the verse at all in this packet, routed by claim-content match). `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` additionally gives a second, partial citation (lines 1–2 only, then an ellipsis into a name and iconographic gloss) alongside its full quotation at `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-44`.

### Textual note
`yama-sonam:c-3-13-7` reports a philological correction: the tantra's phrase ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་དགའི (line 3) means "her retinue gathered around her" (བསྐོར་ = the retinue circling toward her), not "turning the wheel of Dharma outward", nor "encircled by her own light rays", nor "manifold bodily display" — and states that "many commentaries" explain it along those other, rejected lines, contradicting Nyima Sbaspa's own text. `yama-sonam:c-3-13-10` and `yama-sonam:c-3-13-11` further report a specific wording discrepancy in this same line: the Dergé Kangyur Tantra volume *Ca* reads དགའ (dga') as a verb-forming particle attached to "joy," while the Dergé Tengyur volume *Na* (Nyisbepa's own text) instead gives a genitive-connective reading, "of the joyful one" — and states that "most commentators follow the latter."

The verse-quotation claims actually copied into other commentaries' own extractions in this packet show exactly this kind of variation at that word (re-checked character-by-character against the packet's བོད་ཡིག after an initial mis-sort): a bare, unsuffixed དགའ in `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-2` ("...ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་དགའ།...") and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2` ("...ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་དགའ།...") only; the genitive འི (དགའི) everywhere else it can be checked — `yama-sonam:c-3-13-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-44` all read "...ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་དགའི...". This spread of readings is consistent with yama-sonam's report of a genuine textual variance and, at 6-against-2, matches its own claim that "most commentators follow" the genitive reading — though no other commentary in the packet itself discusses the variance as such — only yama-sonam names it as a discrepancy.

### Unique
The textual-note claims above (`yama-sonam:c-3-13-7`, `yama-sonam:c-3-13-10`, `yama-sonam:c-3-13-11`) are unique to that commentary; no other commentary in the packet raises a philological question about this verse's wording.

## Name, epithet, and classification

### Consensus
None — as with the structural sa-bcad labels above, every proper name or classificatory epithet supplied for this homage differs commentary to commentary; this facet is a set of divergent naming traditions.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least five distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"Tārā Who [Fully/Thoroughly] Ripens"** (སྨིན་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ / ཡོངས་སུ་སྨིན་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `yama-sonam:c-3-13-1` uses this in its own section heading ("thirteenth homage, to the Venerable Lady Tārā Who Ripens") and `yama-sonam:c-3-13-12` explicitly attributes the fuller form to Nyima Sbaspa, adding "Rje Dge 'dun grub also follows this" (རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ་ཀྱང་དེའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲངས). This attribution is independently confirmed by `gendun-drub`'s own raw claim `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-4`, which states in its own voice, "she is called here 'Tārā Who Brings to Maturity'" (སྨིན་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — the same core epithet yama-sonam reports for him, re-read and confirmed as a genuine match rather than assumed. Two commentaries, one of them independently corroborating the other's attribution report.
2. **"Blazing Like Fire"** (མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བ） — `yama-sonam:c-3-13-12` attributes this name to Co ne pa (ཅོ་ནེས་མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-1` independently uses the near-identical phrase as its own section heading, "homage to Blazing Like Fire" (བཅུ་གསུམ་པ་མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བ་ལ་བསྟོད་པ) — since tenzin-dhonzang's own claim does not name Co ne pa or cite yama-sonam's report, this is an independent convergence on the same epithet rather than a confirmed citation of the same source, but the wording match (མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བ) is exact.
3. **"[Jetsün] Tārā Victorious in Battle over War and Other Obstacles, Whether with Form or Formless"** (དམག་སོགས་བར་ཆད་གཟུགས་ཅན་གཟུགས་མེད་ཀྱི་གཡུལ་ལས་རྒྱལ་བར་བྱེད་པའི་རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-1`, echoed in abbreviated form by `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-1` ("this 'Tārā Victorious over the Battle and Obstacles of the Form and Formless [Realms]'"). Two commentaries.
4. **"Sgrol-ma dgra-dpung-'joms-ma" ("Destroyer of Enemy Armies")** — `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16`. Unique to this commentary; not paired with either of the above naming traditions.
5. **Functional/thematic classification rather than a proper name** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-1` labels the verse "homage by way of subduing the malicious" (གདུག་པ་ཅན་འདུལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ); `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-1` instead classify it as "praise by way of her wrathful manner of abiding" (already cited above under Structural placement).

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims in this packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3` additionally glosses the word "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself etymologically — "one may apply it in whatever way accords with one's own practice" — a brief, devotionally-framed remark rather than the fuller body/speech-clinging etymology tenzin-dhonzang gave for a different homage elsewhere in the corpus; not paralleled by any other claim in this packet.

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16`'s naming (item 4 above) and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3`'s etymological remark are each unique to their own commentary.

## Iconography — the blazing garland, the stance, and its symbolism

### Consensus
Beyond the direct verse quotations already listed under Structural placement, most commentaries independently restate or gloss two elements of the verse's imagery: the blazing, aeon-ending fire/garland, and the right-extended/left-bent stance.

**The fire/garland imagery** is glossed or restated (beyond a bare verse quotation) in: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-72`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-38`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-3`, `taranatha:c-13-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-45` (18 attestations across 16 commentaries — every commentary in the packet touches this image at least once, whether by direct quotation or gloss).

**The stance (right leg extended, left leg bent)** is glossed or restated in: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-5` (with a supporting citation from Nyima Sbaspa at `yama-sonam:c-3-13-6`), `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-73`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-4`, `taranatha:c-13-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-45`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` (11 commentaries).

### ⚑ Divergences
Only `yama-sonam:c-3-13-5` (supported by `yama-sonam:c-3-13-6`) assigns a specific symbolic function to *each* leg individually: the extended right leg symbolizes that her faithful, devoted retinue is "matured on the excellent path," while the bent left leg symbolizes her fierce destruction of those who harm her devotees. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-73` notes only that this stance is "reversed from the earlier one" (i.e. from an earlier homage's stance), a comparative-structural remark rather than a symbolic reading of the two legs individually. No other commentary in the packet offers a leg-by-leg symbolic gloss, so this is not treated as a corroborated position — it is `yama-sonam`'s own reading alone. (`yama-sonam:c-3-13-5`, `yama-sonam:c-3-13-6`, and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-73` are each also cited above under Consensus, for the separate, uncontested fact that they restate the stance at all — that mention and this claim's own specific symbolic/comparative gloss are two different statements about the same claim, not a claim placed on both sides of one disagreement.)

### Unique
`taranatha:c-13-4` adds a nuance not paralleled elsewhere: her joy is characterized as arising from "inner, undefiled joy" (ནང་ཟག་མེད་ཀྱི་དགའ་བ) from which she does not move — an unusual doctrinal qualifier ("undefiled") on the verse's plain "joy" not found in any other claim in this packet.

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-6` restates the verse's own fourth line ("she thoroughly destroys all the armies of enemies") without further gloss; this and similarly generic restatements are discussed under "The nature of the 'enemy'" below.

## Iconography — body form: colour, faces, arms, and implements

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently agree, in matching near-verbatim phrasing, on a two-armed, three-eyed, peaceful-wrathful, red-bodied form: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-1` ("Victorious in Battle, red-coloured [མདོག་དམར་མོ], both legs in archer's posture, one face, two hands, three eyes, her face at once peaceful and wrathful, right hand in supreme-giving") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-1` (red body, སྐུ་མདོག་དམར་མོ) + `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-2` ("one face, two hands, three eyes, an expression mixing peaceful and wrathful, right in supreme-giving mudra"). Re-reading both claims side by side confirms the match is genuine, not merely thematic: both share the sequence ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས། སྤྱན་གསུམ། ཞལ་ཞི་མ་ཁྲོ...(མཆོག་སྦྱིན) verbatim, differing only in the compound qualifying "peaceful-wrathful" ("ཞི་མ་ཁྲོའི་ཞལ་ཅན" vs "ཞི་མ་ཁྲོ་བོའི་ཉམས་ཅན") and, outside the quoted sequence, in how the leg-posture is worded (palden-sherab's "ཞབས་གཉིས་རྟ་འབབས་སྐྱིལ་ཀྲུང་" vs sangye-nyentrul's "ཞབས་ཕྱེད་སྐྱིལ").

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-2` (2 commentaries)

Both further agree that the left hand holds an utpala, atop which is a vajra with upturned horns (རྡོ་རྗེ་རྭ་གདེངས་པ), from which sparking vajra light-rays (རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཚྭ་ཚྭ) destroy enemy hosts, the light then converging into a protective "vajra tent-canopy of horns" (རྡོ་རྗེའི་རྭ་གུར): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-3`. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` partially corroborates this specific detail — it independently uses the same term "vajra sparks" (རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཚྭ་ཚྭ) radiating from a "raised vajra" to destroy enemies, and its own English gloss further describes the sparks "converging into a vajra-fence" that surrounds and protects the faithful — the same converging-into-an-enclosure motif as palden-sherab's and sangye-nyentrul's "vajra tent-canopy of horns." But because the claim's Tibetan for this specific convergence falls inside the packet's ellipsis-bounded excerpt, the exact term it uses cannot be checked against རྡོ་རྗེའི་རྭ་གུར, so it is counted as a partial, not a third full, attestation of that specific element.

### ⚑ Divergences
Against this two-armed reading, `yama-sonam` independently describes a **four-armed** red form holding weapon-implements rather than mudrās: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-8` ("her body colour is red...one face and four arms...her first right hand holds a sword, the second an arrow; her first left hand holds a wheel, the second a bow"), supported by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own verse at `yama-sonam:c-3-13-9` ("one face, four arms...sword, arrow, and wheel, bow"). One commentary (internally consistent across two of its own claims, one a supporting citation).

Both traditions agree the body is **red** — `yama-sonam:c-3-13-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-1`, and (partially) `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` all independently state a red body colour — but disagree on the number of arms (four vs. two) and on whether she holds a set of four weapons (sword/arrow/wheel/bow) or displays supreme-giving/utpala-with-vajra mudrās. (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-1`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` are each also cited above under Consensus, for the two-armed iconography and vajra-sparks detail respectively; this line cites the same claims only for the narrower, uncontested point that both traditions agree on red body colour — not as a second side of either divergence.)

No other commentary in the packet states an explicit face-count, arm-count, or implement-set for this Tārā; the remaining twelve (`dharmabhadra`, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-drub`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `konchok-thabkhe`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `sungrab-tulku`, `taranatha`, `tenga-tulku`, `tenzin-dhonzang`) describe only the fire-garland and stance (see above) without addressing detailed body form.

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## The nature of the "enemy" destroyed

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is a genuine multi-way divergence over what the verse's "enemy army" (དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང) refers to.

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **Literal external harmers** (human and non-human): `taranatha:c-13-6` — "she thoroughly destroys the enemy — that is, the harm-doing forces of humans and non-humans" (མི་དང་མི་མ་ཡིན་པའི་གནོད་བྱེད). One commentary.
2. **Explicitly glossed as afflictions (ཉོན་མོངས་པ) obstructing the disciples' path**: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3` ("she utterly destroys the afflictions — the enemy-army — of the disciples who delight in circumambulating the Dharma-wheel," དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་པའོ) and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-4` ("she utterly destroys the hosts of the afflictive enemy [ཉོན་མོངས་ཀྱི་དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང་] that obstruct their path to liberation"). Two commentaries, both explicitly using ཉོན་མོངས་ (afflictions) as the gloss.
3. **The two obscurations, or confused self/other grasping, manifesting in the guise of human and non-human harmers**: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-1-2` ("she utterly destroys the two obscurations [སྒྲིབ་གཉིས] of one's own deluded mind's appearance — appearing in the form of human and non-human harmers and enemy hosts") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-2` ("you utterly destroy the army-hosts of the enemy — the confused cognition [འཁྲུལ་ཤེས] grasping at self and other, both the form-possessing and the formless aspects of one's own and others' [deluded] appearance"). Two commentaries — the same pair that shares the two-armed, three-eyed iconography and the "Victorious in Battle" naming tradition above, suggesting a single commentarial lineage rather than two independent witnesses.
4. **A shared formula locating the enemy as "outer and inner enemies within the mindstream(s) of the disciples/those to be tamed"** (གདུལ་བྱའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཕྱི་ནང་གི་དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང), without further specifying its content: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-4`. Re-reading all four confirms the phrase is shared near-verbatim across all four claims. Four commentaries.
5. **A compound definition combining afflictions, negative karma, and literal human/non-human harmers together**: `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-46` ("'the enemy host' [means] the host of afflictions, non-virtuous negative karma, and the obstructing and harming forces of humans and non-humans" — ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་སྡིག་པ་མི་དགེ་བ་དང་། མི་དང་མི་མ་ཡིན་པའི་བར་ཆད). This bridges positions 1 and 2 rather than matching either alone; it is not counted toward either position's attestation list.

Three further claims restate the verse's destruction-of-enemies content too generically to place in any of the positions above: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-74` ("she destroys all discordant factors," མི་མཐུན་པ་ཐམས་ཅད), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-6` ("she thoroughly destroys all the armies of enemies"), and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-3` (a tentative, self-flagged proposal — འགྲེལ་དགོས་སམ་སྙམ་མོ, "should this be explained thus? — I think so" — that the fire-light "ripens devoted disciples while... burning hostile forces"; reported here as a tentative gloss, not an assertion, per its own epistemic marker).

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently offer a completion-stage-with-marks reading that converges on two specific points, confirmed by re-reading both claims side by side:

- **The "fire of the final aeon" is the wisdom-fire of tummo/caṇḍālī**: `taranatha:c-13-7` ("the hidden meaning: the fire of tummo-wisdom [གཏུམ་མོ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ] is like the blazing fire at the end of time") and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-1` ("'like the fire of the final aeon' is the expanse's own extremely hot essence-form, the wisdom-fire, [i.e.] Caṇḍālī's short-A," ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་ཨ་ཤད་ཙཎྜ་ལཱིའོ) — both name the same completion-stage referent (gtum mo / caṇḍālī) for the verse's fire.
- **"Right extended, left bent" is glossed via the rasanā/lalanā channels**: `taranatha:c-13-8` ("'right extended, left bent' is the right and left channels, rasanā and lalanā, or [alternatively] the upward life-force wind and downward-voiding wind") and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-1`–`palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-2` (the moon in rasanā descends from the crown's HAṂ = "right extended"; the sun in lalanā descends from the secret place's A = "left bent"). taranatha offers this as one of two alternative readings ("or..."), while palden-sherab commits to it unconditionally and elaborates it in far greater detail (specific syllables and locations) — the specific claim that both attest, "right/left = rasanā/lalanā," is fully supported by both, but palden-sherab's elaboration goes further than taranatha's.

— attested: `taranatha:c-13-7`, `taranatha:c-13-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-2` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the additional readings below are independent, non-overlapping elaborations rather than incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-13-9`–`taranatha:c-13-11` continue the completion-stage-with-marks reading with material not paralleled in `palden-sherab`: the five elements dissolving in each channel-wheel, manifold displays of playful manifestation arising everywhere, and "joy" as the winds becoming the drop of bliss, thereby destroying the enemy-hosts of Māra and the afflictions.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-4-1` extends the exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks": the fire is self-arisen awareness-emptiness burning all clinging to deluded appearance; the stance is skillful method/compassion united with emptiness-wisdom as transference-free great bliss; and "destroying the enemy host" is the shattering of dualistic grasping across the outer (earth-stone), inner (illusory body), and secret (confused thought) levels — a reading not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the corpus for this verse.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58` is flagged ⚑ ambiguous by the packet itself: it is a "hidden meaning" passage that reads iconographic elements from *several* homages together (^1-12's Amitābha-in-the-locks, ^1-15's Bliss/Virtue/Peace/Pacified, ^1-10's light-garland crown-ornament, and ^1-13/^1-16's "encircled, delighting, right-extended-left-bent") as the four empowerments and the generation/completion stages — it does not belong to any single verse. Insofar as it touches ^1-13, it reads "delighting, encircled on all sides" and "right leg extended, left bent, delighting, encircled on all sides" as symbolizing "the generation-stage aspect of principal-and-retinue and the completion-stage aspect of the channels and winds of sun, moon, and so forth" — broadly consonant with, but too general to specifically corroborate, taranatha's and palden-sherab's rasanā/lalanā readings above. Carried through here with its ambiguity flag rather than silently dropped or silently absorbed as a clean attestation, per the packet's own routing note.

## Practice, visualization, and activity

### Consensus
None as a single shared practice — two distinct visualization traditions are attested, non-overlapping in content, each associated with more than one claim from a single commentary rather than shared across commentaries.

### ⚑ Divergences
Not a doctrinal disagreement, but two genuinely different practice traditions attach to this verse:

1. **Generation-stage vajra-fence visualization**: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-2-2` (visualizing the iconography above, then reciting the vidyā-mantra) and `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-1-3` ("one meditates that light rays of vajra sparks blaze... destroying the enemy's forces without remainder; the light then gathers back... within the vajra-fence enclosure"). Both belong to the same "Victorious in Battle" commentarial pairing already noted above.
2. **Purification-style visualization**: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-5` (external enemies specified concretely as intoxicants, poisons, weapons, and distracting games such as dice, archery, and sport; inner enemy as sin and downfall, visualized exiting as smoke) and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-6` (illness and spirit-harm visualized exiting as excrement, pus, blood, and poisonous creatures through every pore, leaving the body "clear, luminous, and light-natured"). Unique to `tenzin-dhonzang`; its specific catalogue of "external enemies" (dice, archery, sport) is not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

### Unique
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-5` attributes an additional benefit not stated in the root verse itself: she "creates joy, bestowing bliss" (བདེ་བ་སྦྱིན་པའི་དགའ་བར་མཛད). `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-39` attributes a devotional benefit: fierce postures notwithstanding, "if one prays to the venerable lady without any doubt, she instantly, utterly destroys" even a four-division army of enemies. Neither benefit is paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-12`'s final clause characterizes "this goddess's chief enlightened activity" as "guarding against obstacles and hindrances" (བགེགས་དང་བར་ཆད་སྲུང་བར་མཛད). Only this final clause of the claim is cited here as support for that characterization; the claim's preceding content (an account of the Buddha turning the dharma-wheel at various holy sites) is homiletic digression, not itself about this verse's content, and is dispositioned separately below.
