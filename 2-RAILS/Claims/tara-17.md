# Tārā 17 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུ་རེའི་ཞབས་ནི་བརྡབས་པས། / Homage to Ture, Who by the Stamping of Her Feet Shakes the Three Worlds

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Where does each commentary place this verse structurally (a global ordinal "seventeenth", a local "second" within a wrathful-activity/hand-emblem sub-group, or a functional classification with no ordinal), and how is the root verse itself quoted — in full, split across claims, or only partially?
2. What name or epithet, if any, does each commentary give this homage or this specific form of Tārā, and how do the several naming traditions attested in the packet relate to one another?
3. How does each commentary explain the meaning of "Ture," and does any commentary depart from the "Swift One" etymology?
4. What causes the three worlds/mountains to shake — the stamping of her feet, the light rays radiating from the HŪṂ seed-syllable at her heart, or both together — and do any commentaries treat this as a live disagreement?
5. What does "the three worlds" (འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ) refer to in this verse — the named mountains themselves, the netherworld/earth-surface/heaven-above triad, or the desire/form/formless realms — and do commentaries disagree?
6. Which mountains are named as being shaken, and are there textual variants in how the third mountain (Vindhya/Vibhedaka) or its identity is read?
7. What is this Tārā's body colour, number of arms, posture, and hand-implements, and do commentaries diverge on the mudrās she displays?
8. Do any commentaries give a hidden/completion-stage meaning for this verse, and if so, do their readings agree?
9. Does any commentary report a specific liturgical or practical application of this verse?
10. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's report that some commentaries locate the shaking's cause in feet-stamping while others locate it in HŪṂ's light rays — is this a real, wider divergence or local to anon-trinle-char's own cited sources?
11. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's second internal tension — that some commentaries read the HŪṂ-seed as identifying her wrathful nature while Nyima Sbaspa reads it instead as the cause by which she moves Mount Meru?
12. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's explicit rejection of the netherworld/earth/heaven reading of "three worlds," given that this reading is otherwise widely attested (lobsang-dawa, taranatha, sungrab-tulku, anon-utpala, pema-namgyal, tenzin-dhonzang, tenga-tulku)?
13. Does any other commentary corroborate drakpa-gyaltsen's reading of "three worlds" as the desire, form, and formless realms, and how does this relate to anon-trinle-char's citation of the Catuḥśataka commentary for the same triad?
14. Does any other commentary corroborate taranatha's near-verbatim completion-stage-with-marks reading of Meru, the two flanking mountains, the shaking, the three worlds, and the HŪṂ-seed, shared with palden-sherab?
15. Does any other commentary corroborate palden-sherab's, sangye-nyentrul's, and tsultrim-namdak's shared iconography — right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding an utpala topped by a stūpa — against anon-trinle-char's description of both hands holding a moon-disc?
16. Does any other commentary corroborate pema-namgyal's departure from the "Ture = Swift One" etymology, identifying Ture instead as Tārā's own body?
17. Does any other commentary corroborate palden-sherab's and sangye-nyentrul's shared naming tradition ("Immeasurably Subduing … Who Binds the Mouths of Enemies, Bandits, Thieves, and Hunters"), and how does it relate to anon-trinle-char's "Blissful Lady Who Binds Thieves" and tsultrim-namdak's abbreviated "Subduer of the Immeasurable"?
18. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's and sungrab-tulku's shared epithet "Shaker of the Three Worlds" applied directly to Tārā?

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but they mark it in different ways. Nine give an explicit global ordinal, "the seventeenth" (བཅུ་བདུན་པ) or its numeral (༡༧/17): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-1` (via its node's own ༡༧ marker), `sungrab-tulku:c-20-1` (marked "༼༡༧༽"), `taranatha:c-17-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-1` (9 commentaries). Four instead give only a *local* ordinal, "the second" (གཉིས་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the second member of a "praise by way of wrathful activity that shakes the three grounds/realms" sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-1`. The remaining three (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match, not by a visible section number.

Several commentaries fold a functional or epithet-style classification into the same opening clause as their ordinal, rather than using a bare numeral: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-1` ("the seventeenth, homage by way of protecting from the fear of enemies," དགྲའི་འཇིགས་པ་བསྲུང་བའི་སྒོ་ནས), `sungrab-tulku:c-20-1` ("(17) praise by way of the wrathful activity of shaking the three grounds," ཁྲོ་མོའི་ས་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས), and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-1` ("seventeenth, homage to She Who Shakes the Three Worlds," འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་བ་མ་ལ). The four "second"-marked commentaries use closely related "wrathful activity of shaking the three grounds/realms" phrasing for their sub-group label: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-1` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-1` are close paraphrases of each other (ཁྲོ་མོས་ས་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི vs ཁྲོ་མོས་ཁམས་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ནི), while `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-1` phrases it differently again ("praises by way of subduing the three realms through her wrathful stance," ཁྲོ་མོའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱིས་ཁམས་གསུམ་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-1` gives only the bare ordinal "གཉིས་པ་ནི" with no descriptive label of its own in this packet.

Twelve commentaries quote the root verse's four lines in full (as one continuous block or split across adjacent claims):

— full verse quoted attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-3` + `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-2` + `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-58`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-1` (opening two lines) + `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-2` (closing two lines, paraphrased rather than quoted), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-85` + `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-86` + `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-87` (12 commentaries, several via near-verbatim paraphrase rather than a clean four-line block)

These quotations are close but not identical: the packet shows at least three spellings of the second mountain's name across claims — མནྡྷར (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-3`), མནྡྲ་ར (`anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-2`), མནྡ་ར (`gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `taranatha:c-17-5`, `pema-namgyal` — no direct quote), and མནྡཱ་ར (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`) — an orthographic variant across transmissions, not a doctrinal disagreement.

The remaining four commentaries give only a partial or paraphrastic citation in this packet rather than a clean four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `taranatha:c-17-2` (structural opener only, abbreviating with "སོགས", "etc."), and `pema-namgyal` (which paraphrases rather than quoting the verse, being routed here by claim-content match).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the ordinal-marker and label differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad, not disagreement about which verse this is.

### Unique
None beyond the structural notes above.

## Naming and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None as a single shared name — every commentary that supplies a proper name for this specific form of Tārā gives one belonging to one of several distinct, only partly overlapping traditions.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least three naming/classification traditions are attested, plus a functional-label-only group:

1. **"[Lady/Tārā] Who Accomplishes All Bliss"** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-1` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-2` (section heading and structural marker, བདེ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲུབ་མ), corroborated independently by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-4` ("she is called here 'Tārā Who Accomplishes Bliss,'" བདེ་བ་སྒྲུབ་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — the two clauses are close but not character-identical (`anon-trinle-char` includes ཐམས་ཅད, "all"; `gendun-drub` does not). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-13` additionally reports that **two** names occur for this homage in its own tradition: "Tārā Who Accomplishes All Bliss" (བདེ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲུབ་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) and "Blissful Lady Who Binds Thieves" (བདེ་ལྡན་མ་ཆོམ་རྐུན་བཅིང་བ) — the second name overlaps thematically with tradition 2 below but is not character-identical to any claim in that group.
2. **"Immeasurably Subduing [Ārya Tārā], Who Binds [the Mouths of] Enemies, Bandits, Thieves[, and Hunters]"** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-0-1` (full form: དགྲ་ཇག་ཆོམ་རྐུན་རྔོན་པའི་ཁ་འཆིང་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ), `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-1` (abbreviated: དགྲ་ཇག་ཆོམ་རྐུན་འཆིང་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ — omits "mouths of" and "hunters" but shares དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ, "Immeasurable Subduer," verbatim with palden-sherab), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-20` (further abbreviated to just སྒྲོལ་མ་དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ, "Subduer of the Immeasurable," within a longer descriptive passage). Three commentaries share the དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ element exactly; the "binds enemies/bandits/thieves[/hunters]" element is present in all three but with varying inclusiveness. This tradition is thematically related to, but not a verbatim match for, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-13`'s "Blissful Lady Who Binds Thieves" (which shares only the "binds thieves," ཆོམ་རྐུན་...བཅིང་བ, idea, not the དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ epithet).
3. **"She Who Shakes the Three Worlds" / "Shaker of the Three Worlds"** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-1` (heading: འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་བ་མ), corroborated independently by `sungrab-tulku:c-20-3` ("venerable Tārā, Shaker of the Three Worlds," རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་མར) — close in sense, not character-identical (`tenzin-dhonzang` uses གཡོ་བ་མ as a compact epithet-noun; `sungrab-tulku` uses གཡོ་མར within a fuller descriptive clause).
4. **Functional/thematic classification rather than a proper name** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-1` labels the verse "homage by way of protecting from the fear of enemies" (དགྲའི་འཇིགས་པ་བསྲུང་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ); `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-1`, and `sungrab-tulku:c-20-1` instead classify it by activity, "wrathful activity that shakes the three grounds/realms" (already cited above under Structural placement).

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage beyond the bare "Ture" of the root verse, within the claims in this packet.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2` adds a practical note not classed as a name but as a liturgical application (see "Practice and liturgical application," below): both a paṇḍita and a siddha are said to apply this verse readily to protection from thieves on the road — a real-world echo of naming tradition 2's "binds bandits/thieves" theme, but framed as ritual application rather than as an epithet.

## Etymology of "Ture" and the act of homage

### Consensus
Eleven commentaries gloss "Ture" (or its variant transliteration "Tyāre") as meaning "the Swift One" (མྱུར་མ/མྱུར་མོ), the one to whom homage is paid, generally continuing with the same figure's two feet stamping (forcefully, ས་ལ་... བརྡབས་པ) on the ground:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-1`, `taranatha:c-17-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-58` (11 commentaries)

Several of these share strikingly close phrasing for the etymology-plus-stamping clause — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-3` ("ཏུ་རེ་སྟེ་མྱུར་མའི་ཞབས་གཉིས་ནི་དྲག་ཏུ་བརྡབས་པས་སོ"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-2` ("ཏུ་རེའི་སྟེ་མྱུར་མའི་ ཞབས་གཉིས་ནི་དྲག་ཏུ་བརྡབས་པས་སོ"), and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-3` ("ཏུ་རེ་སྟེ་མྱུར་མའི་ཞབས་གཉིས་ནི་ས་ལ་དྲག་ཏུ་བརྡབས་པས་སོ") — near-identical down to the closing པས་སོ, differing only in whether ས་ལ ("on the ground") is spelled out.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement among the eleven — the remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-drub`, `sungrab-tulku`, `tenga-tulku`) simply do not include an explicit "Ture = Swift One" gloss among the claims routed to this packet, though their claims are otherwise consistent with the same reading (feet-stamping, HŪṂ-seed, mountain-shaking).

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-46` departs from the "Swift One" etymology entirely: it glosses "Ture" instead as "Tārā's [own] body" (ཏུ་རེ་སྟེ་སྒྲོལ་མའི་སྐུ), displaying miraculous manifestations as she stamps her feet — a distinct etymological move (identifying Ture with her body rather than translating it as an adjective meaning "swift") not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

## Cause of the shaking — feet-stamping, HŪṂ's light rays, or both

### Consensus
None as a single statement. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-6` itself flags this as a live disagreement between two named positions in its own tradition: "which contradicts [the reading] that the shaking is caused by the stamping of feet" (ཞེས་གཡོ་བྱེད་ཞབས་བརྡབས་པས་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་པ་དང་འགལ་ཞིང). Most other claims in the packet, by contrast, do not frame this as an either/or choice: they simply narrate feet-stamping and HŪṂ-heart light-radiance together, in sequence, without asserting that one is the exclusive cause of the mountains shaking.

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **Feet-stamping alone as the described cause of shaking, no light-ray mechanism mentioned** — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-5` + `c-1-1-17-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `taranatha:c-17-4` + `c-17-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-85` + `c-1-87`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`. Nine commentaries.
2. **HŪṂ's light rays named as (at least also) causing the mountains to shake, alongside or via the stamping** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2` ("from the seed-syllable HŪṂ or its form, light causes Mount Meru... to emanate and shake," ཧཱུཾ་གི་རྣམ་པའམ་གཟུགས་ཀྱི་ས་བོན་ལས་འཕྲོས་པའི་འོད་ཀྱིས་...གཡོ་བར་མཛད), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-1` (feet strike the earth "and" heart-HŪṂ light-rays "radiate and stir in agitation," but the shaking of the mountains is attributed in the next claim, `c-3-1-17-1-2`, to her "wrathful, miraculous power" generally rather than to the light specifically), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-3` + `c-1-2-3-2-4` (mantra and light rays "like vajra-arrow points" are emanated from the heart-HŪṂ "in dependence on" her foot-stamping, and the mountains are then "greatly shaken" — light and stamping presented as a single connected sequence, not as alternatives), `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-1` + `c-18-0-2` (light rays radiate from heart-HŪṂ; shaking is attributed to her "power," མཐུ་སྟོབས, in the following claim, without specifying feet or light exclusively), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-59` (feet-stamping "and" HŪṂ-light radiating are both named, joined by དང), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-5` (citing "another commentary," not itself: light rays from HŪṂ shake the netherworld and the three worlds). Six commentaries name light rays in connection with the shaking, but — except for `anon-trinle-char`'s explicitly quoted "another commentary" and Nyima Sbaspa's contrasting verse — none of them states as sharply as `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-6` that light-rays and feet-stamping are *mutually exclusive* explanations; most narrate both together as complementary, not competing, causes.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-6`'s own internal-tension claim is the only place in the packet where the two readings are explicitly named as contradictory, attributed to **Position 1** ("another, unnamed commentary," quoting ཧཱུཾ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས་ས་འོག་སོགས་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་དོན་ལ་བྱེད་པ) and **Position 2** (Nyima Sbaspa: བདེ་ལྡན་ལྷ་མོས་ཞབས་བརྡབས་པས། ། འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་འཇིགས་པར་བྱེད). No other claim in the packet reproduces this specific two-way framing or names Nyima Sbaspa as a party to it.

### Unique
None beyond the internal tension above, already treated as a Divergence.

## Identity of the HŪṂ-seed — her wrathful nature, or the cause of Meru's motion

### Consensus
None — a second internal tension, again flagged only within `anon-trinle-char`.

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-10` reports the following as explicitly contradictory in its own tradition ("since this contradicts [the prior reading], one should rely on whichever has the more solid source," ཞེས་...འགལ་བས་རྩ་ཁུངས་བརླིང་པ་རེ་ལ་གཞི་བཅོལ་བར་བྱའོ):
- **Position 1 ("some commentaries," at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-9`):** the phrase "seed of HŪṂ's form" describes her *nature* — "she is the very nature of the wrathful goddess born from the seed having the form of the syllable HŪṂ" (ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་གི་རྣམ་པ་ཅན་གྱི་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཁྲོ་མོའི་བདག་ཉིད་མའོ).
- **Position 2 (Nyima Sbaspa, at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-10`):** the HŪṂ-seed is instead the *cause* by which she moves Mount Meru and draws it under her power (ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེས་མགོན། ། རི་རབ་ཀུན་བསྐྱོད་དབང་སྡུད་བསྒོམ).

This "wrathful-nature" identification (Position 1's content, though not attributed to "some commentaries" by name) is independently echoed by several other commentaries' own direct statements: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-4` ("she is the very nature of the wrathful one, born from the seed [syllable] in the form of the letter HŪṂ," ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་གི་རྣམ་པ་ཅན་གྱི་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཁྲོ་མོའི་བདག་ཉིད་མའོ — character-identical to `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-9`'s quoted line), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-3` (same clause, minor spelling variant ཧཱུྃ for ཧཱུཾ), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-3` (paraphrase: "she is the wrathful one born from the seed in the form of the syllable HŪM"), `sungrab-tulku:c-20-3` (paraphrase: "born from the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ, venerable Tārā is the very embodiment of the wrathful aspect"). No claim among these five independently corroborates *Position 2* (Nyima Sbaspa's "cause of moving Meru" reading) — it remains attested only inside `anon-trinle-char`'s own citation.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Identity of "the three worlds"

### Consensus
None as a single statement — a genuine three-way (plus one hedge) divergence over what "the three worlds" (འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ) denotes in this verse.

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **The netherworld / earth's-surface / world-above triad** (ས་འོག / ས་སྟེང / ས་བླ, or the equivalent naga/human/deva-labelled variant) — attested in two sub-wordings:
   - Generic wording, no deity/human/nāga labels: `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `taranatha:c-17-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-5` (4 commentaries — near-identical clause: ས་འོག་ས་སྟེང་ས་བླ, minor connective variation).
   - Explicitly labelled nāga/human/deva realms: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` (ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན, ས་སྟེང་མིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན, ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན) and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4` (the same three terms, reordered: ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན, ས་སྟེང་མིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན, ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན) — character-identical vocabulary, different sequence (2 commentaries).
   - `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-47` gives a third variant of the same basic triad, with its own labels: lower-rebirth/human/higher-realm (ས་འོག་ངན་སོང་, ས་སྟེང་མི་ཡི, ས་བླ་མཐོ་རིས་ཀྱི).
   - `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-59` corroborates the general triad but the claim text is cut off in the packet after "ས་འོག ས་སྟེང," so it cannot be confirmed which specific sub-wording it follows; treated as a partial, unconfirmed attestation, not counted in either sub-group above.
   - Combined, at least seven commentaries attest some version of this triad: `lobsang-dawa`, `taranatha`, `sungrab-tulku`, `anon-utpala`, `tenzin-dhonzang`, `tenga-tulku`, `pema-namgyal`.
2. **The named mountains themselves — Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya — as "the three worlds," with the netherworld/earth/heaven reading explicitly rejected** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-7` ("in this context, taking 'three worlds' to mean the three of netherworld, upper world, and earth's surface is also untenable," སྐབས་འདིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ས་འོག་ས་བླ་ས་སྟེང་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་པའང་མི་འཐད) and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-8` ("the tantra itself... clearly states that 'three worlds' means Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya," རྒྱུད་རང་ལས།...འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རི་རབ་དང་། མནྡར་དང་། འབིབས་བྱེད་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་པར་གསལ་བར་གསུངས་པ). This is the only claim in the packet that directly argues *against* Position 1, rather than simply not mentioning it.
3. **The desire, form, and formless realms** — `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-87` states this directly for the verse's "three worlds" phrase: "she shakes the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm" (འདོད་ཁམས་གཟུགས་ཁམས་གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་གཡོ་བར་བྱེད་པའོ). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-8` separately notes, as a general point rather than a direct assertion for this verse, that "there is also no certainty that 'three worlds' must exclusively be fixed as the netherworld triad, since the commentary on the Catuḥśataka states that it is applied to the three realms" (of desire, form, formless) — thematically the same triad as drakpa-gyaltsen's, but offered by `anon-trinle-char` as a hedge/possibility rather than as its own reading of this verse (its own reading is Position 2, the named-mountains reading). Per Rule 14, `anon-trinle-char`'s epistemic weight here is not upgraded to match drakpa-gyaltsen's direct assertion.
4. **Hedge — both triads offered as alternatives** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2` gives "the desire, form, and formless realms, *or* the netherworld, the earth's surface, and the world above" (འདོད་ཁམས་དང་། གཟུགས་ཁམས་དང་། གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་སམ། ས་འོག་དང་། ས་སྟེང་དང་། ས་བླ) as alternative readings within a single clause, rather than committing to one.

The remaining commentaries (`gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `konchok-thabkhe`, `gendun-drub`, `sangye-nyentrul`) refer to "the three worlds" only generically (e.g. འཇིག་རྟེན་སྲིད་པ་གསུམ, "the three worlds of existence") without specifying which triad they mean, within the claims in this packet.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Named mountains and textual variants

### Consensus
Most commentaries that quote or paraphrase the root verse name the same three: Meru (རི་རབ), Mandara (མནྡྷར/མནྡ་ར/མནྡཱ་ར, spelling varies — see "Structural placement," above), and a third mountain whose name varies between "Vindhya" and "Vibhedaka" (འབིགས་བྱེད). This third name is glossed by several commentaries as equivalent to the Himalaya/Kailāśa or "the black mountain": `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-5` ("Mount Meru Vibhedaka"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4` ("Mount Vibhedaka"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2` ("Vindhya, or the Himalayas"), `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4` ("the black mountain Vindhya"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-2` ("Vibhedaka — that is, the snowy mountain"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-20` ("the piercing mountain").

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-20`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-47`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-8` (14 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement over which mountains are named — only the identity-of-"three worlds" question above (whether the mountains themselves constitute "the three worlds," or stand apart from them) is genuinely contested.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-17-5` names the third mountain not as Vindhya/Vibhedaka but as "the Himalaya (Snow Mountain), where Maheśvara dwells" (རི་བོ་གངས་ཅན་...དབང་ཕ�ྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་གནས་པའི་རི). `taranatha:c-17-7` then adds an explicit textual-variant note not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: "According to most Tibetan copies, which read 'Vibhedaka,' the king of mountains is Vibhedaka; those who have seen the Sanskrit of this text find it reads 'Kailāśa,' which accords with reading it as 'Snow Mountain'... since various readings appear, it is difficult to determine one definitively, so both should be taken as not contradicting one another." No other commentary in the packet raises this Tibetan/Sanskrit textual-variant question.

`taranatha:c-17-10` and `taranatha:c-17-11` add a further unique rhetorical move: an objection ("if the three worlds are made to move, what purpose is served by mentioning Mount Meru and so forth?") answered by the commentary itself ("it is possible that the special abodes where powerful gods reside might not move; therefore [Meru and so forth] are mentioned specifically to show the Noble Lady's great power"). No other claim in the packet poses or answers this question.

## Iconography — body colour, arms, posture, and hand-implements

### Consensus
Four commentaries in the packet give an explicit body colour, and all four describe some shade of red or reddish-yellow:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-11`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-2-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-20` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Beyond the shared red/reddish-yellow colour, the packet's iconographic descriptions split into two distinct hand-implement traditions:

1. **Both hands hold a moon-disc at the heart; one face, two arms; bodhisattva posture on a sun-maṇḍala atop a white lotus** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-11` (སྐུ་མདོག་དམར་སེར། ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས་མ།...ཕྱག་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་ཐུགས་ཀར་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་བསྣམས་པའོ), supported by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own verse at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-12` ("two hands holding the rabbit-bearer [the moon]," ཕྱག་གཉིས་རི་བོང་འཛིན་བསྣམས་པ). One commentary (internally corroborated by its own cited verse source).
2. **Right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding an utpala topped by a stūpa; heart-HŪṂ light rays** — `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-2` (གཡས་མཆོག་སྦྱིན། གཡོན་ཨུཏྤལ་འཛིན་པའི་སྟེང་དུ། མཆོད་རྟེན), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-2-1` (ཕྱག་གཡས་མཆོག་སྦྱིན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་དང་། གཡོན་...ཨུཏྤལ...དབུས་མཆོད་རྟེན་འཛིན་པ), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-20` ("holding a stūpa upon an opened utpala"). Three commentaries, with `sangye-nyentrul` and `palden-sherab` sharing the specific utpala-plus-stūpa detail nearly verbatim (མཆོད་རྟེན vs དབུས་མཆོད་རྟེན, both "stūpa [at the centre]").

Posture also differs where stated: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-11` gives "bodhisattva posture" (སེམས་དཔའི་སྐྱིལ་མོ་ཀྲུང), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-2-1` gives "archer's posture" (རྟ་བབས་སྐྱིལ་ཀྲུང), and `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-2` gives "half-cross-legged" (ཞབས་ཕྱེད་སྐྱིལ) — three distinct posture terms, not reconciled by any claim in the packet.

No commentary in the packet describes both hand-implement traditions together or notes the discrepancy explicitly; this divergence is the page's own observation (per Rule 13), not a claim's.

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give a channel-based "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) reading of the verse's mountains, shaking, and HŪṂ-seed, with several clauses that are character-identical or near-identical between the two:

— attested: `taranatha:c-17-12`, `taranatha:c-17-13`, `taranatha:c-17-14`, `taranatha:c-17-15`, `taranatha:c-17-16`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1` (2 commentaries)

Re-comparing clause by clause: "Mount Meru is the bone of Brahmā" (`taranatha:c-17-12`: རི་རབ་ནི་ཚངས་པའི་རུས་པ) is expanded by `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1` into "Meru is the central channel, the spine, Brahmā's bone" (རི་རབ་ནི་རྩ་དབུ་མ་ཚངས་པའི་རུས་པ་སྒལ་ཚིགས་སོ) — the "Brahmā's bone" clause matches verbatim.
