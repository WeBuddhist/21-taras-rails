## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but mark it differently. Seven give an explicit global ordinal, "the fourteenth" (བཅུ་བཞི་པ): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-1`, `taranatha:c-14-1`, `taranatha:c-14-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-1` (7 commentaries, 9 claims). Four instead give the *local* ordinal "the seventh" (བདུན་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the seventh member of a "praise by way of light-rays radiating from the HŪṂ syllable" (ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`. `sungrab-tulku:c-17-1` uses the same "light-rays from HŪṂ" phrasing but pairs it with the global numeral "(14)" rather than "the seventh" — a hybrid marker. The remaining four (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `karma-maitri`'s non-opener claims aside, `sangye-nyentrul`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match or by the packet's node header, not by a visible ordinal word inside a claim.

Nine commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines, either as one continuous block or split across adjacent claims: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-47`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-75` + `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76` + `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77` (verse split across three claims) (9 commentaries).

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2` and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` are not counted here: re-reading both claims, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2` embeds the verse's first couplet near-verbatim (with an interpolated Mount-Meru gloss), but `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` paraphrases the third and fourth lines rather than quoting them — unlike gendun-drub's parallel split, where the corresponding gloss claim (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3`) is correctly excluded from this count. lobsang-dawa is folded into the partial/paraphrastic group below instead.

These quotations are not verbatim-identical. Re-reading each claim's own བོད་ཡིག: line 3 shows two distinct readings of the phrase before "the syllable HŪṂ" — ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་མཛད (roughly "she who assumes a wrathful frown") in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2` (with HAṂ, see Unique below), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-47`; versus ཁྲོ་གཉེར་སྤྱན་མཛད ("she who makes her frowning eyes wrathful") in `sungrab-tulku:c-17-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-2`. Three commentaries — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-75`/`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76`/`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77`, and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`/`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` — independently number this homage's two couplets as "31" and "32" within their own Tibetan claim text (embedded superscript-style numerals preceding the quoted phrases), a shared external verse-numbering convention distinct from the "fourteenth"/"seventh" ordinal labels discussed above. Re-reading each: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-4` opens "31,32 ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།"; `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-75` has "31 ཕྱག་གཡོན་པ་ས་ལ་བརྡབས"; `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77` both mark "32"; `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`'s own claim title reads "Verse 31" and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3`'s reads "Verse 32." This is a genuine three-commentary corroboration of a shared numbering scheme for the couplets, not a disagreement about which verse this is.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-2`'s own claim title labels its quotation "Verse 15," an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's own extraction (paralleling the same commentary's off-by-one labeling observed on other topic pages), not evidence that gendun-drub disagrees about which verse this is — the packet's spine map routes it here.

The remaining seven commentaries give only a partial or paraphrastic opening rather than a clean four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-2` (and the surrounding word-by-word fragments), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`+`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` (first couplet embedded verbatim, second couplet paraphrased — see above), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-40`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-1`, `taranatha:c-14-3`+`taranatha:c-14-4` (word-by-word gloss rather than a block quote).

None observed as doctrinal disagreement — the ordinal-marker and quotation differences above reflect each commentary's own sa-bcad and extraction style, not disagreement about which verse this is.

## Name, epithet, and classification

### Consensus
None as a single statement — every commentary that supplies a proper name gives a genuinely different one, though several converge on the shared theme "wrathful frown" (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན).

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming traditions are attested:

1. **"Tārā Who Summons"** ('གུགས་བྱེད་སྒྲོལ་མ） — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-2`. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-9` additionally reports this as specifically Nyima Sbaspa's own name for her, alongside two other masters' names for the same verse: "Rje Dge 'dun grub calls her 'Tārā of the Quivering Wrathful Frown' (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་གཡོ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ), and Co ne pa calls her 'Tārā Possessing a Wrathful Frown' (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་གྱི་སྒྲོལ་མ)." Re-reading gendun-drub's own commentary at this slot confirms the first of these attributions directly: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-4` independently states, in its own voice, "འདི་ལ་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་གཡོ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་འབྱུང་ངོ་། ('She is called here "Tārā of the Trembling, Furrowed Brow."')" — the identical phrase ཁྲོ་གཉེར་གཡོ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ that anon-trinle-char attributes to "Rje Dge 'dun grub." This is a direct, verbatim cross-commentary corroboration.
2. **"Wrathful-Browed Ārya Tārā, Destroyer of the Harm of Damsi Obstructors, the Nine Gongpo Brothers, and the Eight Classes"** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-1` (full name). `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1` gives a shorter, partially overlapping name — "Noble Lady with the Wrathful Brow [who] destroys the harm of malicious dam sri spirits and obstructing classes" (གདུག་ཅན་དམ་སྲི་བགེགས་རིགས་ཀྱི་གནོད་པ་འཇོམས་པའི་འཕགས་མ་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན) — sharing the "Damsi obstructors" (དམ་སྲི་བགེགས་རིགས) and "Wrathful-Browed" (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན) elements with palden-sherab's name, but omitting palden-sherab's further enumeration of the "Nine Gongpo Brothers and Eight Classes." `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17` independently names her "Sgrol-ma khro-gnyer-can-mdzad-ma" (སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་མཛད་མ, "Furrow-Browed One"), sharing only the "wrathful-browed" element, with no mention of Damsi or Gongpo spirits in the name itself (though tsultrim-namdak's own claim content elsewhere in this packet does mention harmful obstructing spirits being destroyed — see "Seven underground levels," below).
3. **"She of the earth"** (ས་གཞི) — `taranatha:c-14-1` (section heading), `taranatha:c-14-2`. Unique to this commentary; taranatha's own content elsewhere in the packet also uses "wrathful-browed" descriptively but not as this verse's formal heading.
4. **"She of the Wrathful Frown"** (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན) as the section heading itself — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-1`. Shares the "wrathful frown" element with groups 1 and 2 above but is not identical wording to either.
5. **Functional/thematic classification rather than a proper name** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-1` labels the verse "homage by way of protecting self and others" (རང་གཞན་སྲུང་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ); `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-1` instead classify it by the light-radiating-from-HŪṂ device (already cited above under "Structural placement").

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims in this packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-3` additionally glosses "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) as something "the yogi should apply in a manner concordant with practice" — a brief etymological/practice-framing note not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4` adds a distinct structural note: with this verse, "the seven homages praising the wrathful aspect are complete" (ཁྲོ་མོའི་ཚུལ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་བདུན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་ཅིང་) — identifying this verse as the close of a seven-homage wrathful sub-group. No other commentary in the packet states this closing count explicitly.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-9` (three-master naming report), `taranatha:c-14-1`/`taranatha:c-14-2` ("she of the earth"), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-3` (etymology note), and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4` (closing-count note) are each unique to their own commentary, as detailed above.

## Iconography — the earth-striking gesture and wrathful expression

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries describe the same basic action: she strikes the earth's surface with the palm of her hand (in a threatening/tarjanī gesture) and pounds or stamps it with her foot, with a wrathfully frowning face. The sixteenth, gendun-drub, is not silent but reports a distinct striking implement (her right foot-sole rather than a hand) and is accordingly placed under Divergences rather than folded in here — see below.

Four of the claims below (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-75`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-3`) are also cited under ⚑ Divergences: each supports the shared general gesture (cited here) and additionally specifies which hand strikes (cited there) — the same claim legitimately supporting both, not a mis-sort.

— wrathful frowning expression attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-4`, `taranatha:c-14-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48` (6 commentaries; other commentaries' quotations of the root verse itself also carry ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན/ཁྲོ་གཉེར་སྤྱན་ — see "Structural placement" — but are not re-listed here to avoid double-counting the same textual element)

Seven commentaries additionally gloss "the earth's surface" specifically as Mount Meru together with its (four) continents, rather than the plain earth generically: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-40`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-3` (which further adds "the eight sub-continents"), `taranatha:c-14-3`.

### ⚑ Divergences
Which hand strikes, and what strikes, is not uniform:
- **Left hand strikes**: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-75` ("ཕྱག་གཡོན་པ་ས་ལ་བརྡབས", "strikes the ground with her left hand"), `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-1` ("གཡོན་པའི་ཕྱག་གི་མཐིལ", "the palm of her threatening left hand"), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-3` ("གཡོན་པའི་ཕྱག་མཐིལ", "the palm of her left hand"). Three commentaries.
- **Both hands' palms and both feet's soles**: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` ("ཕྱག་གཉིས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་གྱིས་མནན་ཅིང་ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་", "presses down with the palms of both hands and pounds... with the soles of both feet"). One commentary, not generalized into the Consensus statement above since it disagrees with the single-hand framing.
- **Her right foot-sole strikes the ground (not a hand)**: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3` ("ཁྲོ་མོའི་ཆ་བྱད་ཀྱི་ཞབས་གཡས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་གྱིས་ས་ལ་བསྣུན་ཅིང་", "with her wrathful figure's right foot-sole she strikes the ground, forming a threatening mudrā"). This claim independently attests a striking implement (a foot rather than a hand) that no other claim in the packet reports; it is not folded into the Consensus statement.
- The remaining commentaries either leave the hand unspecified or (per the root-verse quotations already cited under "Structural placement") simply repeat the verse's own unmarked "ཕྱག་" (hand).

### Unique
`sungrab-tulku:c-17-4` and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4` independently attribute light pervading all directions to her two *eyes* specifically — sungrab-tulku: "སྤྱན་གཉིས་ཀྱི་འོད་ཟེར་རབ་ཏུ་འཁྲུགས་པས་ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་འོད་ཟེར་ཁྱབ" ("the light-rays of her two eyes intensely stir, and light pervades all directions"); tenga-tulku: "སྤྱན་གཉིས་རབ་ཏུ་འཁྲུགས་པའི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་ཁྱབ་ཅིང་" ("her two eyes, greatly agitated, emit light rays that pervade all directions") — near-identical phrasing. A distinct light-source from the HŪṂ-syllable light discussed below; corroborated by two commentaries rather than unique to one.

## The HŪṂ syllable — location and the light it emits

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is a genuine multi-way divergence over where the HŪṂ (or HAṂ) letter is located. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-5` itself names this as a live, four-way disagreement among named masters: "As for whose HŪṂ letter this is: Rje Dge 'dun grub states it is the HŪṂ at her heart, while Co ne pa states it is [the HŪṂ] on the palm of her right hand; Ngulchu speaks of a blue HŪṂ letter 'on the sole and palm,' taking it to be the HŪṂ letter on both her foot-sole and hand-palm together; and Jonangpa states it is the HŪṂ letter arising from her nostrils" (བོད་ཡིག: ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་གང་གི་ཡིན་ཞེ་ན། རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ་ཀྱིས་ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་དང་། ཅོ་ནེས་ཕྱག་གཡས་པའི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་ལ། དངུལ་ཆུས་ཞབས་ཕྱག་གི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་སྔོན་པོ་ཞེས་ཞབས་ཕྱག་གཉིས་ཀའི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ལ་མཛད་ཅིང་། ཇོ་ནང་པས་ཤངས་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་སོ།).

### ⚑ Divergences
Re-reading each commentary's own claims in this packet against the four positions anon-trinle-char reports:

1. **HŪṂ at the heart** (matching the report's "Rje Dge 'dun grub" position) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-4` (the commentary's own base-text reading, "ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ལས"), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3` ("ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་གིས", confirming the attribution to gendun-drub directly from that commentary's own text), `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5` ("ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་མཐིང་ཀ་ལས", heart, dark-blue), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-4` ("ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་མཐིང་ཀ", heart, dark-blue — near-identical phrasing to sungrab-tulku's). Four commentaries.
2. **HŪṂ on the right palm** (matching the report's "Co ne pa" position) — `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-5` ("གཡས་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་མཐིལ་ན་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ", "on her right palm is the letter HŪṂ"). One commentary.
3. **HŪṂ (or HAṂ) at both the hand-sole and the foot-sole** (matching the report's "Ngulchu" position, including its "blue" colour descriptor) — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-5` ("ཕྱག་ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་དུ་ཡི་གེ་ཧཾ་སྔོན་པོ་རེ་རེ", blue, hand-and-foot-sole), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4` ("ཕྱག་དང་ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་དུ་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་སྔོན་པོ་རེ་རེ་གནས་པ", blue, hand-and-foot-sole — near-verbatim match to anon-trinle-char's report of Ngulchu's own words), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3` ("ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་མཛད་དེའི་ཕྱག་ཞབས་སུ་གནས་པའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ", HŪṂ residing at "hand and foot," no colour given). Three commentaries.
4. **HŪṂ arising from the nostrils** (matching the report's "Jonangpa" position) — `taranatha:c-14-5` ("ཤངས་ནས་" ... "འཇིགས་པའི་སྒྲ་བསྒྲགས་པས", proclaimed through her nostrils), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41` ("ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན་མཛད་ཤངས་ནས་... ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ", from her nostrils). Two commentaries.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76` offers a fifth, distinct position not among anon-trinle-char's four: light radiates not from a HŪṂ-letter mark at all, but from a **vajra held in her right hand**, upon her *vocally uttering* HŪM aloud ("ངག་ཏུ་ཧཱུྃ་ཞེས་བརྗོད་པས་ཕྱག་གཡས་རྡོ་རྗེ་འཛིན་པ་དེ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས"). No other commentary in the packet shares this reading; it is not counted toward any of the four positions above.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48` each mention the sound or light of HŪṂ without specifying a location among the four positions above; they are treated as silent on this specific sub-question rather than assigned to any position. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-2` is textually uncertain at the relevant point ("ཕྱག་གལ་པ་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་ན་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་", an unclear phrase, possibly a hand-palm reading) and is likewise excluded from all four positions as ambiguous rather than silently assigned.

### Unique
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76` (vajra/vocal-utterance reading, above) is unique to its own commentary.

## The seven underground levels and the beings crushed there

### Consensus
The seven levels are named consistently, with only minor wording variation, wherever a commentary gives the full list: "the ground, the surpassing ground, the groundless, the ground itself, the ground of essence, the excellent ground, and the pure ground" (གཞི། ལྷག་པའི་གཞི། གཞི་མེད་པ། གཞི་ཉིད། བཅུད་ཀྱི་གཞི། གཞི་བཟང་པོ། དག་པའི་གཞི).

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-3`, `taranatha:c-14-9`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-5`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2` (numbered 1–7), `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41` (7 commentaries)

Three of these claims (`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5`) are also cited under ⚑ Divergences below: each names the seven levels (cited here) and separately names which beings are crushed there (cited there) — the same claim legitimately supporting both, not a mis-sort.

### ⚑ Divergences
Which beings are crushed there is not uniform:

- **Generic malevolent classes (nāgas, asuras/yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas, Yama, and the like)** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41`, `taranatha:c-14-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48` (7 commentaries).

- **Specifically Damsi (དམ་སྲི) obstructors, together with Gongpo/Gyandre spirits, their heart and head crushed and their swoon named** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-2` ("དམ་འགོང་རྒྱ་འདྲེ་གདུག་པ...སྙིང་དང་མགོ་བོ་འགེམ...ཆོས་ཉིད་ངང་དུ་བརྒྱལ", crushing heart and head of Damsi and Gyandre spirits, swoon into *dharmatā*), `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2` ("དམ་སྲི་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ནི་སྙིང་དང་མགོ་བོ་འགེམ...ཡུམ་ཆེན་མོའི་ངང་དུ་བརྒྱལ", crushing heart and head of dam sri spirits, swoon into the Great Mother's state), `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5` ("དམ་སྲི་རྣམས་ནི་སྙིང་དང་མགོ་བོ་འགེམས...ཡུམ་ཆེན་མོའི་ངང་དུ་བརྒྱལ", crushing heart and head of damsi demons, swoon into the Great Mother's state). Re-reading these three side by side: `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-0-2` and `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5` share a near-verbatim closing clause ("སྙིང་དང་མགོ་བོ་འགེམ...ཡུམ་ཆེན་མོའི་ངང་དུ་བརྒྱལ"), while `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-2` shares the "heart and head crushed" element but names the resulting state *dharmatā* rather than "the Great Mother" — a genuine partial divergence within this shared tradition, not a full match across all three. Three commentaries total, two of them near-verbatim.

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17` names the crushed party as "harmful obstructing spirits" (གནོད་པའི་བགེགས) delivered to dharmadhātu — a third, intermediate formulation (generic "obstructing spirits" rather than named "Damsi," but ending in *dharmadhātu* rather than the fully generic "scattered into pieces" of the first group). It is not counted toward either group above.

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-6` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-3` were re-read against this question and excluded from the "generic malevolent classes" count above: both describe only the crushing action itself ("light radiating from it causes the seven levels of the earth to crumble"; "with her wrathfully furrowed brow and the sound of 'hūṃ,' she crushes the seven levels of the world") without naming any class of being crushed there, unlike the seven claims counted above which all explicitly name nāgas, asuras, and the like. They are not counted toward either position.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-14-7` (seven levels as successive cave-like layers, great non-human realms) and `taranatha:c-14-8` (rejecting the view that the levels are measured from Mount Meru's summit) are unique doctrinal asides not paralleled elsewhere in the packet. `taranatha:c-14-10` restates that these are "the great worlds beneath the earth," adding no content beyond `taranatha:c-14-7`/`taranatha:c-14-9`.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-4` reports, as an explicitly tentative aside ("flagged for further examination," དཔྱད་པར་བྱའོ), a mahāsiddha's own treatise reading "the seven levels" as "filling" (འགེངས) rather than "crushing" (འགེམས), together with a proposed protective-circle (བསྲུང་འཁོར) drawing method reinterpreting "supreme letter," "the two orders," and "seven levels" as mantra-syllable and maṇḍala elements. No other claim in the packet corroborates or contests this reading; its tentative epistemic status is preserved here rather than upgraded to a settled alternative.

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-2` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-7` share a near-verbatim closing phrase framing the crushing as dispelling obstacles for beings: gendun-gyatso "རིགས་དྲུག་གི་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་བར་གཅོད་སེལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ" ("the Tārā who dispels all obstacles for the sentient beings of the six classes") and karma-maitri "སེམས་ཅན་ཀུན་གྱི་བར་གཅོད་སེལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ" ("the Tārā who dispels the obstacles of all sentient beings") — nearly identical wording. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-5` states the same theme differently ("འདིའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་གཙོ་བོ་བགེགས་ཞི་བར་མཛད་པའོ", "this deity's chief enlightened activity is pacifying obstacles") — a related but not verbatim-matching formulation. Three commentaries share the "dispels/pacifies obstacles" theme; two of them (`gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`) in near-identical wording.

## Extended iconographic forms

### Consensus
Where body colour is specified, it is consistently dark or black: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-6` (black, ནག་པོ), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1` (storm-cloud dark, ཆར་སྤྲིན་ལྟར་གནག་པ), `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1` (black, ནག་མོ), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17` (black, ནག་མོ).

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17` (4 commentaries)

Three of these (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17`) are also cited under ⚑ Divergences below, by the same claim ID: each states a body colour (cited here) and separately describes an overall iconographic form (cited there) — the same claim legitimately supporting both, not a mis-sort. (sangye-nyentrul's body-colour claim, `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-1`, is a different claim from its form-description claim cited below under Divergences, so it is not part of this overlap.)

### ⚑ Divergences
The overall *form* diverges sharply between two traditions:
- **Elaborate wrathful form — three faces, six arms** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-6`: black body, dancing stance trampling a preta on a red-yellow lotus atop a sun-maṇḍala; three faces (black/white/red) and three eyes; six arms holding sword, iron hook, and staff (right) and a blood-filled skull-cup, lasso, and a severed head (left); wears a human-head diadem, skull necklace, tiger-skin skirt, eight-snake ornaments. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-7` attributes a confirming citation to "Nyima Sbaspa's own Sādhana of the Praise in Twenty-One Homages," and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-8` quotes that sādhana's verse, which independently names the same red/white-black/red three-face scheme, eight snakes, tiger-skin, six arms with sword/hook/staff and skull-cup, and additionally specifies "holding Brahmā's head" (ཞགས་པ་ཚངས་པའི་མགོ་འཛིན་མ). One commentary, internally corroborated by its own cited external sādhana source; no other commentary in the packet attests this elaborate form.
- **Simpler generation-stage form — one face, two hands** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-2-1`: right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding, at ear-level atop an open utpala, a pestle; both legs in archer's posture. `sangye-nyentrul:c-15-1-2` independently gives the same basic configuration: seated half-cross-legged, one face, two hands, right in supreme-giving mudrā, left holding an utpala topped with a pestle. Two commentaries, closely matching in content though not verbatim in wording.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-17` partially corroborates the simpler form — black body, left hand holding a pestle atop an utpala — but does not state an arm-count, a mudrā name for the right hand, or a seat posture, so it is not counted as a full third attestation of the "one face, two hands" consensus; it is recorded here as a partial witness.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-6`/`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-7`/`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-8` (elaborate six-armed form, above) is unique among the commentaries in this packet, corroborated only by its own cited external sādhana quotation, not by any other commentary's independent claim.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give an extensive "completion-stage-with-marks" (རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས) reading of this verse, opening with a near-identical clause: taranatha's `taranatha:c-14-11` states "སྦས་དོན་ནི། ས་གཞིའི་ངོས་ནི་ལྟེ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ཏེ།" ("The hidden meaning: the surface of the earth is the navel-maṇḍala"), and palden-sherab's `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-3-1` opens "གསུམ་པ་སྦས་དོན་རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས་ནི། ས་གཞིའི་ངོས་ནི་ལྟེ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ཏེ།" — the same clause, verbatim, following palden-sherab's own section label. Re-reading the two commentaries' continuations claim by claim: taranatha's `taranatha:c-14-12` ("the definitive-meaning goddess of tummo-wisdom fire strikes with her foot and touches with the hand of the flame-tongue," དེ་ལ་གཏུམ་མོ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་ངེས་པའི་དོན་ཀྱི་ལྷ་མོའི་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་བརྡུངས་པ་དང་མེ་ལྕེའི་ཕྱག་གིས་བསྣུན་པའོ) closely parallels palden-sherab's continuation within `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-3-1` ("...ངེས་པའི་དོན་ཀྱི་ལྷ་མོའི་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་བརྡུངས་པ་དང་། མེ་ལྕེས་ཕྱག་གིས་བསྣུན་པའོ") — the same phrase with only minor grammatical variation. Likewise taranatha's `taranatha:c-14-13` (the central-channel fire "coiling... sevenfold," དབུ་མའི་མས་སྣ་ན་ཚངས་པའི་མེ...འཁིལ་བ་ལན་བདུན་པ) parallels palden-sherab's continuation ("དབུ་མའི་མས་སྣ་ན་ཚངས་པའི་མེ་ཙཎྜ་ལཱི...སྦྲུལ་ལྟར་འཁྱིལ་བ་ལན་བདུན"); taranatha's `taranatha:c-14-14` (bodhicitta arriving pacifies "མེའི་གདུང་བ") parallels palden-sherab's "...མེའི་གདུང་བ་ཞི་བ"; and taranatha's `taranatha:c-14-15` ("crushing the channel-knots," རྩ་མདུད་རྣམས་འགེམས་ཤིང) parallels palden-sherab's "ཉོན་རྩ་མདུད་རྣམས་འགེམས་ཤིང་འཇིག་སྟེ་བཀྲོལ་བའོ." The two commentaries' completion-stage-with-marks exegeses are substantially the same teaching, condensed into a single claim by palden-sherab and split across five claims by taranatha. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-3` additionally cites Tāranātha *by name* for a related simile — "like seven stacked roof-layers beneath this earth" (ཐོག་ཕུབ་རིམ་བདུན་ལྟར) — which matches taranatha's own independent phrase "in seven successive cave-like layers" (ཐོག་ཕུགས་རིམ་པ་བདུན) at `taranatha:c-14-7`, making this an explicit, named citation rather than an inferred parallel.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement — palden-sherab's additional fourth-level reading below is a further elaboration, not a competing account of the same point.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-2`, and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-3` extend the exegesis to a fourth, "completion-stage-without-marks" (མཐར་ཐུག་མཚན་མེད་རྫོགས་རིམ) level, framed in Great Perfection terms: the earth's surface as ignorance/ālaya (impure phase) or dharmadhātu itself (pure phase); unwavering devotional faith viewing the guru as buddha as the "ground"; the seven levels as the seven consciousness-collections dissolving into primordial dharmadhātu, actualizing the dharmakāya-state of Samantabhadrī; and view/meditation as the "eye" and "foot" of Trekchö and Tögal respectively. This fourth-level reading is not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the packet.
