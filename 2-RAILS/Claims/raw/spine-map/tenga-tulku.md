> which canonical spine slot's content** — nothing about what the claims say. It never

**Structure of this commentary.** This tree is thematic, not per-homage: the twenty-one
homages are grouped by what they praise rather than numbered `1`–`21` in the tree itself.
`1.2.1` (historical narrative) covers homage 1 alone; `1.2.2.1.1` (peaceful body-aspect)
divides into six nodes covering homages 2–7; `1.2.2.1.2` (wrathful body-aspect) divides into
seven nodes covering homages 8–14; `1.2.2.2` (dharmakāya aspect) is one node, homage 15; and
`1.2.3` (activity aspect) divides into six nodes covering homages 16–21 — a division the
commentary's own closing summary (`c-1-2-3-6-7`) states explicitly in these exact numbers
(1 + 6 + 7 + 1 + 6 = 21). Node-to-homage correspondence is established by each node's own
"Root verse quoted" claim, matched word-for-word against the root text's `^1-1`–`^1-21`
blocks (spelling variants such as `ཏུཏྟཱ་ར`/`ཏུཏྟྭ་ར` or `ཟླ་བའི་རྩེ་མོས`/`ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུས` are the same
witness differences the root text's own frontmatter documents, not different verses). Two
nodes are coarser than the spine and are split by claim: `1.2.3.6` (homage 21) also carries
the commentary's own transition into, and partial quotation of, the closing benefits verse
`^a-1` and its own itemized summary of the twenty-one homages, so it is routed by claim
rather than by node. Nodes `1` and `1.3` each carry one direct claim stating their own
sa-bcad division (`structure`) alongside descendants that belong to specific homage or
benefits slots, so per Rule 4 those two claims are routed separately and the parent nodes
themselves are left out of the Slot map.
