## Brahmā among the worldly gods who worship, serve, or rely upon Tārā

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries — every commentary in the packet except `taranatha` — name Brahmā among a recurring roster of great worldly gods, most often paired with Indra (Śakra), Agni, Vāyu, and Viśveśvara/Maheśvara/Sarveśvara, who worship, praise, serve, or rely upon Tārā. The claims fall into two recognizable patterns, both glossing the same underlying fact:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`, `yama-sonam:c-3-19-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-35`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-36`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` (15 commentaries)

**Pattern one — the canonical fivefold verse.** Eleven commentaries quote or closely gloss the same homage-verse naming five gods together: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`/`sungrab-tulku:c-9-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`/`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9`. The wording is stable across commentaries, e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ། ། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་མཆོད་མ། ། འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། ། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།" — "Homage to her who is worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara, and praised from in front by bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and hosts of yakṣas." Several commentaries then further identify or position the individual deities named — Brahmā specifically as guardian "above" or "of the zenith," or as "master of the trichiliocosm": `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-35`/`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-36`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`/`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-7`/`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3`.

**Pattern two — other homage-verses naming "the kings of the gods' hosts."** A separate set of claims glosses different homage-verses (on divine armor, on the goddess as "queen of the multitude of gods," and elsewhere) that likewise name Brahmā, generically paired with Indra, among the worldly gods relying on or serving Tārā, without repeating the fivefold list: `yama-sonam:c-3-19-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`/`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`/`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22`. Representative: `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`: "གང་ལ་ན། ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ཚངས་པ་དང་། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་། ལྷ་ཕལ་པ་དང་། མིའམ་ཅི་ཡི་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཞབས་ལ་བརྟེན་མ།" — "To whom is homage paid? To she at whose feet the kings of the gods' hosts — Brahmā and Indra, ordinary gods, and the host of kinnaras — all rely." `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-14` gives a third, shorter occurrence in a refrain verse ("ཚངས་དང་ལྷ་དབང", "Brahmā and the lord of gods...") describing Tārā's own radiant form, continuing into the same fivefold pattern.

The commentaries thus corroborate one another on the underlying fact (Brahmā recurs, by name, in the roster of worldly gods associated with Tārā's praise) while differing, unremarkably, in which of the root text's several homage-verses they are glossing at any given point — this is a structural fact about where each commentary's own verse-division places its exegesis, not a substantive disagreement.

None observed.

## Brahmā glossed specifically as "creator of the world"

### Consensus
Three commentaries go beyond the bare list-mention and explicitly gloss Brahmā as the maker or creator of the world, in near-identical language:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1` (3 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-5` states plainly: "ཚངས་པ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་བྱེད་པོར་འདོད་པའོ།" — "Brahmā is held to be the creator of the world." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20`, glossing the same fivefold verse, gives the same identification in inverted word order: "...འཇིག་རྟེན་བྱེད་པོ་འདོད་པ་ཚངས་པ" — "...Brahmā, held to be the creator of the world..." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1` gives a shorter form within its own list-gloss: "སྟེང་གི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བྱེད་པ་པོ་ཚངས་པ་དང་།" — "...and Brahmā, the world's maker, above..." (this claim is also part of the worshipper-list facet above; it is counted there for the bare list-mention and here for its additional "creator" content, per its own distinct assertion).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## Hidden-meaning (tantric) identifications of Brahmā

### Consensus
Two commentaries — `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` — read Brahmā's presence in the worshipper-verse as also carrying a tantric "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན), converging independently on the same two symbolic identifications:

**Elemental correspondence — Brahmā as water:**
— attested: `taranatha:c-6-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` (2 commentaries)
`taranatha:c-6-15`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ལྔ་ནི་འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་སྟེ། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས། མེ་ལྷ་ནི་མེ། ཚངས་པ་ནི་ཆུ། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནམ་མཁའ་འོ།" — "The hidden meaning: Indra and the other four are the five elements — Indra is earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, Vāyu is wind, and Viśveśvara is space." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` gives the identical mapping: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས་དང་། མེ་ལྷ་མེ་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཆུ་དང་། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་དང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ནམ་མཁའ..."

**Central-channel symbolism — Mount Meru as "the bone of Brahmā":**
— attested: `taranatha:c-17-12`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1` (2 commentaries)
`taranatha:c-17-12`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི། རི་རབ་ནི་ཚངས་པའི་རུས་པ།" — "The hidden meaning: Mount Meru is the bone of Brahmā." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1` gives the same identification, made explicit as the central channel: "རི་རབ་ནི་རྩ་དབུ་མ་ཚངས་པའི་རུས་པ་སྒལ་ཚིགས་སོ།" — "Meru is the central channel — the bone of Brahmā, the spine."

**"Brahmā's fire" (ཚངས་པའི་མེ) coiling sevenfold at the central channel's base:**
— attested: `taranatha:c-14-13`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-3-1` (2 commentaries)
`taranatha:c-14-13`: "དབུ་མའི་མས་སྣ་ན་ཚངས་པའི་མེ་སྤུལ་ལྟར་འཁིལ་བ་ལན་བདུན་པ།" — "At the tip of the central channel's opening, the fire of the Brahmā-aperture coiling sevenfold." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-3-1` gives the same image, naming it further as the tummo of Caṇḍālī: "དབུ་མའི་མས་སྣ་ན་ཚངས་པའི་མེ་ཙཎྜ་ལཱི་སྟེ...ལན་བདུན་..." Both readings occur as part of these two commentaries' respective "hidden meaning" sections on the same directional-guardian verse, i.e. the same textual locus that yields the worshipper-list and creator-identification facets above.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-3` gives a further hidden-meaning identification not paralleled by any other commentary in the packet: OM itself is glossed as "the supreme Brahmā" together with the dharmadhātu and dharmakāya — "ཨོཾ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་མཆོག་གི་ཚངས་པ། ཆོས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུ། ཡོན་ཏན་མཐའ་དག་གི་ནོར་བུ་འཛིན་པ།" — "'OṂ' is the supreme Brahmā, the dharmadhātu, the dharmakāya, the jewel holding all qualities..." No other commentary's claims in this packet identify OM with Brahmā.

## Brahmā as exemplar of the worldly power attained as a temporary benefit of Tārā practice

### Consensus
Two commentaries name Brahmā, alongside Indra, as a byword for the elevated worldly rank a practitioner temporarily attains through devotion to Tārā — distinct from Brahmā's own role as a worshipper of the goddess:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3` (2 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`: "...གནས་སྐབས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་ནི། མཐོ་རིས་ཀྱི་ཡོན་ཏན་དང་སྟོབས་འབྱོར་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་ཚངས་པ་ལྟ་བུ་དང་འཁོར་ལོའི་སྒྱུར་བའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལྟ་བུ་འཐོབ། མཐར་ཐུག་གི་ཕན་ཡོན་ནི། སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་..." — "...the temporary benefit is attaining qualities and abundant power like a god-lord such as Indra or Brahmā, or like a wheel-turning king; the ultimate benefit is...buddhahood." `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3` makes the same two-tier structure explicit: "...བསོད་ནམས་ཀྱི་དབང་དུ་ན་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་ཚངས་པ་ལྟ་བུ། མཐར་ཐུག་གི་དབང་དུ་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་..." — "...in terms of merit, like Indra or Brahmā; ultimately, the state of buddhahood..." In both claims, Brahmā functions as a simile for worldly eminence rather than as one of the goddess's own worshippers.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## Narrative and iconographic episodes involving Brahmā, beyond the worshipper-list

### Consensus
None — this facet is the packet's clearest example of Brahmā functioning outside the bare list-mention pattern, and each instance below is a single commentary's own, non-overlapping episode.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Three commentaries each give a distinct narrative or iconographic use of Brahmā, none paralleled elsewhere in the packet:

- **`yama-sonam:c-3-14-8`** — quoting a verse attributed to "Nyima Sbaspa" describing a wrathful Tārā-form's attributes, this claim's own quotation ends: "ཞགས་པ་ཚངས་པའི་མགོ་འཛིན་མ།" — "...a lasso, and holding Brahmā's head." Here Brahmā is not a worshipper but a subjugated figure whose severed head becomes one of the wrathful deity's hand-attributes — the sharpest departure from the worshipper-list pattern in the entire packet.
- **`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-3`** — glossing the etymology of "homage," this claim recounts that Indra and Brahmā were the two gods who once requested the Buddha to turn the Dharma-wheel, Indra offering a white conch and Brahmā a golden thousand-spoked wheel: "...སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་བདག་པོ་ཚངས་པ་གཙུག་ཕུད་ཅན་གྱིས་གསེར་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་རྩིབས་སྟོང་ཅན་ཕུལ་ནས་བདེ་བཞིའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསྐོར་བར་སྐུལ་བ་པོ་བརྒྱལ་[sic]་བྱིན་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཡིན།" — "...Brahmā, master of the trichiliocosm, with a topknot, offered a golden thousand-spoked wheel, urging [the Buddha] to turn the dharma-wheel of the four joys — these are the requesters, Indra and Brahmā." This episode is not about Tārā at all; it is invoked only as etymological background for the word "homage," giving Brahmā a canonical Buddhist narrative role independent of this text's own praise.
- **`tsultrim-namdak:c-4-8-1`** — the packet's most developed narrative use of Brahmā: merchants shipwrecked in a storm south of India "prayed to Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva and others, without effect," before a Buddhist lay-devotee among them recites Tārā's ten-syllable mantra and the storm instantly stills: "...ཚོང་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཚངས་པ་དང་། ཁྱབ་འཇུག །དབང་ཕྱུག་སོགས་ལ་གསོལ་བ་བཏབ་ཀྱང་མ་ཕན...སྒྲོལ་མ་དྲན་ནས་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པ་སྒྲོལ་མའི་སྔགས་བཟླས་ཤིང་གསོལ་བ་བཏབས་པས་དེ་མ་ཐག་རླུང་རྣམས་ཞི།..." This is the only claim in the packet that stages Brahmā as a directly-invoked, failed alternative to Tārā — the episode this topic's own brief specifically anticipated ("a narrative role distinct from a bare list-mention").

## "Brahmā-voice" (ཚངས་དབྱངས) as an epithet for melodious or divine speech

### Consensus
None — the two instances below use the same Sanskrit-derived idiom (brahmasvara) independently, in different contexts, and neither claim states that its usage matches the other's.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are not a disagreement, only two non-corroborating uses of a shared idiom (see Unique).

### Unique
- **`tsultrim-namdak:c-1-17`** states that the Tāriṇī-mantra itself arose "from the melody of her [Tārā's] Brahmā-voice, like the kalapiṅka bird's song": "ཚངས་དབྱངས་ཀཱ་ལ་པིང་ཀའི་དབྱངས་ལས་ནི། །བྱུང་བའི་ཏྟ་ར་ཡི་གཟུངས་ཀྱི་སྔགས..." Here "Brahmā-voice" describes a quality of Tārā's own voice, not the god Brahmā.
- **`tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-105`** describes a generation-stage mudrā practice in which placing the joined palms at the throat "establishes...the melodious Brahmā-voice speech" (གསུང་ཚངས་དབྱངས), one of four powers established by four hand-placements (crown, hairline, throat, heart): "ཐལ་མོ་མགྲིན་པར་བཞག་པས་གསུང་ཚངས་དབྱངས..." This too describes a quality of speech attained in practice, not the god Brahmā himself.

Both claims are recorded here rather than folded into the worshipper-list facet because their own content is not about Brahmā the worldly god at all, but about an idiom built on his name; per Q9, they are not a corroborated pair, only two independent occurrences of the same convention.

## Claims reviewed, not separately cited

- `palden-sherab:c-0-40` — names a Dharma-king's honorific epithet, "Chos rgyal Tshangs pa lha'i me tog" ("Dharma-king Brahmā's-Flower"), identified in this claim as Trisong Detsen, within a transmission-lineage note. A different referent — a king's honorific epithet built on the name "Brahmā" — not the deity Brahmā treated elsewhere in this packet.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2` — quotes the "Sūtra of the Questions of Brahmaviśeṣacintin" (ཚངས་པ་ཁྱད་པར་སེམས་ཀྱིས་ཞུས་པ), a Mahāyāna sūtra named for the bodhisattva-interlocutor Brahmaviśeṣacintin. A different referent from the deity Brahmā.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-4` — reproduces the root praise's full twenty-one homage-verses as continuous recitation text, including the fivefold worshipper-verse already cited above via `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`/`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9`; this claim adds no content about Brahmā beyond what those two claims already attest, so it is logged here rather than cited independently.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-9-10` — a closing aspiration-verse naming "Brahmā, Indra, Viṣṇu and their hosts" among the gods who will fill every land with flowers of blessing. The direction of action is inverted from every claim in the worshipper-list facet (here the gods, including Brahmā, are the ones bestowing blessing on beings, rather than worshipping Tārā), so it is not folded into that facet's consensus statement.
