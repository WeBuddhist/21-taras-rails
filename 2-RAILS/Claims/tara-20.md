# Tārā 20 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཉི་མ་ཟླ་བ་རྒྱས་པའི། / Homage to She Whose Sun-and-Moon Eyes Dispel Epidemic Disease

6. What specific benefits — beyond dispelling epidemic disease itself — does each commentary attribute to this homage or its mantra?
7. Do any commentaries quote the same mahāsiddha's verse on "wisdom eyes" destroying disease and affliction, and if so, do they attribute it to the same source?
8. Do any commentaries offer a completion-stage or other hidden-meaning (སྦས་དོན) reading of this verse's sun/moon/HARA/TUTTĀRE imagery, and if so, do their readings agree?
9. Does any commentary report a divergent liturgical, ritual, or alternative-visualization application of this verse?
10. Does any other commentary corroborate gendun-drub's own internally-flagged tension between a "right eye = sun, left eye = moon" reading and a "wrathful eye = sun, peaceful eye = moon" reading?

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but none of the fifteen non-silent ones number it identically. Seven mark it with a global ordinal, "the twentieth" (ཉི་ཤུ་པ/བ) or a bare numeral: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-20-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-0-1`, `taranatha:c-20-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-1`, `sangye-nyentrul` (numeral "༢༠" in its own node title, not repeated inside a claim's own text in this packet). Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the fifth" (ལྔ་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the fifth member of a "praise by way of dispelling epidemic disease" (རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་... སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-1`. `sungrab-tulku:c-23-1` uses the same descriptive phrase but pairs it with the global numeral "(20)" instead of "the fifth."

The descriptive phrase itself recurs across both numbering styles: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-1`'s ལྔ་པ་རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི། is character-identical, after the ordinal word, to `sungrab-tulku:c-23-1`'s ༼༢༠༽རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ། — the same clause རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ under two different ordinal systems. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-1`'s ཉི་ཤུ་བ་རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི། is close but drops འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-1`'s ལྔ་པ་རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི། matches `anon-utpala` up through སེལ་བའི་ and then diverges (སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི rather than འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི). `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-1`'s ལྔ་པ་རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ནི། names "Tārā's activity" (སྒྲོལ་མའི་འཕྲིན་ལས) rather than "praising" (བསྟོད་པ) — a real wording difference, not a corruption of the others. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-1` gives only the bare ordinal ལྔ་པ་ནི། without restating the epidemic theme in the claim text itself.

## Name and epithet of this Tārā form

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper epithet for this form of Tārā gives one of several **different** ones; this facet is a set of divergent naming traditions, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least three distinct naming traditions are attested, plus one commentary's unique framing by iconographic feature rather than by epithet:

1. **"Source of Accomplishments/Attainments"** (དངོས་གྲུབ་འབྱུང་གནས) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-1` ("the Venerable Lady Source of Accomplishments"), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-6` (colophon-line at ^0-246–^0-247 identifying this sādhana as "of the venerable Tārā that makes the source of accomplishments manifest"), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-4` ("she is called here 'Tārā, Source of Attainments'"). Two commentaries: `anon-trinle-char`, `gendun-drub`.
2. **"Forest-Dweller Clothed in Leaves"** (རི་ཁྲོད་(ལོ་)མ་གྱོན་མ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-0-1` ("Jetsün Ārya Tārā, Forest-Dwelling One Clothed in Leaves, Who Dispels Epidemic Disease"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-21-1-1` ("Noble Lady Wearer of Mountain-Ascetic Robes, Who Dispels Epidemic Disease," རི་ཁྲོད་ལོ་གྱོན་མ), `sungrab-tulku:c-23-3` ("venerable Tārā, Clad in Leaves in the Mountain Retreat," རི་ཁྲོད་ལོ་མ་གྱོན་མ), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-23` ("Lha-mo Sgrol-ma ri-khrod-ma, 'Mountain-Dwelling Goddess'"). Four commentaries share this tradition, though the exact wording (with or without ལོ་, "leaf/leaves") is not identical across all four.
3. **Named by the iconographic feature of the verse itself, "the sun"** (ཉི་མ) — `taranatha:c-20-1` ("Twentieth, homage to [she of] the sun[-and-moon eyes]"). Unique to this commentary; it does not use either the "Source of Accomplishments" or "Forest-Dweller" epithet anywhere in this packet's claims.
4. **No proper epithet, only the functional/thematic section label** "dispelling epidemic disease" already cited under Structural placement: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-1` (in addition to its group-1 epithet above), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-1` (in addition to its group-2 epithet above), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-1`.

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims routed to this slot in this packet.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-20-1`'s "sun" framing (group 3, above) is unique in the packet.

## Iconography — the sun-and-moon eyes

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries (all but the silent `gendun-gyatso`) describe Tārā's two eyes as radiant like — or compared to — the full sun and moon, with clear or blazing light.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-94`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-95`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-20-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-52`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-21-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-3`, `taranatha:c-20-2`, `taranatha:c-20-7`, `taranatha:c-20-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-67`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-68` (15 commentaries)

> Note on reuse: `taranatha:c-20-7`, `taranatha:c-20-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-52`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-95`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-3`, and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-6` are each cited again below under ⚑ Divergences, for the specific right/left or wrathful/peaceful eye-assignment each one takes. Each claim genuinely and fully supports both the general shared-imagery statement here and its own specific position below; this is not a claim sitting on two sides of one disagreement (Rule 11), since each is assigned to at most one position there.

### ⚑ Divergences
Within this shared "sun-and-moon eyes" imagery, commentaries diverge on **which eye is sun-like/wrathful and which is moon-like/peaceful** — and on whether this is a right/left split within a single form at all, or a distinction between two separate forms.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-2` itself flags this as a live, two-way tension within its own source: **Position 1**, right eye like the full sun, left eye like the full moon (ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཉི་མ་ཟླ་བ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་སྤྱན་གཡས་པ། ཟླ་བ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་སྤྱན་གཡོན་པ།), with no wrathful/peaceful qualifier attached to either eye; **Position 2**, introduced with "though it is also said," the wrathful eye is like the full sun and the peaceful eye is like the full moon (ཉི་མ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་ཁྲོ་མོའི་སྤྱན། ཟླ་བ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་ཞི་བའི་སྤྱན།), without fixing which of these is right and which is left.

1. **Right eye = sun (fire/wrathful function), left eye = moon (nectar/peaceful function)** — corroborating gendun-drub's Position 1: `taranatha:c-20-7` ("the light of the right eye frightens harmful beings and burns sin and suffering — hence it is light like the sun") + `taranatha:c-20-8` ("the light-rays of the left eye grant longevity, resources, and happiness — hence it is moonlight"); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-1-1` ("from whose right eye — the full sun — blazing light-rays radiate, destroying all disease-lords... from whose left, the moon, a stream of nectar flows down, healing disease"); `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-52` ("the right eye's fire-like light of the sun burns away all enemies... the left eye's nectar-like moonlight increases longevity, wisdom"). Four commentaries: `gendun-drub` (Position 1), `taranatha`, `palden-sherab`, `pema-namgyal`.
2. **Right eye = moon, left eye = sun (the reverse assignment)** — `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-95` ("her right eye is symbolized by the moon, the left by the sun," སྤྱན་གཡས་པ་ཟླ་བ་གཡོན་པ་ཉི་མས་མཚོན་པ). This is the only claim in the packet with this specific right/left assignment; it directly contradicts position 1 above rather than merely varying the wording, and no other commentary in the packet corroborates it.
3. **Sun and moon as two separate forms/aspects (wrathful and peaceful), not a right/left split within one form** — corroborating gendun-drub's Position 2: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-3` ("her two, right and left, wrathful eyes are like a full sun, and her two, right and left, peaceful eyes are like a full moon" — i.e. the wrathful form's *pair* of eyes is sun-like and the peaceful form's *pair* is moon-like); `sungrab-tulku:c-23-3` (near-identical structure: "the two eyes, right and left, of venerable Tārā's wrathful [aspect] are like the full sun; the two eyes, right and left, of venerable Tārā's peaceful [aspect] are like the full moon"); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-2` ("two wrathful eyes, left and right... like a full sun, and two peaceful eyes... like a full moon"); `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-3` (the wrathful Venerable Mother's two eyes, right and left, blaze red like the sun) together with `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-6` (the peaceful Venerable Mother's two eyes, right and left, blaze white like the moon) — two separate visualization instructions for two separate forms, matching the same pattern. Five commentaries: `gendun-drub` (Position 2), `anon-utpala`, `sungrab-tulku`, `lobsang-dawa`, `tenzin-dhonzang`.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-68` gives both readings in immediate sequence without choosing one, in a manner directly comparable to `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-2`'s own two-position framing: "the pairing of sun and moon [signifies] the occasion of her peaceful-and-wrathful form" (matching position 3) "or [alternatively]: the light-rays of her right eye are hot and burning... the light-rays of her left, moon-like eye [are cool]" (matching position 1's right=sun assignment, continued in `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-69`).

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-3` describes "that very Venerable Lady's two eyes, possessing a wrathful aspect, [as] like the sun and moon" without splitting which eye is sun and which is moon, or whether "wrathful" applies to one eye or both — this does not clearly fit any of the three positions above and is treated as a general, unresolved statement rather than assigned to a side.

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-20-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-3`/`anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-4`, and `taranatha`'s own heading `taranatha:c-20-1` describe the eyes generally as sun-and-moon-like without taking an explicit position on right/left or wrathful/peaceful, and are not counted toward any of the three positions.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-3` gives its own reason-clause framing, distinct from the others': the eyes see clearly "all phenomena obscured by subtle factors of place and time and all unobscured phenomena" — an epistemic/omniscience framing rather than the compassionate-gaze-upon-suffering-beings framing found in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-4` (her eyes see beings "degraded by fierce epidemic illness... and by its underlying cause, the afflictions") or `taranatha:c-20-2`–`taranatha:c-20-3` (she "gazes brilliantly... upon beings" and thereby "establishes all suffering beings, such as those in the lower realms, in happiness"). These three framings share the general theme of a compassionate or perceiving gaze but are not verbatim or structural matches of one another and are not treated as mutually corroborating.

## The mantra — HARA (HARA) and TUTTĀRE

### Consensus
Across the packet, the near-universal core claim is that the doubled wrathful syllable HARA together with the peaceful syllable TUTTĀRE (or TUTTĀRA) — recited as part of a longer vidyā-mantra — dispels severe, often explicitly "difficult to cure" epidemic disease.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-96`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-20-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-20-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-21-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4`, `taranatha:c-20-4`, `taranatha:c-20-5`, `taranatha:c-20-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-70`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-23` (16 claims across 15 commentaries; `gendun-gyatso` silent)

A shared ten-syllable "root" or "peaceful" mantra, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ, is given in full by several commentaries: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4`, `taranatha:c-20-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-70`.

> Note on reuse: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-5`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-23` are each cited again below under ⚑ Divergences, for the specific full spelling of the wrathful mantra each one gives. Each claim genuinely and fully supports both the shared "HARA-plus-TUTTĀRE dispels epidemic disease" statement here and its own distinct spelling below; the reuse is deliberate, not a mis-sort.

### ⚑ Divergences
The wrathful mantra with "HARA" is given in several different full spellings, not a single shared string: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-5` gives OṂ NAMAḤ TĀRE NAMO HĀRE HWA HĀRE SVĀHĀ; `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4` gives OṂ NAMAḤ TARE NAMO HARE HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-5` gives a nineteen-syllable form, OṂ NAMA TĀRE NAMO HARE HŪM HARE SVĀHĀ HARA HARA PHAT; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-2-2` gives an extended form combining both aspects, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE NAMAḤ TĀRE NA HA MO RA HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ; `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53` gives the short form OṂ HARA HARA TUTTĀRE SVĀHĀ; `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-23` gives a further variant, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE NA MA TVA RA MA NO HĀ RA SVĀHĀ. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4` names the mantra as "hara hara phaṭ... tuttāra" without spelling it letter-by-letter with OṂ/SVĀHĀ.

The etymological gloss of "HARA" itself also diverges: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-4` glosses hāra as "seizer" (འཕྲོག་པ), a reading shared by `taranatha:c-20-13`'s completion-stage gloss of "HARA" as *seizing/liberating from* the two extremes (see Hidden meaning, below, for the specific wording "liberating" vs "seizing").

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-7` reports a textual-critical note not found in any other claim in this packet: the Kangyur's own tantra exemplar reads "TĀRA twice" (ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད), which "many commentaries" retain owing to "a widespread scribal error," but Nyima Sbaspa's own earlier *Sādhana of the Twenty-One Praises to Tārā* has "HĀRA twice" — and, in accordance with this, "most commentators — Rje Dge-'dun-grub and others" — who read "HĀRA twice" are said to be correct. No other claim in the packet raises this variant explicitly, though `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-5-3` — the raw claims file in this corpus attributed to the commentator this note names — does itself read ཧ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད ("HARA twice"), consistent with (though not independent corroboration of) `anon-trinle-char`'s report about that author's reading.

`taranatha:c-20-9` glosses its own root-verse phrase "uttering TA RA twice" (ཏ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད) — not "HĀRA twice" — as identifying TĀRE (twice) together with TUTTĀRE, i.e. the ten-syllable peaceful mantra already introduced. No clean quotation of taranatha's own root-verse line 3 survives elsewhere in this packet (see "Structural placement and root-verse citation," above, where taranatha's quotation is logged as paraphrase only), so this gloss is the only packet evidence for taranatha's own wording of that phrase. Read against `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-7`'s report that "many commentaries" retain a "TĀRA twice" reading it calls a scribal corruption of "HĀRA twice," this is consistent with — though, since taranatha's own claim never raises the scribal-error question itself, not independent corroboration of — that report. This connection is the page's own reading of the two claims together, not a claim either commentary makes itself.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-23`'s mantra spelling (above) does not match any other commentary's wrathful, peaceful, or combined mantra string in the packet.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4` frames its mantra-gloss as "an explanation applied to the definitive meaning" (ངེས་དོན་ལ་སྦྱར་བའི་བཤད་པ), tying the mantra's effect specifically to destroying "the epidemic disease of the karma of lower rebirths and of the afflictions" — see the cross-reference to the mahāsiddha's verse this claim draws on, under "A mahāsiddha's verse on wisdom-eyes," below.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give a completion-stage-with-marks reading of the sun/moon/HARA/TUTTĀRA imagery, opening with a clause that is **character-identical** between the two: ནང་གི་ཁུ་རྡུལ་གཉིས་ཡིན་ལ། ("...are the two inner [substances, i.e. seminal fluid and blood]"), found in `taranatha:c-20-11` (preceded by ཉི་ཟླའི་བརྡས, "by the sign of sun and moon") and in `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-1` (preceded by ཉི་མ་དང་ཟླ་བ་ནི, "as for sun and moon"). A second clause is also character-identical: `taranatha:c-20-14`'s གདུང་བ་སྟེ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཡིན་ལ ("burning, that is, austerity," glossing TUTTĀRE/TUTTĀRA) matches `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2`'s གདུང་བ་སྟེ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཡིན་ལ word for word. The two commentaries also converge, in substance though not verbatim, on reading the two eyes as the two mantra-syllables positioned above and below the central channel/vajra-body (`taranatha:c-20-12`: "the two mantras above and below the central channel"; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-1`: "the two mantras A and HAṂ, above and below the vajra-body"), and on reading the sun-pressed/moon-frozen imagery as dissolving into the changeless state to dispel an "epidemic of the [seminal] drop" (`taranatha:c-20-15`; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2`).

— attested: `taranatha:c-20-11`, `taranatha:c-20-12`, `taranatha:c-20-14`, `taranatha:c-20-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2` (2 commentaries)

> Note on reuse: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2` is cited again below under ⚑ Divergences, for its own gloss of HARA as "seizing" (against taranatha's "liberating"). The claim's own text contains both the TUTTĀRE/austerity clause cited here (character-identical to taranatha's) and the separate HARA clause cited below; both statements are genuinely and independently supported by its content, so this is not a claim sitting on two sides of one disagreement (Rule 11) — the Consensus and Divergence citations are of two different clauses within the same claim block.

### ⚑ Divergences
Within this otherwise closely-matching shared reading, the two commentaries diverge on the etymological gloss of "HARA" itself: `taranatha:c-20-13` glosses HARA as "liberating" (སྒྲོལ་བ), "by bliss-emptiness liberating from the two extremes of existence and peace" (བདེ་སྟོང་གིས་སྲིད་ཞི་གཉིས་ཀྱི་མཐའ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་བ); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2` instead glosses HARA as "seizing" (འཕྲོག་པ), "through the union of bliss and emptiness, the enemy of existence-and-peace is seized" (བདེ་སྟོང་ཟུང་འཇུག་གིས་སྲིད་ཞིའི་དགྲ་འཕྲོགས་པ). Both readings invoke the identical doctrinal pairing (bliss-emptiness overcoming the existence/peace duality), but the verb applied to "HARA" itself is opposite. `palden-sherab`'s "seizing" reading matches the plain word-gloss given independently in the mantra facet by `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-4` ("hāra... meaning 'seizer'"); `taranatha`'s "liberating" reading is not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-4-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-4-2` extend the exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks": sun-and-moon as the ground-expanse's primordial purity and spontaneous presence, the eyes' radiant light as arising "through properly practicing Trekchö and Tögal... the four lamps and so forth," and the doubled HARA-TUTTĀRA as "the supreme path of primordial-purity Trekchö" instantly seizing and dispelling saṃsāra's "terrifying, dreamlike deluded appearance." This Dzogchen-styled fourth level is not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the packet.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4`'s mantra-gloss, tying "hara hara phaṭ... tuttāra" to destroying "the epidemic disease of the karma of lower rebirths and of the afflictions" and framed as "an explanation applied to the definitive meaning" (ངེས་དོན), draws on the mahāsiddha's verse cited in `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-2` (see "A mahāsiddha's verse on wisdom-eyes," above) rather than on the substance-and-channel completion-stage scheme shared by `taranatha` and `palden-sherab`. It is recorded here as a distinct, commentary-specific "definitive meaning" reading, not folded into either of the two positions above.
