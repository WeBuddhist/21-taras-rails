## Structural placement and root-verse quotation

### Consensus
Eight commentaries mark this passage with an explicit ordinal — "third" (གསུམ་པ) or an equivalent numeral marker — identifying it as the third homage, before glossing the verse.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-1`, `taranatha:c-3-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-1` (8 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Four commentaries instead mark this same node "second" (གཉིས་པ), not "third." Three of them — Anon-Utpala, Gendun Drub, and Tenga Tulku — give a near-identical formula in their own claim text, "the second, [praise] by way of body-colour, hand-emblem, and cause" (Tenga Tulku's variant reads "body" [སྐུ] in place of "cause" [རྒྱུ] as the third listed element). The fourth, Lobsang Dawa, also marks the node "second" but its own claim gives only the bare ordinal marker — "གཉིས་པ་ནི།" ("As for the second:") — with no mention of body-colour, hand-emblem, or cause; the fuller formula sometimes associated with this claim is the raw claims file's own bracketed editorial gloss (`[body-color, hand-implements, and their cause]`), not Lobsang Dawa's own words, so it is not attributed to the claim here (per Rule 12). This is the same nested-numbering phenomenon attested for the prior homage in this corpus — these four commentaries are numbering a sub-element within this homage's own internal structure (body-colour/hand-emblem/cause is apparently counted as the "second" descriptive element, after an initial name/identification element), not the homage's position among the twenty-one as a whole. No commentary in this corpus states outright which of the two schemes the other is using; this page records the difference as a taxonomy split rather than resolving it, per Rule 4.

- Position 1 (ordinal "third," homage-level count): the eight commentaries listed under Consensus above.
- Position 2 (ordinal "second," nested sub-element count): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-1`.

### Unique
Anon-Trinle-Char gives two further structural markers beyond its heading (already cited under Position 1 above): a second, near-duplicate marker restating "as for the third homage, to the venerable Lady Tārā of the Perfections," and a transitional line identifying the object of homage and asking what cause is seen for faith. Neither adds ordinal or doctrinal content beyond what its heading claim already establishes, so they are recorded here rather than double-counted in the Consensus above. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-2`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-4`

Taranatha similarly gives a second, transitional structural claim — "regarding the third verse, 'homage to gold[-stemmed], blue [lotus],' etc." — which repeats the ordinal and heading content of its own claim already cited under Position 1 above, serving only to introduce the word-by-word gloss that follows. `taranatha:c-3-2`

Drakpa-Gyaltsen, Pema Namgyal, Sangye Nyentrul, and Tsultrim Namdak contribute no separate structural-ordinal claim to this packet (their routed claims begin directly with word-glosses or the root-verse quotation — see "Claims reviewed, not separately cited").

## Root-verse quotation and the "gser sngo" / "ser sngo" textual variant

### Consensus
Eight commentaries quote the verse's opening line(s) in a form beginning "ཕྱག་འཚལ་...སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི། ...པདྨ(ས)་ཕྱག་ནི་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ།" (with the closing two lines, "སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།", given in full or in part) — "Homage to her, [golden/yellow]-blue, born from water, whose hand is adorned by a lotus; whose very domain is generosity, diligence, austerity, peace, patience, and meditative concentration." Two sub-groups of this shared quotation diverge on a single syllable:

**"གསེར་སྔོ" (gold-blue, with the "ga" prefix):**

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2` (4 commentaries)

**"སེར་སྔོ" (yellow-blue, without the prefix):**

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-2` (4 commentaries)

Gendun Drub's own quotation — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་སེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི། །པདྨས་ཕྱག་ནི་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ།" — reads "སེར་སྔོ" with no "ག" prefix at all, so it belongs in the without-prefix group above, not the with-prefix group (corrected from an earlier mis-categorization). It also gives only the verse's first two lines rather than the full four, so it is recorded as a partial attestation of the shared quotation above, per Rule 9.

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the base wording beyond the two-way spelling split recorded above. Konchok-Thabkhe explicitly attests, as its own separate etymological claim, that this exact spelling variation is a live issue among the commentaries it knows — see "Etymology notes," below, which cites the same claim.

### Unique
Lobsang Dawa's quotation (split by this commentary into "Verse 7" and "Verse 8") is a paraphrastic expansion rather than a verbatim quotation of the terse four-line form — it inserts "གསེར་ལྟར་མཛེས་ཤིང་ཅུང་ཟད་སྔོ་བ" ("beautiful like gold, slightly blue") and specifies "ཕྱག་གཡོན" (left hand) directly inside the verse-line itself, neither of which appears in the terse forms quoted by other commentaries. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`

Sungrab-Tulku's claim `sungrab-tulku:c-6-3` glosses "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ" (homage) itself as "bowing respectfully to the object of homage, venerable golden-hued Tārā" — a brief structural/word-gloss rather than a further verse quotation; recorded here as part of the structural material for this facet rather than a fresh doctrinal point.

## Iconography — body colour ("golden-blue")

### Consensus
Six commentaries describe her **body** (not merely her lotus) as golden-yellow with a bluish tinge.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3` (6 commentaries)

Of these, Anon-Utpala alone specifies the hue with a particular simile — "like the gold of the Jambu river" (འཛམ་བུ་ཆུ་བོའི་གསེར་ལྟར) — rather than plain gold; re-read against the other five claims in the Consensus list, none of them states this river simile, so it is not presented as shared by all six, per Rule 9. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`

### ⚑ Divergences
The corpus does not agree on how the compound "gser/ser sngo" (gold/yellow-blue) distributes across body and lotus, nor on the exact hue:

- **Position 1 — gold describes the body; blue describes the lotus she holds, not the body itself:** Konchok-Thabkhe, Palden-Sherab, and Sangye-Nyentrul each describe her body as simply golden, and separately describe the lotus in her hand as blue — without stating that her body itself carries a blue tinge.

  — attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-1` (3 commentaries)

- **Position 2 — both gold and blue describe the lotus, not her body at all:** Taranatha's heading and word-gloss read "gser sngo" as "gold[-stemmed], blue[-petaled]," describing the lotus's stem and flower colour rather than Tārā's own complexion; no separate body-colour statement appears in this commentary's claims for this verse.

  — attested: `taranatha:c-3-1`, `taranatha:c-3-3` (1 commentary)

- **Position 3 — green complexion (blue and yellow read as mixing):** Tsultrim-Namdak glosses "possessed of a green body-colour," explaining that blue and yellow "mixed together" produce green.

  — attested: `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (1 commentary)

- **Position 4 — reddish tinge rather than blue:** Gendun-Gyatso's gloss describes her as "somewhat reddish in hue," rather than blue-tinged.

  — attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1` (1 commentary)

- **Position 5 — "blue-green" tinge:** Tenzin-Dhonzang describes her complexion as "tinged slightly blue-green" rather than plain blue.

  — attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-3` (1 commentary)

These five positions were re-read against the Consensus wording above and against each other; none restates the majority "gold body with blue tinge" formula closely enough to be folded into it, per Rule 9.

### Unique
Drakpa-Gyaltsen's gloss addresses only the "gold" component of the compound — "her complexion is clear like the light of refined gold" — without any statement about a blue (or other) tinge, so it is recorded separately rather than counted toward either the Consensus or any Divergence position above. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-12`

Gendun-Drub's own gloss of "blue and gold" construes the compound differently from the shared Consensus formula above: it makes blue the primary body-colour descriptor ("སྔོ་བ་ནི། སྐུ་མདོག" — "blue [is] her body colour") and treats gold as indicating a separate, cross-referenced quality ("gold indicates a clear/translucent hue," attested via the epithet "Golden-coloured Tārā" used elsewhere) rather than as a second body-colour term alongside blue. This inverts which colour is treated as the primary body-descriptor and which as a cross-referenced epithet-quality, relative to the Consensus formula above, so this claim is not folded into that count. See "Epithets and identity," below, where the same claim is cited for its epithet content. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-3`

## Iconography — hand-emblem (lotus / utpala)

### Consensus
Commentaries agree that her hand is adorned by a water-born lotus (padma) or blue utpala flower, its petals opened and blossoming.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-7`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-13`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-18`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `taranatha:c-3-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (15 commentaries; Drakpa-Gyaltsen contributes two claims)

Of these, ten specify that it is her **left** hand (ཕྱག་གཡོན) that holds the lotus:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-18`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4` (10 commentaries)

Five of the sixteen additionally specify that the lotus's petals open level with, or facing toward, her ear:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-7`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4` (5 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the base image (a lotus-adorned hand); the remaining commentaries in the Consensus list above simply do not specify laterality or ear-level detail in their routed claims, rather than stating a competing description.

### Unique
Pema-Namgyal alone describes the lotus in botanical/poetic detail beyond the corpus's usual formula — reddish-yellow, gold-sheened filaments, an "ageless" stem full of leaves and fruit — glossed as the source of the epithet "Lovely One" (ཡིད་འོང་མ); see "Epithets and identity," below. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-18`

## The six pāramitās as her cause and domain of practice

### Consensus
Thirteen commentaries state that this Tārā arises from, is identified with, or has made her domain the practice of the six pāramitās (perfections) named in the verse — generosity, diligence, austerity/discipline, peace/wisdom, patience, and meditative concentration.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-22`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `taranatha:c-3-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-12` (13 commentaries)

Anon-Trinle-Char's phrasing describes her as having made "the ten perfections" (ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ） her domain, and Gendun-Drub's phrasing likewise refers to "the ten pure perfections" (ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་དག་པ), even though each lists only the same six items named in the verse. This page notes the numeral discrepancy as it stands in both claims, without resolving it — neither claim explains the "ten," and no other commentary in the corpus uses this numeral for this verse.

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the core relationship (Tārā ↔ the six pāramitās) beyond the distinct schemes recorded separately in "Alternate correspondence schemes," below, which is where genuinely competing correspondence-mappings for the same six terms are collected.

### Unique
Karma-Maitri's and Drakpa-Gyaltsen's word-by-word enumerations of the individual terms (generosity, diligence, patience, concentration) are thinner fragments that do not themselves state the "domain/cause" doctrine explicitly within the routed claim text; they are recorded in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited," below, rather than folded into the Consensus above, per Rule 9.

## Word-gloss: "austerity" (དཀའ་ཐུབ)

### Consensus
Fifteen commentaries gloss "austerity" (དཀའ་ཐུབ) as moral discipline (ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས) — several explicitly via the etymological play that discipline is called "austerity" because it is difficult (དཀའ） to guard, yet she is able (ཐུབ) to guard it.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-17`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `taranatha:c-3-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (15 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Konchok-Thabkhe alone bundles "austerity" together with "peace" as a single compound term equalling discipline ("དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས"), rather than glossing "austerity" alone as discipline and "peace" as a separate item (see "Word-gloss and divergence: peace," below, and "Alternate correspondence schemes"). Re-read against the Consensus wording above, this claim's own text does not isolate "austerity" the way the other fifteen do, so it is not folded into the Consensus count. (Karma-Maitri's austerity claim, already cited in the Consensus above, was checked against this same possible pattern: it states "austerity, that is, moral discipline — by that, afflictions are pacified," a verb clause giving a rationale for the austerity=discipline equation, and it does not itself name or fold in "peace" [ཞི་བ] as a second verse-word — a different grammatical move from Konchok-Thabkhe's compound-noun bundling of the two verse-words, so it is not counted as a second instance here.)

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3` (1 commentary)

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## Word-gloss and divergence: "peace" (ཞི་བ)

### Consensus
None — the corpus is divided on what "peace" refers to, so no single gloss is recorded as consensus; see Divergences.

### ⚑ Divergences

**Position A — "peace" refers to wisdom (ཤེས་རབ), directly or as wisdom's pacifying function:**

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `taranatha:c-3-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (9 commentaries)

**Position B — "peace" refers to the pacification of afflictions or discordant factors, not to wisdom specifically:** Drakpa-Gyaltsen and Gendun-Gyatso gloss "peace" as the pacifying of afflictions (ཉོན་མོངས་ཞི་བ) in general terms; Gendun-Drub's gloss of the same word instead names a specific list of six discordant factors it pacifies (miserliness, laziness, poor discipline, anger, distraction, wrong views) rather than the generic term "afflictions." All three share the underlying claim that "peace" names a *pacifying* function rather than an equation with wisdom, but Gendun-Drub's specific six-item list is not verbatim identical to the other two's generic wording, so it is recorded as part of the same position without claiming word-for-word agreement.

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-18`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-5` (3 commentaries)

**Position C — "peace" refers to being stainless (i.e., unstained by six specific faults), not to wisdom or affliction-pacification as such:** Tenga-Tulku's claim explicitly glosses "the domain of concentration" as wisdom (aligning with Position A on that separate phrase) but then, addressing the word "peace" (ཞི་བ) itself directly, glosses it instead as her being unstained by six faults matched one-to-one to the six perfections — see "The six perfections as antidotes to six faults," below, for the specific pairing.

— attested: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` (1 commentary)

**Konchok-Thabkhe's own explicit divergence**, recorded as a marked internal tension in its raw claims file, reports that earlier commentaries it surveys split between applying "peace" and applying "field of conduct" (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ） to the perfection of wisdom, while this commentary instead follows a verse of Nyima Sepa identifying Tārā herself with wisdom as the basis of the other five perfections (see "Alternate correspondence schemes," below, for the full content of this position):

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-4` (1 commentary)

**Anon-Trinle-Char's own meta-observation** corroborates that this exact split is visible within its own tradition: "on the meaning of 'peace,' some [commentaries] apply it to the perfection of wisdom, while others take it to mean the pacifying of what is discordant with the ten perfections." This claim does not itself take a side; it is cited here as native, in-corpus evidence that commentators were themselves aware of the Position A / Position B split recorded above.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-8` (1 commentary)

### Unique
Drakpa-Gyaltsen additionally glosses a different word in the verse — "སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ" ("she whose domain [it is]") — as referring to wisdom, keeping this separate from its own "peace" = pacification-of-afflictions gloss (Position B above). This is the same "domain"-word-to-wisdom mapping that Konchok-Thabkhe's divergence (above) reports as one option among earlier commentaries. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-21`

## Alternate correspondence schemes for the six terms

### Consensus
None — each scheme below is attested by a single commentary; see Unique.

### ⚑ Divergences
None as a two-sided disagreement between named positions; each of the three schemes below stands alone against the corpus-wide six-pāramitā consensus recorded in "The six pāramitās as her cause and domain of practice," above, rather than against one another.

### Unique
- **Gendun-Gyatso** offers an explicit alternative ("yang na," "or alternatively") to its own six-pāramitā reading of the same verse (already cited under "The six pāramitās" above): renunciation, patience, meditative concentration, and the wisdom whose object is suchness — described as together constituting the full perfection of the six pāramitās under a different four-term breakdown. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-3`
- **Konchok-Thabkhe** identifies Tārā herself with the perfection of wisdom directly, describing the other five items (generosity, diligence, the peace of austerity/discipline, patience, meditative concentration) as the field of conduct or basis relied upon by that wisdom — a wisdom-as-basis-of-five scheme, rather than treating all six as co-equal parallel terms. This is the claim underlying Konchok-Thabkhe's own explicit ⚑ divergence recorded in "Word-gloss and divergence: peace," above. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`
- **Tsultrim-Namdak**, in a claim drawn from later in the same commentary (bounded by a "Verse 3 quoted" marker, per the packet's routing note), reads the verse's items as the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment (བྱང་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་ཆོས་སུམ་ཅུ་རྩ་བདུན) rather than the six pāramitās — even though this commentary's own earlier claim on this same verse (`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-12`, cited under "The six pāramitās" above) reads the identical items as the six perfections. This page does not resolve the tension between the two readings; both are carried through transparently as this commentary's own claims, per Rule 6. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`

## The six perfections as antidotes to six faults

### Consensus
Two commentaries — Sungrab-Tulku and Tenga-Tulku — independently give the same one-to-one pairing of each of the six perfections to the specific fault it counters: generosity/miserliness, discipline/poor conduct (or immorality), patience/anger, diligence/laziness, concentration/distraction, and wisdom/delusion (or faulty knowledge). Both claims were re-read in full to confirm the six pairings genuinely match across the two commentaries (allowing for the two claims listing the six pairs in a different order).

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None between the two commentaries above — their pairings were checked and correspond exactly, fault-for-fault and perfection-for-perfection.

### Unique
Gendun-Drub's gloss of "peace" (already cited under "Word-gloss and divergence: peace," above) states that "peace" pacifies a list of six discordant factors — miserliness, laziness, poor discipline, anger, distraction, wrong views — but, unlike the two-commentary Consensus above, does not distribute these one-to-one across all six perfections; it ties the entire list to the single word "peace." Re-read against the Consensus statement above, it supports only a partial, related idea (that the perfections as a set counter negative qualities) rather than the full one-to-one pairing scheme, so — per Rule 9 — it is recorded here as a related but independently-standing claim rather than folded into the two-commentary count. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-5`

## Esoteric two-stage (generation-stage / completion-stage) reading

### Consensus
None — no two commentaries independently give a matching tantric-stage reading for this verse (contrast the two-commentary correspondence found for the prior homage in this corpus); each commentary's material below is recorded as its own contribution.

### ⚑ Divergences
None between the commentaries below — they do not contradict one another; rather, each develops a distinct layer or a distinct visualization, and no commentary states that its own reading excludes another's.

### Unique
**Palden-Sherab** gives the corpus's most extensive tiered reading, moving through four levels of meaning for this verse:
- a literal/word-meaning level (already cited under "Iconography — body colour" and "hand-emblem," above, for its plain description); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`
- a general/doctrinal level identifying her mastery of the six perfections and the ten powers as Bhagavatī Tārā (already cited under "The six pāramitās," above, and under "Epithets and identity" and "Other unique doctrinal notes," below); `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-3`
- a generation-stage visualization: yellow "Serma," lotus-and-moon seat, archer's posture, right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left holding an utpala topped by a wish-fulfilling jewel raining down whatever is desired, light rays streaming from her body with the radiance of a rising sun, while one meditates on gaining mastery of bodhisattva conduct and the ten powers; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-2-1`
- a completion-stage-with-marks ("hidden meaning") reading mapping the six perfections onto the union of the secret lotus and the vajra-hand, bliss, clarity-emptiness, and freedom from self-grasping; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-2`
- a completion-stage-without-marks ("ultimate meaning") reading in which the six perfections and the paths/bhūmis become spontaneously complete within self-arisen, contrivance-free wisdom, and Tārā is realized as ground-expanse Samantabhadrī; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-1`
- a citation from the *Sūtra of the Questions of Brahmaviśeṣacintin* giving the ultimate, non-referential form of each of the six perfections (non-grasping is generosity, non-guarding is discipline, non-abiding is patience, non-striving is diligence, non-thinking is concentration, non-referencing is wisdom). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2`

**Taranatha** gives a comparable but independent "hidden meaning" (གསང་དོན/སླས་དོན) reading, mapping the union of the water-dwelling lotus and the vajra-hand-sign to discipline/austerity, the bliss of concentration, and the wisdom that pacifies conceptuality through realizing emptiness, followed by diligence, patience, and generosity read in the same register, concluding that the verse "teaches the conduct of secret mantra."

— attested: `taranatha:c-3-6`, `taranatha:c-3-7`, `taranatha:c-3-8` (1 commentary, 3 claims)

**Sangye-Nyentrul** gives its own generation-stage visualization, related in theme to Palden-Sherab's (a wish-fulfilling jewel atop the utpala, granting all that is desired; light rays radiating to pervade space) but independently worded and not identical in its specific mudrā and posture details.

— attested: `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-1-3` (1 commentary, 2 claims)

These three commentaries' generation-stage material shares the motif of a wish-granting jewel and radiating light on the utpala, but the claims were re-read and do not restate each other's wording closely enough to be presented as a single corroborated consensus statement; the thematic resemblance is noted here as an observation of this page, not as a claim made by any one of them (per Rule 13).

### Unique

**Tenzin-Dhonzang** gives a word-by-word etymology of "ཕྱག་འཚལ" (homage) itself: "ཕྱག" as dispelling obstacles to the power of virtue in accomplishing Dharma, and "འཚལ" as drawing to oneself the full accumulation of favourable conditions for accomplishing Dharma — distinct from the corpus's other, plainer glosses of "homage" as simple respectful bowing (e.g. Sungrab-Tulku's `sungrab-tulku:c-6-3`, already cited under "Root-verse quotation," above).

## Other unique doctrinal notes

### Consensus
None — each note below is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None.

### Unique
- **Konchok-Thabkhe** notes that the order of the six perfections in this verse does not quite match their well-known standard order, explaining that the first three generally belong to the accumulation of merit and the last to the accumulation of wisdom, with diligence belonging to both — so moving diligence earlier in the verse's list, while keeping the rest in order, is not considered a fault. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-5`
