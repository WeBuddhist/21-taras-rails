# Goddess (as a generic/class term, distinct from Tārā) — ལྷ་མོ།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that
> pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and *goddess as a
> generic term* is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead, following the same
> convention as the sibling global topics `deva`, `mantra`, `gdon`, `mara`,
> `vetala`, `wheel`, `pacification`, `three-jewels`, `ushnisha`, `hum-syllable`,
> `tara-mantra`, `indra`, `brahma`, `agni`, and `vayu`. No deterministic
> packet-assembly script exists for it, so the gather step was performed
> directly by the consolidating agent: `rg -c 'ལྷ་མོ'` across all sixteen files
> at `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` (142 claim-block hits total), followed
> by extracting and manually reading **every single matching claim block in
> full**, in every one of the sixteen files, with no sub-agent fan-out. Every
> quotation below was copied character-for-character from that block's own
> **བོད་ཡིག:** field — never retyped or reconstructed from memory.
>
> **Scope boundary (per the task brief, strictly applied) — and what the gather
> actually found.** ལྷ་མོ ("the goddess") is this corpus's single most common
> standing address and epithet for Tārā herself — every one of the twenty-one
> homages calls her ལྷ་མོ repeatedly in exegesis, and several commentaries
> (`yama-sonam`, `tenzin-dhonzang`, `tsultrim-namdak` above all) use it as
> their default third-person reference to her throughout. Of the 142 claim-block
> hits, the overwhelming majority — including all 21 hits in
> `yama-sonam`, all 20 in `tenzin-dhonzang` bar one, all 24 in
> `tsultrim-namdak`, and effectively every hit in `drakpa-gyaltsen`,
> `gendun-drub`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`,
> `pema-namgyal`, `sungrab-tulku`, `tenga-tulku`, and `dharmabhadra` — are Tārā
> self-reference and were excluded on inspection: `Referent: FIG-1 (stated/node)`
> in the claim's own tagging, where FIG-1 is registered as Tārā (སྒྲོལ་མ /
> རྗེ་བཙུན་མ) in that file's own Grounding index. This includes cases where a
> claim names one of Tārā's own twenty-one aspect-forms by an epithet built on
> ལྷ་མོ (e.g. `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-1-1`'s "Goddess of Knowledge Who Subjugates,"
> `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-1-1`'s "Radiant Goddess," `konchok-thabkhe:c-1-3-4`'s and
> `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-2`'s "wisdom goddess") — these are still Tārā under a homage-specific
> name, not a different deity, and are excluded on the same basis (each is
> already the primary content of its own `tara-NN.md` spine-slot page: `tara-05`,
> `tara-21`, `tara-20` respectively).
>
> After excluding every Tārā self-reference, **nine claims across five
> commentaries** contain content that is genuinely about ལྷ་མོ referring to
> someone other than Tārā, or functioning as a true class/structural term. This
> is a real but thin result — the corpus is not organised around any goddess
> besides Tārā, so what remains are incidental mentions (a Sarasvatī aside, a
> reincarnation-correspondence list, the stock earth-goddess of the directional-
> guardian list, one nature-deity kenning already partly covered on `deva.md`,
> and one commentary's naming of Tārā's own attendant retinue) rather than a
> body of doctrine about "goddesses" as such. Eleven of the sixteen commentaries
> are silent on this narrow scope even though most of them use ལྷ་མོ dozens of
> times for Tārā herself.

## Questions asked

1. Does ལྷ་མོ ever name a specific female deity other than Tārā anywhere in this corpus, and which ones?
2. Among the standard set of directional/earth-guarding deities (otherwise male: fire-god, wind-god, Yama, Rākṣasa, Kubera, Īśāna, water-god), is the earth-guardian consistently gendered female (ལྷ་མོ), and how do commentaries name her?
3. Does ལྷ་མོ appear as a classificatory/poetic epithet for a subtype of nature-deity (e.g. lake-goddesses) distinct from Tārā, paralleling the mountain-/tree-god pattern already treated on `deva.md`?
4. Does any commentary describe a named or numbered entourage of goddesses (as opposed to a single deity) surrounding Tārā, and are the entourage members themselves called ལྷ་མོ?
5. Which ལྷ་མོ occurrences are properly out of scope for this page because they refer to Tārā herself, and how should that boundary be drawn given the term's near-universal use for her throughout the corpus?

## Named goddesses other than Tārā mentioned incidentally

### Consensus
No consensus is possible — each attestation names a different figure, in a different narrative or doctrinal context, with no overlap between commentaries. Recorded as two separate Unique attestations below.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-0-18`, in the commentary's own opening homage-and-lineage narrative, names Sarasvatī (དབྱངས་ཅན་ལྷ་མོ, "the melodious goddess") as having manifested in human form specifically to help compile Padmasambhava's secret teachings: "སངས་རྒྱས་པདྨའི་གསང་ཆེན་བཀའ་བསྡུའི་ཆེད། དབྱངས་ཅན་ལྷ་མོ་སླར་ཡང་མི་གཟུགས་སྤྲུལ།" — "For the sake of compiling Buddha Padma[sambhava]'s secret teachings, the goddess Sarasvatī again manifested in human form." This is unrelated to Tārā and to the twenty-one homages proper; it belongs to the commentary's own lineage-history preamble.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-21` quotes a reincarnation-correspondence list (mapping bodhisattvas and worthies of a former buddha's retinue onto figures of Śākyamuni's own retinue) that names two further female figures by the title ལྷ་མོ, neither of them Tārā: "...འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ལྷ་མོ་ཆེན་མོ་སྒྱུ་འཕྲུལ་དུ་གྱུར་ཏོ། །དཔལ་ལྡན་ལྷ་མོ་ནི་གྲགས་འཛིན་ནོ། །..." — "...Avalokiteshvara [World-Lord] became the great goddess Māyā [Queen Māyādevī, the Buddha's mother]; glorious Śrīdevī (དཔལ་ལྡན་ལྷ་མོ) became Grags-'dzin [Yaśodharā]..." Both "the great goddess Māyā" and "glorious Śrīdevī" are titles built on ལྷ་མོ for named Buddhist female figures distinct from Tārā, occurring in a purely genealogical aside with no other connection to the twenty-one homages.

## The earth-goddess as the one female member of the standard directional-guardian list

### Consensus
Four commentaries name an "earth-goddess" (སའི་ལྷ་མོ / ས་ཡི་ལྷ་མོ, sometimes with the proper name བསྟན་མ, "Tenma") as the guardian of the earth or nadir within the standard list of directional/elemental guardian deities that Tārā's mantra or activity is said to summon or that otherwise appears in her exegesis — she is consistently the sole female-gendered figure among otherwise male guardians (fire-god, wind-god, Yama, Rākṣasa, Kubera, Īśāna, water-god, Brahmā):

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-5`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1`, `taranatha:c-11-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` (4 commentaries)

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-5` gives her a specific power and a proper name in a ritual-instruction context (facing west during power-rites): "...སའི་ལྷ་མོ་བསྟན་མ་གླིང་བཞིའི་ས་ལ་དབང་བར་རྒྱུད་སྡེ་ལས་གསུངས་པས་ན་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཅེས་པ་དེ་གཉིས་ལ་སྦྱར།" — "...and the tantra class states that the earth-goddess Tenma has power over the four continents — hence 'Various Lord' applies to these two [the water-god and Tenma]." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-7` places her explicitly within the eight-then-ten-then-fifteen directional-guardian enumeration, as the nadir counterpart to Brahmā at the zenith: "དེ་ལྟར་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བརྒྱད། སྟེང་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བ་ཚངས་པ།འོག་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བ་ས་བདག་སའི་ལྷ་མོབསྟན་མ་བཅས་ལ་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བ་བཅུར་གྲག..." — "Thus [we have] the eight guardians of the directions; adding Brahma as guardian of the zenith and the earth-lord goddess Tenma as guardian of the nadir, these are renowned as the ten guardians of the directions..." (quoted exactly as the source spaces it, including the missing tsheg between ལྷ་མོ and བསྟན་མ). The same commentary's `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` gives the parallel ten-guardian list in an etymological gloss of "homage," again placing her last among nine otherwise male figures: "...མེ་ལྷ་དང་། ཚངས་པ། རླུང་ལྷ། གཤིན་རྗེ། འབྱུང་པོ། གནོད་སྦྱིན། སྲིན་པོ། དབང་ལྡན། ཆུ་ལྷ། ས་ཡི་ལྷ་མོ་དང་..." — "...the fire-god, Brahma, the wind-god, Yama, elemental spirits, yakshas, rakshasas, Ishana, the water-god, and the earth-goddess..." `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1` names her among the hosts Tārā herself is said to be able to summon as servants: "...ས་གཞི་ཆེན་པོ་འདི་སྐྱོང་བའི་ས་ཡི་ལྷ་མོ་དང་། ཀླུ་རྒྱལ་དགའ་བོ་སོགས་དང་...་" — "...the earth-goddess who guards this great earth, the nāga king Nanda ('Joyful One') and others..." `taranatha:c-11-3` names the same class (plural, "earth-goddesses") as summoned by Tārā's vidyā-mantra: "...སའི་ལྷ་མོ་ལ་སོགས་པ།" — "...the earth-goddesses, and so on." (Only the embedded "earth-goddess/-es" clause of `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1` and `taranatha:c-11-3` is cited here per Rule 9 — the surrounding sentence in both is otherwise an address to or statement about Tārā herself, already the primary content of `yaksha.md`, `indra.md`, `mantra.md`, and `tara-11.md`.)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — commentaries vary only in whether she is named by the proper name བསྟན་མ (Tenma) or left as the bare generic "earth-goddess," and in singular vs. plural form, not in her identity or gender.

### Unique
None beyond the four elaborations above.

## Lake-goddesses as a nature-deity subtype

### Consensus
Not applicable — single attestation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-19-4`, glossing the twenty-first homage, names "lake-goddesses" (མཚོའི་ལྷ་མོ) alongside mountain-gods and tree-gods as one of the "special classes of gods" (ལྷའི་ཁྱད་པར): "འཇིག་རྟེན་དུ་ཕན་གནོད་བྱེད་པ་རིའི་ལྷ་དང་ཤིང་གི་ལྷ་དང་། །མཚོའི་ལྷ་མོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་ནི་ལྷའི་ཁྱད་པར་ཏེ།" — "The mountain gods and tree gods, and lake goddesses and so on, who benefit and harm in the world, are the special classes of gods." This same claim is already cited on `deva.md` (facet: "Nature-deities... as a special class of ལྷ") for its mountain-god/tree-god content, alongside `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65`'s parallel wording — but `tsultrim-namdak`'s parallel passage uses the bare word ལྷ (མཚོ་ཡི་ལྷ, "lake-god," not ལྷ་མོ) for the same referent, so only `taranatha`'s own wording specifically genders this class ལྷ་མོ. No other commentary in the corpus repeats a ལྷ་མོ-gendered version of this triad, so it is recorded here as a single-commentary attestation of the term, not a corroborated consensus.

## Tārā's own attendant retinue described as a set of goddesses

### Consensus
Not applicable — single attestation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-2`, glossing the sixteenth homage via a mahāsiddha's verse, names four attendant deities surrounding Tārā collectively as "four retinue goddesses" (འཁོར་གྱི་ལྷ་མོ་བཞི): "...ཕྱག་འཚལ་བའི་ཡུལ་ཡིད་གཞུངས་མ་དང་། དྲན་པ་མ་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་མ་དང་། བློ་འཕེལ་མ་སྟེ་འཁོར་གྱི་ལྷ་མོ་བཞིས་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་བའི་སྐུ་ལས་རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བའི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་མི་ཤེས་པའི་དགྲ་ཡི་ལུས་ནི་རྣམ་པར་འགེམས་མ།" — "...the object of homage is Firm-Mindedness (ཡིད་གཞུངས་མ), Mindfulness (དྲན་པ་མ), Wisdom (ཤེས་རབ་མ), and Increasing Intellect (བློ་འཕེལ་མ) — she [Tārā] is surrounded by these four retinue goddesses, whose joyful light, radiating from her body, crushes the body of the enemy of ignorance." The four named figures are distinct individuals within Tārā's maṇḍala, not Tārā herself, and are collectively labelled ལྷ་མོ by the commentary's own wording; no other commentary in the corpus names or numbers this retinue.
