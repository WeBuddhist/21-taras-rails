## The mantra's full formula and recitation variants

### Consensus
The mantra's core, ten-syllable form is given as:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ།
**English:** OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-6`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-0-2`, `taranatha:c-20-10`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-9`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-11`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-2` (8 commentaries: anon-utpala, gendun-drub, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, taranatha, tenzin-dhonzang, tsultrim-namdak)

Several of the same commentaries additionally frame the OṂ...SVĀHĀ syllables as bracketing the three-syllable vidyā-mantra proper (TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE) taught by Tārā herself: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-7`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-4` (Spang lo chen po's verse rendering, opening and continuation of the same quotation, split across ^0-6/^0-7), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-3`, `taranatha:c-20-9` (identifying "uttering TARA twice" with TĀRE-twice-and-TUTTĀRE, "what was earlier called the speech of the ten syllables"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3` (glossing the mantra syllable by syllable: OṂ bestows siddhi and fortune, TĀRE TUTTĀRE "is the meaning of Tārā's own name," SVĀHĀ pacifies sin and increases virtue).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary in this packet disputes the ten-syllable core formula itself (divergence over an adjacent syllable, "TĀRA" vs. "HĀRA," belongs to the epidemic-verse mantra below, not to this core formula).

### Unique
- `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4` — pairs the peaceful ten-syllable mantra with a distinct *wrathful* name-mantra, OṂ NAMAḤ TARE NAMO HARE HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ, presenting the two as Tārā's "peaceful and wrathful" name-mantras together (also cited under "Dispelling epidemic disease" for its stated effect).
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-11` — states the two root mantras (this peaceful one, and a fourteen-syllable wrathful one) between them generate all twenty-one verses of the praise, "and there are likewise twenty-one instances of homage."
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-2` — situates recitation of the ten-syllable mantra within a wrathful generation-stage visualization, where HŪṂ-light from the heart destroys sorcery-obstacles.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-4` — specifies the mantra is recited "arising at dawn," arrayed around the seed-syllable TAṂ at the heart.

## TĀRE — etymology and meaning

### Consensus
TĀRE is treated as naming Tārā herself.

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏཱ་ར་ནི་སྒྲོལ་མ།
**English:** Tāra means "Tārā."

— attested: `pema-namgyal:c-2-3-2` (1 commentary explicit; partially corroborated by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-3`, whose own verse-quotation addresses her as "Tārā, Tāre, the heroine," and by `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3`, which states "TĀRE TUTTĀRE is the meaning of Tārā's own name" — 3 commentaries in total touching this identification, though only pema-namgyal states it as a standalone gloss)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-1` — glosses TĀRE specifically as "liberator from saṃsāra" (ཏཱ་རེ་། །འཁོར་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ), part of a three-way division of the mantra's syllables (see Q16 above) not corroborated elsewhere in the packet.

## TUTTĀRE — etymology and meaning

### Consensus
TUTTĀRE is glossed as the syllable that dispels fear.

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཡིས་འཇིགས་པ་སེལ་མ།
**English:** Tuttāre — "she who dispels fear."

— attested: `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-7`, `sungrab-tulku:c-26-6` (root phrase re-quoted: "by TUTTĀRE dispels all fear"), `sungrab-tulku:c-26-7` (etymology built on the same phrase); partially corroborated by `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-4` ("by tuttāre she dispels all fear," within Spang lo chen po's verse rendering) and `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3` ("TĀRE TUTTĀRE is the meaning of Tārā's own name") (3 commentaries stating the fear-dispelling gloss directly: pema-namgyal, sungrab-tulku, gendun-drub)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `sungrab-tulku:c-26-7` — gives a second, more specific etymology alongside the "dispels fear" gloss it also attests under Consensus: TUTTĀRE is read as "anguish" (གདུང་བ), likened to a mother's anguished love for her only child, from which the fear-dispelling function follows ("she dispels...the eight fears and sixteen fears"). This "anguish/compassionate-longing" sense is not stated by any other commentary in this packet at the plain-sense level. (See "Completion-stage" below for a separate, tantric "burning" reading of the same Tibetan root གདུང་བ by palden-sherab and taranatha — the page notes the resemblance but does not treat the two registers as one claim, since palden-sherab and taranatha both explicitly frame theirs as *sbas don*, hidden meaning, distinct from the plain sense.)

## TURE — etymology and meaning

### Consensus
TURE is glossed as "the Swift One" (མྱུར་མ).

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏུ་རེ་ནི་མྱུར་མ་སྟེ།
**English:** Ture means "the swift one."

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-3`, `taranatha:c-17-3`, `taranatha:c-21-1-8`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1` (10 commentaries: anon-utpala, karma-maitri, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, sungrab-tulku, taranatha, tenzin-dhonzang, sangye-nyentrul; partially corroborated by `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-74`, "her mind is extremely swift," and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-4`, whose verse-quotation on the same phrase reads "by ture she grants every benefit" rather than glossing "swift" directly)

Several of the "swift" attestations elaborate the same point with a simile: `sungrab-tulku:c-11-3` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1` both say she is "swift like lightning" (གློག་ལྟར་མྱུར་བ), assuming a terrifying form to protect beings from frightening deluded appearance — a shared elaboration, not a divergence, since both commentaries agree.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-1` — the utterance "ture" itself, merely proclaimed, protects beings from the great terror of Māra and the like (functional rather than etymological).
- `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-3` — Ture protects from "the eight great fears of speech."
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-8` — glosses Ture as "she who bestows one's own aim, others' aim, and the ultimate aim, all of them."
- `sungrab-tulku:c-26-8` and `sungrab-tulku:c-26-9` — root phrase "by TURE bestows all benefits," with etymology: temporary benefit (wealth, longevity, merit, power) and ultimate benefit (liberation, omniscience).
- `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3` — states simply that "Homage to Ture" teaches the name of the Venerable Lady (naming function, no further gloss).
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-74` — "Ture" as one whose mind is extremely swift and who, with supreme tender compassion, works for beings' welfare "during all six periods of day and night" (also cited under "Protection from spirits, zombies, and yakṣas" for its preceding content).
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-1` — glosses TURE specifically as "liberator from sickness" (ན་བ་རྣམས་ལས་སྒྲོལ), part of the three-way syllable division noted under Q16.

## TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE HŪM fills the three realms and treads the seven worlds

### Consensus
The root verse describing TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE HŪṂ filling the desire, form, and formless realms, and treading the seven worlds underfoot, is quoted or glossed by a wide majority of the corpus:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ཧཱུཾ་ཡི་གེས།། འདོད་དང་ཕྱོགས་དང་ནམ་མཁའ་གང་མ།། འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་པོ་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་མནན་ཏེ།། ལུས་པ་མེད་པར་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་མ།།
**English:** Homage to her who, with the letters TUTTĀRA HŪṂ, fills the desire, form, and formless [realms]; who treads the seven worlds underfoot; who is able to summon [all beings] without exception.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`, `taranatha:c-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3` (10 commentaries: anon-trinle-char, anon-utpala, gendun-drub, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, sungrab-tulku, taranatha, tenga-tulku, tenzin-dhonzang)

The commentaries converge on reading this verse as identifying the mantra-sound (TUTTĀRA) with light-rays radiating from a mantra-garland, and the syllable HŪṂ with compassion inseparable from emptiness, together pervading the three realms: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the plain sense of the verse (a separate, explicitly tantric "hidden meaning" reading of the same verse by palden-sherab is recorded under "Completion-stage" below, not here, per that commentary's own two-layer presentation).

### Unique
- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-4` — reads the verse as identifying Tārā's mind (non-referential compassion, slightly wrathful) together with her speech (the mantra sound), rather than glossing the mantra syllables alone.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4` — glosses the "three realms" terms individually: desire realm as the five classes of hell-beings through desire-gods, "directions" as the form realm, "space" as the formless realm.

## TURE as "Great Terror," destroyer of Māra's warriors

### Consensus
A separate root verse names her TURE, "Great Terror" (འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ), who destroys the warriors of Māra:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུ་རེ་འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ།། བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་མ།།
**English:** Homage to Ture, the greatly fearsome one, who utterly destroys the māra-heroes.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-49`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-2-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-2`, `taranatha:c-8-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11` (12 commentaries: anon-utpala, drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, konchok-thabkhe, palden-sherab, pema-namgyal, sungrab-tulku, taranatha, tenga-tulku, tenzin-dhonzang, tsultrim-namdak)

Several commentaries add that the terrifying form is adopted deliberately, without her mind ever leaving peaceful dharmadhātu, in order to protect beings: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1`, `taranatha:c-8-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3` — identifies "the māras' champions" specifically as the afflictions (the four māras), and her wrathful face as slaying, with their latent imprints, both the afflictive and the cognitive obscurations.
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-2` — glosses "great terror" as her protecting beings from "the eight great terrors" through the vidyā-mantra "ture."
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28` — attaches a distinct mantra form, OṂ TURE SVĀHĀ, whose "great, fearsome might" instantly destroys the four māras' warriors and retinues.
- `taranatha:c-8-3` — the mantra recited is OṂ TURE, by whose power Tārā manifests in the terrifying form from the "naturally peaceful expanse."
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-4` — merely the Venerable Mother's mouth proclaiming the utterance OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ dispels great terror (འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ), on the same node as `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-2` above. (Not a claim about destroying obscurations — that content belongs to `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-4` alone, per the "Other benefits" facet above.)

## Ture's feet-stamping shakes the three worlds

### Consensus
A further root verse describes Ture's feet stamping the ground — the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ (or HAṂ) — causing Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya, and the three worlds, to shake:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུ་རེའི་ཞབས་ནི་བརྡབས་པས། ། ཧཱུཾ་གི་རྣམ་པའི་ས་བོན་ཉིད་མ། ། རི་རབ་མནྡྷར་དང་འབིགས་བྱེད། ། འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རྣམས་གཡོ་བ་ཉིད་མ།
**English:** Homage to her who, by the stamping of Ture's feet, is herself the seed of the form of HŪṂ; who herself shakes Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya, the three worlds.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-85`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-46`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-2` (12 commentaries: anon-trinle-char, anon-utpala, drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, gendun-gyatso, konchok-thabkhe, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, pema-namgyal, sungrab-tulku, tenga-tulku, tenzin-dhonzang)

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-85` reads this verse as the point at which she is generated in her wrathful-body form; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-1` and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-46` similarly read it as her wrathful iconographic generation, with the HŪṂ-seed as her heart-life-force syllable.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2` — reads the mountain-shaking specifically as subduing bands of thieves, noting "both the paṇḍita and the siddha find this section readily applicable to protection from thieves on the road."
- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-17-4` — the shaking specifically "terrifies" the worlds resting on the three mountains' slopes, as a further elaboration of the effect.

## Subjugating Māra and worldly gods through mantric laughter

### Consensus
A third root-verse group describes her joyous, crown-adorned laughter — the syllable TUTTĀRA (or, per some commentaries, the doubled TĀRA) — bringing Māra and the world under her power:

**བོད་ཡིག:** བཞད་པ་རབ་བཞད་ཏུ་ཏྟཱ་ར་ཡིས།། བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་དུ་མཛད་མ།།
**English:** ...who, greatly laughing "tuttāra," brings the māras and the world under power.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-62`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-33`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`, `taranatha:c-10-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-36`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` (15 commentaries: anon-trinle-char, anon-utpala, drakpa-gyaltsen, gendun-drub, karma-maitri, konchok-thabkhe, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, pema-namgyal, sangye-nyentrul, sungrab-tulku, taranatha, tenzin-dhonzang, tsultrim-namdak)

The commentaries specifically naming the māra subjugated concur it is the desire-realm lord Māra Paranirmitavaśavartin (variously "Dgaʼ-rab-dbang-phyug"): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-1-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-36`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4` — names the laughing syllable as "TĀRA" rather than "TUTTĀRA," glossing "the laughing 'TĀRA' sound subdues the māras and the eight great worldly gods." This is a difference in which specific syllable the commentary quotes from the root verse's laughter-line, not a disagreement about the effect described — the claim is retained under Consensus above for the shared effect, and noted here only for this wording detail; it names "the eight great worldly gods" as the subjugated party where most other commentaries name Māra Paranirmitavaśavartin (see the Consensus paragraph above).
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3` — extends the subjugated party to include "the tīrthika world that have become dark forces," alongside Māra.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` — the same laughter subjugates a longer list: Brahmā, Indra, gods, nāgas, human kings, queens, ministers, and householders, "granting the siddhis that subjugate all to the faithful."
- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-5` — explicitly derives her epithet "She Who Draws the Māras and the World under Power" (བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་སྡུད་མ) from this two-fold action (laughter and the earlier feet-stamping).

## Dispelling epidemic disease

### Consensus
A root verse pairs the doubled syllable HARA/HĀRA with TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE to dispel the most virulent epidemic disease:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཧཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ཡིས། ། ཤིན་ཏུ་དྲག་པོའི་རིམས་ནི་སེལ་མ།
**English:** ...who, by uttering "hāra" twice and by TUTTĀRE, dispels the most virulent epidemic.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-2-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-21-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4`, `taranatha:c-20-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-7` (12 commentaries: anon-trinle-char, anon-utpala, gendun-gyatso, konchok-thabkhe, lobsang-dawa, palden-sherab, pema-namgyal, sangye-nyentrul, sungrab-tulku, taranatha, tenzin-dhonzang)

Several commentaries give the extended fifteen-syllable form of this mantra, adding HARA HARA (or HA RA twice) and PHAṬ to the ten-syllable root: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-2-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-4` (cited primarily under "Dispelling poison" below, since its own gloss frames the same fifteen-syllable count around outer/inner poison rather than epidemic), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-20-7`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as a doctrinal disagreement.

### Unique
- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-20-7` — a textual-critical note (see Q17): the Kangyur's own tantra exemplar reads "TĀRA twice" (ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད), which "many commentaries have retained...owing to a widespread scribal error," but the commentary argues, citing Nyima Sbaspa's earlier sādhana and "Rje Dge 'dun grub and others," that "HĀRA twice" is the correct reading — with the "HĀRA-twice" mantra given as OṂ NAMAḤ HARE NAMO HARE SVĀHĀ, distinct from the ten-syllable TUTTĀRE mantra. No other commentary in this packet discusses this textual question directly; each simply proceeds with one reading (most, including `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-5-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4`, `taranatha:c-20-4`, read "HARA" without comment).
- `sungrab-tulku:c-23-4` — attributes the epidemic-dispelling power jointly to two full name-mantras, one "wrathful" (OṂ NAMAḤ TARE NAMO HARE HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ) and one "peaceful" (the ten-syllable OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ), rather than to a single doubled syllable.
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-20-4` — frames the illness dispelled specifically as "the epidemic disease of the karma of lower rebirths and of the afflictions," an explanation it calls "applied to the definitive meaning."

## Dispelling poison

### Consensus
A shorter cluster of claims describes an extended mantra form, doubling TĀRA/TARA and adding PHAṬ, to dispel poison:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏཱ་ར་ཏཱ་ར་ཕཊ།
**English:** TĀRA TĀRA PHAṬ.

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-4` (4 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-2` does not belong in this list: its own extended form (see Unique below) uses a single TĀRA, never doubled, so it does not attest the "doubling TĀRA/TARA" form described here.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-2-2` — gives a different extended form, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SARVA VIṢA TĀRA HŪṂ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ (a single, undoubled TĀRA), recited while visualizing white Great Peacock Tārā and nectar light-rays subduing "stationary and moving" poison.
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49` — notes the mantra may be recited either as "the tantra's own direct teaching," OṂ TARA TARA PHAṬ, or as OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE PHAṬ SVĀHĀ, treating the two as interchangeable for pacifying rabies and snakebite poison specifically.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-4` — counts the form as fifteen syllables (the ten-syllable root plus TĀRA TĀRA PHAṬ) dispelling "outer and inner poison."

## Protection from spirits, zombies, and yakṣas; restoring life-force

### Consensus
A root verse (peaceful power, arraying the "three suchnesses") describes her destroying hosts of spirits (gdon), zombies (ro langs), and yakṣas (gnod sbyin):

**བོད་ཡིག:** གདོན་དང་རོ་ལངས་གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་རྣམས།
**English:** hosts of spirits, zombies, and yakṣas.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-6-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-74`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` (5 commentaries: palden-sherab, tenga-tulku, tenzin-dhonzang, tsultrim-namdak; `palden-sherab` contributes two claims). Of these, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7` supports only two of the three terms — zombie-sorcery and yakṣas — and does not itself use the term for spirits (gdon); see its own Unique entry below.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` specifically describe the mantra-light instantly recalling the life-force (bla tshe) of the sick, stolen by these classes of spirits.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7` — attributes this specifically to the ten-syllable mantra "OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ," swiftly destroying zombie-sorcery and yakṣas "who steal one's vital luster."
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` — ties the protection to a three-seed-syllable visualization (white OṂ, red ĀḤ, blue HŪṂ at crown, throat, and heart) rather than to the TĀRE/TUTTĀRE/TURE syllables themselves, alongside noting "mere prayer to her recalls" the stolen life-force.

## Other benefits attributed to mantra recitation

### Consensus
A narrow point of agreement: reciting or merely proclaiming the mantra destroys sin or obscuration.

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་ཤེས་བྱའི་སྒྲིབ་པ་
**English:** the afflictive and cognitive obscurations.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-8` (2 commentaries: anon-trinle-char, tenzin-dhonzang)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-3` — Tārā herself taught the vidyā-mantra "to destroy great sins and the like" (verse 34 context).
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-43` — the heart mantra TĀRE TUTTĀRE, recited "countless myriads...of times," pacifies the ten unwholesome deeds and the five crimes without interval, together with their bodily and mental results.
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-54` — a threefold repetition of TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE is "capable of accomplishing all activities," arranged specifically to pacify illness, spirits, and misdeeds/obscurations together.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-6` — the mantra's "chief enlightened activity is dispelling the suffering of poverty and destitution."
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-4` — visualization with the mantra purifies the three sufferings, six saṃsāric faults, and eight sufferings, which "exit in the form of soot and thick black smoke."
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-4` — reciting the mantra as "armor" (through the three perceptions of deity, mantra, and dharmakāya) dispels quarrels from others and bad dreams.

## Completion-stage and "hidden meaning" (sbas don) readings of the syllables

### Consensus
Both commentaries that undertake tantric completion-stage exegesis of this mantra converge on one point: TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE's hidden meaning is "burning" (གདུང་བ), specifically the fire of tummo/caṇḍālī.

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ནི་གདུང་བ་སྟེ་གཏུམ་མོའི་མེའོ།
**English:** "Tuttāra" is the burning heat, that is, the fire of tummo.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1`, `taranatha:c-10-11`, `taranatha:c-20-14`, `taranatha:c-5-7` (2 commentaries: palden-sherab, taranatha)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on this specific point — both commentaries agree TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE means "burning"/tummo-fire in the hidden-meaning register. They diverge, however, in how far they extend the completion-stage apparatus: palden-sherab elaborates a full channel/cakra/drop mapping across several root verses (see Unique below), while taranatha's hidden-meaning glosses in this packet remain brief single-line identifications. This is a difference in *elaboration*, not a contradiction, and is recorded as a distinguishing feature rather than a ⚑ divergence.

### Unique — palden-sherab (completion-stage, elaborated)
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` — on the crown-ornament/laughter verse: the crown's "great joy" is co-emergent bliss stabilized at the crown; the eightfold laughter is the drop's eightfold forward-and-reverse movement through the four cakras; "Māra" is one's own innate conceptual attachment; "the world" is ordinary contaminated craving of body, speech, and mind, brought under power within uncontaminated great-bliss wisdom.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-4-1` — on the feet-stamping verse (completion-stage-without-marks): the stamping foot is unwavering faith seeing the guru as buddha; the HŪṂ-seed is self-arisen wisdom manifest through the guru's pith instructions; the three mountains/worlds shaking teaches the three kāyas' spontaneous presence liberating within self-awareness.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-3-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-4-3` — on the poison-verse (completion-stage with and without marks, respectively): "the divine lake" is the vajra-body; the twice-spoken TĀRA (read as "liberating") liberates from the afflictive and cognitive obscurations and destroys the three channels' poison; in the "without marks" reading, this same liberation is reframed as realization of dharmatā purifying dualistic conceptual "poison."
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-3-2` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2` — on the spirits/zombies/yakṣas verse: the "three suchnesses" are the three visions of the bardo of dharmatā; spirits/zombies/yakṣas correspond respectively to channels/drops/conceptual thought (or, in the second reading, to suffering/karma/affliction); "TURE, supreme" is co-emergent bliss-emptiness wisdom, or — for the sharpest disciples — instantaneous recognition-liberation without meditation.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-4-1` — on the three-realms verse (completion-stage-without-marks): TUTTĀRA HŪṂ is the abiding-nature dharmadhātu bodhicitta, self-arisen awareness-wisdom; "treading" each realm underfoot corresponds to the self-liberation of the five sense-consciousnesses, then of mental consciousness and the afflicted mind.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-2` — on the great-terror verse: bliss-emptiness non-dual wisdom is itself "the great terrifier" (because it terrifies those of inferior fortune); "Ture," swift, names the swift accomplishment of direct realization through this path.

### Unique — taranatha (hidden meaning, brief)
- `taranatha:c-22-1-4`, `taranatha:c-22-1-5`, `taranatha:c-22-1-6` — a three-syllable triplet distinct from the "burning" gloss above: TĀRE is "the wisdom arisen from the dharmakāya"; TUTTĀRE is "the body arisen from the primordial dharma"; TURE is "the great compassion arisen from the ineffable dharma," that is, emanation and enlightened activity.
- `taranatha:c-8-12` — "Ture" in its hidden sense is "swiftly traversing the higher paths" to complete buddhahood.
