# Buddha — སངས་རྒྱས།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that
> pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and *Buddha as a
> foundational epithet/category* is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead,
> like the sibling pages `mantra`, `conqueror`, and `tathagata`. No
> deterministic assembly script exists for this topic, so the gather step was
> done directly: `rg "སངས་རྒྱས"` against all sixteen
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files (150 raw hits before filtering),
> followed by reading every matching claim block (heading through the next
> heading) to judge relevance, copying **བོད་ཡིག** text character-for-character
> from the raw file — never retyped or paraphrased from memory.
>
> **Relationship to `conqueror.md` and `tathagata.md`.** རྒྱལ་བ ("Victor," Skt.
> *jina*) and དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ ("Tathāgata") are honorific epithets for buddhas;
> སངས་རྒྱས is the plain, most literal word for "buddha" itself — the term a
> commentary reaches for when it is naming the category directly (etymology,
> the soteriological goal of practice, "primordially buddha," an honorific
> applied to a living master) rather than an epithet's rhetorical register.
> The three pages' facets overlap in *referent* (the fourth-homage uṣṇīṣa
> verse, "mother of the buddhas," the colophon's Vairocana attribution, the
> Amitābha-in-locks image, the Dundhubhisvara origin narrative) without
> overlapping in the *literal term gathered* — this page cites only claims
> whose own བོད་ཡིག contains སངས་རྒྱས, and cross-references the sibling pages
> wherever the same underlying idea is attested there under a different
> lexeme, rather than re-arguing it here.
>
> **Compounds and proper names excluded.** སངས་རྒྱས is the head of several
> words with a different referent than "Buddha the epithet": རང་སངས་རྒྱས
> ("pratyekabuddha," a distinct class of noble being), and personal names
> built on the syllable (སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་སྤྲུལ "Sangye Nyentrul," ཕ་དམ་པ་སངས་རྒྱས
> "Phadampa Sangye"). Per the gather instructions these are recorded in
> "Excluded as a different sense" below rather than silently included or
> silently dropped.

## Questions asked

1. What does the plain term "Buddha" (སངས་རྒྱས) mean in this corpus as the practitioner's own soteriological goal, and how uniformly do commentaries close their benefit-verse exegesis on this note?
2. Do commentaries etymologize Tārā's own name(s) — "Tārā," "Heroine," "Ture" — through the concept of attaining or bestowing buddhahood, and is this the same claim as her own attainment of buddhahood via the six perfections?
3. Do commentaries identify the praised goddess as "mother of all buddhas" using this term specifically (as opposed to the parallel epithet རྒྱལ་བ treated on the sibling page `conqueror`), and in what wording?
4. Do any commentaries describe Tārā as the embodiment of all buddhas' enlightened activity, as distinct from being their mother/source?
5. How do commentaries narrate Tārā's origin — from Avalokiteśvara's tears, and from her past life before the Buddha Dundhubhisvara — and how does each narrative invoke buddhahood?
6. How do commentaries attribute authorship of the praise itself to "the perfectly complete Buddha" Vairocana, and how does this compare to the parallel attribution already treated on `tathagata` and `conqueror`?
7. Does any commentary treat "Buddha" as the first of the Three Jewels, and in what ritual or symbolic contexts does this appear?
8. Is there a genuine divergence in how commentaries map the triad Bliss/Virtue/Peace onto higher rebirth, liberation, and buddhahood?
9. Which commentaries place the Buddha Amitābha within the goddess's matted locks, using this term rather than the parallel term དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ already treated on `tathagata`?
10. Does any commentary read "buddha" as a primordial, already-present nature (ye sangs rgyas) rather than a future attainment, and how does this differ from the goal-oriented sense elsewhere in the same corpus?
11. Is "Buddha" ever used as an honorific epithet for a historical master (Padmasambhava, Śākyaśrī, a lineage guru) rather than for a canonical buddha, and how does this differ from its doctrinal senses?
12. Where does the term appear only as part of a longer, unrelated compound or proper name (རང་སངས་རྒྱས "pratyekabuddha," ཕ་དམ་པ་སངས་རྒྱས "Phadampa Sangye," སངས་རྒྱས་མཉན་སྤྲུལ "Sangye Nyentrul"), and how is this distinguished from the standalone epithet?

## Buddhahood as the practitioner's ultimate soteriological goal

### Consensus
Across the corpus, commentaries glossing the root text's closing benefit-verses converge on the same final term: after temporary benefits (empowerment, protection, worldly attainments), the ultimate result of reciting the praise is stated as "the state of buddhahood" (སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང), reached "ultimately" (མཐར་ཐུག) or "finally":

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-3-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-3-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-8`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-10`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-3-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-14`, `taranatha:c-22-1-24`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-7-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-5-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-6-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-8` (12 commentaries)

The wording is close to formulaic: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-8` glosses the closing line "སངས་རྒྱས་གོ་འཕང་མཐར་ཐུག་དེར་འགྲོ། ། ཐུན་མོང་གི་འབྲས་བུའང་།" — "'One goes to the ultimate state of buddhahood' — [this is stated as] a general result also." `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10` quotes the verse whole: "རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས།། མྱུར་དུ་དབང་ནི་བསྐུར་བར་འགྱུར་ལ། འདི་ལས་ཆེ་བ་ཉིད་ནི་འཐོབ་ཅིང་༎ སངས་རྒྱས་གོ་འཕང་མཐར་ཐུག་དེར་འགྲོ།།" — "the seventy million conquerors will quickly grant empowerment; one obtains something even greater than this, and goes to the ultimate rank of buddhahood." `karma-maitri:c-1-2-10` gives the same idea as bare prose: "མཐར་ཐུག་ཏུ་ཡང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་ཡང་མཐར་ཐུག་པ་དེར་འགྲོ་བ་ཉིད་དོ།" — "ultimately, one goes to the very ultimate state of buddhahood." `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-3-1` gives the identical closing clause with an added description of what buddhahood itself involves: having traversed the ten bhūmis and five paths, one reaches "ཤིན་ཏུ་ཐོབ་པར་དཀའ་བའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་གོ་འཕང་མཐར་ཐུག་པ" — "that ultimate state called buddhahood, extremely difficult to attain." `tsultrim-namdak:c-6-8` closes a wisdom-empowerment aspiration on the same note: "ཚེ་གཅིག་སངས་རྒྱས་འགྲུབ་པར་བྱིན་གྱིས་རློབས༔" — "bless me to attain buddhahood in a single lifetime." `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1` frames the whole benefit sequence this way: temporary common attainments in this life, then "མཆོག་གི་དངོས་གྲུབ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་མཐར་ཐུག་དེར་འགྲོ" — "the supreme attainment leads to the ultimate state of Buddhahood."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every claim in this facet agrees that buddhahood is the ultimate, unsurpassed result of the practice; commentaries differ only in how much of the surrounding temporary-benefit sequence they attach before naming it.

### Unique
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-2` glosses the same benefit-verse's obstacle rather than its goal: "སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་དེའི་བར་དུ་(བཅོད་,གཅོད་)པའི་དུག་ནི་(དྭག་,དྭགས་)པོ་ཆེན་པོ་ལོག་ལྟ་ཉིད་དོ།" — "the poison obstructing progress up to the state of Buddhahood is the great poison of wrong view." This is kept separate from the Consensus statement per the full-statement-support rule, since it attests the goal only as a backdrop to naming its obstruction, not the attainment-claim itself.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-2` gives a related but distinct claim about generation-stage practice: visualizing oneself as the deity is undertaken not merely to invoke blessing but chiefly "མ་འོངས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གཟུགས་སྐུ་བསྒྲུབ་པའོ" — "to accomplish the future form-kāya of buddhahood." Kept separate since its subject is a specific meditative technique's purpose, not the benefit-verse formula.

## Tārā's own name, and her own attainment of buddhahood

### Consensus
Several commentaries independently etymologize Tārā's name(s) by tying them to the concept of attaining, or delivering others to, buddhahood:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-1-5`, `yama-sonam:c-3-1-10`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4`, `taranatha:c-8-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-1-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-47`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-66`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-67` (5 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-1-5` derives "Tārā" (Deliverer) from her having delivered beings and "བླ་མེད་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སར་བཀོད་པས" — "established [them] in the state of unsurpassed complete buddhahood." Its companion `yama-sonam:c-3-1-10` derives "Heroine" (dpa' mo) the same way, from the same three-part etymology of her name: "མཐར་སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་དོན་དུ་སངས་རྒྱས་པ་ཡང་བུད་མེད་ཀྱི་རྟེན་ལ་རྒྱས་པས་ན་དཔའ་མོའམ" — "finally she attained buddhahood for the sake of beings also on a female basis — hence 'Heroine.'" `tenga-tulku:c-1-1-6` gives the same derivation in one line: "སྒྲོལ་མ་ནི་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ས་ལས་སྒྲོལ་བར་མཛད་པས་ནའོ།" — "she is called 'Tārā' because she liberates all beings to the level of buddhahood." `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4` etymologizes "Heroine" (dpa' mo) the same way: "སངས་རྒྱས་གཞན་ལས་འཕྲིན་ལས་མྱུར་མ་དང་བདུད་སྡེ་འཇོམས་པས་ན་དཔའ་མོ" — "since her activity is swifter than other buddhas' and she destroys the host of māras, she is 'Heroine.'" `taranatha:c-8-12` derives "Ture" the same way: "ཏུ་རེ་སྟེ་མྱུར་བར་ལམ་གོང་མ་རྣམས་བགོད་ནས་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐོབ་པའོ།" — "'Ture' — that is, swiftly traversing the higher paths, one attains complete buddhahood." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-66` derives both halves of "Tārā" this way: "སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་འཆིང་བ་ལས་མ་བསྒྲལ་བ་རྣམས་བསྒྲལ་བར་བྱའོ" (having attained buddhahood herself, she resolves to liberate others from the two obscurations); its companion claim `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-67` continues the same derivation toward beings not yet liberated from cognitive obscurations.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — all etymologies converge on the same idea (attaining or bestowing buddhahood), differing only in which name or name-element each derives.

### Unique
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19` give a related but distinct claim — not a name-etymology but an account of *how* Tārā herself became a buddha: through the six perfections. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` states her body arose from generosity, discipline, patience, diligence, concentration, and wisdom, and "ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་སངས་རྒྱས་ཏེ" — "in dependence on the six perfections she became a fully and perfectly awakened buddha." `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19` gives the same list of six perfections as "སངས་རྒྱས་པའི་རྒྱུ" — "the cause of buddhahood" — and derives from it her epithet "Daughter of the Victors." Kept separate from the Consensus etymologies above since neither claim glosses a specific name-syllable; both are about the cause of her own attainment.

## "Mother of all buddhas" — the goddess as source of the buddhas

### Consensus
Independently of the parallel epithet རྒྱལ་བ treated on `conqueror.md`, several commentaries identify the goddess directly as mother of all buddhas using སངས་རྒྱས itself:

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-0-3`, `gendun-drub:c-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-1-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-12`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-5` (5 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-0-3` opens the whole commentary with this identity: "དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀུན་གྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས་མ། །རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་གྱུར་བཅོམ་ལྡན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ།" — "To Bhagavatī Tārā, the activity of all Buddhas of the three times, who is the mother of the Victorious Ones." Its companion `gendun-drub:c-1-2` quotes the source scripture's own voice: "འཇམ་དཔལ་ཡུམ་འདི་ནི་དུས་གསུམ་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་མོ།" — "Mañjuśrī, this mother is the mother of all Buddhas of the three times"; `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-12` gives the same scriptural line as part of its own origin-narrative. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3` explains this as the very reason buddhas pay her homage: she is the essence of Prajñāpāramitā, and "སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ནི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་འཁྲུངས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ནའོ།" — "because all buddhas are born in dependence on the perfection of wisdom." `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6` gives the identical doctrinal route: "སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ནི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་འཁྲུངས་པ་ཡིན་པས" — its transitional claim `sungrab-tulku:c-7-5` frames this as "the reason all tathāgatas or buddhas bow in homage to venerable Tārā." `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4` quotes Gendün Drup's own praise to White Tārā: "འཇིགས་པ་ཀུན་སྐྱོབས་དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི། །ཡུམ་གྱུར་དགེ་མ་ཁྱོད་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།" — "protector from all fears, mother of the buddhas of the three times, virtuous one, homage to you." `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-2`, `c-8-1-3`, and `c-8-1-5` each give iconographic-praise verses repeating the same formula in slightly different wording: "རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀུན་དེ་ཡི་སྲས། །འདོད་སྦྱིན་སྐུ་ནི་འཆང་བའི་ཡུམ།" ("the offspring [and] the mother of all complete buddhas"), "དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱེད་པའི་ཡུམ།" ("mother who gives birth to all buddhas of the three times"), and the same line repeated in `c-8-1-5`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every claim in this facet agrees on the identity, only varying in wording and doctrinal justification (scriptural voice vs. Prajñāpāramitā reasoning vs. praise-verse).

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4` gives a related but distinct claim, already partly attested on `conqueror.md` (its companion `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-2` is cited there): rather than naming her mother of buddhas alone, it enumerates "the four types of noble offspring" she is mother of — "སྲས་བཞི་ནི། ཉན་ཐོས་དང་། རང་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་དང་། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པའོ།" — "the four offspring are: śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and [noble] buddhas." Cited here (not on `conqueror.md`) because this instance of the claim names "buddhas" as one of the four classes using the standalone term, distinct from the compound རང་སངས་རྒྱས ("pratyekabuddha") appearing earlier in the same sentence.

## Tārā as embodiment of all buddhas' enlightened activity

### Consensus
A smaller cluster of claims describes Tārā not as buddhas' mother/source but as the very embodiment of their combined activity:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-1-16`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-22`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-3-2-1` (3 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-1-16` gives the earliest instance of this idea in the packet, explaining why Tārā is non-dual with Avalokiteśvara: "དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཐུགས་སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོའི་རང་གཟུགས་སུ་གྲུབ་པ་ཡིན་ནོ།" — "she is established as the very embodiment of the great compassion of all buddhas of the three times." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-4` states the parallel claim for activity rather than compassion: "སྒྲོལ་མ་ནི་དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་རང་གཟུགས་ཡིན་པས" — "Tārā is the very embodiment of the activity of all buddhas of the three times." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-5` gives an extended parallel, comparing her to Avalokiteśvara (all buddhas' love), Mañjughoṣa (their wisdom), and Vajrapāṇi (their power): "སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་རང་གཟུགས་ལྷར་ཤར་བ་རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཡིན" — "Venerable Tārā is the deity-form arisen as the enlightened activity of all buddhas." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-5` gives the same idea in a mantra-practice context: the HŪM syllable is "སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས་རང་གཟུགས་གཅིག་ཏུ་བསྡུས་པའི་ངོ་བོ" — "the essence in which the enlightened activity of all buddhas is gathered into a single form." `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-3-2-1` ties the same claim to her origin, praising her as "སངས་རྒྱས་ཀུན་གྱི་ཕྲིན་ལས་བདག" — "embodiment of the activity of all buddhas," arisen from Amitābha's lotus-family and Avalokiteśvara's face.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5` gives a related image specific to a hand-mudrā: the three fingers forming Tārā's heart-mudrā signify her as "དུས་གསུམ་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་འདུས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད" — "the embodiment of the union of all buddhas of the three times" — emanation of past buddhas, source of future buddhas, regent of present buddhas. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-22` gives the same embodiment-claim in a closing dedication verse: "དུས་གསུམ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀུན་གྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས་མ།" — "the enlightened activity of all buddhas of the three times."

## Origin narrative: Avalokiteśvara establishes beings in buddhahood, yet weeps for those still suffering

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently narrate the same origin episode with the same causal structure — Avalokiteśvara has already placed beings in buddhahood, yet weeps because saṃsāra is not yet empty, and Tārā is born from his tears:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-4-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-4` (2 commentaries)

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-4` states: "འཕགས་པ་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་གིས་གདུལ་བྱ་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་བཀོད་པར་མཛད་ན་ཡང་། སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་གནས་སྟོང་དུ་མེད་པ་མ་ཟད་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཆེན་པོ་ཡོད་པ་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས།སྤྱན་ཆབ་མང་དུ་བསིལ་བས་ཆུ་ཡི་རྒྱུན་དུ་གྱུར།" — "even though noble Avalokiteśvara established all beings to be tamed in the state of buddhahood, since the abodes of beings were not empty and great suffering still remained, many cooling tears fell and became a stream of water" — from which the utpala flower, and Tārā, arose. `sungrab-tulku:c-4-13` gives the identical structure: "སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱིས་འགྲོ་བ་སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་བཀོད་པར་མཛད་ན་ཡང་། … འཁོར་བའི་གནས་སུ་སེམས་ཅན་ལྷག་ཡོད་པ་མ་ཟད་སྡུག་བསྔལ་སྣ་ཚོགས་པ་མྱོང་བཞིན་པ་གཟིགས་ནས་སྤྱན་ཆབ་མང་དུ་བབས" — "although Avalokiteśvara established sentient beings in the state of buddhahood, seeing that beings still remained in the abodes of saṃsāra, experiencing manifold sufferings, many tears fell from his eyes."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Origin narrative: the Buddha Dundhubhisvara (Drumsound) and the princess's first bodhicitta

### Consensus
This episode is already fully treated on `tathagata.md` under the parallel term དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ (the same commentaries there narrate the identical past-life story). This page cites only the instances where the commentary's own wording uses the plain term སངས་རྒྱས instead:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-13` (3 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-2-1` opens: "སྔོན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྔ་སྒྲ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་བྱུང་སྟེ … སངས་རྒྱས་རྔ་སྒྲའི་འཁོར་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་དང་ཉན་ཐོས་ཀྱི་དགེ་འདུན་སོགས་མང་པོ་ལ་ལོ་དུ་མར་མཆོད་པ" — the princess made offerings "to that Buddha Drumsound's retinue" for many years. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2` gives the same episode: "སངས་རྒྱས་རྔ་སྒྲ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་བྱུང་། … དེས་སངས་རྒྱས་ …" (text continues into the next claim). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-10` and `c-2-1-1-13` continue the same narrative into the prophecy and the Bhagavān's instruction to Mañjuśrī. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-2` narrates the princess's devotion to "the Bhagavan Dundhubhisvara" and her offerings to "སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ཉན་ཐོས་དང་། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་དགེ་འདུན" — "the Buddha's retinue of śrāvakas and the bodhisattva saṅgha."

## The praise's own attribution: spoken by "the perfectly complete Buddha" Vairocana

### Consensus
The colophon-attribution already treated on `tathagata.md` (under དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ) and `conqueror.md` (under རྒྱལ་བ) recurs here in commentaries whose own colophon wording names Vairocana with the plain term:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-20`, `gendun-drub:c-z-1`, `palden-sherab:c-4-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-5`, `taranatha:c-22-1-39`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-0-6` (6 commentaries)

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-20` states: "བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཆེན་པོས་བསྟོད་པ་གསུངས་པ།" — "This praise of the Bhagavatī Tārā was spoken by the perfectly complete Buddha, the great Vairocana." `gendun-drub:c-z-1` closes the whole commentary on the identical formula: "རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱིས་བསྟོད་པ།" `pema-namgyal:c-3-5` and `palden-sherab:c-4-1` both quote the identical closing line, palden-sherab's continuing to name the source tantra ("the seven-hundred-chapter king of Tārā tantras"). `taranatha:c-22-1-39` gives the same attribution with the same "renowned" framing. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-0-6` opens its whole commentary by naming this the praise "made by the perfectly complete Buddha Vairocana himself to Tārā."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every claim agrees on Vairocana as the speaker under this term; see `tathagata.md`'s own Divergences note (from `yama-sonam`) on whether the specific fourth-homage uṣṇīṣa names Vairocana or all Tathāgatas generically — that divergence is reported there, not duplicated here, since it does not itself use སངས་རྒྱས.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Buddha as the first of the Three Jewels

### Consensus
Several commentaries treat "Buddha" (སངས་རྒྱས) as the first member of the standard refuge triad, in both doctrinal-symbolic and ritual-liturgical contexts:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-9-6`, `yama-sonam:c-3-9-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-6-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-7-1-4` (3 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-9-6` explains the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels: "རྒྱུ་སངས་རྒྱས་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་ཆོས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གཉིས་ལས་བྱུང་" — "its cause arises from the two jewels of Buddha and Dharma." Its companion `yama-sonam:c-3-9-7` quotes master Nyima Sbaspa's praise confirming the same triad: "སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས་ལས་ལེགས་སྐྱེས་ཤིང་། ། དགེ་འདུན་ཀུན་སྐྱོང་བརྩེ་ཆེན་མ།" — "well born from Buddha and Dharma; great, loving one who nurtures the entire Saṅgha." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` reads Tārā's own body-speech-mind as embodying all Three Jewels: "སྐུ་དགེ་འདུན། གསུང་དམ་ཆོས། ཐུགས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཏེ་དཀོན་མཆོག་ཀུན་འདུས" — "her body is the Sangha, her speech the sacred Dharma, her mind the Buddha — the union of all Three Jewels." Its companion `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-6` quotes the Cakrasaṃvara root tantra: "བླ་མ་སངས་རྒྱས་བླ་མ་ཆོས། ། དེ་བཞིན་དགེ་འདུན་བླ་མ་སྟེ།" — "the guru is the Buddha, the guru is the Dharma, likewise the guru is the Sangha." `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-0-3` gives the ritual (refuge-taking) context: "སངས་རྒྱས་དང་ཆོས་དང་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་རིན་པོ་ཆེ་རྣམ་པ་གསུམ་ནི་ཐུན་མོང་གི་སྐྱབས་ཡུལ" — "the common objects of refuge are the Three Jewels — Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha." `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-6-1` and its cross-referenced repetition `c-7-1-4` open the "prayer before the field of merit" with the identical formula, adding the Three Roots (guru, yidam, ḍākinī) above it.

## The triad Bliss/Virtue/Peace, mapped to higher rebirth, liberation, and buddhahood

### Consensus
Two commentaries map the root text's triad "Bliss / Virtue / Peace" onto a graded soteriological sequence culminating in buddhahood, citing the same external verse:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-15-5`, `yama-sonam:c-3-15-6` (1 commentary)

`yama-sonam:c-3-15-5` states the tantra's own intent is that "བདེ་མ་མངོན་མཐོ། དགེ་མ་ཐར་པ། ཞི་མ་སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སྦྱར་བ་ཉིད" — "applying 'Bliss' to higher rebirth, 'Virtue' to liberation, and 'Peace' to buddhahood." Its companion `c-3-15-6` confirms this by quoting Nyima Sbaspa: "ལྷ་མོ་བདེ་མས་མཐོ་རིས་སྦྱིན། ། དགེ་མས་ཐར་པ་སྦྱིན་པ་སྟེ། ། དེ་བཞིན་ཞི་མས་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས།" — "Goddess, by Bliss you grant higher rebirth; by Virtue you grant liberation; likewise, by Peace, complete buddhahood."

### ⚑ Divergences
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-2` records the same mapping as a genuine internal divergence within that single commentary's own review of the tradition — **Position 1** (earlier commentaries, her own qualities): the triad is grounded in Tārā's own nature (undefiled bliss, freedom from two obscurations, freedom from harshness) rather than a graded soteriological sequence. **Position 2** (a mahāsiddha's verse, adopted here): "ལྷ་མོ་བདེ་ལས་མཐོ་རིས་སྦྱིན། ། དགེ་མས་ཐར་བ་སྦྱིན་པ་སྟེ། ། དེ་བཞིན་ཞི་མས་རྫོགས་སངས་རྒྱས།" — the same verse in substance as the mahāsiddha-citation quoted in the Consensus paragraph above — grounding the triad instead in what she *bestows*, culminating in buddhahood. `konchok-thabkhe` explicitly adopts Position 2.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Primordial buddhahood / buddha-nature (ye sangs rgyas) — Atiyoga completion-stage readings

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is a single commentary's own recurring exegetical layer.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — palden-sherab explicitly frames this as a "hidden" or "ultimate" doctrinal reading layered atop each verse's plain sense, not a competing literal interpretation (matching the same commentary's practice already documented on `tathagata.md`'s Completion-stage/Atiyoga facet).

### Unique
Eight claims, all from `palden-sherab`, read "buddha" not as a future attainment but as an already-present nature (ye sangs rgyas / gdod nas sangs rgyas), reached each time the ultimate ("mtshan med rdzogs rim," completion-stage-without-marks) layer of that commentary's fourfold exegesis is applied to a verse:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-0-21`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2`

`palden-sherab:c-0-21` opens the commentary praising Padmasambhava's realization: "རིག་རྩལ་དབང་ཐོབ་སྣང་སྲིད་ཡེ་སངས་རྒྱས།" — "having attained power over the play of awareness, [he realized] all appearance-existence as primordially buddha." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-4-1` gives the fullest statement of the doctrine: the three capacities of awareness (knowing, loving, ability) are "ངོ་བོར་ཡེ་སངས་རྒྱས་པ་ཉིད་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར" — "in essence, primordially buddha as the three kāyas." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-4-1` reads the fourth homage's uṣṇīṣa itself through this lens: ordinary deluded winds dissolving is "ཡེ་རྫོགས་ཡེ་སངས་རྒྱས་པའོ" — "primordially complete, primordially buddha," identified with the goddess's own boundless victorious conduct. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-1` gives the devotional correlate of the same doctrine: unwavering faith is defined as "བླ་མ་སངས་རྒྱས་སུ་བལྟ་བའི་འགྱུར་མེད་དད་པ" — "unwavering faith that views the guru as buddha." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-4-1` repeats this devotional framing ("བླ་མ་ལ་སངས་རྒྱས་དངོས་སུ་ལྟ་བའི … དད་མོས") alongside the same primordial-awareness doctrine. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-4-1` and `c-3-1-16-4-5` apply the identical "ཡེ་སངས་རྒྱས་པ" formula to, respectively, the destruction of the transitory-collection view and the maṇḍala of body-speech-mind. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2` extends the doctrine to instantaneous awakening: "ནངས་རྟོགས་ན་ནངས་སངས་རྒྱས། ནུབ་རྟོགས་ན་ནུབ་སངས་རྒྱས།" — "if realized in the morning, one is buddha that morning; if realized in the evening, one is buddha that evening."

## "Buddha" as an honorific epithet for historical masters

### Consensus
None — every claim below names a different master, so no two claims restate the same statement, though the pattern (applying the term "Buddha" honorifically to a post-canonical human teacher) recurs across a single commentary's colophon and historical-transmission narrative, with one echo elsewhere.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Nine claims — eight from `palden-sherab`, one from `tsultrim-namdak` — apply "Buddha" honorifically to a master rather than to a canonical buddha of a pure land:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-0-18`, `palden-sherab:c-0-33`, `palden-sherab:c-2-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-4-4`, `palden-sherab:c-4-5`, `palden-sherab:c-4-9`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-9-7`

`palden-sherab:c-0-33` names Padmasambhava directly: "སངས་རྒྱས་གཉིས་པ་པདྨ་སཾ་བྷ་ཝ་གདན་དྲངས།" — "[they] invited … the second Buddha Padmasambhava." The same epithet recurs as a citation-source across the commentary's own doctrinal quotations: `c-2-5` and `c-3-1-16-4-4` both cite "ཨོ་རྒྱན་སངས་རྒྱས་གཉིས་པ" ("Orgyen, the second Buddha['s] …") introducing quotations from his *Great Commentary on the Mañjuśrī-nāmasaṃgīti*; `c-3-1-1-3-1` cites "སངས་རྒྱས་གཉིས་པ་པདྨ" as one authority behind the Anuyoga completion-stage exegesis; `c-3-2-1-3` cites "ཨོ་རྒྱན་སངས་རྒྱས་གཉིས་པས" for a quotation on faith. `c-0-18` names him "སངས་རྒྱས་པདྨ" in connection with Sarasvatī's re-manifestation to compile his secret teachings. `c-4-4` narrates his historical role in Tibet as "ཁྱད་པར་སངས་རྒྱས་པདྨ་སཾ་བྷ་ཝས" — "especially, Buddha Padmasambhava [entrusted the especially secret … cycles of Tārā]." `c-4-5` applies the parallel epithet to a different master, the Kashmiri paṇḍita Śākyaśrī: "མ་བྱོན་སངས་རྒྱས་བདུན་པ" — "the seventh future buddha." `palden-sherab:c-4-9`'s own scribal colophon extends the same honorific pattern to the author's own teachers: he is described as having served "སངས་རྒྱས་དངོས་ལྟ་བུའི་དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན་དུ་མའི་ཞབས་རྡུལ" — "the foot-dust of many spiritual friends comparable to actual buddhas." `tsultrim-namdak:c-9-7` applies the identical honorific pattern to the Situpa's own root guru, the Karmapa: "སངས་རྒྱས་དངོས་སྣང་ཆོས་ཀྱི་རྗེས" — "from the actual, visible Buddha, the Dharma's lord."

## Buddhas (and bodhisattvas) as venerators who devotedly rely on her

### Consensus
None — only two claims, from two different commentaries, and they address slightly different aspects (a structural division vs. an explanatory reason).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-0-7` divides the peaceful-body homages into three, the third being homage "སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔས་བསྐུར་བའི་སྒོ་ནས" — "via what buddhas and bodhisattvas confer." `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-1` frames an entire structural division of the fourth homage around the same pairing: "རྒྱལ་བ་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་གུས་པས་བསྟེན་ཅིང་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བས" — "the Victorious Buddhas and the great bodhisattvas devotedly rely on her and pay her homage" — dividing the explanation into "the manner in which the buddhas devotedly rely on her" and "the manner in which the great bodhisattvas devotedly rely on her" (the buddhas' half is then explained by `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3`, cited above under "Mother of all buddhas"). ## "Buddha" in canonical and liturgical citations

### Consensus
Several commentaries quote canonical verses or standard Mahāyāna liturgy (chiefly the *Bhadracaryā-praṇidhāna*'s seven-branch practice) in which "buddha(s)" appears as the object of prostration, offering, or request — the same pan-Mahāyāna liturgical register `conqueror.md` documents for རྒྱལ་བ, here attested under the plain term:

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-15`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-95`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-100`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-101`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-3-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-5-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-6-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-1-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-1-2-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-4-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-5-1` (2 commentaries)

`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-1-1` quotes the *Bhadracaryā-praṇidhāna*'s prostration verse: "དུས་གསུམ་གཤེགས་པ་མི་ཡི་སེང་གེ་ཀུན།" — "to all the human lions who have gone [thus] in the three times" — glossing "human lions" as buddhas. `c-5-2-4-2-1-1-1` continues with the same text's dharmadhātu-filled-with-buddhas verse — the identical passage `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-95` also quotes: "རྡུལ་གཅིག་སྟེང་ན་རྡུལ་སྙེད་སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས། … ཐམས་ཅད་རྒྱལ་བ་དག་གིས་གང་བར་མོས།" — "upon a single atom dwell buddhas as numerous as atoms … I believe the entire dharmadhātu is filled by all the victors" (the same verse `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-95` cites under this facet appears independently on `conqueror.md`, cited there for its རྒྱལ་བ half; here for its སངས་རྒྱས half). `c-5-2-4-2-1-2-1` glosses "sugata" (བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་པ) as "སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས" — buddhas who have actualized the resultant state. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-15` quotes a verse on the Buddha's apparent passing: "སངས་རྒྱས་མྱ་ངན་མི་འདའ་ཞིང་། … སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ནི་གདུལ་པའི་ཕྱིར། མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ་ཉེ་བར་སྟོན།" — "the Buddha does not pass into nirvana … for the sake of taming beings, [the Buddha] displays passing into nirvana" — the same idea `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-6-1`'s seven-branch "requesting non-passing" branch enacts liturgically. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-100` quotes the *Sūtra of Offering Lamps* on the merit of faith toward the Buddha; `c-3-101` quotes the *Avalokiteśvara Sūtra* on the merit of a single prostration to a stūpa or image, "as great … as revering countless millions of buddhas"; `c-4-10-5` quotes Atiśa's verse to the Medicine Buddha.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are citations from a shared liturgical/scriptural pool, not competing claims.

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-3-1` gives the confession branch's enumeration of the five heinous acts, one of which is "སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྐུ་ལ་ངན་སེམས་ཀྱིས་ཁྲག་འབྱིན་པ" — "maliciously drawing a buddha's blood." `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-5-1` gives the request-teaching branch addressed to "སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་བླ་མ་དགེ་བའི་བཤེས་གཉེན" — "buddhas, bodhisattvas, [and] virtuous gurus." `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-4-1` and `c-5-2-4-2-5-1` give the rejoicing and further request-teaching verses of the same liturgy, each naming buddhas/victors among the venerated.
