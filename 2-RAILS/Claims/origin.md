# Tārā's Origin Narrative — ལོ་རྒྱུས

## Questions asked

1. Which commentaries give Tārā's origin narrative its own dedicated section (rather than folding it into homage 1), and how do they mark that separation structurally?
2. What narrative sequence do commentaries give for Princess Yeshe Dawa/Jñānacandrā and Buddha Dundhubhisvara (Drumsound) — her devotion, the debate over male/female rebirth, her vow, her attainment, her renaming, and the prophecy?
3. Do commentaries diverge in the details of this narrative — the world-realm's name, who urges her toward a male rebirth, the specific meditation described, or the exact wording of the samādhi's name?
4. Besides the Yeshe Dawa narrative, what other origin accounts for Tārā appear in the packet, and how do they relate to (or differ from) the Yeshe Dawa narrative and each other?
5. Do any commentaries in this packet give an etymology for an epithet in connection with the origin material, and are these etymologies corroborated elsewhere?
6. Does any commentary append recitation-benefit material directly onto its origin/history section, and is this benefit material corroborated by any other commentary's origin-routed claims?
7. Does any commentary give an account of how the Tārā tantra itself (as opposed to Tārā's personal origin) was transmitted into Jambudvipa, and what claims does it make about the tantra's authenticity or compilation?
8. Where a commentary raises a doctrinal question about whether all twenty-one Tārās are one being or twenty-one distinct beings, or whether the tantra's teacher and compiler are of one continuum, does it record more than one position, and are both positions attributed?
9. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's report of two named-but-unattributed positions on whether all twenty-one Tārās are emanations of Khadiravani Tārā, or twenty-one distinct families (tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-11)?
10. Does any other commentary corroborate tsultrim-namdak's report of a second origin account — Tārā's vow to protect beings from all harm (tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-11)?
11. Does any other commentary corroborate tsultrim-namdak's report of a third origin account — Tārā born from Avalokiteśvara's tears, proclaimed by the Buddha as "mother of all buddhas of the three times" (tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-12)?
12. Does any other commentary corroborate lobsang-dawa's origin claim that Tārā arose from a hundred million pollen-stamens of Avalokiteśvara's lotus-face (lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6), and does any commentary explain the relationship between this "pollen" motif and tsultrim-namdak's "tears" motif?
13. Does any other commentary corroborate sungrab-tulku's specific pairing of the wrathful aspect with dusk prayer and the peaceful aspect with dawn prayer (sungrab-tulku:c-2-8)?
14. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's extended doctrinal defence — via reductio arguments and scriptural citation — that the tantra's teacher (Vajradhara) and its compiler (Ānanda/Vajrapāṇi) are of a single continuum (tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-16–c-2-20)?
15. Does any other commentary corroborate the shared detail in sungrab-tulku and tsultrim-namdak that Yeshe Dawa attained tolerance of non-arising while "enjoying the qualities of desire" (འདོད་པའི་ཡོན་ཏན) in the royal palace (sungrab-tulku:c-2-4, tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-7)?
16. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's claim that Avalokiteśvara himself taught a hundred thousand Tārā-mantra tantras, attributed by tenzin-dhonzang to Jetsün Tāranātha (tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-7)?

## Structural placement — this material as its own section

### Consensus
Four commentaries in this packet route material to a dedicated origin/history rubric rather than folding it into homage 1's own exposition, and three of the four use the term ལོ་རྒྱུས ("history"/"origin-story") explicitly in their own section title or opener:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-15` (section-title restatement, "སྒྲོལ་མའི་ལོ་རྒྱུས་མདོར་བསྡུས།", "The brief history of Tārā"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-1` (section heading announcing "ལོ་རྒྱུས་དང་གླེང་གཞི་བཤད་པ", "explaining the origin-story and narrative setting", as the first of a two-part division), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-2` ("དང་པོ་ནི།", "as for the first [praise by way of history]") (3 commentaries)

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-10` marks the same kind of structural break with a forward-announcing heading, "འཛམ་བུ་གླིང་དུ་སྒྲོལ་མའི་རྒྱུད་ཇི་ལྟར་བྱུང་ཚུལ" ("How the Tārā tantra arose in Jambudvipa"), but this heading opens node 2 — the tantra's own transmission history (see "Transmission of the Tārā tantra into Jambudvipa," below) — rather than the Yeshe Dawa narrative itself, which in this commentary's own outline sits inside a prior, unlabelled node 1 (titled in the packet header "དང་པོ་རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མའི་རྣམ་ཐར་", "first, the venerable Tārā's life-story").

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-3` immediately follows its own structural opener with a bare identification of the homage's addressee ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ་གང་ལ་ན་སྒྲོལ་མ་སྟེ", "to whom is homage paid? To Tārā, that is —"), routed here as part of the same section-opening sequence rather than as origin content in its own right.

Three commentaries differ from the other three in *where* this material sits in their own outline: `sungrab-tulku` and `tenzin-dhonzang` (node 1) give it a dedicated top-level node of its own (registered at node 2 and node 1 respectively, per the packet header), and `tsultrim-namdak` nests it as the first of two sub-parts of its own introductory node (2.1.1) — all three outside any homage-1 numbering. `lobsang-dawa`, by contrast, nests its origin material *inside* homage 1's own node numbering (1.2.1) rather than carving out a separate top-level section; this is the same pattern the packet's twelve silent commentaries report for themselves (their origin content sits inside their own homage-1 node), except that lobsang-dawa's spine map still routes its claims to this slot rather than to `tara-01`, presumably because node 1.2.1 is itself explicitly titled by ordinal ("the first [praise by way of history]") in a way the silent commentaries' homage-1 nodes are not.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement — the structural differences above reflect each commentary's own outline granularity, not disagreement about what the material is.

### Unique
None beyond the structural notes above.

## The Yeshe Dawa / Buddha Dundhubhisvara narrative

### Consensus
Three commentaries — `sungrab-tulku`, `tenzin-dhonzang`, and `tsultrim-namdak` — independently give the same core biographical narrative for Tārā's origin as a former princess. All three name the Buddha of that former age identically, རྔ་སྒྲ (Drumsound/Dundhubhisvara), and name the princess identically, ཡེ་ཤེས་ཟླ་བ (Yeshe Dawa):

— Buddha named "Drumsound" attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2` (3 commentaries)
— Princess named "Yeshe Dawa" attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2` (3 commentaries)

Two of the three — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-1` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2` — additionally name the world-realm identically, verbatim: འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་འོད་ ("the world-realm called Various Lights"). `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1` does not name the realm in this packet.

— world-realm name attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-2` (2 commentaries)

All three narrate that the princess made extensive offerings/devotion to that Buddha and his retinue over a vast span of years, then generated bodhicitta and vowed to work for beings' welfare specifically in a female body until saṃsāra is emptied:

— offerings/devotion attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-3` (3 commentaries)
— vow to work in female form until saṃsāra is emptied attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-6` (3 commentaries)

All three then narrate that she attained tolerance/patience of the non-arising of phenomena through meditation, accomplished a samādhi whose name and effect is "liberating beings," and was thereupon renamed Tārā:

— attainment of tolerance of non-arising attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-7` (3 commentaries)
— samādhi accomplished, name changed to Tārā attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-9` (3 commentaries)

All three close this beat with Buddha Drumsound's prophecy that she would bear the name (Goddess) Tārā until she attains unsurpassed enlightenment:

— prophecy attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-10` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
The three commentaries agreeing on this narrative's broad shape diverge on several specific points of wording and content:

1. **Who urges her toward a male rebirth.** `sungrab-tulku:c-2-2` names "བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་དང་ཉན་ཐོས་རྣམས" (bodhisattvas and śrāvakas) as the ones who urge Yeshe Dawa to pray for a male body; `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-3`–`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-4` instead names "དགེ་སློང་རྣམས" (monks). `tenzin-dhonzang`'s parallel claim (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-3`) compresses the whole episode into "she vowed" without narrating an urging episode at all — silent on this specific beat rather than contradicting it.
2. **The exact wording of the samādhi's name.** `sungrab-tulku:c-2-5` names it "སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་འཁོར་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་བ" ("liberating beings from saṃsāra"), while `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-4` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-8` both use the identical phrase "འགྲོ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲོལ་བ" ("Liberator of All [Beings]") — a real wording difference, not merely a translation variant, since `sungrab-tulku`'s own Tibetan omits འགྲོ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད and includes འཁོར་བ་ལས instead.
3. **The meditation preceding the attainment.** `sungrab-tulku:c-2-4` specifies she meditated while "སྐྱིད་སྤྱོད་" partaking of the five qualities of desire — form, sound, smell, taste, touch (འདོད་པའི་ཡོན་ཏན་ལྔ་ ... གཟུགས་སྒྲ་དྲི་རོ་རེག་བྱ་) — in the royal palace; `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-7` shares the phrase འདོད་པའི་ཡོན་ཏན ("qualities of desire") and the palace setting but does not itemize all five senses; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-4` gives no equivalent detail at all, stating only that she attained the tolerance.
4. **The prophecy's scope.** `sungrab-tulku:c-2-6` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-6` both state the prophecy concerns her *name* alone ("she would bear solely the name Goddess Tārā"); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-10` reports a broader prophecy that she would also always take a *female form* as her basis at every stage of the path — an addition not present in the other two claims.

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-5` gives an extended philosophical justification for the princess's vow not paralleled in `sungrab-tulku` or `tenzin-dhonzang`: her reply that ultimately there is "no self, no person, no consciousness" and that "male" and "female" are merely conventional labels, the products of "the deluded confusion of the world's inferior minds."

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-8` additionally specifies that, once the samādhi was accomplished, she would "forgo food" every morning and evening until beings were established in liberation — a practice detail not found in `sungrab-tulku`'s or `tenzin-dhonzang`'s parallel claims.

## Further Avalokiteśvara-derived origin accounts

### Consensus
None as a single statement — the two claims in this packet that trace Tārā's origin to Avalokiteśvara use two different generative images and appear in two different commentaries with no overlapping wording.

*(Framing note, not itself an attestation: the packet's twelve silent commentaries each report, in their own silence entries, that they carry a similar Avalokiteśvara-derived origin account — most describing a lotus/pollen motif, a minority a tears motif — but route it inside their own homage-1 node rather than into this slot. Those entries are silence records, not claims, and are not cited here; they explain why this facet holds only two attestations despite the motif being corpus-wide.)*

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **Pollen from Avalokiteśvara's lotus-face** — `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6`: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་མགོན་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཞལ་གྱི། །གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་ནི་བྱུང་བའི་ལོ་རྒྱུས་...", "she arose from a hundred million pollen-stamens (གེ་སར་) of the lotus-face of Avalokiteśvara, protector of the three worlds." This claim gives no further narrative context in the packet — it is a single origin-verse gloss, not embedded in a larger sequence.
2. **Tears of Avalokiteśvara, with the Buddha's proclamation "mother of all buddhas"** — `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-12`: "འཕགས་པ་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་འཁྲུངས་པ་ཡིན་ནོ", "she was born from the tears (སྤྱན་ཆབ་) of Ārya Avalokiteśvara" — a different noun entirely from `lobsang-dawa`'s གེ་སར (pollen-stamen). This claim continues in the same breath: "the Bhagavān proclaimed: 'Mañjuśrī, this mother is the mother of all buddhas of the three times.'" `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-13` closes the episode with the Bhagavān instructing Mañjuśrī to hold the praise in mind and then uttering it together with its dhāraṇī-mantra — framing this "tears" account as the immediate narrative context for the praise's own first utterance, a role `lobsang-dawa`'s pollen account does not carry in this packet.

No claim in the packet states that these are the same event told two ways, or explains the relationship between the two images; they are recorded here as two distinct, non-corroborating attestations.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-11` records a third, separate origin element in the same sequence — a vow to guard and protect beings from all harm, for which she is said to be "praised above other deities" — presented in the packet as a second origin-account distinct from both the Yeshe Dawa narrative and the tears account above. No other commentary in the packet corroborates this vow.

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-11` (protective vow, above) and the full "tears" sequence (`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-12`–`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-13`) are unique to this commentary within the packet; `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6` (pollen account) is likewise unique to its commentary within the packet.

## Etymologies attached to the origin material

### Consensus
None — both etymologies in this facet come from a single commentary and are not corroborated elsewhere in the packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-4` glosses the epithet "Heroine" (དཔའ་མོ): "སངས་རྒྱས་གཞན་ལས་འཕྲིན་ལས་མྱུར་མ་དང་བདུད་སྡེ་འཇོམས་པས་ན་དཔའ་མོ", "since her activity is swifter than other buddhas' and she destroys the host of māras, she is 'Heroine.'"

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-5` glosses the epithet "All-seeing" (ཀུན་གཟིགས་མ): "དེའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི་སྐད་ཅིག་གི་ནམ་མཁའི་གློག་དང་འདྲ་བར་ཤེས་བྱ་ཀུན་གཟིགས་ཀྱིས", "her wisdom-eye, like an instant's flash of lightning in the sky, sees all objects of knowledge — she is 'All-seeing.'"

Both are routed to this slot at the claim level rather than the node level (per the packet's own ⓘ routing note for `lobsang-dawa`, node 1.2.1's remaining content is treated as its own history/origin material "matching root ^1-1 term for term"); neither etymology concerns the origin narrative itself, and neither is paralleled by any claim from another commentary in this packet.

## Recitation benefits appended to the brief history

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is drawn from a single commentary's continuation of its own history section into recitation-benefit material, with no parallel among the other three contributing commentaries' origin-routed claims.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Immediately after narrating the Yeshe Dawa story, `sungrab-tulku`'s own "brief history" node continues into a block of benefit and practice material, all unique to this commentary within the packet:

- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-7` quotes a tantra on the benefit of remembering Tārā at dusk and dawn — fearlessness, pacification of misdeeds, and freedom from lower rebirths.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-8` specifies that the dusk prayer is directed to Tārā "in wrathful manner" (ཁྲོ་བོའི་ཚུལ) and the dawn prayer to her "in peaceful manner" (ཞི་བའི་ཚུལ) — a paired timing/aspect correspondence not paralleled elsewhere in this packet.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-9` describes temporary benefits (protection from the eight and sixteen fears) and ultimate benefits (pacification of misdeeds, destruction of lower-realm suffering) from one-pointed prayer.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10` quotes a verse on the seventy million conquerors granting empowerment to the practitioner.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11` and `sungrab-tulku:c-2-12` identify the object of this prayer as "Jetsün Tārā, also called Nyurma [the Swift One]," empowered into the state of Mother Prajñāpāramitā by those seventy million conquerors, and restate the temporary/ultimate benefits of praying to her.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-2-13` and `sungrab-tulku:c-2-14` quote and explain a verse on wishes (sons, wealth) being fulfilled and obstructing spirits destroyed through reciting two, three, or seven times with faith.

No claim from `lobsang-dawa`, `tenzin-dhonzang`, or `tsultrim-namdak` in this packet addresses recitation timing, the wrathful/peaceful correspondence, or the two/three/seven-times benefit tradition; this material is recorded as a single commentary's own extension of its history section, not as a corroborated finding.

## Transmission of the Tārā tantra into Jambudvipa and its authenticity

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this entire facet is unique to `tenzin-dhonzang`, whose commentary devotes its own second node (following the Yeshe Dawa narrative) to how the Tārā tantra itself came into this world.

### ⚑ Divergences
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-11` is an internal-tension claim: the commentary itself records two named-but-unattributed positions on the nature of the twenty-one Tārās —
- **Position 1** ("dam pa kha cig," some sages): "དམ་པ་ཁ་ཅིག་གིས་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱང་སེང་ལྡེང་མའི་སྤྲུལ་པ་ཡིན་ཞེས་གསུངས", "some sages say all twenty-one Tārās are emanations of Khadiravani Tārā."
- **Position 2** ("khag cig," others): "ཁག་ཅིག་གིས་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱང་རིགས་མི་འདྲ་བ་ཉེར་གཅིག་ཡིན་ནོ་ཞེས་གསུངས", "others say all twenty-one Tārās are twenty-one distinct families."

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-12` gives the commentary's own practical resolution rather than adjudicating between the two: at the time of supplication, one may visualize either configuration, since either way there is an auspicious connection to being taken under the care of all twenty-one. No other commentary in the packet reports or corroborates either position.

### Unique
The remainder of this node, all unique to `tenzin-dhonzang` within the packet, builds a sustained doctrinal case for the tantra's authenticity:

- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-7`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-9` (bridging from node 1): Avalokiteśvara taught a hundred thousand Tārā-mantra tantras "in this present eon" — attributed by the commentary to Jetsün Tāranātha's own statement (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-7`); these tantras were also "spoken again" by "our own Teacher," as stated in the *Dakini's Secret Bindu* tantra he taught (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-8`); and a further quoted verse locates a tantra as taught by "the Lion of the Śākyas" atop Mount Malaya (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-9`).
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-1`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-7`: an analogy to the sūtra tradition's three councils after the Buddha's parinirvāṇa (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-1`); the secret-mantra tantra classes taught by Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara, and Guhyapati (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-2`); the claim that everyone who heard the mantra vehicle attained siddhi (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-3`), with specific siddhi-types attained by more than a hundred thousand beings over one to two hundred years (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-4`), prophesied as accomplished through Tārā's own compassion (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-5`); the tradition's transmission at Nālandā and Tibet's four great dharma lineages (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-6`); and past masters relying on Tārā as their yidam (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-7`).
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-8`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-10`: a quoted first-homage verse (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-8`); the identification of the Twenty-One Homages as generally renowned as "the praise of Khadiravani Tārā, chief of all Tārās" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-9`), which in detail becomes "the praise of the Twenty-One Tārās" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-10`) — the immediate lead-in to the `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-11` divergence above.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-13`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-14`: a doctrinal aside reconciling multiple Tārā-forms with the tenet that all buddhas are a single continuum (illustrated by an actor changing costumes) (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-13`), with scriptural basis from the *Suvarṇaprabhāsottama Sūtra* for teaching non-ultimate emanations (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-14`).
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-15`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-20`: a sustained argument, opened by a quoted verse that "the Buddha does not pass into nirvāṇa" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-15`), against an objection from Kunga Döndrub's *Guhyasamāja Ṭīkā* 54 that teacher and tantra-compiler must be of different continua since Ānanda and Vajrapāṇi are worldly-renowned as distinct compilers (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-16`); resolved by identifying Ānanda with the bodhisattva Samantabhadra, who is identified with the compiler Vajrapāṇi, who is of no continuum other than the teacher Vajradhara (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-17`); reinforced by two reductio arguments — an ordinary śrāvaka Ānanda could not otherwise have retained all 84,000 dharma-bundles in one hearing (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-18`), nor could an ordinary bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi otherwise have retained the whole of the Vajrayāna teachings (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-19`) — and closed with a scriptural proof from the tantra "The Uncommon Secret, Great Yoga" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-20`).
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-21`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-22`: a quoted correspondence list identifying figures from the Buddha's śrāvaka-vehicle retinue with tantric deities (Mañjuśrī as King Śuddhodana, Avalokiteśvara as Queen Māyā, Samantabhadra as elder Ānanda, Vairocana as Śākyamuni himself, and others) (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-21`), followed by a closing dedication verse invoking "the green-blue, one-faced, two-armed Swift and Peaceful One, the mother holding an utpala flower" (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-22`).

No claim from `lobsang-dawa`, `sungrab-tulku`, or `tsultrim-namdak` in this packet addresses the tantra's transmission history, its councils, its compiler's identity, or the one-being/twenty-one-beings question; this entire facet is recorded as a single commentary's own material.
