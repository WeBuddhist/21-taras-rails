# Tārā 9 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་སོར་མོས། / Homage to She Whose Heart Is Adorned by the Three-Jewels Mudrā

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Where does each commentary place this verse structurally (a global ordinal "ninth", a local "second" within a hand-emblem sub-group, or no explicit ordinal), and how is the root verse itself quoted — in full, split across claims, or only partially?
2. What name, epithet, or classificatory label does each commentary give this homage or this specific form of Tārā?
3. What are the two hand-mudrās described in the root verse, and how does each commentary gloss them (finger positions, what is held, what each mudrā symbolizes)?
4. Is the "wheel" (འཁོར་ལོ) of the root verse a mark on the palm of the right hand, an ornament atop the utpala in the left hand, or a gloss for "the [ten] directions" themselves — and does the light radiate from that wheel-mark, from Tārā's own body, or from both?
5. What is this Tārā's body colour, and how many arms does she have?
6. From what fears or dangers does this Tārā protect, and how many are enumerated?
7. Do any commentaries give a completion-stage or other "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) reading of the Three-Jewels mudrā, and if so, do their readings agree?
8. Is this mudrā characterized as a samaya-mudrā, and does correctly forming it invoke the deity's blessing or actual presence?
9. Does any commentary report divergent liturgical or ritual applications of this verse?
10. Does any other commentary support anon-trinle-char's report that "some commentaries" read the light-rays as radiating from the wheel-mark on the palm while "others" read them as radiating from her body?
11. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-trinle-char's citation of Nyima Sbaspa's sādhana describing a four-armed, red-bodied form holding an Aśoka branch, against palden-sherab's and sangye-nyentrul's two-armed, green-blue-bodied form?
12. Does any other commentary corroborate palden-sherab's and sangye-nyentrul's placement of the "wheel" atop the utpala held in the left hand, rather than on the palm of the right hand?
13. Does any other commentary corroborate the specific sixteen-fears enumeration (eight outer + eight inner, each paired with a symbolic image) shared by palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul?
14. Does any other commentary corroborate taranatha's and palden-sherab's shared "three substances" (seminal fluid, subtle drop, wind) reading of the Three-Jewels mudrā's hidden meaning?
15. Does any other commentary corroborate tenzin-dhonzang's reading of the mudrā as signifying the union of body/speech/mind with Sangha/Dharma/Buddha, the three kāyas, and the three roots?
16. Does any other commentary corroborate konchok-thabkhe's and tenzin-dhonzang's shared theme of connecting this mudrā's iconography to "the buddhas of the three times" — and do they locate that symbolism in the same iconographic element?
17. Does any other commentary corroborate karma-maitri's and pema-namgyal's naming of the left-hand mudrā as a "refuge-granting mudrā" (སྐྱབས་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ), distinct from "Three-Jewels mudrā"?
18. Does any other commentary corroborate anon-utpala's report that this division (and the next) is classified among the "wrathful" homages in its own sa-bcad despite showing no wrathful iconographic feature?

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but they do not all mark it the same way. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the ninth" (དགུ་པ/དགུ་བ) or its numeral (༩/9): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1` (via its node's own ༩ marker), `sungrab-tulku:c-12-1` (marked "༼༩༽"), `taranatha:c-9-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-1`. Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the second" (གཉིས་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the second member of a "praise by way of the right- and left-hand emblems" (ཕྱག་མཚན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1` (these three share the near-identical opening clause གཡས་གཡོན་གྱི་ཕྱག་མཚན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི, with `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-1` character-identical and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1` differing by dropping the connector གྱི་ after གཡོན་ and by ending བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི rather than བསྟོད་པ་ནི). `sungrab-tulku:c-12-1` also uses the "hand-emblems" phrasing but pairs it with the global numeral "(9)" rather than "the second." The remaining three (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match, not by a visible section number.

Ten commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines (either as one continuous block or, in their own extraction, split across two or more adjacent claims):

— full verse quoted attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-57`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-58`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2` (10 commentaries)

These quotations are close but not verbatim-identical: line 3 alone shows three distinct particle readings after འཁོར་ལོ — no particle in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-2`; the instrumental ས (འཁོར་ལོས) in `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-57`; and the genitive འི (འཁོར་ལོའི) uniquely in `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-2`'s own claim title oddly labels its half of the quotation "Verse 10" even though it is routed here by the spine map as verse 9's content — an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's own extraction, not evidence that gendun-drub disagrees about which verse this is.

The remaining six commentaries give only a partial or paraphrastic opening in this packet rather than a clean four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `taranatha:c-9-2` (which explicitly abbreviates with "སོགས", "etc."), and `pema-namgyal` (which does not quote the verse at all in this packet, being routed here by claim-content match).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the ordinal-marker differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad, not disagreement about which verse this is.

### Unique
None beyond the structural notes above.

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name or classificatory label for this homage gives a **different** one; this facet is genuinely a set of divergent traditions, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"Tārā/Protector of the Khadira or Śiṃśapā Forest"** (སེང་ལྡེང་ནགས་ཀྱི་སྒྲོལ་མ), usually paired with a "protects from all fears" qualifier — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-6` (bare epithet, "she is called here 'Tārā of the Khadira Forest'"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-1` + `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-3` ("Tārā of the Śiṃśapā Forest, Protector from All Assembled Fears": འཇིགས་པའི་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པའི་སེང་ལྡེང་ནགས་ཀྱི་སྒྲོལ་མ), `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-2` + `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-1` ("Tārā of the Khadira Forest... protector from the eight/sixteen [fears]"; its qualifier reads འཇིགས་པའི་ཚོགས་ཀུན་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པའི, using ཀུན rather than ཐམས་ཅད for "all" — a real lexical variant from palden-sherab's phrase, not the same words), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12` ("Sgrol-ma of the Khadira/Acacia forest," qualifier འཇིགས་པའི་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་སྐྱོབ་པར་མཛད་མ, matching palden-sherab's ཐམས་ཅད wording rather than sangye-nyentrul's ཀུན). Four commentaries in total: `gendun-drub`, `palden-sherab`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `tsultrim-namdak`.
2. **Named via the Three-Jewels mudrā/theme itself**, but not identically: `taranatha:c-9-1` ("Ninth, homage to [she of] the Three Jewels," དཀོན་མཆོག་) and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-1` ("She Who Signifies the Three Jewels," དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ལ) both use the verb མཚོན ("signify/symbolize"); `sungrab-tulku:c-12-3` instead calls her "venerable Tārā, the Worshipper of the Three Jewels" (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཆོད་མ), using མཆོད ("worship/make offering to") — a different root entirely, naming her as a devotee rather than as the embodiment/symbol of the Three Jewels. Three commentaries, two distinct sub-readings.
3. **"Bestower of the Supreme"** (མཆོག་སྩོལ་མ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-1`. Unique to this commentary; it does not use the Khadira/Śiṃśapā-forest epithet found in group 1 anywhere in this packet's claims.
4. **Functional/thematic classification rather than a proper name** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-1` labels the verse "homage by way of protecting others" (གཞན་སྲུང་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ); `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-1` instead classify it by iconographic device, "praise by way of the right- and left-hand emblems" (already cited above under Structural placement).

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims in this packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-3` additionally glosses the word "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself etymologically — "ཕྱག" as dispelling ordinary appearance and clinging, "འཚལ" as optionally generating divine pride — within a devotional framing of the guru/Tārā as an unfailing refuge; this etymology is not paralleled by any other claim in the packet and is not folded into any consensus above.

`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-5` adds a distinct structural note: this division and the next (a fifth sa-bcad division not in this packet) are classified in its own outline among the "wrathful" (ཁྲོ) homages, "since they arose as part of that group," even though — the claim itself states — "they do not display a wrathful demeanour" (ཁྲོ་ཉམས་མི་སྣང་). No other commentary in this packet reports this classification for homage 9.

### Unique
`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-5` (wrathful-group classification, above) and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-3` (etymology, above) are each unique to their own commentary.

## Iconography — the two hand-mudrās

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries describe the same basic left-hand configuration: the index, middle, and little fingers raised while the thumb and ring finger form a pair (either touching fingertip-to-fingertip or holding a flower stem — see Divergences below), together forming the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ), held at or adorning the heart.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `taranatha:c-9-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (15 commentaries)

Fifteen commentaries likewise describe the same basic right-hand configuration: the right hand forms the "supreme-giving" (མཆོག་སྦྱིན, varada) mudrā, and a wheel is somehow associated with it, from which light radiates outward, filling all directions and outshining/overpowering all other light (the precise location of the wheel and the source of the light are treated separately below, under "The wheel of light").

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-57`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-58`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-7`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-31`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `taranatha:c-9-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (15 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Within the left-hand consensus, commentaries split on whether the thumb-ring pair actively **holds a flower stem** or merely **touches fingertip to fingertip** with no implement:
- Holding a flower stem (usually named utpala, though `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-5` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55` instead use a padma-based term): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (7 commentaries).
- Fingertips merely joined, no implement named: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `taranatha:c-9-4` (4 commentaries).
- `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3` gives both descriptions in immediate sequence — "joined" (སྦྱར) followed by "holding the stem of an utpala" — rather than choosing one.
- `karma-maitri`, `gendun-gyatso`, `lobsang-dawa` describe the three raised fingers without specifying what the thumb-ring pair does either way.

`sungrab-tulku:c-12-4` describes a different pairing altogether: its left thumb and **middle** finger (མཐེ་མོ་དང་གུང་མོ) hold the utpala stem, while the index, **ring**, and little fingers (མཛུབ་མོ་དང་སྲིན་ལག་མཐེའུ་ཆུང) are the three raised — the reverse of every other commentary's thumb-and-ring-finger pairing. This is a genuine, internally consistent variant, not an extraction error (the raised set and the paired set are complementary and add up to five fingers either way); no other commentary in the packet shares this specific swap. Because it disagrees with the "thumb and ring finger paired" element of the Consensus statement above, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-4` is not included in that Consensus attestation list.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1` labels its own left-hand description "the Three-Jewels-symbolizing supreme-giving mudrā" (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་མཆོག་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ), attaching the term "supreme-giving" (མཆོག་སྦྱིན) — which every other commentary in this packet reserves for the *right*-hand gesture — to the left-hand gesture as well. No other claim in the corpus does this; it is not generalized into either Consensus statement above.

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-3` and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30` both additionally name the left-hand mudrā a "refuge-granting mudrā" (སྐྱབས་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) — a second name for the same gesture, alongside "Three-Jewels mudrā," not given by any other commentary in this packet. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30` further adds two symbolic readings not found elsewhere: holding the utpala at the heart symbolizes "weighing the samaya-continuum in her heart," and turning its opening toward the left ear symbolizes "that hearing liberates one's own mindstream."

## The wheel of light — location and source

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is a genuine three-way divergence over what the root verse's "wheel" (འཁོར་ལོ) is and where the light comes from. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-8` itself names this as a live disagreement: "As for the light rays in this context, some commentaries state that they are the radiance of the wheel-mark on the palm of her right hand, while others state that they are the radiance of her body — the positions differ" (སྐབས་འདིའི་འོད་ཟེར་ནི། འགྲེལ་པ་འགའ་ཞིག་ལས། ཕྱག་གཡས་པའི་མཐིལ་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོའི་འོད་ཟེར་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་པ་དང་། གཞན་དག་སྐུའི་འོད་ཟེར་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་པས་བཞེད་པ་མི་འདྲ་ཡང་།).

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **Wheel as a mark on the palm of the right hand; light radiates from that mark** — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` ("light-rays radiating from the wheel adorning the palm of her right, boon-granting hand"), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-5` ("by the light of this wheel-emblem she overwhelms..."), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6` ("all light rays radiate from the wheel-adorned part of that mudrā's palm"), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-58` ("light rays radiate from the wheel on her hand"), `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` ("on the palm...a thousand-spoked wheel, whose beautiful light-rays dispel..."), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` ("on its palm a thousand-spoked wheel design...radiates light"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2` ("from the wheel-marked palm...radiating light"). Seven commentaries.
2. **"Wheel" glossed as the [ten] directions themselves (not a physical mark); light radiates from her own body** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-6` ("the wheel of all directions...the ten directions...are adorned by...radiances shimmering from her own body"), corroborated by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-9` (citing Nyima Sbaspa) and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-10` ("since it is stated to be light arisen from herself, it is not stated to be the radiance of a hand-implement"); `taranatha:c-9-7` ("the wheel of directions, that is, all the buddha-fields of the ten directions...are pervaded and adorned by the...mass of light-rays arising from her own body," རང་གི་སྐུ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་འོད་ཟེར). Two commentaries.
3. **Both body and wheel-mark named together as sources, with the wheel located atop the utpala held in the *left* hand rather than on the right palm** — `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-2` ("left holding an utpala atop which a wheel blazes with light rays;...light rays radiate from her body and the wheel," སྐུ་དང་འཁོར་ལོ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-1` (utpala held at ear-level, "opening at its pollen-centre" a wheel) + `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-2` ("light-rays radiating from her body and the wheel," using the identical clause སྐུ་དང་འཁོར་ལོ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས found in `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-2`). Two commentaries.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-3` is a hedged, partial witness: it states that her "own mass of light" adorns "the wheel of all directions and intermediate directions without exception" (ཕྱོགས་དང་ཕྱོགས་མཚམས་མ་ལུས་པའི་འཁོར་ལོ) — closer in structure to position 2's "wheel = the directions" reading, since no palm or hand-implement is mentioned in this claim — but it never states, as `anon-trinle-char` and `taranatha` do, that the light comes specifically "from her body." It is not counted toward position 2's attestation above; treat it as ambiguous on this specific point.

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-2` is textually garbled at the point that would settle this question (its OCR text reads "...ཇི་ཆལ་ས་འཕྲོས་པའི..." where a clearer verb is expected) and is therefore also excluded from all three positions above, though it is cited for the general iconography Consensus in the previous facet.

No claim in the packet reconciles positions 1–3 or acknowledges that alternatives exist, apart from `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-8`'s own two-way framing (which does not mention position 3 at all).

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-7` offers a fourth, doctrinally distinct account of the "wheel": rather than debating its location or the light's source, it connects the wheel-mark to the thirty-two major marks (མཚན) of a buddha's body — "her hands adorned with the wheel-[mark] on every side, and the soles of her feet marked with the excellent wheel-pattern designs...the thirty major marks" — and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-8` continues with the eighty minor marks (དཔེ་བྱད་བཟང་པོ་བརྒྱད་ཅུ). No other claim in the packet frames the wheel-mark this way.

## Body colour and number of arms

### Consensus

### ⚑ Divergences
**Red, one face, four arms** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-13` ("her body colour...is said to be the colour of padmarāga [ruby], hence red; her lower seat is a red lotus and moon disc; she has one face and four arms"), supported by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own Twenty-One Homages sādhana at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-11` (first pair of hands forms the "mudrā of great joy," second right hand performs a dance gesture, second left hand holds a branch of the sorrowless [Aśoka] tree, causing a rain of jewels), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-12` ("apart from the second left hand, no other hand-implement is held...that implement is the sorrowless tree"), and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-14` (Nyima Sbaspa's verse: "red like the bandhūka flower...with four arms; two and two, in the nature of great joy, holding the sorrowless tree"). ## Protection from fears

### Consensus
Six commentaries associate this homage with protection from fear(s) in general terms (without necessarily giving the specific sixteen-fold enumeration below):

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12` (6 commentaries)

Of these, two — `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul` — additionally give the *same* specific enumeration: sixteen great fears in two sets of eight (an "outer" set and an "inner" set), each fear paired with the same symbolic image and the same underlying affliction. Re-comparing the two lists claim by claim: both pair elephant/delusion, iron-shackle/craving, ocean(-wave)/desire, flesh-eating-demon/doubt, fire/hatred, thief/wrong-view, snake/jealousy, and lion/pride for the outer eight, and both give an *identically ordered* inner eight — king's-punishment(anger), enemy(quarrel/violence), demon(deceit), leprosy(shamelessness), loss-of-friends(faithlessness), poverty(miserliness), thunderbolt(anger/grudge/jealousy), loss-of-purpose(laziness). The two commentaries differ only in the internal ordering of the *outer* eight, not in which sixteen images/afflictions are named or how the inner eight are ordered.

— sixteen-fears enumeration attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-5`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-5` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary in the packet gives a conflicting count or a conflicting set of fears; the sixteen-fears tradition above is simply not attested outside `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul`.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-3` additionally frame the protection as extending "to the beings of the ten directions" and describe the Tārā of this homage as a "reverend lady who adorns [beings] with the ornament of protecting and guarding the entire expanse of the wheel of directions."

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-8` names this goddess's "chief enlightened activity" as "protecting from harm" specifically, and separately quotes a verse (not further identified in this claim) naming protection "from spirits, planets and constellations, untimely death and sinful dreams, harm from poison, weapons, fire and water" — a benefit-list not paralleled by any other claim in this packet.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give a "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) reading of the Three-Jewels mudrā centred on three substances, opening with a clause that is **character-identical** between the two: དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ནི་ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ་སྟེ ("'symbolizing the Three Jewels' is the three of seminal fluid, subtle-drop, and wind") — found verbatim in both `taranatha:c-9-9` and the opening of `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1`. Beyond that opening clause the two commentaries diverge in phrasing: `taranatha:c-9-10` continues in unbroken prose (the three, gathered into the mudrā, are "fully bound" at the heart-drop of each channel-wheel, giving rise to the tummo wisdom-body's light pervading all directions), while `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` continues as a word-by-word gloss of the verse's own phrases ("fingers is...", "heart is...", "adorned is...", "agitated with masses of her own light is...") — the same underlying completion-stage-with-marks doctrine, differently structured, not a second verbatim match.

— attested: `taranatha:c-9-9`, `taranatha:c-9-10`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the additional readings below are independent, non-overlapping elaborations rather than incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-9-11` adds a further hidden-meaning gloss not paralleled in `palden-sherab`: "from the power of holding [this mudrā], recollection arises" (འཛིན་པའི་སྟོབས་ལས་རྗེས་དྲན་འབྱུང་བའོ).

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-4-1` extends the exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks": the three doors (body, speech, mind) awaken as the essence of "symbolizing the Three Jewels," destroying the "twenty peaks" of the innate transitory-collection view, so that one becomes buddha "within Samantabhadrī's expanse" — a Great-Perfection-styled culmination not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the corpus.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` gives an unrelated triadic reading, not overlapping with taranatha/palden-sherab's three-substances scheme: her body is the Saṅgha, speech the Dharma, mind the Buddha (union of the Three Jewels); body/speech/mind are also the three kāyas and the three roots (guru/yidam/ḍākinī). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5` further reads the three raised fingers themselves as signifying "the embodiment of the union of all buddhas of the three times" (emanation of past buddhas, source of future buddhas, regent of present buddhas), supported by a citation from the Cakrasaṃvara root tantra at `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-6` on the guru as the embodiment of the Three Jewels. This entire scheme belongs to `tenzin-dhonzang` alone; it is not corroborated by, and does not overlap in content with, `taranatha`'s or `palden-sherab`'s three-substances reading above.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4`, quoting Gendün Drup's own praise to White Tārā (a different text), reports that "the utpala symbolizes the buddhas of the three times while the mudrā itself shows the nature of all Three Jewels gathered as one" — a partial thematic echo of `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5`'s "buddhas of the three times" reading, but anchored in a *different* iconographic element (the utpala flower, not the three raised fingers) and sourced from a different text entirely (a separate White Tārā praise, not this homage's own root verse). The two claims share a theme, not a specific claim; they are not treated as corroborating one another.

## Practice, samaya-mudrā, and liturgical application

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `tsultrim-namdak` independently characterize this hand-gesture as a samaya-mudrā (དམ་ཚིག་གི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) whose correct performance invokes the deity's blessing or actual presence:

— attested: `taranatha:c-9-3`, `taranatha:c-9-5`, `taranatha:c-9-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-34` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-9-3` states plainly that "the utpala-mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels is Tārā's samaya-mudrā"; `taranatha:c-9-5`–`taranatha:c-9-6` add that if followers correctly form the same mudrā, "Bhagavatī herself [appears] in the bodily aspect of binding that same mudrā"; `taranatha:c-9-8` further specifies that, for the follower, light rays actually move from the buddha-fields of the ten directions and "actually arrive" (དངོས་སུ་འབྱོན་པའོ) when invited by the mudrā. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-34`'s own extracted content states, in the same vein, that these are samaya-mudrās: "when practitioners form the mudrās in this way, the deities bestow their blessing; and when invoked by these mudrās, the goddess Tārā, together with her retinue, actually arrives from the pure realms of the ten directions, radiating light." The two commentaries independently converge on the same idea — correctly formed mudrā invites the deity's actual, not merely symbolic, presence — though worded quite differently and via different specific claims.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-5` reports a single, unparalleled note on divergent liturgical *application* (not doctrinal meaning) of this verse: "the great Jowo" applies this line to the liturgical section on protection, while "a mahāsiddha" applies it instead to a consecration ritual — both, the claim says, based on the tantra's own statement that the wheel of directions is filled with the light of her body, "intending the transformation of all animate and inanimate things into the pure display of deities." No other claim in the packet mentions either liturgical application, so this is recorded as a single commentary's report rather than a corroborated or contested divergence.
