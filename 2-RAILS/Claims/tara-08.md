## Structural placement and verse citation

### Consensus
Seven commentaries mark this passage with an explicit ordinal marker for "the eighth," and five commentaries additionally quote the root verse's full four lines together (with minor orthographic/case variants across copies — see below); a further two commentaries quote it split across adjacent claims.

— ordinal heading attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-2`, `taranatha:c-8-1`, `taranatha:c-8-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-1` (7 commentaries). Six of these spell the ordinal with the word བརྒྱད་པ; `sungrab-tulku:c-11-1` instead marks it with the parenthesized numeral ༼༨༽, not the word — the same structural function (an explicit "this is the eighth" marker) but not the same lexical attestation, so it should not be read as a seventh instance of བརྒྱད་པ specifically.

— full four-line verse quoted together attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2` (5 commentaries)

— full verse quoted split across two adjacent claims: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-2` (line 1) + `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-3` (lines 2–4) (1 commentary)

— structural marker (opens with the same ordinal, "the eighth" (བརྒྱད་པ), as the heading claim, but restates it as a structural frame rather than quoting verse text) attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-2`

The five full-quote claims are close but **not verbatim-identical** to one another — a spot check turns up real orthographic/case variants, not just transcription noise: `sungrab-tulku:c-11-2` has ཆེན་པོ (not ཆེན་མོ) and རྣམས་པར (not རྣམ་པར) where the other five have ཆེན་མོ / རྣམ་པར, and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2` has the instrumental ཆེན་མོས where the others have the plain ཆེན་མོ. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2`'s own raw presentation additionally separates each of the four lines with "…" — an extraction artifact (possibly marking non-contiguous source blocks) rather than a textual variant; the quoted verse content itself is still the same four lines. Quote any specific commentary's verse-line from its own claim only, not as a stand-in for another's.

Two commentaries quote only part of the verse in this packet: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-2` gives only the first two lines (its own heading, oddly, calls this "Verse 9"), and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-27` gives roughly the first three and a half lines before the excerpt is cut off at དགྲ. Both are additionally cited below for their doctrinal content, not merely as partial quotations.

Two commentaries (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-1`) further frame this homage as the *first* of a group of **seven** wrathful-form homages within their own sa-bcad, rather than only as "the eighth" in flat sequence — both independently give the same number "seven" (བདུན) for this group, though their descriptions of what unifies the group differ slightly, including the term for "wrathful-form" itself, which is not a shared fixed phrase: gendun-drub has ཁྲོ་མོའི་རྣམ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ ("praise by way of the wrathful aspect"), "the quality of overcoming Māras and the two obscurations"; tenga-tulku has ཁྲོ་མོའི་ཚུལ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ ("homage by way of the wrathful manner"), "her wrathful face destroying māras and all obstacles" — synonymous but textually distinct terms (རྣམ་པ vs. ཚུལ). No other commentary in this packet states this specific "seven wrathful homages" grouping.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed beyond the ordinal/grouping note above, which is not a genuine disagreement (the "eighth-in-flat-sequence" and "first-of-seven-wrathful" framings are compatible descriptions of the same verse from different sa-bcad levels).

### Unique
`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-2` gives only a bare section-opener ("As for the first [purifying māras and obscurations]:") without quoting verse text.

## Etymology of "Ture"

### Consensus
Five commentaries gloss "Ture" as meaning "swift" (མྱུར་མ / མྱུར་བ), several adding the simile "swift as lightning" (གློག་ལྟར་མྱུར་བ).

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1` (5 commentaries)

Of these, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1`, and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1` share the "swift as lightning" (གློག་ལྟར་མྱུར་བ) simile specifically — close in wording though not identical (e.g. `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1` opens ཏཱུ་རེ་ནི་གློག་ལྟར་མྱུར་བ་སྟེ, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-3` opens ཏུ་རེ་ནི། གློག་ལྟར་མྱུར་བ་སྟེ — the same clause, differing in the vowel length of "Ture" (ཏཱུ་རེ long vs ཏུ་རེ short) as well as a shad).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-3` states only that "Ture" "teaches the name of the Venerable Lady," without glossing it as "swift" or offering any other semantic content — a weaker, partial attestation that does not itself support the "swift" consensus above.

## Meaning of "great terror" (འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་མོ) applied to Tārā

### Consensus
Six commentaries read "great terror" as Tārā's own wrathful manifestation or form — i.e. she herself becomes, or is addressed as, "the great terror/terrifier" — rather than as a description of some other being.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4` ("her body-manifestation is extremely wrathful"), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-49` ("because she terrifies Māra, she is 'greatly terrifying'"), `taranatha:c-8-3` ("Tārā herself comes to manifest in a form of great terror"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-1` ("she rises in the form of the Great Terrifier"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1` ("manifest... in the form of a great terrifier"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11` ("manifests a form of great terror from the sphere of peace") (6 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-7` reports, as an exegetical note rather than its own stated position, that unnamed "some commentators" read "great fear" in this context as referring instead to *wrathful goddesses* (ཁྲོ་མོ), while "others" (also unnamed) read it as referring to *Māra's heroes* (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ) themselves — i.e. as a description of what is destroyed, not of Tārā. **Neither alternative reading is independently attested by any other claim in this packet's 16-commentary corpus** — no claim here glosses "great fear" as the wrathful goddesses, and no claim equates "great fear" with "Māra's heroes" (the two terms are always treated as grammatically distinct elements of the verse elsewhere in the corpus). This divergence therefore rests on a single commentary's own report of a wider tradition, not on two identifiable positions within this packet.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Identity of "Māra's heroes" (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ)

### Consensus
Thirteen commentaries read "Māra's heroes" as denoting the four māras (བདུད་བཞི) collectively — six of them naming all four explicitly (the affliction-māra, the aggregate-māra, the death-lord-māra, and the deva-putra-māra), seven referring to "the four māras" more generically (naming one or two examples, or using "and so on/others").

— attested (all four māras named explicitly): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` (6 commentaries)

— attested (four māras referenced generically, without all four separately named): `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28` (7 commentaries)

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-4` adds a specific detail not shared by any other claim in the corpus: that the host of māras is destroyed "merely by hearing her name" (མཚན་ཐོས་པ་ཙམ་གྱིས) — this vivid particular belongs to tenga-tulku alone and should not be read into the other twelve attestations above.

### ⚑ Divergences
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-50` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-51` hold that "Māra's heroes" refers *directly* only to the māra of the afflictions (ཉོན་མོངས་པའི་བདུད); the other three māras (aggregate, death-lord, deva-putra) are destroyed only "incidentally" (ཞོར་ལ) as a byproduct of defeating the affliction-māra, not as separate direct objects of the verse. This is a genuinely different structure of explanation from the Consensus above, where all four māras are direct objects of "destroys." No other commentary in this packet states this incidental/byproduct structure.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-8-4` glosses "Māra's heroes" as "the generals" (དམག་དཔོན) — a distinct gloss that does not invoke the four-māras framework at all in this claim.

## Iconographic traditions

### Consensus
None. Four commentaries give iconographic detail beyond "a wrathful face": three describe one of **two different bodies** (Tradition A / Tradition B below), and a fourth is a partial witness aligning with one of them.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Tradition A — golden, one face, four arms, seated on a makara:** `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-8` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-9`, corroborated by a verse citation from Nyima Sbaspa at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-10`, describe this Tārā as golden-coloured, one-faced, four-armed, seated half-cross-legged on a makara atop a red lotus and moon disc, holding an aśoka branch and a jewel (right hands) and a lotus and vase (left hands).

**Tradition B — dark-red, one face, two arms, right hand in supreme-giving mudrā:** `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-2-1` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-1`/`sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-2` both describe a dark-red-bodied, one-faced, two-armed form, right hand in supreme-giving (མཆོག་སྦྱིན) mudrā. The three-syllable fragment གཡས་མཆོག་སྦྱིན ("right: supreme-giving") is verbatim-identical between the two claims; beyond that phrase the two descriptions diverge in wording and in one substantive detail — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-2-1` gives the leg-posture as རྟ་འབབས་སྐྱིལ་ཀྲུང (archer's stance) and the left hand as a Three-Jewels mudrā at ear level atop an open utpala bearing a blazing vajra, while `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-2` gives the posture as ཞབས་ཕྱེད་སྐྱིལ (half-cross-legged) and the left hand simply as holding an utpala topped by a blazing vajra with a garland of sparks. These are close corroborating descriptions of the same broad iconographic family, not a verbatim match, and the leg-posture term differs outright.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11` also gives the body colour as dark-red (aligning with Tradition B) but adds that she holds a vajra in her left hand directly, without mentioning an utpala or a supreme-giving right hand — a partial, not fully matching, third witness to the dark-red tradition.

No claim in the corpus reconciles or comments on the difference between Tradition A and Tradition B; they are presented as flat description in their respective sources with no acknowledgment of an alternative.

### Unique
None beyond the above (all iconographic claims are folded into Traditions A/B).

## The wrathful frown on the lotus-face: purpose and (non-)literal sense

### Consensus
Fourteen commentaries state, in varying wording, that her otherwise lotus-like/beautiful face (ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཞལ) takes on a wrathful frown (ཁྲོ་གཉེར) — this is the single most widely attested statement in the packet. Ten of the fourteen state the causal link to destroying enemies within the very claim cited below; four do not — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-52`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-53`, and `taranatha:c-8-5` describe only the face-and-frown, with the "destroys enemies" clause completed by a separate, adjacent claim from the same commentary (`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-54`, `taranatha:c-8-6` — all already cited elsewhere on this page, the first two under "Identity of enemies," the third in this section's next paragraph); `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-5` likewise stops at the frown alone, its own "destroys enemies" clause sitting in the separate claim `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-6` (also cited under "Identity of enemies"). These four claims are kept in the attestation list below because they fully support the *face-and-frown* half of the statement — they are not padding for the "destroys enemies" half, which they do not themselves state. (Two of the fifteen claim IDs below are both from `drakpa-gyaltsen`, so the commentary count is fourteen, not fifteen. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` is dual-cited: already attested above under "Identity of Māra's heroes" for its four-māras content, its own closing clause — "her beautiful, captivating lotus-face take[s] on a wrathful frown" (མཛེས་ཤིང་ཡིད་དུ་འོང་བའི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་པདྨའི་ཞལ་རས་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་དང་ལྡན་པར་མཛད་པས) — independently supports this facet's Consensus statement too.)

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-52`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-53`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-11-5`, `taranatha:c-8-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-29` (14 commentaries)

Within this group, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-3` and `sungrab-tulku:c-11-5` share a closing formula — "...ཆོས་དབྱིངས་མཉམ་པ(་ཉིད)་ཀྱི་ངང་དུ་གསོད་ཅིང་བཅོམ་པར་མཛད་མ(འོ)" ("...slays and destroys [them] in the state of the equality of the dharmadhātu") — that is close between the two but differs in two respects, not one: `sungrab-tulku:c-11-5` drops the པ in མཉམ་(པ་)ཉིད, and lacks the closing phrase ཁྱེད་ལ ("to you") that closes `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-3`'s clause (…མཛད་མ་ཁྱེད་ལའོ། vs …མཛད་མའོ།). But the two claims name **different objects** for that destruction: `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-0-3` names the two obscurations (སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་དགྲ་བོ) as what is destroyed, while `sungrab-tulku:c-11-5` names outer enemies (ཕྱི་རོལ་གྱི་དགྲ་བོ). This near-identical closing phrase should not be read as the two commentaries making the identical substantive claim — they share the frame, not the referent. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-3` uses a related but textually distinct phrase, ཞི་བ་ཆོས་སྐུའི་ངང་དུ (the state of peaceful dharmakāya), not ཆོས་དབྱིངས་མཉམ་པ་ཉིད (equality of the dharmadhātu) — a different formula, not a third instance of the same one.

`taranatha:c-8-6` additionally identifies the enemies destroyed by this action as "those who hate the teaching, harm many beings, and create obstacles for practitioners," calling Tārā "the Mother" in this connection — a specific characterization not paralleled elsewhere in this facet.

### ⚑ Divergences
`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5` restricts the effect of the wrathful frown to the **cognitive obscuration only** (ཤེས་སྒྲིབ) — "she slays all enemies, that is, all cognitive obscurations, without exception" — omitting the afflictive obscuration that the "Identity of enemies" facet below shows is paired with it everywhere else in the corpus. No other commentary makes this cognitive-only restriction.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-6` and `taranatha:c-8-7` both independently supply a non-literal reading of "slays" (གསོད་མ): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-6` states that "the word 'slays' is merely a symbolic expression" (མཚོན་དོན་ཙམ་ཡིན་ནོ), following its explanation that the lotus-face symbolizes the venerable lady's own exhaustion of faults such as attachment and aversion; `taranatha:c-8-7` states, independently and in different terms, that the killing described here "is free of any actual intention or action of killing or harming" (འཆི་བའམ་གནོད་པའི་བསམ་སྦྱོར་མེད་པར་འགྱུར་བའོ). These are two separately worded but convergent non-literal glosses from two different commentaries — genuinely corroborating (both explicitly deny a literal killing), though phrased quite differently and reached via different explanatory routes (symbolic-lotus vs. absence-of-intent), so they are kept here as two Unique attestations of the same underlying point rather than folded into one Consensus statement.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-29` adds the simile that she slays enemies "as effortlessly as the sun dries the dew from grass-tips" — a vivid image not found in any other claim; not generalized to the Consensus above.

## Identity of "enemies" (དགྲ་བོ)

### Consensus
Five commentaries gloss "enemies" (དགྲ་བོ) at a doctrinal level as the two obscurations — the afflictive obscuration, obstructing liberation, and the cognitive obscuration, obstructing omniscience.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-8`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-31`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-3` (5 commentaries)

Of these, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-8` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` share a verbatim-identical core phrase: ཐར་པའི་དགྲ་ཉོན་སྒྲིབ་དང་། རྣམ་མཁྱེན་གྱི་དགྲ་ཤེས་སྒྲིབ ("the afflictive obscuration, the enemy of liberation, and the cognitive obscuration, the enemy of omniscience") — word-for-word identical between the two. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-31` states the same underlying idea in different, non-matching wording (བར་དུ་གཅོད་པ, "obstructs," rather than དགྲ, "enemy of"). `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-3` states it with the same "བར་དུ་གཅོད་པ" construction as tsultrim-namdak and additionally specifies the obscurations are destroyed "together with their imprints" (བག་ཆགས་དང་བཅས་པ) — a detail unique to gendun-drub, not shared by the other four attestations and not generalized to them. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-3` compresses the same pairing into ཉོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི་དགྲ་བོའི་བགེགས་དཔུང ("the host of enemy-obstructors of the afflictive/cognitive [obscurations]"), its own distinct phrasing.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-4` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-5` similarly frame the destruction as reaching "afflictive and cognitive obscurations" (ཉོན་མོངས་པ་དང་ཤེས་བྱའི་སྒྲིབ་པ) / "outer and inner enemies" respectively, consistent with but not identical in wording to the five-claim Consensus above; kept separate here since `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-4` frames this as the effect of the mantra-sound (see Benefits, below) rather than a bare identification of "enemies," and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-5` frames it as "outer and inner enemies" rather than the two-obscurations pair specifically.

`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-7` and `sungrab-tulku:c-11-5` (already cited above) both separately describe "outer/external enemies" (ཕྱི་རོལ་གྱི་དགྲ་བོ) destroyed "without remainder" — a related but distinct framing (outer vs. the two-obscurations pair) from the same general theme.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-54` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-6` both gloss "enemies" more loosely as "the afflictions and other discordant factors" (མི་མཐུན་པའི་ཕྱོགས་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ) / "enemies such as the kleshas," without the specific liberation/omniscience pairing.

### ⚑ Divergences
See `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-5` under "The wrathful frown," above: it names *only* the cognitive obscuration as the enemy destroyed by the frown, where every other claim in this facet that specifies both obscurations pairs the cognitive with the afflictive obscuration. Not re-cited here to avoid citing it on two sides of two different facets' consensus/divergence pairs; see that section for the claim text.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-8` together give, by a wide margin, the richest elaboration of "inner enemies" in the corpus: beyond the afflictive/cognitive pair, they map a further nine enemy-virtue pairs onto the six perfections and related qualities — miserliness vs. generosity, corrupt discipline vs. ethics (quoting the *Abhidharmakośa* on corrupt discipline), anger/hatred vs. patience, "four kinds of laziness" vs. diligence, excitement/distraction/sinking vs. meditative stability, delusion/ignorance vs. wisdom, heedlessness vs. conscientiousness, and shamelessness vs. shame/modesty, and pride vs. faith/respect. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-8` also names this deity's "chief enlightened activity" as dispelling the obstacles of Māra and quotes the *Legdrima* (ལེགས་བྲིས་མ) on protection from "disease, epidemics, harmful spirits, untimely death, bad dreams, ill omens" and all fears. No other commentary in the corpus offers a comparably extended enemy-taxonomy; this elaboration is not generalized to any other claim above.

## Alternate epithets or titles for this homage / this Tārā form

### Consensus
None. Every commentary in this packet that names the homage with more than the bare ordinal "eighth" gives a **different** title.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least six distinct titling traditions are attested, genuinely different rather than compatible variants of one name:

1. **"Venerable Lady Bestower of Power, Destroyer of Māras"** (རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་བདུད་འཇོམས་དབང་སྟེར་མ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-2`.
2. **"Tārā Who Grants Supreme Empowerment"** (དབང་མཆོག་སྟེར་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-1-4`.
3. **"Tārā/Heroine Unconquered by Others [who] Shatters Others' Weak Points/Spells"** (གཞན་མི་རྒྱལ་བའི་དཔའ་མོ, paired with མཚང་འབྲུ་གཞོམ་པ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11`. These three share the exact phrase གཞན་མི་རྒྱལ་བའི་དཔའ་མོ ("Heroine Unconquered by Others") word for word, making this the corpus's one genuine three-way title consensus, though the "shatters weak points/spells" portion is paraphrased somewhat differently in each — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-0-1` uses གཞན་རྒོལ་མཚངས་འབྲུ་གཞོམ་པའི (with an extra ས in མཚངས), while `sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-1` has རྒོལ་བའི་མཚང་འབྲུ་གཞོམ་པའི and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-11` has གཞན་རྒོལ་མཚང་འབྲུ་སོགས་ གཞོམ་པར་མཛད་མ (both without the ས) — a small but real orthographic difference alongside the larger paraphrase, not merely three copies of one fixed phrase.
4. **"Praise by way of reversing others' curses and the like"** (གཞན་གྱི་བྱད་ཁ་སོགས་བཟློག་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-1`.
5. **"Praise by way of the wrathful manner of abiding"** (དྲག་པོའི་བཞུགས་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) — `sungrab-tulku:c-11-1`.
6. **"Vanquisher of Māras and Enemies"** (བདུད་དང་དགྲ་བཅོམ་མཛད་མ) — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-1`.

Titles 1, 4, 5, and 6 each appear in exactly one commentary in this packet; title 2 in exactly one; title 3 in three. The remaining commentaries (`taranatha`, `karma-maitri`, `gendun-gyatso`, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `konchok-thabkhe`'s sister claims, `anon-utpala`, `lobsang-dawa`, `tenga-tulku`, `pema-namgyal`) use only the bare ordinal or a generic descriptive framing (e.g. tenga-tulku's "her wrathful face destroying māras and all obstacles" — already cited under "Structural placement") without a fixed proper-name title. No claim in the corpus reconciles these six traditions or acknowledges that other titles exist.

### Unique
None beyond the six positions enumerated above.

## Completion-stage and other "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) readings

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give a strikingly close three-part hidden-meaning reading of the verse, close enough in two places to be near-verbatim rather than merely thematically similar:

— "Destroying Māra's hero" = "the conduct of being victorious over all directions": `taranatha:c-8-8` (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ་འཇོམས་པ་ནི་ཕྱོགས་ལས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྤྱོད་པའོ) and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-1` (བདུད་ཀྱི་དཔའ་བོ་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་པ་ཕྱོགས་ལས་རྣམ་རྒྱལ་སྤྱོད་པའོ) — differ in word order, the presence/absence of the ནི particle, and a word-form compression (taranatha's རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི contracted to palden-sherab's རྣམ་རྒྱལ); still the closest verbatim match found anywhere in this packet across two different commentarial texts.
— "Great terror" = the non-dual wisdom of bliss-emptiness, which "terrifies"/"startles" those of inferior fortune: `taranatha:c-8-10` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-2` — close in content and largely overlapping vocabulary (བདེ་སྟོང་...ཡེ་ཤེས་ནི་སྐལ་དམན་...འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན), but not verbatim: `taranatha` has གཉིས་སུ་མེད་པའི (elaborated "non-dual") vs. `palden-sherab`'s གཉིས་མེད, and `taranatha` ends ཆེན་པོའོ (masculine/generic) vs. `palden-sherab`'s ཆེན་མའོ (feminine, agreeing with Tārā).
— "Ture"/"swift" = the swiftness of this method-path in accomplishing realization of higher stages: `taranatha:c-8-12`–`taranatha:c-8-13` (swiftly traversing the higher paths to the twelve bhūmis) and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-2` (continuation: "because that method-path swiftly accomplishes direct realization of the path, she is 'Ture,' that is, 'swift'") — same idea, independently worded.

— attested: `taranatha:c-8-8`, `taranatha:c-8-10`, `taranatha:c-8-12`, `taranatha:c-8-13`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-2` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement; the three sources below diverge only in that they are independent, non-overlapping hidden-meaning schemes rather than incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-8-9` and `taranatha:c-8-11` add two further hidden-meaning glosses not paralleled in `palden-sherab`: the wrathful frown symbolizes "engaging in divine attire" and thereby "the accomplishment of the wisdom-body" (`taranatha:c-8-9`); and wisdom together with the wisdom-body "overcome the enemy — all obscurations without exception" (`taranatha:c-8-11`).

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-4-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-4-2` extend the exegesis to a fourth level — "completion-stage-without-marks" — citing the *Mañjuśrī-māyājāla-tantra* on the unborn "great life-force," and conclude that the self-liberation of all impure wind-mind in Great Perfection awareness-emptiness reveals "the primordial self-awareness, the co-emergent heroine... **Vajravārāhī**" — a striking, unique culmination naming a different deity altogether from Tārā. No other claim in the corpus reaches this identification; palden-sherab's own generation-stage claim (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-2-1`, in "Iconographic traditions" above) and its first two hidden-meaning levels (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-1`–`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-1`–`palden-sherab:c-3-1-8-3-2`) all name Tārā, not Vajravārāhī — the Vajravārāhī identification belongs specifically and only to this fourth, "without marks" level of palden-sherab's own four-level structure.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-5` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-6` quote a mahāsiddha's verse and apply their own, differently-sourced completion-stage reading (protection from eight inner terrors; slaying the two-obscuration "enemy" together with its habitual winds; the four empowerments as the basis for reading "conferring empowerment, giving all" into the verse). Crucially, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-5` frames this explicitly as tentative — the commentary states it "flags for examination" (དཔྱད་པར་བྱའོ) whether this gloss should be adopted, rather than asserting it as settled. This tentative framing is preserved here and not upgraded to an endorsed reading. This scheme does not overlap with taranatha's or palden-sherab's three-part correspondence above.

## Benefits and mantra power

### Consensus
Two commentaries specifically associate the mantra "Ture" with protection from the "eight great fears/terrors" (འཇིགས་པ་ཆེན་པོ་བརྒྱད), a named doctrinal category (aṣṭamahābhaya) rather than a generic reference to danger.

— attested: `karma-maitri:c-1-1-8-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-8-2` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`gendun-gyatso:c-1-8-1` states relatedly, but without naming the "eight" category specifically, that merely proclaiming the utterance "ture" protects beings "from the great terror of māras and so on" — a related but not identical claim, kept separate from the Consensus above per the full-statement-support rule.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-8-4` states that merely proclaiming the great sound of the vidyā-mantra destroys the afflictive and cognitive obscurations that obstruct liberation and omniscience — a mantra-power claim distinct from the "eight fears" pairing.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28` states that by the power of the deity-mantra OṂ TURE SVĀHĀ she instantly (སྐད་ཅིག་ལ) destroys all the māra-warriors and their retinues, in whatever form the four māras appear.

`taranatha:c-8-3` (already cited under "Meaning of 'great terror'") also frames this as a mantra-power claim: by the power of reciting the vidyā-mantra "OM TURE" and so on, Tārā manifests in the form of great terror.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-28` similarly frames the manifestation of the great-terror form and the destruction of Māra's chiefs as an effect of reciting the family-mantra "OṂ TĀRE" and so on.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-30` adds, distinctly, that she "slays every enemy without exception who obstructs the practitioner with harm, and pacifies every harmful intention and its execution without exception" — a benefit framed around the practitioner's protection rather than the mantra's destructive power per se.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-4` is the only claim in the corpus to quote the full extended dhāraṇī "OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ" (rather than "Ture" or "OM TURE" alone) as what dispels great terror; it also glosses "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself as dispelling attachment to one-sided peace, or as bodhicitta practice — a further, unique etymological point on "homage" not found elsewhere in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-8` (already cited under "Identity of enemies") separately quotes the *Legdrima* for benefits specific to protection from disease, epidemics, harmful spirits, untimely death, bad dreams, and ill omens — not paralleled by any other claim.

`sangye-nyentrul:c-9-1-3` gives a bare visualization instruction: one meditates that afflictions, others' weak points, and curses are destroyed — framed explicitly as the "visualization sequence" (དམིགས་རིམ) rather than a doctrinal benefit-claim.
