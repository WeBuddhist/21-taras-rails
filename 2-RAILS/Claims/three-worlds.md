# The Three Worlds — འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This is a **global cross-cutting topic**, not one of the twenty-two registered
> spine slots (`tara-01`…`tara-21`, `benefits`) — there is no `assemble_packet.py`
> / spine-map packet for it. The gather step was performed manually: every raw
> tree-guided claims file was searched for the string འཇིག་རྟེན (both the bare
> term and the compound འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ), and every genuinely on-topic claim block
> was copied character-for-character from the search output into this page's
> drafting notes before any synthesis was written. Tibetan quoted here is
> reproduced verbatim from those blocks; no quotation was retyped from memory.
>
> **Merged scope, per the article-worthiness filter's verdict:** this page treats
> both **འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ།** ("the three worlds," queue rank 44) and the more general
> **འཇིག་རྟེན།** ("world"/the general *loka* concept, queue rank 76, term 71) as one
> subject — the corpus does not keep the two terminologically separate. Every
> commentary that names "the three worlds" also uses the bare term "world"
> (in the singular, the plural, and "the seven worlds") to describe the same
> referents — the netherworld, the earthly and celestial *loka*-realms, the
> beings who inhabit them, and Tārā's power over them — so a page that covered
> only the three-word compound would arbitrarily fragment claims that
> commentators themselves treat as one continuous discussion.

## Questions asked

1. Who is identified as "Protector of the Three Worlds," and what role does this epithet play in Tārā's origin narrative?
2. What do commentaries state the "three worlds" consist of — netherworld/earth/heaven, or the three realms of desire, form, and formlessness?
3. Do any commentaries treat these two readings of "three worlds" as interchangeable, or hold one over the other?
4. What do commentaries identify as "the seven worlds," and do they agree on the enumeration?
5. Does any commentary argue that "seven worlds" is a corrupted or scribal reading, and that "three worlds" is the more textually correct one?
6. Does any commentary independently corroborate or complicate that textual-critical argument?
7. What power does Tārā exercise over the (seven) worlds in the praise's fifth homage, and how is it described?
8. What is shaken in the praise's seventeenth homage — the mountains, the three worlds, or both, and how are they related?
9. Does any commentary dispute the majority reading of what "the three worlds" names in that verse?
10. What is the source of the internal tension yama-sonam itself flags about what causes the three worlds to shake?
11. How is "the world," together with Māra, drawn under Tārā's power in the tenth homage?
12. Which worldly gods are said to worship Tārā, and what cosmological role (e.g. as "creator of the world") do commentaries attribute to any of them?
13. Do any commentaries give the "three worlds" or "seven worlds" a hidden, tantric (completion-stage) meaning, and what is it?
14. Does any commentary corroborate palden-sherab's completion-stage reading of the three worlds as channels?
15. Are "world" (འཇིག་རྟེན།) and "three worlds" (འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ།) treated by the commentaries as one continuous topic, or does the corpus mark any distinction between the general term and the specific triad?

## Epithet: Avalokiteśvara, "Protector of the Three Worlds" — Tārā's origin (homage 1)

### Consensus
Every one of the sixteen commentaries in this packet glosses the praise's first homage's phrase "the Protector of the Three Worlds, lotus-faced" (འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་མགོན་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཞལ་གྱི།) by identifying Avalokiteśvara as that protector, and states that Tārā arose from the pollen of a lotus grown from his tears of compassion. This epithet is the term's very first appearance in the text, and the shared point of entry for every commentary's discussion of "the three worlds":

— attested: `taranatha:c-1-9`, `yama-sonam:c-3-1-19`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-8`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-13`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4` (16 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-1-9` states plainly: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གྱི་མགོན་པོ་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཞལ་རས་ཀྱི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་པདྨ་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྤུལ་ཞིང་བྱུང་བའོ།" ("[Tārā] emanated and arose from a myriad of lotus-pollen born of the water-born face of Avalokiteśvara, protector of the three worlds"). `sungrab-tulku:c-4-12` gives the same identification in near-identical wording: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གྱི་མགོན་སྐྱབས་ནི། ལོངས་སྐུའི་རྣམ་པ་འཕགས་པ་སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཡིན།" ("The protector and refuge of the three worlds is Ārya Avalokiteśvara in the aspect of the enjoyment body"). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-8`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-13`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-1-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-1-6`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-1-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3`, and `yama-sonam:c-3-1-19` all restate the same identification, several (`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-8`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-11`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`) going on to enumerate the three worlds themselves — see the next facet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed at the level of *who* the protector is — every commentary agrees it is Avalokiteśvara. The divergence over what "the three worlds" he protects actually denotes is recorded in the next facet.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3` uniquely folds this identification into a further doctrinal move, naming the resulting goddess as "རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཀར་མོ་དང་སྔོན་མོ་ཉིད" — Jetsün Drolma the White and the Blue — rather than leaving the origin-narrative generic to Tārā's peaceful/green form; no other commentary in this packet ties the three-worlds origin-narrative to a specific colour-form of the goddess.

## Defining "the three worlds"

### Consensus
A large majority of commentaries that gloss the term explicitly define "the three worlds" as the nāga-world below the earth, the human world upon the earth, and the deva-world above the earth:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-8`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-11`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-13`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `taranatha:c-17-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-3` (12 commentaries)

`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-8` gives the clearest triple gloss: "ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་སྟེང་མིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ" ("the three worlds — the subterranean nāga-world, the earthly human world, and the celestial deva-world above"). `sungrab-tulku:c-4-11` states it as a standalone definitional claim in the same terms: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ནི། ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་སྟེང་མིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བཅས་ཡིན།" `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-1-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-7`, and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-13` restate the same three-tier list (nāga/human/deva, ordered below/on/above the earth) at homage 1; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-1-7`, `taranatha:c-17-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, and `sungrab-tulku:c-17-3` restate it again independently while glossing homage 17 (see "Shaking the three worlds," below) — a second, later point in the text where the same definition recurs. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3` gives the identical list with an added doctrinal gloss, that Avalokiteśvara holds the four immeasurables toward "ས་འོག་ཀླུ་དང་། ས་སྟེང་མི་དང་། ས་བླ་ལྷ་སྟེ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གྱི་མགོན་པོ" (nāgas below, humans on the earth, gods above — the protector of these three worlds).

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries flag that "the three worlds" is not exclusively fixed to the netherworld/earth/heaven triad, and may instead — or additionally — name the three realms of saṃsāric existence (desire, form, and formlessness; ཁམས་གསུམ):

- **yama-sonam** — glossing homage 1's own occurrence of the term, states both readings are acceptable: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ཡང་ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་སྟེང་མི་ཡི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་པའམ་ཡང་ན་བཞི་བརྒྱ་པའི་ཟླ་འགྲེལ་ལྟར་ཁམས་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་ཀྱང་རུང་ངོ་།" ("'three worlds' can mean either the nāga-world below, deva-world above, and human world on the surface, or — as in the commentary on the *Catuḥśataka* — it may be applied to the three realms; either usage is acceptable") — `yama-sonam:c-3-1-19`.
- **palden-sherab** — glossing homage 17's occurrence, offers the same two readings side by side without adjudicating between them: "འདོད་ཁམས་དང་། གཟུགས་ཁམས་དང་། གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་སམ། ས་འོག་དང་། ས་སྟེང་དང་། ས་བླ་སྟེ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རྣམས" ("the desire, form, and formless realms — or the netherworld, the earth's surface, and the world above — the three worlds") — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2`.

Neither claim states that one reading excludes the other; both are recorded as an acknowledged ambiguity in what the compound denotes, standing alongside — not contradicting — the twelve-commentary consensus above, which simply does not raise the alternative.

### Unique
None beyond the two ambiguity notes above.

## "The seven worlds" — enumeration, and the seven/three textual variant (homage 5)

### Consensus
None as a single statement — commentaries that enumerate "the seven worlds" (glossing the fifth homage's "she treads the seven worlds with her feet") split between two distinct lists, recorded as competing positions below rather than flattened into one.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Enumeration by class of being** (nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras, devas):

— attested: `taranatha:c-5-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18` (3 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-5-6` gives its own list: "འདིར་ནི་མཐུ་དང་ལྡན་པའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ཏེ། ཀླུ་དང་ཡི་དྭགས་དང་ལྷ་མ་ཡིན་དང་། །མི་དང་རིག་པ་འཛིན་པ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི་དང་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་ནོ།" `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18` give the same seven classes (order varies slightly), each independently.

**Enumeration by realm-tally** (the desire realm's three lower rebirths + its two higher rebirths [human, god] = five, plus the form realm and the formless realm = seven):

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-3` (5 commentaries)

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31` glosses: "འདོད་ཁམས་ཀྱི་འགྲོ་བ་ལྔ་དང་། གཟུགས་ཁམས་དང་དྲུག གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་དང་བདུན་མནན་པའོ།" ("she treads the five classes of beings of the desire realm, the form realm as the sixth, and the formless realm as the seventh"). `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4` give the same tally. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-3` — an internal-tension claim in its own source — attributes this same position ("འགྲོ་བ་རྒྱུད་ལྔ་། དེའི་སྟེང་དུ་ཁམས་གོང་མ་གཉིས་བསྣན་པས་བདུན་ཡིན") explicitly to "Jetsün Drakpa Gyaltsen," corroborating that this is a named, attributed position rather than an anonymous convention.

**A reported third position** (six classes of beings plus the intermediate state = seven):

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3` (2 commentaries)

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-3` reports, unattributed ("འགའ་ཞེ་ན"), that "some say" the seven worlds are "འགྲོ་བ་རིགས་དྲུག་སྲེང་པ་བར་དེ་དང་བདུན" (the six classes of migrating beings plus the intermediate state). `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3` gives the identical tally independently, glossing the same verse: "རིགས་དྲུག་བར་དོ་དང་བཅས་པའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་པོ."

**A tantric (completion-stage) reading** identifies the seven worlds with channel-cakras rather than any realm or class of being at all:

— attested: `taranatha:c-5-10`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-5-10` states: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ནི་རྩ་འཁོར་ལོ་ལྔ་དང་། །མེ་རླུང་གི་འཁོར་ལོ་གཉིས་ཏེ་བདུན" ("the 'seven worlds' are the five channel-cakras plus the two cakras of fire and wind — seven in all"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1` gives the same tally in nearly identical wording: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ནི་རྩ་འཁོར་ལོ་ལྔ་དང་། མེ་རླུང་འཁོར་ལོ་གཉིས་སོ།" — this reading is discussed further under "Hidden/tantric readings," below.

**Whether "seven worlds" is even the textually correct reading**, as against "three worlds," is a further, distinct dispute:

- `yama-sonam:c-3-5-6` reports that Tibetan images of Tārā caption this passage "seven worlds" but that the many circulating explanations for the number have no clear source, and states this "appears to be a scribal error" (ཡིག་ནོར་ཡིན་པར་མངོན). Building on this across four further claims — `yama-sonam:c-3-5-8` and `yama-sonam:c-3-5-10` (quoting Nyima Sbaspa's *sādhana* twice, each time reading "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་པོ་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་མནན་ཏེ" — "presses the *three* worlds with her feet"), and `yama-sonam:c-3-5-11` (a philological conclusion: "གསུང་སྔ་ཕྱི་གསུམ་གྱི་ནང་དུ་མཐུན་པར་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལས་བདུན་མི་འབྱུང་བས... འགྲེལ་མཛད་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ལ་ཁུངས་བསྟན་པ་གཅིག་ཀྱང་མ་མཐོང་ངོ་།" — "because three citations, early and late, consistently say 'three worlds' and 'seven' does not occur in any of them... not a single commentator has shown a source for 'seven worlds'") — this commentary argues "three worlds" is the demonstrably correct reading at this verse specifically, not merely an available alternative. `yama-sonam:c-3-5-13` further notes the resulting interpretive difficulty is unresolved even under its own preferred reading (how a seated goddess presses either three or seven worlds underfoot).
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-4` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-5` address the same variance far more cautiously: a mahāsiddha's own treatise is quoted reading "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་པོ་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་མནན་ཏེ" (three worlds, not seven), and the commentary concludes only that "གསུམ་དང་བདུན་ནོར་ལུགས་མེད་པས་འགྱུར་སྐྱོན་མིན་ལ། ཡི་གེ་མ་དག་པ་ཡང་མིན་པར་མངོན" ("since there is no pattern of confusing 'three' with 'seven,' this is evidently not a corrupted reading, nor a scribal error") — treating "three worlds" as a legitimate documented variant among Sanskrit exemplars of the tantra (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-5`), without concluding, as `yama-sonam` does, that "seven" itself is in error.

These two commentaries independently corroborate that a "three worlds" variant of this verse circulated outside the root text this vault's sixteen commentaries otherwise gloss, but reach different verdicts on which reading is authoritative — `yama-sonam` favours "three," `konchok-thabkhe` treats both as legitimate.

### Unique
None beyond the positions recorded above.

## Treading the worlds underfoot, summoning them without exception (homage 5)

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in this packet gloss the praise's fifth homage — "she treads the seven worlds with her feet, able to summon [all beings] without exception" — as an act of Tārā's power over the (seven) worlds, exercised through the mantra TUTTĀRE HŪṂ filling the desire, form, and formless realms:

— attested: `taranatha:c-5-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-5-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-7-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-16` (16 commentaries)

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-2` states it concisely: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་པོ་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་མནན་ཏེ། དེ་ཐམས་ཅད་རིག་སྔགས་ཀྱི་མཐུས་ལུས་པ་མེད་པར་དབང་དུ་འགུགས་པར་མཛད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ" ("having trodden the seven worlds beneath her feet, she brings all of them without exception under her power through the might of the vidyā-mantra"). `sungrab-tulku:c-8-5` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-2` both extend the claim to name "the three worlds" explicitly as the scope of what is summoned — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-2`: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གྱི་ཁྱོན་ཐམས་ཅད་གཅིག་ཀྱང་ལུས་པ་མེད་པར་དབང་དུ་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་པའི་མཐུ" ("the might able to summon, without a single exception, the entirety of the three worlds under her power"), showing that "the seven worlds" trodden underfoot and "the three worlds" summoned are treated as the same referent at different granularities within a single commentary's own gloss. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-5` glosses the same act as an expression of compassion rather than domination alone: pressing the worlds with her feet means she "holds them firmly," not casting them aside.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every commentary treats this as an act of Tārā's protective/summoning power, differing only in emphasis (subjugation vs. compassionate embrace), not in the underlying claim.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-5` uniquely quotes a mahāsiddha's verse describing this same act as filling and pervading "ཁམས་གསུམ" (the three realms) with the HŪṂ-sound specifically, rather than the seven-worlds enumeration any other commentary at this verse uses — a variant formula, discussed above under the seven/three textual dispute.

## Shaking the three worlds — Meru, Mandara, Vindhya (homage 17)

### Consensus
Most commentaries glossing the praise's seventeenth homage ("she shakes Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya, and the three worlds") read the three named mountains and "the three worlds" as two related but distinct things: her foot-stamping shakes the mountains, and by extension the three worlds — netherworld below, human world on the earth, and deva-world above — together with the beings living in them:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-87`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-17-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-47`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`, `taranatha:c-17-6` (12 commentaries)

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4` gives the pattern plainly: "རི་རབ་དང་རི་མནྡཱ་ར་དང་རི་ནག་པོ་འབིགས་བྱེད་སོགས། །འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་སྟེ་ས་འོག་ས་སྟེང་ས་བླ་དང་བཅུད་ཀྱི་སེམས་ཅན་དང་བཅས་པ་རྣམས་གཡོ་བའི་མཐུ" ("the power to shake Mount Meru, Mount Mandara, Mount Vibhedaka, and so on — the three worlds (the underworld, the earth's surface, and the world above) together with their sentient inhabitants"). `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` restate the identical two-part structure (mountains, then separately the netherworld/human/deva triad) nearly word for word. `taranatha:c-17-6` gives the shortest form: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་སྟེ་ས་འོག་ས་སྟེང་ས་བླ་རྣམས་ཀྱང་གཡོ་བར་བྱེད་དོ།" — and continues, in three further claims (`taranatha:c-17-9`, `taranatha:c-17-10`, `taranatha:c-17-11`), with a grammatical note that the locative "in the three worlds" functions accusatively, a rhetorical objection asking why Meru is named at all if the three worlds already shake, and an answer: the named mountains are singled out to show that even the most stable, powerful abodes are not exempt.

### ⚑ Divergences
`yama-sonam` alone disputes this majority structure, arguing instead that "the three worlds" in this specific verse grammatically *is* the three named mountains, not a separate netherworld/human/deva triad shaken in addition to them:

- **Majority position (implicit in the twelve-commentary consensus above):** the mountains are examples of what shakes; "the three worlds" names the netherworld/earth/heaven triad as an additional, distinct object of the shaking.
- **yama-sonam's position:** "སྐབས་འདིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ས་འོག་ས་བླ་ས་སྟེང་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་པའང་མི་འཐད་དེ། ... རྒྱུད་རང་ལས། རི་རབ་མནྡར་དང་འབིགས་བྱེད། ། འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རྣམས། ཞེས་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་རི་རབ་དང་། མནྡར་དང་། འབིབས་བྱེད་གསུམ་ལ་བྱེད་པར་གསལ་བར་གསུངས་པ" ("in this context, taking 'three worlds' to mean the netherworld, upper world, and earth's surface is untenable... because the tantra itself, in 'Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya — the three worlds,' clearly states that 'three worlds' means Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya themselves") — `yama-sonam:c-3-17-7`, `yama-sonam:c-3-17-8`. The same claim allows, as a further general note (not specific to this verse), that "three worlds" may elsewhere be read as the three realms (desire/form/formless) per the *Catuḥśataka* commentary — the same alternative it raised at homage 1.

Neither side's claim states that it is aware of the other's specific position; the divergence is recorded here as a genuine disagreement about what the compound denotes at this verse, since the two readings are mutually exclusive as stated (either the mountains *are* the three worlds, or they are examples that cause a separate three worlds to shake).

Separately, `yama-sonam` also records — as an internal tension within its own commentary rather than a disagreement between different commentaries in this packet — a dispute over what *causes* the three worlds to shake at this verse:

- **Position 1 (an unnamed "other commentary," quoted at `yama-sonam:c-3-17-5`):** "ཧཱུཾ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས་ས་འོག་སོགས་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་དོན་ལ་བྱེད་པ" — light rays radiating from the HŪṂ-seed cause the shaking.
- **Position 2 (Nyima Sbaspa, quoted at `yama-sonam:c-3-17-6`):** "བདེ་ལྡན་ལྷ་མོས་ཞབས་བརྡབས་པས། ། འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་གཡོ་འཇིགས་པར་བྱེད།" — the blissful goddess's feet-stamping causes it.

`yama-sonam:c-3-17-6` states explicitly that its own commentary holds these two readings contradict each other — flagged with ⚑ in the raw file itself, not inferred by this consolidation.

### Unique
Four commentaries give this verse a further, hidden (completion-stage) meaning in which "the three worlds" names something other than any cosmological realm at all — see "Hidden/tantric readings," below.

## Māra and "the world" drawn under Tārā's power (homage 10)

### Consensus
Glossing the praise's tenth homage — "she brings Māra and the world under her power" through the sound of her mantra-laughter TUTTĀRA — commentaries across the corpus treat "the world" (འཇིག་རྟེན) here as the mundane sphere of beings under Māra's sway, subjugated together with him:

— attested: `taranatha:c-10-6`, `yama-sonam:c-3-10-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-63`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-10-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-33`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-11-1-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-3-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-2-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` (16 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-10-5` gives the fullest etymological statement, deriving the epithet itself from the act: "དེ་ལྟ་བུའི་རྒྱུ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་ཐམས་ཅད་དབང་དུ་མཛད་པས་ན་མཚན་ཡང་། བདུད་དང་འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་སྡུད་མ། །ཞེས་གྲགས་སོ།" ("through these two causes she brings all māras and the world under power, and so she is renowned by the name 'She Who Draws the Māras and the World under Power'"). `gendun-gyatso:c-1-10-2` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-10-3` both name the specific figure subjugated — Māra Paranirmitavaśavartin ("Dgaʼ-rab-dbang-phyug"), lord of the desire realm — together with "all the world" that has fallen under his sway. `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-13` both extend the list of subjugated worldly figures to kings, ministers, householders, and renunciants.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-4-1` give this verse a completion-stage reading in which "Māra" and "the world" both denote inner obscurations rather than any external figure or realm — recorded under "Hidden/tantric readings," below.

## Worship by the great worldly gods; Brahmā as "creator of the world"

### Consensus
Several commentaries — chiefly glossing the sixth homage — describe Tārā as worshipped by the great gods "of the world" (Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Maheśvara and others), and at least three independently record the belief, without endorsing it, that Brahmā is held to be the creator of the world:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1` (12 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-5` records the creator-belief in its sparest form: "ཚངས་པ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་བྱེད་པོར་འདོད་པའོ།" ("Brahmā is held to be the creator of the world"). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-20` gives the identical claim independently: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བྱེད་པོ་འདོད་པ་ཚངས་པ" ("Brahmā, held to be the creator of the world"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-3` extends the same theme to a further figure, Maheśvara, "གཞན་ཡང་འཇིག་རྟེན་བྱེད་པ་པོར་འདོད་པའི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ" ("further, Maheśvara, who is [also] held to be a maker of the world"). In every case the claim is that other traditions or unnamed parties hold this belief about Brahmā's or Maheśvara's cosmological role — no commentary in this packet states that the belief is correct; the verse's own point in each gloss is that this very creator-god worships Tārā.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-6-8` adds a further, distinct point not paralleled elsewhere in this packet: even the very beings "most renowned in the world" (འཇིག་རྟེན་ན་ཆེས་ཆེ་བར་གྲགས་པ) — not only Brahmā and the directional gods named in the verse — become objects of Tārā's worship, generalizing the verse's specific list into an unrestricted claim about worldly greatness as such.

## Hidden/tantric readings: the worlds as channels and cakras

### Consensus
None — every claim in this facet is a single commentary's own completion-stage exegesis; no two commentaries give an identical tantric-physiology mapping, though four independently instantiate the same general convention of reading "world(s)" as channels, cakras, or states of ordinary consciousness rather than cosmological realms.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are parallel, non-competing hidden-meaning readings, not disagreements over one shared claim.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-17-15`, glossing homage 17: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ནི་སྟེང་འོག་གི་དབྱེ་བས་རྩ་གསུམ་གསུམ་དྲུག་གོ།" ("the three worlds, divided by upper and lower, are the three channels — making six").

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1`, glossing the same verse in far greater tantric detail: Meru is the central channel (the spine, "ཚངས་པའི་རུས་པ"); the two flanking mountains are the right and left channels; the mountains' "shaking" is bodhicitta filling them; and "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ནི་སྟེང་འོག་གི་དབྱེ་བས་རྩ་གསུམ་གསུམ་དྲུག་གོ" — the three worlds, again, as three channels making six (word-for-word the same gloss as `taranatha:c-17-15`, though the two claims are not cross-referenced by either commentary and this page treats them as independent instantiations of a shared tantric convention rather than one citing the other). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-3-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-10-4-1` extend the same interpretive mode to homage 10: there, "Māra" is one's own innate conceptual attachment and "the world" is ordinary, contaminated craving-sensation of body, speech, and mind — being "victorious over the three worlds" becomes, at the ultimate level, the dissolution of all appearance of the three realms of existence into the single drop of self-aware wisdom.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, glossing homage 5: the seven worlds trodden underfoot are "the desire realm's three lower rebirths, its two higher [god and human] rebirths, and the seven worlds of the form and formless realms" — pressed by feet that themselves embody "the essence of emptiness-compassion" (སྟོང་ཉིད་སྙིང་རྗེའི་སྙིང་པོ་ཅན), reading the trampling itself, not only the worlds trampled, as a symbol.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-5` quotes a mahāsiddha's verse using "ཁམས་གསུམ" (three realms) and "སྲིད་གསུམ" (three states of existence) interchangeably with "the seven worlds" in a mantra-recitation context — corroborating, per Q14, that a completion-stage or generic-Vajrayāna register readily substitutes "three" for "seven" in this material, though this claim does not itself present a worked-out channel/cakra physiology the way `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` do.
