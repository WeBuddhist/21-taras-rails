## Questions asked

1. In what contexts do the commentaries name Vāyu among the great worldly gods who worship Tārā, and which homage-verse of the root praise do these glosses attach to?
2. Do commentaries further identify Vāyu's directional-guardian role (northwest), and is that direction stated consistently across every commentary that gives one?
3. Do commentaries place Vāyu among a wider set of ten directional guardians summoned into Tārā's service, distinct from the bare worshipper-list mention?
4. Does any commentary give Vāyu a distinctive epithet or cosmological function beyond the bare worshipper-list name, and do these characterizations converge or diverge across commentaries?
5. What tantric "hidden meaning" (sbas don) identification do commentaries give for Vāyu, and does it converge with the elemental scheme used for the other worshipper-gods?
6. Does any external tantric citation (e.g. Cakrasaṃvara mandala material) independently corroborate Vāyu's directional-guardian identity?
7. Which of the sixteen commentaries are silent on Vāyu, if any?

## Vāyu among the great worldly gods who worship Tārā

### Consensus
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries — every commentary in the packet except `taranatha` — name Vāyu (རླུང་ལྷ) among the recurring roster of great worldly gods, alongside Indra, Agni, Brahmā, and Viśveśvara/Sarveśvara, who worship, praise, or rely upon Tārā. The wording is stable across commentaries, closely following or quoting the same homage-verse, e.g. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-3`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ། ། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་མཆོད་མ། ། འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། ། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།" — "Homage to her who is worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Sarveśvara, and praised from in front by bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and hosts of yakṣas."

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-37`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21` (15 commentaries)

Most of these claims go on to specify Vāyu's directional identity as guardian of the **northwest**, stated with complete consistency wherever a direction is given at all: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-5` ("ནུབ་བྱང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཏེ་སྣ་ཚོགས་རྟ་ཅན" — "the northwest guardian Vāyu of manifold aspect, that is, 'Possessor of Manifold Mounts'"), `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3` ("ནུབ་བྱུང་གི་རླུང་ལྷ" — "Vāyu of the northwest"), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2` ("ནུབ་བྱང་རླུང་ལྷ" — "the wind-god in the northwest"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2` ("ནུབ་བྱང་གི་རླུང་ལྷ" — "Vāyu of the northwest"), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3` ("ནུབ་བྱང་རླུང་ལྷ" — "Vāyu of the northwest"), `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5` ("ནུབ་བྱང་གི་རླུང་ལྷ" — "Vāyu in the northwest"). No commentary in the packet places Vāyu in a different direction within this facet.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` gives the fivefold worshipper-verse a wrathful colouring not found in the other claims: in its quotation, the great worldly gods (including Vāyu) "tremble" (འདར་) as they reverently worship the wrathful emanation Gdon-rigs-rnams-'joms-ma, rather than simply paying calm homage. This is one claim's own framing of the same underlying worshipper-list fact, not a separate consensus.

## Vāyu among the ten directional guardians summoned into Tārā's service

### Consensus
Two commentaries — `tenga-tulku` and `tenzin-dhonzang` — go beyond the worshipper framing above and place Vāyu within a fuller list of ten directional guardians (ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ / ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ) who are not merely worshippers but are summoned by Tārā to accomplish activity entrusted to them:

— attested: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` (2 commentaries)

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`: "ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ་ནི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ་པོ་སྟེ་སྟེང་དུ་ཚངས་པ། ཤར་དུ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན། ཤར་ལྷོར་མེ་ལྷ། ལྷོར་གཤིན་རྗེ། ལྷོ་ནུབ་ཏུ་སྲིན་པོ།ནུབ་བྱང་དུ་རླུང་ལྷ། བྱང་དུ་གནོད་སྦྱིན། བྱང་ཤར་དུ་དབང་ལྡན།འོག་ཏུ་ས་བདག་བཅས་ཀྱི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་དང་། དེའི་འཁོར་དང་བཅས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་རྗེ་བཙུན་མའི་ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཏཱཾ་གྱི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་བཀུག་པར་མཛད་ཅིང་། ཅི་བཅོལ་བའི་ལས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲུབ་པ་..." — "The 'earth-protectors' are the ten directional guardians: Brahmā above; Indra in the east; Agni in the southeast; Yama in the south; Rākṣasa in the southwest; **Vāyu in the northwest**; Kubera in the north; Īśāna in the northeast; and the earth-lord below — these hosts, together with their retinues, are all summoned by the light rays of the syllable TĀṂ at the Venerable Lady's heart, and accomplish whatever activity is entrusted to them." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` gives the same "summoned into servitude" framing: "ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བའི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ་སྟེ་མེ་ལྷ་དང་། ཚངས་པ། རླུང་ལྷ། གཤིན་རྗེ། འབྱུང་པོ། གནོད་སྦྱིན། སྲིན་པོ། དབང་ལྡན། ཆུ་ལྷ། ས་ཡི་ལྷ་མོ་དང་། དེ་དག་གི་འཁོར་གྱི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་འཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པའི་བྲན་དུ་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་པའི་མ" — "She is Tārā, the mother able to summon into servitude, for accomplishing enlightened activity, the ten guardians of the earth — the fire-god, Brahma, **the wind-god**, Yama, elemental spirits, yakshas, rakshasas, Ishana, the water-god, and the earth-goddess." Both claims name Vāyu among the ten and both use the same "summoned to serve/accomplish activity" framing, independently of each other's wording.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6` cites an external tantric source — notes on the Cakrasaṃvara subtle-drop body-maṇḍala from "The Entry-Point to the Ocean of Great Bliss" — that independently lists eight directional guardians including Vāyu in the northwest: "ནུབ་བྱང་དུ་རླུང་ལྷ" ("of the northwest — the wind-god"), alongside Indra (east), Yama (south), the water-god (west), Vaiśravaṇa (north), Agni (southeast), a rākṣasa-lord (southwest), and Īśāna (northeast). This citation is offered as an external doctrinal parallel for the directional-guardian scheme generally, not as a statement about Tārā's own worship; no other commentary in the packet cites this specific Cakrasaṃvara source for Vāyu.

## Vāyu's own epithets and cosmological function

### Consensus
None — every characterization of Vāyu beyond the bare worshipper-list name is a single commentary's own wording, and no two commentaries converge on the same formulation (see Unique below).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the three characterizations below describe different facets of Vāyu (an epithet, a cosmogonic function, a cosmological power) rather than making incompatible claims about the same point.

### Unique
Three commentaries independently give Vāyu a distinctive characterization beyond the bare worshipper-list name, each in different terms:

- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-5` names Vāyu "Possessor of Manifold Mounts" (སྣ་ཚོགས་རྟ་ཅན), glossing his epithet "of manifold aspect" (སྣ་ཚོགས་ཏེ): "ནུབ་བྱང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཏེ་སྣ་ཚོགས་རྟ་ཅན་དང་" — "the northwest guardian Vāyu of manifold aspect, that is, 'Possessor of Manifold Mounts.'"
- `taranatha:c-6-6` identifies Vāyu with the cosmogonic function of shaping the physical world: "རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གཟོའི་གནས་དང་བཀོད་པའི་ཁྱད་པར་བྱེད་པའོ།" — "Vāyu is the one who effects the particulars of the world's formation and arrangement." (This claim's own content is a definitional gloss on Vāyu, offered in the course of taranatha's exegesis of the same worshipper-verse; it does not itself assert that Vāyu worships Tārā, which is why `taranatha` is not counted in the worshipper-list facet's attestation list above, per Rule 9.)
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24` gives Vāyu the power to move the three states of existence: "སྲིད་པ་གསུམ་གཡོ་བར་ནུས་པའི་རླུང་ལྷ" — "Vāyu, able to move the three states of existence" (this claim is also cited above in the worshipper-list facet for its bare identification content; here it is additionally cited for this distinct functional epithet).

## Hidden-meaning (tantric) identification of Vāyu with the wind element

### Consensus
Two commentaries — `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` — read Vāyu's presence in the worshipper-verse as also carrying a tantric "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན), converging independently on the same elemental correspondence already noted for the other four worshipper-gods in `brahma.md` and `agni.md`:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-15`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ལྔ་ནི་འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་སྟེ། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས། མེ་ལྷ་ནི་མེ། ཚངས་པ་ནི་ཆུ། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནམ་མཁའ་འོ།" — "The hidden meaning: Indra and the other four are the five elements — Indra is earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, **Vāyu is wind**, and Viśveśvara is space." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` gives the identical mapping within its own completion-stage-with-marks exegesis: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས་དང་། མེ་ལྷ་མེ་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཆུ་དང་། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་དང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ནམ་མཁའ་ཡིན་པས་ན་..." — "Indra is earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, **Vāyu is wind**, and Viśveśvara is space — thus the elemental winds dissolving into the drop is 'worship.'" Vāyu-as-wind is, like Agni-as-fire, an identification where the deity's own name and the assigned element already coincide in ordinary usage (རླུང་ལྷ, "the wind-god," mapped to རླུང་, "wind") — both commentaries state it as a matter of course, without further comment on this coincidence.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## Claims reviewed, not separately cited

- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34` — this claim's own English gloss and stated referent (FIG-3, Indra) restrict its content to Indra; the quoted Tibetan fragment happens to include the running series "...མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ། ། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག..." because the commentary's root-verse quotation is split across several sequential per-deity claims (c-1-34 Indra, c-1-35 Agni, c-1-36 Brahmā, c-1-37 Vāyu). Citing it again for Vāyu content would misattribute Indra's own claim per Rule 9; the dedicated Vāyu claim `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-37` is cited above instead.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-3` — this claim's own content is primarily the narrative of Indra and Brahmā requesting the Buddha to turn the Dharma-wheel; it ends mid-list with "...these are the requesters Indra and Brahma; also the fire-god, and" (a sentence trailing directly into the next claim, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, which is the claim actually cited above for Vāyu content). Because this claim's own text never reaches the word རླུང་ལྷ, it is not itself a Vāyu hit and is logged here only for completeness given its adjacency to the cited claim.
