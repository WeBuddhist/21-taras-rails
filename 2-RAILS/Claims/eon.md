# Eon — བསྐལ་པ།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather note:** `eon` is a global cross-cutting topic, not one of the 21
> registered Tārā-verse spine slots, so there is no `assemble_packet.py` /
> spine-map packet for it. The claims below were located by searching every
> commentary's `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/<id>.md` file for the string
> བསྐལ་པ, then, once an unexpected first-pass silence turned out to be an
> orthographic-variant miss (see "Orthography," below), a second pass for the
> attested variant བསྐལ་བ across all sixteen files. Each hit's full claim
> block was then read (and, where a hit's immediate context contained an
> adjoining claim clearly continuing the same exposition, that adjoining
> claim too) directly from the raw file. Every quotation on this page is
> copied character-for-character from that read, never retyped from memory.
>
> **Scope note:** this page treats བསྐལ་པ (eon/kalpa) as a recurring
> cosmological time-unit concept across the corpus — the standard "fire at
> the end of the aeon" simile, its doctrinal and completion-stage readings,
> and every other attested use of the term (antiquity, meditative duration,
> hyperbolic dedication-unit) — rather than re-doing the full iconographic
> consolidation of the thirteenth homage (ཕྱག་འཚལ་བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར),
> which is `2-RAILS/Claims/tara-13.md`'s territory. Claims are cited here
> only for what they say about the eon/kalpa itself.

## Questions asked

1. What is the standard root-verse image associated with བསྐལ་པ (eon) in this corpus, and how consensually is it attested?
2. Do commentaries gloss "the final aeon['s fire]" with an explicit cosmological definition, and if so what?
3. Do commentaries read the eon-ending fire as symbolically identical to Tārā's own wisdom (ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ）?
4. Does any commentary elaborate the eon-ending fire with the "seven suns" cosmological detail?
5. Does any commentary offer a completion-stage ("hidden meaning") reading of the eon-ending fire, and if so what?
6. Does any commentary use "countless eons" as a temporal marker for Tārā's compassionate activity or the antiquity of her name, outside the fire-simile verse?
7. Does any commentary describe eons of meditative absorption as part of Tārā's own iconographic description?
8. Does any commentary invoke "eons as numerous as atoms of a buddha-field" as a hyperbolic unit in a dedication or aspiration prayer?

## Identity — the "fire at the end of the aeon" simile (thirteenth homage)

### Consensus

**All commentaries in the packet except one attest the corpus's standard root-verse image: Tārā dwelling amid a blazing garland "like the fire of the final aeon" (བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར / བསྐལ་པ་མཐའ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར), in the posture of right leg extended and left bent, destroying enemy armies** — whether the commentary quotes the verse in full, in part, or paraphrases its image:

— attested: `taranatha:c-13-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-38`, `yama-sonam:c-3-13-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-44`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-72`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2` (16 commentaries)

The root-verse wording, where quoted in full, runs close to identical across the packet, e.g. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-2`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བསྐལ་པ་མཐའ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར། །འབར་བའི་ཕྲེང་བའི་དབུས་ན་གནས་མ། །གཡས་བརྐྱངས་གཡོན་བསྐུམས་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་དགའ། །དགྲ་ཡི་དཔུང་ནི་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་མ།" ("Homage to her who dwells amid a blazing garland like the fire at the end of an aeon; right leg extended, left leg bent, encircled with delight, who utterly destroys the enemy's army") and `sungrab-tulku:c-16-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-2`, `yama-sonam:c-3-13-3` in near-identical form. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-16` gives the same image within a separate work's verse-by-verse deity list inside the same raw file ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་བསྐལ་པ་མཐའ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར... སྒྲོལ་མ་དགྲ་དཔུང་འཇོམས་མ་"). `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-2` give more compressed paraphrases rather than the full verse. `tenga-tulku` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-44` each supply a second claim continuing the same exposition, already folded into the count above. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2` quotes two external verses (a mahāsiddha's, and "the Jowo's") before restating the image in its own words: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བའི་ཡུལ་བསྐལ་བ་ཐ་མ་སྟེ། འཇིག་དུས་ཀྱི་མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བའི་འཕྲེང་བའི་དབུས་ན་གནས་མ།" ("the object of homage abides at the final aeon, amid a garland blazing like the fire of the age of destruction").

**Orthography.** Two spellings of "eon" are attested across the corpus: བསྐལ་པ (the standard spelling, used by every commentary above except one) and བསྐལ་བ (a pa/ba orthographic variant), which is the only spelling `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2` uses for this concept — its raw file never once uses བསྐལ་པ. A first search pass for the string བསྐལ་པ alone therefore returned zero hits in `konchok-thabkhe`'s raw file, which would have wrongly recorded it as silent on this topic; a second pass searching for བསྐལ་བ located this commentary's thirteenth-homage section (2.13, covering the same wrathful-abiding verse the other fifteen commentaries gloss with the eon-ending-fire simile) and its claim `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-13-2`. The same བསྐལ་བ variant also recurs in three further claims from `tenzin-dhonzang` and two from `tsultrim-namdak`, cited below under "Eon as the setting for scriptural transmission," "Recurrence of the fire-simile as an alternative visualization," and "Eons as a hyperbolic unit of duration or benefit." No claim in the packet treats the two spellings as denoting different concepts; this page uses བསྐལ་པ throughout its own prose, preserving each claim's own spelling inside verbatim quotations.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed regarding the basic image. Every attestation converges on the same cosmological simile (the fire that consumes the world at the end of an aeon) applied to Tārā's blazing, wrathful appearance in the thirteenth homage.

### Unique

None beyond the minimal-form attestations already folded into the Consensus statement above.

## Cosmological gloss — "the final aeon" as "the fire at the time of the world's destruction"

### Consensus

A majority of the commentaries above additionally supply an explicit word-gloss identifying "the final aeon['s fire]" as the fire that consumes the world at the time of cosmic destruction ('jig pa'i dus kyi me), rather than leaving the phrase unglossed:

— attested: `taranatha:c-13-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1` (8 commentaries)

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-13-2` gives the clearest single-clause gloss: "བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མ་སྟེ་འཇིག་པའི་དུས་ཀྱི་མེ" ("'The final aeon['s fire]' is glossed as 'the fire at the time of the world's destruction'"). `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3` gives the fullest elaboration: "བསྐལ་པ་མཐའ་མའི་མེ་ནི། ཉི་མ་བདུན་ལས་ཚ་བའི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་ས་རྡོ་རྣམས་སྲེག་པ་ལྟར" ("'the fire at the end of an aeon' — like the searing light-rays of seven suns burning earth and stone"), which also supplies the "seven suns" detail (see below). `taranatha:c-13-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-3`, and `gendun-gyatso:c-1-13-1` all give close paraphrases of the same gloss ("the fire at the time of destruction/the destruction of an aeon").

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed. No commentary offers a conflicting definition of what the "final aeon['s fire]" refers to cosmologically.

### Unique

None beyond the attestations above.

## Doctrinal reading — the eon-ending fire as Tārā's own wisdom (ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ)

### Consensus

Nine commentaries, beyond the bare cosmological image, explicitly identify the blazing fire itself as Tārā's own non-dual wisdom (ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ / ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་དཔུང), rather than leaving the fire as an external cosmological comparison only:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-38`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-16-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-45` (9 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-16-3` is the clearest single-clause statement: "བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མ་སྟེ་འཇིག་པའི་དུས་ཀྱི་མེ་དཔུང་ཆེན་པོ་ལྟར་ངོ་བོ་གཉིས་སུ་མེད་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཡིན་པ་ལ་རྣམ་པ་མེ་དཔུང་འབར་བའི་ཕྲེང་བའི་དབུས་ན་གནས་པའམ" ("like the great mass of fire at the time of destruction, the final aeon — [this is] her non-dual wisdom in essence, appearing in the aspect of a blazing mass-of-fire garland, in the midst of which she dwells"). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-45` makes the identification most explicit as a hermeneutical instruction: "མལ་གྱི་མེ་ལས་ཚ་བས་ན་དེ་དང་འདྲ་བ་ནི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་ལ་གོ་དགོས་པས" ("because it is hotter than ordinary fire, what is comparable to it must be understood as the fire of wisdom"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-14-0-1`: "ངོ་བོ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ལ་རྣམ་པ་མེ་དཔུང་འབར་བའི་ཕྲེང་བའི་དབུས་ན" ("in essence [she is] wisdom, [appearing] in the aspect of a garland of blazing masses of fire"). `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-38` frames it as an inner-to-outer correspondence: "ནང་དུ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་སྒྲོན་མེ་འབར་བས་ཕྱི་རུ་ཁྲོ་ཚུལ་བསྐལ་པའི་དུས་ཀྱི་མཐའ་མའི་མེ་ལྟར" ("because the lamp of wisdom blazes within, outwardly, in wrathful aspect, [she appears] like the fire at the end of the aeon"). `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-1-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-6-2`, and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-3` all use the compound ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་(དཔུང) in the same construction.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed. No commentary in the packet denies or offers an alternative to the fire-as-wisdom reading; the remaining commentaries in "Identity," above, simply do not add this layer, which is a difference in how much doctrinal detail each supplies rather than a conflicting position.

### Unique

None beyond the attestations above.

## Seven-suns cosmological elaboration

### Consensus

Two commentaries elaborate the standard "fire at the end of the aeon" image with the specific Abhidharma cosmological detail that this fire is hotter than, or coincides with, the rising of seven suns at the aeon's end:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-13-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-72` (2 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-13-4`: "དུས་མཐར་བསྐལ་པ་འཇིག་པའི་ཚེ། ཉི་མ་བདུན་ལས་ཀྱང་ཚ་བའི་མེ་འཇིག་གི་མེ་ལྟ་བུའི། མེ་སྤུངས་དྲག་པོ་ཕྲེང་བ་བཞིན་དུ་བར་མ་ཆད་དུ་འབར་བའི་དབུས་ན" ("at the end of time, when the aeon is destroyed, in the midst of a fierce mass of fire, blazing uninterruptedly like a garland — like the fire of destruction, hotter even than seven suns"). `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-72`: "བསྐལ་པ་མཐའ་མ་ཉི་མ་བདུན་ཤར་བའི་དུས་སུ་ས་རྡོ་རི་བྲག་ཐམས་ཅད་མེ་ལྟར་འབར་ནས་འོང་བས" ("at the aeon's end, when seven suns rise, earth, stones, and mountains all come to blaze like fire"). This claim also appears above under "Identity," attesting the base image; it is cited a second time here specifically for its seven-suns elaboration (Rule 9: the two facets are separate statements, and this claim supports both in full).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

None beyond the corroborated pair above.

## Completion-stage ("hidden meaning") readings of the eon-ending fire (Palden Sherab)

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — the two readings below are complementary stages of one commentary's own exegetical scheme (completion-stage-with-marks, then completion-stage-without-marks/ultimate), not competing positions.

### Unique

Palden Sherab alone, in a four-stage exegesis of the thirteenth homage (word-meaning → general meaning/generation-stage → hidden meaning/completion-stage-with-marks → ultimate meaning/completion-stage-without-marks), supplies two distinct completion-stage readings of the eon-ending fire not found in any other commentary in the packet:

- **Completion-stage-with-marks: the fire as Caṇḍālī's inner heat.** `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-3-1`: "གསུམ་པ་སྦས་དོན་རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས་ནི། བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མའི་མེ་ལྟ་བུ་ནི་དབྱིངས་ཀྱི་རང་གཟུགས་རབ་ཚ་བ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་མེ་ཨ་ཤད་ཙཎྜ་ལཱིའོ།" ("As for the third, the hidden meaning according to the completion-stage-with-marks: 'like the fire of the final aeon' is the expanse's own extremely hot essence-form, the wisdom-fire — [i.e.] Caṇḍālī's short-A"). The same claim reads the extended/bent legs as the descent of the Rasanā-moon and Lalanā-sun from the crown and secret place.
- **Completion-stage-without-marks (ultimate): the fire as self-arisen awareness-emptiness.** `palden-sherab:c-3-1-13-4-1`: "བཞི་པ་མཐར་ཐུག་མཚན་མེད་རྫོགས་རིམ་ལྟར་ན། བསྐལ་པ་ཐ་མ་མེ་ནི་རིག་སྟོང་རང་བྱུང་མངོན་གྱུར་པས་འཁྲུལ་སྣང་གི་འཛིན་ཞེན་ཐམས་ཅད་ལྷག་མེད་བསྲེགས་ནུས་པའོ།" ("As for the fourth, the ultimate meaning according to the completion-stage-without-marks: 'the fire of the final aeon' is self-arisen awareness-emptiness manifest, capable of burning without remainder all clinging to deluded appearance").

## "Countless eons" — antiquity of Tārā's name and compassionate activity (Anon. Trinlé Char)

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary, in a different verse-context from the thirteenth-homage fire simile (the etymological opening on the name "Tārā," near the text's outset).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

`yama-sonam:c-3-1-5`, glossing the etymology of the name "Tārā" (Deliverer), states that the goddess has borne this name since countless eons ago, on the strength of her deliverance of beings: "བསྐལ་པ་དཔག་ཏུ་མེད་པའི་སྔོན་རོལ་ནས་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་པའི་མཚན་གྱིས་བསྔགས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ།" ("she has been praised by the name 'Tārā' since countless eons ago"). This is the only claim in the packet using བསྐལ་པ as a marker of temporal antiquity for Tārā's own enlightened activity, rather than as part of the thirteenth-homage cosmological simile.

## Eons as a hyperbolic unit of duration or benefit (dedication and benefit-comparison contexts)

### Consensus

None — the three items below come from two commentaries and use the unit in three distinct ways (buddha-field atoms, a round "hundred," and a comparison against continuous buddha-worship); they are not independent attestations of one shared statement and so are kept as attributed Unique items rather than merged.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — the three usages are compatible (all use large or hyperbolic numbers of eons as a rhetorical unit), not conflicting.

### Unique

- **Eons as numerous as the atoms of a buddha-field, requesting buddhas' continued presence.** `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-6-1`, in the "requesting non-passing into nirvāṇa" branch of a seven-branch prayer appended to the sādhana material in this raw file (not the root twenty-one-homage praise itself): "བསྐལ་པ་ཞིང་གི་རྡུལ་སྙེད་བཞུགས་པར་ཡང་། །བདག་གིས་ཐལ་མོ་རབ་སྦྱར་གསོལ་བར་བགྱི།" ("may they remain for aeons as many as atoms of the field — with palms joined, I make this request").
- **"A hundred aeons" of stability for lineage-holders, in a colophon dedication.** `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-12`, closing the same raw file's fifth work: "...བསྟན་འཛིན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་རྣམས་ཞབས་པད་བསྐལ་བརྒྱར་བརྟན་ཅིང་..." ("...may teaching-holders' lives be firm for a hundred aeons..."). This claim uses the བསྐལ་བ orthographic variant (see "Orthography," above).
- **A single prostration outweighing worship of countless buddhas across countless eons.** `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-101`, quoting "the Avalokiteśvara Sūtra": "སངས་རྒྱས་བྱེ་བ་ཁྲག་ཁྲིག་ལ་བསྐལ་བ་བྱེ་བར་བསྟི་སྟང་བྱེད་པས། ཕྱི་མའི་དུས་སུ་མཆོད་རྟེན་དང་སྐུ་གཟུགས་ལ་ཕྱག་གཅིག་འཚལ་བ་ན་ཕན་ཡོན་ཆེ་བར་གསུངས།" ("it is taught that offering a single prostration to a stūpa or image in later times has as great a benefit as revering countless millions of buddhas for countless eons"). This claim also uses the བསྐལ་བ variant, within a run of scriptural citations on the general benefits of homage that this raw file attaches to the corpus's closing verses, not to the thirteenth homage itself.
