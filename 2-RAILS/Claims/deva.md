# Deva/god (as a being-class) — ལྷ།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that
> pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and *deva/god as a
> being-class* is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead, following the same
> convention as the sibling global topics `mantra`, `gdon`, `mara`, `vetala`,
> `wheel`, `pacification`, `three-jewels`, `ushnisha`, `hum-syllable`,
> `tara-mantra`, `indra`, `brahma`, `agni`, and `vayu`. No deterministic
> packet-assembly script exists for it, so the gather step was performed
> directly: `rg -n 'ལྷ'` across all sixteen files at
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` (filtered with a syllable-boundary
> pattern — ལྷ followed by tsheg, shad, ར, ས, or འི — to drop the large class
> of unrelated Tibetan syllables that merely contain ལྷ as a substring: ལྷོ
> "south," ལྷག "surplus"/ལྷག་མཐོང་ "insight," ལྷན་ "together," ལྷོད་ "relaxed,"
> ལྷུང་ "fall," ལྷམ་ "shoe," etc.), followed by manual triage of every
> remaining claim block. Every quotation below was copied character-for-character
> from that block's own **བོད་ཡིག:** field — never retyped or reconstructed
> from memory.
>
> **Scope boundary (per the task brief, strictly applied).** This page treats
> ལྷ only as a *generic class-noun* — "a god," "the gods," "deva-" as a
> cosmological or adjectival modifier — never as part of a name or epithet
> whose referent is one specific, already-identified deity. The filtered
> search returned several hundred claim-block hits; the great majority were
> **categorically excluded, not entered into this packet at all**, because
> their content is about a specific referent, not the class:
> - **ལྷ་མོ ("the goddess") referring to Tārā herself** — by a wide margin the
>   single most frequent hit in every one of the sixteen files, since ལྷ་མོ is
>   commentaries' standing epithet for the text's own subject throughout every
>   homage. Tārā is a specific named deity (indeed *the* deity this whole vault
>   is about), not the being-class ལྷ; every occurrence of ལྷ་མོ, ལྷ་མོའི་སྐུ
>   ("the goddess's body"), ལྷའི་སྐུ ("the divine body," of Tārā), and similar
>   phrases referring to Tārā is excluded.
> - **Compounds naming a specific deity already fully treated on its own
>   consolidated page**: མེ་ལྷ (Agni — `agni.md`), རླུང་ལྷ (Vāyu — `vayu.md`),
>   ལྷའི་དབང་པོ / ལྷ་དབང་ / ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་(རྣམས་ཀྱི་)རྒྱལ་པོ (Indra's standard
>   epithets — `indra.md`), and the fivefold worshipper-verse's own root-text
>   quotations when cited purely for identifying those named gods (already
>   fully attested on those four pages).
> - **ལྷ་མིན ("non-god," asura/demigod)** — a distinct being-class defined by
>   *negating* ལྷ, not an instance of it; e.g. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-77`,
>   `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-7-7`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`'s own list. Not
>   entered as ལྷ-content, though `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2` is cited below for
>   its *other* content (the sevenfold rebirth-class list, which does include
>   ལྷ as one member).
> - **Personal/place epithets built on the syllable ལྷ but naming something
>   else entirely**: e.g. `palden-sherab:c-0-40`'s honorific "Dharma-king

>   `palden-sherab:c-0-18`'s "the goddess Sarasvatī" (དབྱངས་ཅན་ལྷ་མོ) reborn in
>   human form; and section/node headings that merely repeat a root-verse
>   phrase already excluded above.
>
> **Ambiguous cases, judged explicitly rather than silently resolved (per
> Rule 6):**
> - `taranatha:c-6-3` ("བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ལྷའི་འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོའོ།" — "Indra is

>   devaputra-māra" (ལྷའི་བུ, literally "the god's-child [māra]") as one of
>   the four standard māras. This does use ལྷ generically (not naming an
>   individual god), but its own content is a technical doctrinal
>   classification of a *māra*, not a statement about devas as worshippers,
>   a cosmological realm, or a being-class in the senses the other facets
>   below establish. **Judged: excluded as off-topic for this page's facets**,
>   logged below rather than silently dropped.

2. Do commentaries classify ལྷ as one of the five/six/seven classes of migrating beings in the rebirth scheme, and how much internal structure (e.g. a sixfold subdivision of the desire-realm devas themselves) do they supply?

4. Does ལྷ appear as a poetic epithet or kenning (e.g. "deva-lake" for the moon) independent of any specific named deity, and do commentaries diverge on how literally or figuratively to read it?
5. Do any commentaries name a special class of nature-deities (mountain-, tree-, lake-gods) as a subtype of ལྷ, distinct from the named worshipper-gods already treated on indra.md/brahma.md/agni.md/vayu.md?

## The deva-realm as one of the cosmological "three worlds"

### Consensus
Six commentaries explicitly name the deva-realm (ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན / ལྷ་སྟེ་...འཇིག་རྟེན) as the uppermost of the "three worlds" (འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ) — nāga-world below the earth, human world on the earth, deva-world above — most often while glossing Avalokiteśvara's epithet "protector of the three worlds," or while describing the three worlds shaken by Tārā's power:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-19`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` (6 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-4-11` gives the cleanest bare definition: "འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ནི། ས་འོག་ཀླུའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་སྟེང་མིའི་འཇིག་རྟེན། ས་བླ་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བཅས་ཡིན།" — "The three worlds are: the nāga world below the earth, the human world on the earth, and the god world above the earth." `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-8` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-19` give the same threefold division while identifying Avalokiteśvara as its protector. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` both use the identical threefold division in the context of Tārā's HŪṂ-power shaking Mount Meru and the three worlds. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-1-3` gives it as part of the account of Tārā's own origin from Avalokiteśvara's tears.

Nine further commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-drub`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `konchok-thabkhe`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `taranatha`) also gloss Avalokiteśvara's "protector of the three worlds" epithet somewhere in their raw files, but their own wording names only "ས་འོག / ས་སྟེང་ / ས་བླ" ("below / on the surface / above [the earth]") without using the word ལྷ to label the upper world explicitly (e.g. `karma-maitri`'s own claim: "ས་འོག་ས་སྟེང་ས་བླའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་"). This is a difference in how explicitly each commentary names the upper world, not a disagreement about its content — those nine claims accordingly contain no ལྷ-hit and are not entered into this packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## ལྷ as one of the classes of migrating beings in the rebirth scheme

### Consensus
Fourteen commentaries gloss the standard "seven worlds" (འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན) trampled or summoned by Tārā as the five, six, or seven classes of migrating beings in the rebirth scheme, and every version of this list that specifies its members includes ལྷ (deva) as one class, standing alongside hell-being, preta, animal, human, (asura,) and the form/formless-realm beings:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`, `taranatha:c-5-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-7` (14 commentaries)

The fullest explicit enumerations: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5`: "དམྱལ་བ། ཡི་དྭགས། དུད་འགྲོ། མི། ལྷ་སྟེ་འདོད་ཁམས་ཀ�ྱི་འགྲོ་བ་རྒྱུད་ལྔ་དང་།" — "Hell-being, preta, animal, human, and deva — the five classes of migrators of the desire realm." `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`: "འདོད་པའི་ཁམས་ནི། དམྱལ་བ་དང་། ཡི་དྭགས། དུད་འགྲོ། མི། འདོད་ལྷ་སྟེ་ལྔ་དང་། ཕྱོགས་དང་ནི། གཟུགས་ཁམས། ནམ་མཁའ་ནི། གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་བཅས" — "the desire realm, namely the five of hell-beings, hungry ghosts, animals, humans, and desire-gods; 'the directions' meaning the form realm; 'space' meaning the formless realm." `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2` gives a sevenfold variant that inserts asuras and splits humans from kinnaras: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ཏེ། ཀླུ། ཡི་དྭགས། ལྷ་མ་ཡིན། རིག་འཛིན། མི། མིའམ་ཅི། ལྷ་སྟེ་བདུན་པོ་ཞབས་ཀྱིས་མནན་པ་..." — "The seven worlds — nāgas, pretas, asuras, vidyādharas, humans, kinnaras, and devas — are trodden underfoot..." `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4` gives the fullest structural gloss of all seven: "འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་པོ་ནི། འདོད་ཁམས་ལ་ངན་སོང་གསུམ། ལྷ་དང་མི་ཡི་གནས་ཏེ་གནས་རིས་ལྔ་དང་། དེའི་སྟེང་དུ་གཟུགས་ཁམས་དང་གཟུགས་མེད་ཁམས་བཅས་བདུན་པོ་..." — "The seven worlds are: the three lower realms of the desire realm; the abodes of gods and humans, five abodes [in all]; and above these the form realm and formless realm — seven in all." `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-7`, and `gendun-gyatso:c-1-14-2` all give shorter forms of the same fivefold/sixfold-plus-bardo count ("རིགས་དྲུག་བར་དོ་དང་བཅས་པའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་པོ," etc.), each independently confirming that ལྷ (or, in `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, the doublet "ལྷ་མི་གཉིས," "the two higher [rebirths], god and human") is one of the classes summoned or trodden. `taranatha:c-5-6` gives the same sevenfold list in its own independent wording: "འདིར་ནི་མཐུ་དང་ལྡན་པའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་བདུན་ཏེ། ཀླུ་དང་ཡི་དྭགས་དང་ལྷ་མ་ཡིན་དང་། མི་དང་རིག་པ་འཛིན་པ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི་དང་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་ནོ།" — "Here, the seven powerful worlds are: nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras, and the deva world."

One commentary gives further internal structure to the deva-class itself: `taranatha:c-19-2`, glossing a later homage's "hosts of gods," specifies that within the desire realm the deva-class further divides into six: "ལྷའི་ཚོགས་པ་སྟེ་འདོད་པའི་ཁམས་ཀྱི་ལྷ་རིགས་དྲུག་པོ་རྣམས་ཡིན་ལ།" — "'hosts of gods' are the six classes of gods of the desire realm." No other commentary in this packet gives this internal sixfold subdivision.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed. Commentaries vary between five-, six-, and seven-part enumerations of the same underlying rebirth scheme (whether asuras and the bardo state are counted separately or folded in), but no commentary excludes ལྷ from the list or disputes its place in it — this is a difference of enumeration granularity, not a substantive disagreement.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-19-2`'s sixfold internal subdivision of the desire-realm devas (see above) is not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

## "Gods and humans" (ལྷ་མི) as the collective beneficiaries of worldly, as opposed to ultimate, reward

### Consensus
Two commentaries name "gods and humans" (ལྷ་མི) as a collective doublet denoting the recipients of higher, worldly rebirth-happiness, distinguished from the ultimate reward of liberation:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-1-5-2` (2 commentaries)

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-4`, glossing the epithet "Bliss" (བདེ་མ): "རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་གང་ལ་བསྟེན་ཅིང་མཆོད་པའི་སྒྲུབ་པ་པོ་རྣམས་ལ་མངོན་མཐོ་ལྷ་མིའི་བདེ་བ་སྩོལ་བར་མཛད་པས་ན་བདེ་མ།" — "because, for practitioners who rely on and worship her, the venerable lady bestows the higher rebirth of gods and humans, she is 'Bliss.'" `pema-namgyal:c-1-5-2`, in a different context (the request for teaching suited to different capacities), names "ཐུན་མོང་བ་མཐོ་རིས་ལྷ་མི་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཐེག་པ" — "the vehicle common to [both] higher rebirth, [that is,] the two of gods and humans" — as one of the vehicles the Dharma-wheel accommodates, alongside the greater and lesser vehicles.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the two attestations above.

## "ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ" (deva-lake) as a poetic epithet for the moon

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries quote or gloss the eighteenth homage-verse's phrase "ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ" / "ལྷའི་མཚོ" ("deva-lake" or "divine lake") as a kenning for the moon-disc Tārā holds, marked with the sign of a deer or hare:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-18-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-88`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-18-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-48`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-21-2`, `taranatha:c-18-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-3-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-18-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-61`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-21` (16 commentaries)

The root-verse wording is stable, e.g. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-3-2`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པའི།། རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅད་ཕྱག་ན་བསྣམས་མ།།" — "Homage to her who holds in her hand [the moon marked with] the sign of the hare, resembling a divine lake." Most commentaries then gloss the epithet with a simple simile, e.g. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-3-3`: "ལྷའི་མཚོ་ལྟ་བུའི་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་རི་བོང་གི་རྟགས་ཅན" — "the moon-disc marked with a hare, like a divine lake"; `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-2`: "ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པ་སྟེ། ཉ་གང་བའི་རི་དྭགས་རྟགས་ཅན་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར" — "in the form of a divine lake: the full-moon maṇḍala marked with the sign of a deer." `karma-maitri:c-1-1-18-2` extends the image to a lake of nectar: "ལྷ་ཡི་བདུད་རྩིའི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་ཅན" — "she has the nature of a lake of divine nectar." `sangye-nyentrul:c-19-0-1` gives the same nectar-lake image: "ལྷ་ཡི་ཡུལ་གྱི་བདུད་རྩིའི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པའི་དཀར་ཞིང་" — "white like the aspect of the nectar-lake in the divine realm."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed regarding what the epithet names (the moon-disc); the commentaries diverge, without conflict, only in *how figuratively or literally* to unpack "deva-lake" — see Unique, below, for the readings distinctive enough to record.

### Unique
Three commentaries offer readings of "deva-lake" beyond the plain moon-disc simile, each independent:
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-18-4` reports a mahāsiddha's citation tracing the epithet to a Vedic myth: "འཕགས་མ་ལྷ་ཡི་ངོ་འཛིན་ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ནི། སྔོན་རི་རབ་ཀྱིས་བསྲུབས་ཤིང་བྱས་ཏེ་རྒྱ་མཚོ་བསྲུབས་པ་ལས་ཉི་ཟླ་གཉིས་ཐོན་པར་རིག་བྱེད་ཀྱི་གཞུང་ལས་འབྱུང་བ་དང་བསྟུན་པའོ།" — "'the Noble Lady is recognized as [the deity] of the lake' accords with the Vedic account that Mount Meru once churned the ocean, producing the sun and moon."
- `taranatha:c-18-2` reads the epithet non-literally, as the practitioner's own mind: "ལྷའི་མཚོ་སྟེ། ཡིད་ཀྱི་མཚོའམ་རིང་བུ་དལ་གྱིས་འབབ་པ་དང་འདྲ་བའི་རྣམ་པ་ཅན་ནི།" — "'the divine lake' — that is, the mind's lake, or [something] resembling a stream flowing gently at a distance."
- `palden-sherab` gives the epithet two further "hidden meaning" readings at its two deepest exegetical levels — completion-stage-with-marks: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-3-1`: "ལྷའི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པ་ནི་རྡོ་རྗེ་ཕུང་པོའི་གྲོང་ཁྱེར་ཀྱི་ལུས་ཡིན་ལ།" — "'the divine lake' is the body, the citadel of the vajra-aggregates"; and completion-stage-without-marks: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-18-4-1`: "ལྷ་ཡི་མཚོ་ཡི་རྣམ་པ་ནི་གནས་ལུགས་རྫོགས་པ་ཆེན་པོའོ།" — "'the divine lake' is the abiding-nature Great Perfection." No other commentary in the packet carries the epithet through this many exegetical levels.

## Nature-deities (mountain-, tree-, lake-gods) as a special class of ལྷ

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently name a class of "special" (ཁྱད་པར) local gods — of mountains, trees, and lakes — as a subtype of ལྷ distinct from the great worldly gods already treated on `indra.md`/`brahma.md`/`agni.md`/`vayu.md`:

— attested: `taranatha:c-19-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-19-4`: "འཇིག་རྟེན་དུ་ཕན་གནོད་བྱེད་པ་རིའི་ལྷ་དང་ཤིང་གི་ལྷ་དང་། །མཚོའི་ལྷ་མོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་ནི་ལྷའི་ཁྱད་པར་ཏེ།" — "The mountain gods and tree gods, and lake goddesses and so on, who benefit and harm in the world, are the special classes of gods." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` gives the same triad in its own wording, glossing the same verse: "འཇིག་རྟེན་ན་ཕན་གནོད་བྱེད་པའི་རི་ཡི་ལྷ་དང་། ཤིང་གི་ལྷ་དང་། མཚོ་ཡི་ལྷ་སོགས" — "the mountain-gods, tree-gods, lake-gods, and other nature-spirits that benefit and harm in the world."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the corroborated pair above.

## "ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ" / "འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ" as the classificatory term for the category of worldly gods

### Consensus
Five commentaries use "ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ" ("great god[s]") or "འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ" ("worldly god[s]") as a structural, classificatory label for the *category* into which Indra, Agni, Brahmā, and Vāyu are individually placed — content about the term itself, distinct from the identity-content already cited on those four deities' own pages:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-5` (5 commentaries)

`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-1` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-1` give near-identical bare section-openers naming the category with no individual god yet mentioned: "ལྔ་པ་འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་བཀུར་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི།" / "...བསྟོད་པ་ནི།" — "Fifth, praising through being honoured by the great worldly gods." `sungrab-tulku:c-9-1` gives the same as a numbered section marker: "༼༦༽འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་བཀུར་བའི་བསྟོད་པ།" `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3` places the same category as one item in a sixfold taxonomy of praise-types: "...འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་བཀུར་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ...་" — "...praise by way of being honored by the great gods of the world..." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-5` uses the category in an *a fortiori* argument distinct from all of the above: "འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་དང་། ...བྱེད་ན་ངེད་ཚོ་མི་རྣམས་ལྟ་ཅིག་སྨོས་དགོས་" — "If even the great mundane gods prostrate to her...then what need is there to mention us humans?" — treating "the great worldly gods" as a category whose devotion sets a rhetorical benchmark, independent of which individual gods belong to it.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-5`'s *a fortiori* framing (see above) is not paralleled by the other four claims, which use the category only as a structural section-label.
