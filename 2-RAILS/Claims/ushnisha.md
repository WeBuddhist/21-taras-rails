## Questions asked

1. What text do commentaries quote as the root verse addressed to the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa, and how consistent is the wording across transmissions?
2. By what name or epithet do commentaries identify the deity addressed in this verse, and do all of them use the same epithet?
3. How do commentaries characterize the goddess's relation to the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa — as a simile (comparison), as a location (dwelling atop/at the crown), or as a source (emanated from the uṣṇīṣa)?
4. What reason, if any, do commentaries give for why she is honored as, dwells at, or emanates from the uṣṇīṣa?
5. Does any commentary read "uṣṇīṣa" through completion-stage or subtle-body (channel/wind/drop) doctrine, and if so, how?
6. Does any commentary associate a second deity — Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā (Gtsug-tor-gdugs-dkar-mo) — with the Tathāgatas' crown-protuberance?
7. Does any commentary cite the uṣṇīṣa in connection with the Buddha's thirty-two major marks or a visionary/cognitive function?
8. Does any commentary attribute a particular exegetical reading of the uṣṇīṣa explicitly to a named master (e.g. "Jonangpa," "Longchenpa") rather than presenting it as its own view?
9. Does any commentary read the verse's mention of the uṣṇīṣa as corresponding to a specific step of generation-stage ritual (deity-empowerment)?
10. Does any commentary connect the "uṣṇīṣa" epithet to a cosmically expanded scope — e.g. "crown-jewel" of the buddhas of all ten directions and five families?

## The root verse and its attestation

### Consensus
Ten claims across eight commentaries quote the root verse's opening line — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར།" ("Homage — [she of] the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas") — verbatim, or near-verbatim subject to minor orthographic/punctuation variation across transmissions (e.g. lobsang-dawa's claim spells the word "གཙུག་གཏོར" rather than "གཙུག་ཏོར", and sungrab-tulku's `sungrab-tulku:c-7-3` spells it "གཙུག་ཏོག" — orthographic variants of the same term, not treated as a substantive divergence).

Six commentaries give the full four lines: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར། མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ(ར)་སྤྱོད་མ། མ་ལུས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་པའི། རྒྱལ་བ(འི)་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བསྟེན་མ།" — "Homage to her [dwelling at] the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa, whose conduct is victorious over the limitless, utterly relied upon by the victors' sons who have attained the perfections without exception."

— attested (full four lines): `yama-sonam:c-3-4-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-8`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13` (6 commentaries)

Three commentaries give only the opening line, or the opening two lines, rather than the full four:

— attested (opening line only, or first two lines): `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-2` (3 commentaries; sungrab-tulku is counted once here even though it also gives the full four lines at `sungrab-tulku:c-7-2` above, per Rule 15's unique-commentary count)

Anon-Trinle-Char's claim frames the quotation as drawn "from the tantra" (རྒྱུད་ལས), rather than presenting it purely as the root praise-verse itself — a framing distinct from the other seven attestations, which quote it as the praise's own fourth verse. `yama-sonam:c-3-4-3`

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary quotes a substantively different wording of the verse.

### Unique
None beyond the framing note above.

## Identity of the deity addressed: the epithet Uṣṇīṣavijayā

### Consensus
Five commentaries identify the deity of this homage-verse by a shared epithet built on "གཙུག་ཏོར(ག)་རྣམ་(པར་)རྒྱལ་མ" — Uṣṇīṣavijayā / "Crown-Victorious."

— attested (base epithet): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (5 commentaries)

Of these, three further qualify the epithet with "འཆི་མེད་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ(་པ)" ("Deathless Life-Accomplishing"): palden-sherab's claim reads "འཆི་མེད་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ", sangye-nyentrul's reads the identical "འཆི་མེད་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ", and tsultrim-namdak's reads "འཆི་མེད་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་པ་གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ". Sungrab-tulku's and tenzin-dhonzang's claims give only the base epithet, without this qualifier, so per Rule 9 the "Deathless Life-Accomplishing" statement is not extended to all five.

— attested (with "Deathless Life-Accomplishing" qualifier): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (3 commentaries)

Of the same three, two additionally describe her body colour as golden: sangye-nyentrul's claim states "སྐུ་གསེར་མདོག" ("her body has a golden colour"), and tsultrim-namdak's states "སྐུ་མདོག་གསེར་གྱི་མདོག་ཅན" ("golden-hued").

— attested (golden body colour): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary contradicts the "Uṣṇīṣavijayā" identification; see Unique below for a differently-named epithet that does not conflict with it (it is simply not attested elsewhere).

### Unique
Gendun-Drub's commentary alone gives this Tārā the distinct epithet "གཙུག་ཏོར་རྒྱན་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ" — "Tārā Adorned as the Crown-Ornament" — a name built on the same "uṣṇīṣa" root but not identical to "Uṣṇīṣavijayā" attested above; it is not independently corroborated by any other commentary in the packet. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-5`

## Nature of her relation to the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa

### Consensus
None — the corpus does not converge on a single characterization of the goddess's relation to the uṣṇīṣa. Three distinct framings are attested, recorded as ⚑ Divergences below rather than flattened into one statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
Three positions on how the goddess relates to the Tathāgatas' crown-protuberance:

**Position 1 — simile: she is compared to, or honored as, the uṣṇīṣa (an ornament/object-of-worship comparison, not a claim about her literal location or origin):**

Drakpa-Gyaltsen: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟ་བུའོ།" ("she is like the crown-protuberance of all the Tathāgatas"). Gendun-Gyatso: she is one "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང། གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་མཆོད་པའི་གནས་སུ་སྒྱུར་ཅིང" ("whom all Tathāgatas make into an object of worship like the uṣṇīṣa"). Karma-Maitri: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་མཆོད་པའི་གནས་སུ་གྱུར་ཅིང" — near-identical wording. Konchok-Thabkhe: she is "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་བཀུར་བའི་འོས་སུ་གྱུར་པ" ("worthy of being honored even by all tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance"). Gendun-Drub: since she is mother of all Tathāgatas "དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་འཁུར་ཞིང" ("she is borne like the crown-ornament atop the head").

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-23`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3` (5 commentaries)

**Position 2 — locative: she dwells at, or abides atop, the crown of the uṣṇīṣa itself (a spatial claim, not merely a comparison):**

Anon-Trinle-Char: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་བཞུགས་ཤིང" ("you abide at the crown-uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas' heads"). Anon-Utpala: "དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་གྱི་རྩེ་ན" ("at the very crown of the uṣṇīṣa on their heads" — locative case). Lobsang-Dawa: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་གཏོར་ན" ("at the crown-protuberance of the Tathāgata" — locative case).

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-4-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2` (3 commentaries)

**Position 3 — emanation: she arises from, or is emanated out of, the uṣṇīṣa — a causal/origin claim, several specifying the Tathāgata Vairocana as its source:**

Anon-Trinle-Char reports this reading explicitly as "Jonangpa's interpretation" (ཇོ་ནང་པས་...འགྲེལ་བ), not as its own default reading: "ཇོ་ནང་པས་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པ་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལས་རིག་སྔགས་ཀྱི་ཡེ་ཤེས་སྤྲུལ་པ་ལྷ་མོ་འདིས་བདག་གིར་མཛད" ("this goddess has taken possession of the wisdom-emanation of the vidyā-mantra arising from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana"). Palden-Sherab, without attributing the reading to a named source: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལས་རིག་སྔགས་ཡེ་ཤེས་ལྷ་མོར་སྤྲུལ་པ" ("emanated as the vidyā-mantra wisdom-goddess from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata"). Sangye-Nyentrul: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལས་སྤྲུལ་པ" ("an emanation from the crown-protuberance of the Tathāgatas"). Taranatha, without naming Vairocana in the same claim's own following clause: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱི་གཙུག་གཏོར་ལས" ("from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana"). Tsultrim-Namdak's two claims independently give the same Vairocana-sourced reading — `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13` ends mid-word naming "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པར་སྣང" (Vairocana, truncated in the packet), and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` gives the same emanation content in substance ("emanated from the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa," per that claim's own English gloss), though its own Tibetan wording does not contain the exact string "...ལས་...སྤྲུལ་པ" quoted for the other attestations above — the emanation-position claim rests here on the claim's English gloss rather than a verbatim Tibetan match.

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-4-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-1`, `taranatha:c-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (5 commentaries)

No commentary in the packet reconciles these three framings or states that one supersedes the others.

### Unique
Taranatha's commentary separately gives a ritual-correlation reading distinct from all three positions above: among a list of phrase-to-generation-stage correspondences, "the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa" (དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར) is said to indicate "ལྷ་དབང་བསྐུར་བ" ("the deity-empowerment [step of the sādhana]") — a hermeneutic mode about ritual structure, not a claim about the goddess's ontological relation to the uṣṇīṣa. `taranatha:c-21-4-16`

Pema-Namgyal's commentary expands the object of homage beyond a single Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa to a cosmic scope: she is "ཕྱོགས་བཅུའི་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔའི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་གཙུག་གཏོར་གྱི་ནོར་བུ" ("the crown-jewel of the assembled tathāgatas, the victors of the five families throughout the ten directions"), consecrated as "མཐའ་ཡས་པའི་དབུ་རྒྱན་རིགས་ཀྱི་བདག་མོ" ("mistress of the boundless crown-ornament family") — a related but more expansive framing than the simple simile/locative/emanation positions above, not independently corroborated. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20`

## Rationale and status: mother of all buddhas as basis for supreme veneration

### Consensus
Four commentaries independently give the same rationale for the goddess's connection with the uṣṇīṣa: because she is the mother of all Tathāgatas/victors, she is honored as, or dwells at, their crown-protuberance.

Anon-Trinle-Char: "ལྷ་མོ་ཁྱོད་ནི་རྒྱལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས་དེའི་མཚོན་དོན་དུ།...བཞུགས" ("because you, goddess, are the mother of all victors, as a symbol of that you abide..."). Anon-Utpala: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས་..." ("because she is the mother of all Tathāgatas..."). Gendun-Drub: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་འཁུར་ཞིང" ("since she is the mother of all Tathāgatas, she is borne like the crown-ornament..."). Konchok-Thabkhe: she is "རྒྱལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསྐྱེད་པའི་ངེས་དོན་གྱི་ཡུམ་མཆོག" ("the supreme definitive-meaning mother who gives rise to all victors"), hence "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་བཀུར་བའི་འོས་སུ་གྱུར་པ" ("worthy of being honored even by all tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance").

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-4-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Tenzin-Dhonzang gives a different rationale for her supreme status, not the "mother of buddhas" doctrine above but a geographic simile: Uṣṇīṣavijayā is "the highest object of veneration among all places where all beings offer melodious praise, make aspiration-prayers, or circumambulate," compared explicitly to Tibet being the highest land of Jambudvīpa and Everest/Kailash its highest mountains. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-5`

Tsultrim-Namdak's claim gives a cognate but not identical rationale — she is "mother of the bodhisattva heirs of the victors who have attained all ten pāramitās of the ten bhūmis" (mother of the bodhisattva-heirs specifically, rather than of the buddhas/Tathāgatas themselves as in the Consensus above), so it is recorded here separately rather than folded into the four-commentary count. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7`

## Completion-stage / subtle-body readings of "uṣṇīṣa"

### Consensus
None — this reading is attested only within a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — but the four claims below are not all exegesis of *this verse* (the uṣṇīṣa-deity homage, verse 4); two of them are Palden-Sherab's generic subtle-body doctrine from entirely different homages, drawn in only because they use the word *gtsug tor* as a body-landmark term for the physical crown, not as the deity's name. They are split into two groups below rather than presented as one four-part exegesis.

### Unique
**Verse-4-specific completion-stage readings** (two claims, both explicit exegesis of the uṣṇīṣa-deity homage itself). Per Palden-Sherab's own stated fourfold exegetical scheme elsewhere in this corpus (word-meaning → Prajñāpāramitā; general-meaning → generation-stage; hidden-meaning → completion-stage-with-marks; ultimate-meaning → completion-stage-without-marks), both of these are the **hidden-meaning** (third) tier, not two different tiers:
- Completion-stage-with-marks (hidden-meaning tier): the Tathāgata as the wisdom of the four joys draws great-bliss wisdom to the crown, where it dissolves — "གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ" ("this is Uṣṇīṣavijayā") — while the ten winds, once purified in their respective places, become dharmakāya wisdom. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-3-1`
- Completion-stage-without-marks (ultimate-meaning tier): "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ནི་ཐེག་པ་རིམ་དགུའི་རྩེ་མོ་རྫོགས་ཆེན་ཨ་ཏི་ཡོ་ག" ("the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa is the peak of the nine successive vehicles, the view of the Great Perfection Atiyoga"); the dissolution of transference-prone winds into ground-liberation, established as the great dharmakāya, is identified — as the great unobstructed rainbow body — with "the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa, Jetsün Ārya Tārā 'of boundless victorious conduct.'" `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-4-1`

**Other completion-stage passages that use the term *gtsug tor*, from unrelated homages** (kept separate — not exegesis of this verse):
- From Palden-Sherab's exegesis of homage 1 ("Swift Heroine"), citing Longchenpa: of the twenty-one channel-knots of the Avadhūtī, Rasanā, and Lalanā, release of paired knots yields the ten bhūmis (in twofold aspect), and "ཐ་མས་གཙུག་ཏོར་རྩེ་མོའི་ས་མཆོག་གྲུབ" — "the last one, at the crown protuberance, accomplishes the supreme bhūmi at its very peak" — here *gtsug tor* is the anatomical top of the head in a generic subtle-body doctrine, not the uṣṇīṣa-deity of this verse. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-4`
- From Palden-Sherab's exegesis of homage 11 ("Poverty-Dispelling, Wealth-Bestowing Tārā"): the earth-element's pure essence is summoned and stabilized "གཙུག་ཏོར་ནས་ནོར་བུའི་བར་དུ" ("from the crown to the jewel-tip"), and the wrathful HŪṂ-syllable's channel-drop travels from the secret place through the point between the eyebrows, stabilizing "གཙུག་ཏོར་བར་དུ" ("between there and the crown"), accomplishing samādhis such as the "treasury of space" — again generic subtle-body doctrine from a different homage. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-3-1`

No other commentary in the packet extends any reading to this subtle-body tier.

## A second uṣṇīṣa-associated deity: Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā

### Consensus
None — the two commentaries that raise this deity each make a distinct point about her, not a shared assertion; see Unique below.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Palden-Sherab's commentary invokes a second uṣṇīṣa-named deity, Bhagavatī Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā (བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་མ་གཙུག་ཏོར་གདུགས་དཀར་མོ, "White-Parasol-of-the-Crown-Protuberance"), across two claims:
- Her mantra-and-mudrā armor of protection is said to generate joy and power in the practitioner and "ཀུན་ནས...སྒྲོལ་བར་བྱེད་པ" — utterly dispel sorcery, curses, quarrels, and evil dream-omens. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-2`
- A generation-stage visualization describes "མི་ཕམ་གཙུག་ཏོར་གདུགས་དཀར་མོ" ("Invincible Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā"), white-coloured, seated in archer's posture, one face and two hands, from whose body vajra-sparks radiate destroying sorcery and curses (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-1`), continuing directly into the guarding eightfold ornament-armor that dispels bad dreams arising from disturbed channels, winds, and drops (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-2`, marked "(continued)" from the claim above).

Neither of these two claims states that Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā herself arises from or is identical with the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa — the connection is nominal (her own name contains "uṣṇīṣa") rather than an origin-claim, so they are not merged with the following claim.

Tenzin-Dhonzang's commentary separately quotes a verse making an explicit origin-claim about this same deity: "བདེ་གཤེགས་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟ་མི་མངོན་པ་ལས། ཉེར་འཁྲུངས་རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་ཡུམ་གདུགས་དཀར་མོ" — "From the invisible crown-protuberance of the Sugata arose the White Parasol [Sitatapatra], mother of all victors." This is a distinct assertion (an emanation-claim, cognate to Position 3 of the "Nature of her relation" facet above, but concerning Sitātapatrā rather than the verse's own Tārā) not independently corroborated by Palden-Sherab's two claims, which make no such origin-statement. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-6`

## The uṣṇīṣa among the Buddha's marks

### Consensus
None — attested by a single commentary only.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Tenzin-Dhonzang's commentary cites the *Sutra of Inconceivable Secrets* on the uṣṇīṣa's visionary function, distinct from every other facet on this page (a general Buddhology point about the Tathāgata's marks, not specifically about this verse's goddess): "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་གྱིས་ཀྱང་གཟིགས་སོ།...དེ་ལྟར་མཚན་རེ་རེས་ཀྱང་གཅིག་ཅར་ཕྱོགས་བཅུ་རྣམས་སུ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས་རབ་འབྱམས་ཐམས་ཅད་གཟིགས་སོ" — "The Tathāgata sees also with his crown-protrusion...thus, with each and every mark, he simultaneously sees all the boundless world-realms in the ten directions." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-14`
