# བརྩོན་འགྲུས། — Diligence (vīrya)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Does any commentary offer a definitional gloss of the term "diligence" (brtson 'grus) itself?
2. How does the root text's own third homage stanza present diligence, and do the commentaries converge on reading it as one of the six pāramitās constituting Tārā's own domain of practice?
3. Do any commentaries subdivide diligence into further types, or place it within the two accumulations (merit/wisdom)?
4. What do commentaries identify as diligence's opposite, antidote relationship, or "enemy"?
5. Where diligence appears within an extended ten-pāramitā list, how is it positioned relative to the ten bodhisattva grounds?
6. Do any commentaries situate diligence specifically within tantric or completion-stage practice?
7. Do any commentaries treat the practitioner's own diligence as a causal factor — for receiving Tārā's blessing, or for sustaining Dharma practice and the teaching generally?
8. Do any commentaries offer an ultimate (negation-based) or esoteric/hidden-meaning reading of diligence, distinct from its conventional definition?

## Identity — definitional gloss

### Consensus
None — only one commentary offers a bare definitional gloss of the term itself; see Unique.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
**drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-16** glosses the term with a minimal tautological definition: "'Diligence' (brtson 'grus) is the perfection of diligence" (བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ ནི་ བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའོ)। No other commentary in this gather offers a standalone word-gloss on the term separate from listing it among the six pāramitās (see next facet).

## Diligence as one of the six pāramitās — Tārā's own domain of practice (root verse 3)

### Consensus
The praise's third homage stanza states that diligence, together with generosity, austerity/moral discipline, wisdom, patience, and meditative concentration, is Tārā's own domain of practice (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ). Twelve commentaries attest this, either by quoting the verse directly or by paraphrasing/glossing the same six terms as her domain without quoting it verbatim.

Quoted directly, e.g. tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་སེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱིས། ། པདྨས་ཕྱག་ནི་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ། ། སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། ། བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ། །" ("Homage — she whose hand is adorned with a water-born lotus, yellow-tinged blue; she whose very sphere of practice is generosity, diligence, austerity-discipline, patience, and meditative stability.")

— attested (direct quotation of the verse): `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2` (7 commentaries)

— attested (paraphrase/gloss naming the same six as her domain, without quoting the verse verbatim): `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-4` (5 additional commentaries: lobsang-dawa, konchok-thabkhe, pema-namgyal, sangye-nyentrul, karma-maitri; tsultrim-namdak, yama-sonam, and dharmabhadra already counted above)

**Total: 12 commentaries** (tenzin-dhonzang, tenga-tulku, tsultrim-namdak, sungrab-tulku, dharmabhadra, yama-sonam, palden-sherab, lobsang-dawa, konchok-thabkhe, pema-namgyal, sangye-nyentrul, karma-maitri).

**Elaboration — causal framing (same facet, narrower attestation).** Three of the above commentaries go further and read the verse's "domain of practice" as the causal source (རྒྱུ) from which the goddess herself arose or acted, rather than merely describing her present activity:
- tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5 — "ལྷ་མོ་འདི་རྒྱུ་གང་ལས་བྱུང་ཞེས་ན...དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཉིད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་མངོན་སུམ་གྱིས་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་དུ་བྱས་པ་ཉིད་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལའོ།" (she is Tara, arisen precisely from directly making the six perfections her actualized domain of practice).
- tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4 — "རྒྱུ་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལས་བྱུང་བ་ཡིན་ཏེ...ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་བསྐྱེད་པ་ཡིན་ཞིང་" (her body arose from the cause of the six perfections; she was generated in dependence on the six perfections). This same claim adds that she is furthermore unstained by the fault opposing each perfection — for diligence, "unstained by the fault of laziness" (བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ལ་ལེ་ལོའི་སྐྱོན་གྱིས་མ་གོས་པ）.
- palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2 — "རྒྱུ་སྦྱིན་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དང་། དེ་བཞིན་དུ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང...ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་གི་སྤྱོད་པ་དང་བཅས...མངའ་དབང་བསྒྱུར་ཞིང...དབང་བཅུའི་ཡོན་ཏན་མངོན་དུ་བྱས་པ" (the causal perfections, including diligence, are her domain; through the conduct of the six perfections she exercises mastery and actualizes the ten powers) — palden-sherab alone extends this reading to the ten powers (དབང་བཅུ), detailed further at `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-3`, which is not separately cited here as it does not itself name diligence.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Count/scope of the enumeration.** gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4 glosses the same iconographic gesture (the lotus held at the heart, described in root verse 3) as "the sign of the ten pure perfections" (ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་དག་པའི་རྟགས), and then lists the same six terms every other commentary lists for this verse (generosity, diligence, austerity/discipline, patience, meditative absorption, wisdom) — "སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས། བཟོད་པ། བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཏེ། ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་རྣམས་སོ།" This is an apparent internal inconsistency between the "ten" label and the six terms actually enumerated; the raw claim does not resolve it, and no other commentary in this gather uses the "ten" framing for this particular verse (contrast the genuinely ten-item lists at Facet 5 below, which name four additional pāramitās by name and are a different textual locus).

### Unique
None beyond the elaboration and divergence entries above.

## Diligence's internal structure and classification

### Consensus
None — the two claims below come from different commentaries addressing different aspects of diligence's internal structure; content is not corroborating and is kept separate per Rule 10.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed (not a disagreement — two commentaries simply supply different, non-overlapping classificatory material).

### Unique
- **tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-6** gives a fivefold subdivision of diligence, within a fuller threefold/fivefold breakdown of all six perfections: "བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ལ་གོ་ཆའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས། སྦྱོར་བའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས། མི་འགོངས་པའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས། མི་ལྡོག་པའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས། ཆོག་པར་མི་འཛིན་པའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ལྔ།" — armor-like diligence, the diligence of application, the diligence that does not grow discouraged, the diligence of non-reversion, and the diligence not satisfied with little.
- **konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-5** notes that, in the standard order of the six perfections, the first three belong to the accumulation of merit and the last to the accumulation of wisdom, "with diligence belonging to both" (བརྩོན་འགྲུས་གཉིས་ཀའི་ཆ་ལ་གཏོགས་པར་གསུངས་པས), which the commentary offers to explain why this text's own verse places diligence earlier than the standard ordering without this being a fault.

## Diligence and its opposite (laziness)

### Consensus
Diligence is identified as the specific antidote to laziness (ལེ་ལོ), one of six perfection–fault pairings.

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5` ("ལེ་ལོའི་གཉེན་པོ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས" — diligence as the antidote to laziness, among all six perfection/fault pairs), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` ("བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ཀྱི་དགྲ་ལེ་ལོ་རྣམ་པ་བཞི" — the enemy of diligence is the fourfold laziness, among the outer/inner enemies of the six perfections) (2 commentaries)

**Elaboration (tenzin-dhonzang only).** The same commentary separately quotes an additional verse praising bodhisattvas' diligence in overcoming laziness: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-8` — "དེ་ལྟར་ཤེས་ནས་ལོ་ལོ་ཀུན་སེལ་བའི། ། རླབས་ཆེན་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་རྒྱལ་སྲས་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་བརྩམས།" ("Understanding this, the bodhisattva children apply themselves to the vast diligence that dispels all laziness.")

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the elaboration above.

## Diligence within an extended ten-pāramitā enumeration (linked to the ten bhūmis)

### Consensus
In an extended ten-pāramitā list correlated one-to-one with the ten bodhisattva grounds (ས་བཅུ), diligence occupies the fourth position, following generosity, ethical discipline, and patience, and preceding meditative absorption, wisdom, method, power, aspiration, and (a second, distinct) primordial wisdom.

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` ("སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས། བཟོད་པ། བརྩོན་འགྲུས། བསམ་གཏན། ཤེས་རབ། ཐབས་མཁས། སྨོན་ལམ། སྟོབས། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4` ("ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་ནི། སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་དང་། ཐབས་དང་། སྟོབས་དང་། སྨོན་ལམ་དང་། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ནོ") (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None.

## Diligence in tantric / completion-stage practice

### Consensus
None — both claims below come from a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Only palden-sherab situates diligence within tantric completion-stage yogic practice specifically, in two claims:
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-3-3` — "བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དང་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ཀྱིས་གཞུང་བསྲངས" (through bodhicitta and diligence the central channel's course is straightened), within a description of the twenty-one channel-knots being loosened through yogic practice.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-3` — "ཤེས་རབ་དང་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ཟབ་མོའི་ལམ་གྱི་སྲོག་ཤིང་བཟུང་ནས" (taking wisdom and diligence as the life-tree of the profound path), within an exposition of view-and-meditation on the Trekchö/Tögal paths.

## Diligence as a causal factor sustaining practice, blessing, and the teaching

### Consensus
None — the two commentaries below address different specific causal roles (receiving Tārā's blessing vs. upholding the Buddhist teaching generally) and are not corroborating; kept separate per Rule 10.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
- **palden-sherab** (2 claims) names the practitioner's own diligence as one of the causal factors for receiving Tārā's blessing: `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-2` — special diligence in "rising from bed without sleeping even at dusk and dawn" (བརྩོན་འགྲུས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་སྲོད་དང་ཐོ་རངས་ལའང་མི་ཉལ་བར་མལ་སྟན་ལས་ལྡངས་པར་བྱས་ནས) while reciting the praise with samādhi and mindfulness; `palden-sherab:c-3-2-1-4` — a Vinaya citation naming "faith, diligence, mindfulness, samādhi, and great wisdom" (དད་པ་དང་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དྲན་པ་དང་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་དང་ཤེས་རབ་ཆེན་པོ) as the causes through which Tārā's compassion arises as blessing without partiality.
- **tsultrim-namdak:c-9-14** names diligence, undertaken as the companion of unbiased learning and of meditating on what is learned, as one of three factors indispensable for upholding the Buddha's teaching (quoting Situ Pema Nyinjed): "ཐོས་དོན་སྒོམ་པས་རང་རྒྱུད་དུལ་བ་དང་།། དེ་ཡི་གྲོགས་སུ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་རྩོམ་པ་གསུམ།། སངས་རྒྱས་བསྟན་པ་འཛིན་ལ་མེད་ཐབས་མེད།།"

## Ultimate (negation-based) definition of diligence

### Consensus
None — single-commentary attestation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed. (Not in tension with the conventional definition elsewhere on this page — the raw claim itself frames this as the "ultimate, non-referential form" of the six perfections, a distinct register from the conventional descriptions, not a competing conventional-level claim.)

### Unique
palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2 cites the Mahāyāna Sūtra of the Questions of Brahmaviśeṣacintin, giving an ultimate/negation-based definition for each of the six perfections, including diligence: "མི་འཛིན་པ་ནི་སྦྱིན་པའོ། མི་བསྲུང་བ་ནི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་སོ། མི་གནས་པ་ནི་བཟོད་པའོ། མི་བརྩོན་པ་ནི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་སོ། མི་སེམས་པ་ནི་བསམ་གཏན་ནོ། མི་དམིགས་པ་ནི་ཤེས་རབ་བོ།" ("Non-grasping is generosity. Non-guarding is discipline. Non-abiding is patience. Non-striving is diligence. Non-thinking is meditative absorption. Non-referencing is wisdom.")

## Esoteric / hidden-meaning reading of diligence

### Consensus

### Unique
taranatha:c-3-7, under an explicit "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) heading, glosses diligence (alongside patience and generosity) in an inner/esoteric register distinct from its conventional sense: "དེ་ལས་མི་མངའ་པའི་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང...", i.e. "the diligence not [otherwise] possessed by that" — paired in the same claim with an esoteric reading of patience ("the patience that equalizes all happiness and suffering within that state") and generosity ("the generosity that abandons self-grasping"). The raw claim does not further unpack what "that" (དེ) refers to; no other commentary in this gather offers a comparable hidden-meaning reading of diligence specifically.
