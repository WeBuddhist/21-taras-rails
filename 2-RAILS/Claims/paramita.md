# ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ། — Pāramitā, the Perfections (the doctrinal category)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather method note.** No packet-assembly script exists for this topic (it
> is not one of the 21 registered Tārā-verse spine slots — it is `global`).
> Per this batch's task instructions, the gather was done directly: `rg -n`
> against all sixteen `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files for every
> attested spelling of the term — full ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ, contracted
> ཕར་ཕྱིན, and the bare-vowel variant ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན (no ཏུ) — followed by
> `Read` of each hit's full claim block, copied character-for-character.
>
> **Relationship to the four sibling pāramitā pages.** This session already
> consolidated `2-RAILS/Claims/dana.md` (giving), `dhyana.md` (concentration),
> `kshanti.md` (patience), and `virya.md` (diligence) — each covering its own
> member's individual content (definitional glosses, antidote pairings,
> tantric readings of *that one pāramitā*, etc.). This page does **not**
> re-derive that material. It covers only what is genuinely about the
> **category itself**: the six/ten-fold list as a structure, its count and
> classification, its role and placement across the text (root verses 3 and
> 4), and claims about "the Perfection" (ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ / ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ) in a
> generic or collective sense rather than naming one specific member. Where a
> claim ID already appears on a sibling page for its individual-pāramitā
> content, it is either cross-referenced without re-quotation, or — where the
> *same* claim also carries genuinely category-level content the sibling page
> did not use it for (e.g. a claim about the six-fold antidote *structure* as
> a whole, cited on `virya.md` only for its diligence/laziness pairing) — it
> is cited again here for that distinct purpose, per this batch's own
> instructions not to defer to the sibling pages.
>
> **The śīla/prajñā gap.** śīla (ethical discipline, the fifth pāramitā) and
> prajñā (wisdom, the sixth) were **not** selected as standalone topics by the
> article-worthiness filter that produced this batch. The gather surfaced
> independent material for both — see the dedicated section near the end of
> this page — which is flagged explicitly rather than absorbed silently into
> this category page or left unmentioned.

## Questions asked

1. Beyond the six pāramitās individually treated on the sibling pages (dāna, vīrya, kṣānti, dhyāna), what do commentaries say about the pāramitā category itself — its count, structure, or role as a doctrinal framework?
2. How does root verse 4 (homage to Uṣṇīṣavijayā, "she who has attained the pāramitās without exception") get glossed — as the six-pāramitā list already named in verse 3, or as an extended ten-pāramitā list correlated with the ten bodhisattva grounds?
3. Do any commentaries give a completion-stage or "hidden meaning" reading of the pāramitā set as a whole (as opposed to one member)?
4. Do any commentaries treat the pāramitās collectively as the causal source of Tārā's own enlightened body or qualities?
5. Do any commentaries identify Tārā, or the "mother" principle generally, with Prajñāpāramitā specifically — the Perfection of Wisdom as a personified or textual category, rather than merely "wisdom" as the sixth pāramitā member?
6. Where do śīla (ethical discipline, the fifth pāramitā) and prajñā (wisdom, the sixth) appear in this material, given that neither was selected as its own standalone topic by the article-worthiness filter?
7. Do any commentaries classify the pāramitā list differently from a six- or ten-fold scheme (e.g. absorbing it into a larger category, or splitting off a subset)?

## The six pāramitās as Tārā's domain of practice (root verse 3) — cross-reference

Root verse 3's six-term list (generosity, diligence, austerity/discipline,
peace/wisdom, patience, meditative concentration) as Tārā's own "domain of
practice" (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ) is exhaustively attested, member by member, on the four
sibling pages (`dana.md`, `virya.md`, `kshanti.md`, `dhyana.md`), which
between them cite the verbatim quotation and paraphrase claims from
essentially every commentary in the corpus. This page does not repeat that
attestation list. What follows is only the material about the list **as a
count/structure**, not previously surfaced on the sibling pages.

### Consensus

None — the count-divergence material below is itself the subject of ⚑
Divergence, not consensus; see below.

### ⚑ Divergences

The sibling pages (`dana.md` §"Root verse", `virya.md` §"root verse 3")
already record three divergent counts for this list: **ten pāramitās**
(`palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8`,
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-6`), **five pāramitās with wisdom as basis rather
than list-member** (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`), and **subsumed into the
thirty-seven factors of enlightenment** (`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`). This page
adds two further attestations of the same underlying divergence not used by
either sibling page:

- **anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-8** records the divergence explicitly as a
  disagreement *among the commentarial tradition itself*, rather than simply
  adopting one reading: "བཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅེས་གསུངས་པ་ནི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཀྱིས་ཉོན་མོངས་ཞི་བའི་
  དོན་དུ་འགྲེལ་པ་དུ་མས་བཀྲལ་ཞིང་། ཞི་བའི་དོན་ལ་འགའ་ཞིག་གིས་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་
  ལ་སྦྱར་བ་དང་། གཞན་དག་ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུའི་མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་ཞི་བའི་དོན་དུ་བྱས་ནས་
  སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་གྱིས་ཤེས་རབ་བསྟན་པར་གསུངས་སོ།" ("many commentaries explain
  'austerity' as the pacification of affliction through discipline; on the
  meaning of 'peace,' some apply it to the perfection of wisdom, while others
  take it as the pacifying of what is discordant with the ten perfections,
  explaining that 'domain' thereby teaches wisdom") — i.e. this commentary
  itself reports that some predecessors read a ten-count into this same verse.
- **gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-3** offers a fourth, otherwise unattested
  correspondence for the same six-term span: "ཡང་ན་ཉོད་མེང་པ་དག་ཆགས་དང་བཅས་
  པ་བཞི་བ་སྟེ་སྤངས་པ་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཉིད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་
  ཅན་གྱི་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་བར་གྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ཏེ། ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ཡོངས་སུ་རྫོགས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་
  ཅན་ཡིན་ནོ་ཞེས་པའོ།" ("or alternatively: renunciation together with the four
  defilements — i.e. abandonment — patience, concentration, and the wisdom
  whose object is suchness: she has the nature of one who has fully
  perfected the six perfections"), introduced with "ཡང་ན" ("or
  alternatively") as an explicit second reading offered alongside its own
  main gloss, not a rejection of it.

### Unique

- **taranatha:c-3-5** glosses the list in fully generic terms, naming no
  individual member at all: "ཕར་ཕྱིན་ནི་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་ཡིན་ལ། ལྷ་མོ་
  ཁྱོད་ནི་དེ་ལ་སྤྱོད་པ་མོའོ།" ("the perfections are your very domain of
  activity; goddess, you are she who moves within them") — the only claim in
  the packet that states the category itself, unqualified by a specific
  count or member-list, as Tārā's domain.
- **tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-12** gives the same generic framing:
  "...ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་མཐར་ཕྱིན་པའམ་མངའ་བརྙེས་པ་ནི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྱེད་ཀྱིས་སྤྱོད་
  ཡུལ་དུ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་གྱུར་པ་ལའོ།" ("she who has perfected, or attained
  mastery of, the six perfections — homage to Tārā, you for whom all the
  perfections have become your domain of activity").

## The extended ten-pāramitā list correlated with the ten bodhisattva grounds (root verse 4)

### Consensus

Root verse 4 — the homage to Uṣṇīṣavijayā ("she who has attained the
pāramitās without exception, relied upon utterly by the victors' sons") — is
glossed by **all sixteen commentaries in the packet** as referring to an
**extended ten-pāramitā list** (the six already named in verse 3, plus
method/skilful-means, power, aspiration, and a second, distinct primordial
wisdom), correlated one-to-one with the **ten bodhisattva grounds** (ས་བཅུ).
This is the single most widely and uniformly attested category-level reading
in the corpus — every commentary that reaches this verse reads "the
pāramitās, without exception" as the complete ten-fold, ground-correlated
set, not merely a repetition of verse 3's six.

Representative verbatim quotation, tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-2: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་
གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར། ། མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ། ། མ་ལུས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་
ཐོབ་པའི། ། རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བསྟེན་མ། །" ("Homage — she of the crown-
protuberance of the Tathāgatas, whose conduct is boundlessly victorious,
relied upon utterly by the victors' sons who have attained the perfections
without exception").

The explicit ten-item enumeration, palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4: "ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་
བཅུ་ནི། སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་
དང་། ཤེས་རབ་དང་། ཐབས་དང་། སྟོབས་དང་། སྨོན་ལམ་དང་། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ནོ།" ("the
ten perfections are: generosity, discipline, patience, diligence, meditative
absorption, wisdom, method, power, aspiration, and [primordial] wisdom"),
paired in the same claim with the ten-bhūmi names.

— attested (root verse quoted and/or the ten-pāramitā/ten-bhūmi correlation
glossed explicitly): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-6`,
`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-5`,
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-25`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-4`,

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed as a disagreement — every commentary that glosses this verse
converges on the ten-count/ten-bhūmi reading; no commentary glosses verse 4
as merely repeating verse 3's six. (The count divergence recorded above,
under "root verse 3," concerns whether *that* list is six, ten, five, or
subsumed — it is not in tension with the fact that verse 4's own list, where
glossed, is uniformly ten.)

### Unique

- **konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-5** is the only claim to state explicitly *why*
  the tradition treats verses 3 and 4 as distinct rather than redundant,
  offered as the author's own view ("ཁོ་བོ་ཅག་སྨྲའོ", "we say"): "འདི་ནི་དུས་
  གསུམ་འཕགས་པ་ཞེས་གསོ་བྱ་གསོ་བྱེད་དང་བསྐྱེད་བྱ་སྐྱེད་བྱེད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དུ་གྱུར་ཚུལ་
  གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ཡིན་ལ། ཤོ་ལོ་ཀ་སྔ་མས་ནི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་གཞན་ལྔ་དེའི་གྲོགས་སུ་གྱུར་
  ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ཡིན་ནོ" ("this verse [4] praises her as the mother who
  nurtures and generates beings of the three times, whereas the previous
  verse [3] praised her by way of her companionship with the other five
  perfections") — consistent with this same commentary's own five-plus-basis
  reading of verse 3 (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`, already on `dana.md`),
  extending that reading across both verses rather than contradicting it.

## Tantric "hidden meaning": the ten pāramitās as the ten purified winds

### Consensus

Two commentaries, independently and in the same completion-stage register,
read the ten-pāramitā list of root verse 4 as the ten inner winds (རླུང་བཅུ)
purified into wisdom, rather than (or in addition to) the conventional
ten-perfections reading:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-3-1`, `taranatha:c-4-11` (2 commentaries)

palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-3-1: "གསུམ་པ་སྦས་དོན་རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས་ནི། ...གཙུག་ཏོར་
རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ། ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ་སྟེ་རླུང་བཅུ་གནས་དག་པས་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཆོས་སྐུ་ནི་རྒྱལ་བ་
སྲས་བཅས་ཡུམ་ཆེན་ཡིན་ནོ།" ("as for the third, the hidden meaning, according to
the completion-stage-with-marks: ...Uṣṇīṣavijayā — the ten perfections, that
is, the ten winds, once purified in their [respective] places, [become]
dharmakāya wisdom, the great mother of the victors and their heirs").
taranatha:c-4-11 states the same identification independently and more
tersely: "ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ་སྟེ་རླུང་བཅུ་གནས་གྱུར་པས།" ("the ten perfections, that is,
the ten winds, having undergone transformation —").

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above.

## The pāramitās collectively as the causal source of Tārā's own enlightened body and qualities

### Consensus

Four commentaries state that Tārā's own enlightened body, qualities, or
buddhahood arose *from the practice of the pāramitās as a set* — a claim
about the category's causal role, distinct from any individual member's
content:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-22`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`,
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-1`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-6-5` (4 commentaries: drakpa-gyaltsen, tenga-tulku,
palden-sherab, sungrab-tulku)

drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-22 states it most directly: "ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་པོ་དེ་
སྤྱད་པ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མའི་སྐུ་ཐོབ་སྟེ། དེ་མཚན་པ་ལས་ཨུཏྤལ་མཚོན་པའོ།" ("from practicing
those six perfections she attained Tārā's body; that is symbolized by the
utpala flower"). tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4 gives the fullest version,
combining the causal reading with the six-fold antidote structure in one
claim: "རྒྱུ་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལས་བྱུང་བ་ཡིན་ཏེ...ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་
བསྐྱེད་པ་ཡིན་ཞིང...ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་སྐྱོན་གྱི་རྣམ་པས་མ་གོས་པ...ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་བརྟེན་
ནས་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་སངས་རྒྱས" ("[her body's] cause was the six perfections...
generated in dependence on the six perfections...unstained by any fault
regarding the six perfections...in dependence on the six perfections she
became a fully and perfectly awakened buddha"). palden-sherab gives two
distinct claims in the same register: c-3-1-3-1-2 ("[her] domain is the
causal perfection of generosity, and likewise [the other five]...with the
conduct of these six perfections...[she] exercises mastery, and has
actualized the qualities of the ten powers which none can overcome") and
c-3-1-3-4-1, a completion-stage-without-marks reading in which "ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་
དྲུག་དང་ས་ལམ་ཆིག་རྫོགས་སུ་འགྱུར" ("the six perfections and the paths and
bhūmis become complete all at once") in the state of self-arisen wisdom.
sungrab-tulku:c-6-5 supplies the fullest single statement of the six-fold
antidote structure underlying this causal claim: "སེར་སྣའི་གཉེན་པོ་སྦྱིན་པ་
དང་། ལེ་ལོའི་གཉེན་པོ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང...ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་སྤྱོད...ཡུལ་དུ་འགྲོ་
བ་སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་འཁོར་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་ཞིང་བྱང་སེམས་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་པ་དབང་བསྒྱུར་བར་མཛད་
མ" ("generosity as the antidote to miserliness, diligence as the antidote to
laziness...[thus] she practices the six perfections, thereby liberating
sentient beings from saṃsāra and mastering the conduct of a bodhisattva") —

claim is cited for its structural completeness (all six pairings stated

for.

## Prajñāpāramitā personified: Tārā identified with "the Perfection" itself

### Consensus

Five commentaries — independently of the six/ten-pāramitā list context —
identify Tārā, or the "mother" principle she embodies, specifically with
**Prajñāpāramitā** (ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ, the Perfection of Wisdom) as
a personified source-principle from which all buddhas are born, rather than
merely naming wisdom as the sixth item in a list:

— attested: `pema-namgyal:c-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-2-12`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6`,
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`,
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-69` (5 commentaries:
pema-namgyal, sungrab-tulku, tenga-tulku, konchok-thabkhe, tenzin-dhonzang)

pema-namgyal:c-0-2 states it as a bare identification, in the commentary's
own opening doxography: "ཆོས་སྐུ་ཤེས་རབ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་མ་ཡུམ།" ("the dharmakāya is
the mother Prajñāpāramitā"). sungrab-tulku gives the fullest version across
three claims: c-2-12 ("ཡུམ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པར་དབང་བསྐུར་བའི་རྗེ་བཙུན་
སྒྲོལ་མའམ་མྱུར་མ", "Jetsün Tārā, also called Nyurma, who was empowered into
[the state of] Mother Prajñāpāramitā"), c-2-11 (the resulting temporal and
ultimate benefits of supplicating her in that identity), and c-7-6 ("རྗེ་
བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ནི། ཡུམ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་ངོ་བོ་ཡིན...སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་
ཅད་ནི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་འཁྲུངས་པ", "Venerable Tārā is the very essence of
Mother Prajñāpāramitā; and since all buddhas are born in dependence on
prajñāpāramitā..."). tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3 reasons to the same
conclusion from the tathāgatas' own homage to her: "རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ནི་ཡུམ་ཤེས་རབ་
ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་ངོ་བོར་གྱུར་པ་ཡིན...དེས་ན་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ནི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་གྱི་རང་
གཟུགས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བའོ" ("the Venerable
Lady is the very essence of the mother, the perfection of wisdom...therefore,
since the Venerable Lady is the very form of the perfection of wisdom, the
tathāgatas pay homage to her"), with c-1-3-3-3 giving the same
supplication-benefit structure as sungrab-tulku's c-2-11.
konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2 identifies the object of homage in verse 4 the same
way: "རྒྱལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསྐྱེད་པའི་ངེས་དོན་གྱི་ཡུམ་མཆོག་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་
མ" ("the supreme definitive-meaning mother who gives rise to all victors —
the perfection of wisdom"). tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-69 grounds the identification
in an external canonical citation, quoting the *Analysis of the Limits [of
the Perfection Vehicle]* (ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད): "རང་བཞིན་གྱི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན། རང་བཞིན་
གྱི་ཡུམ། རང་བཞིན་གྱི་མྱང་འདས། རང་བཞིན་གྱི་ཆོས་སྐུ། ། རང་བཞིན་རྣམ་དག དོན་དམ་
བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དོན་གཅིག" ("natural perfection of wisdom, natural mother, natural
nirvāṇa, natural dharmakāya, natural purity, and ultimate truth are all one
in meaning").

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — no commentary contests the Prajñāpāramitā identification;
those that do not raise it are simply silent on it, not opposed to it.

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above.

## Doxographical placement: the six pāramitās as characteristic of the sūtra vehicle specifically

### Unique

`tsultrim-namdak:c-9-2`, in a colophon-region overview of the Buddha's
teaching condensed into three vehicles, places "the six pāramitās" as the
defining content of the **Mahāyāna sūtra vehicle** specifically, distinct
from both the śrāvaka vehicle (four truths) and the vajrayāna (four tantra
classes' generation/completion stages): "ཐེག་ཆེན་ཟབ་མོ་ལྟ་བ་དང...ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་
པ་དྲུག་ཏུ་འདུས...རྒྱུད་སྡེ་བཞི་དྲུག་བསྐྱེད་རྫོགས་ཀུན" ("the Mahāyāna's profound
view...gathered into the six pāramitās...[distinct from] the vajrayāna's
four tantra classes' generation and completion stages"). No other commentary
in the packet offers a comparable three-vehicle doxographical placement of
the pāramitā category as such.

### Consensus

None — single-commentary attestation.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

## The śīla/prajñā gap

Neither śīla (ethical discipline, the fifth pāramitā) nor prajñā (wisdom,
the sixth) was selected as its own standalone article-worthy topic in this
batch. This gather surfaced independent material bearing on both, which is
recorded here rather than silently absorbed into this category page or left
unmentioned:

- **prajñā** is, if anything, the richest single vein of pāramitā-adjacent
  material in the entire corpus outside the six/ten-list content itself: the
  "Prajñāpāramitā personified" facet above gathers five independent
  commentaries identifying Tārā or the mother-principle with the Perfection
  of Wisdom specifically, several with sustained doctrinal argument and one
  with an external canonical citation. None of that material duplicates
  anything on a sibling pāramitā page (there being no `prajna.md`), so it
  currently exists only here, filed under the category page rather than a
  dedicated prajñā page. A future `prajna.md` consolidation would draw
  substantially on this facet.
- **śīla** has comparatively thin but real attestation:
  `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-8` quotes the *Guru Puja* directly on the "perfection
  of ethics" as its own vow-based practice, independent of the six/ten-list
  context: "བླ་མཆོད་ལས། སོ་ཐར་བྱང་སེམས་གསང་སྔགས་སྡོམ་པ་ཡི། ། བཅས་མཚམས་སྲོག་
  གི་ཕྱིར་ཡང་མི་གཏོང་ཞིང...ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་རྫོགས་པར་བྱིན་གྱིས་རློབས" ("from
  the Guru Puja: 'grant your blessing that I never abandon, even at the cost
  of my life, the boundaries of the pratimokṣa, bodhisattva, and secret-
  mantra vows...and that I perfect the perfection of ethics'"). This is a
  single-commentary, single-claim attestation — thinner than the material
  behind any of the four sibling pages — but real, and is logged here rather
  than silently dropped. (The same commentary supplies a structurally
  parallel Guru Puja citation for dhyāna, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-10`,
  already within `dhyana.md`'s territory and not re-cited here.)

Neither gap is filled by this page; both are noted so a future consolidation
run knows the material exists and roughly how much of it there is.
