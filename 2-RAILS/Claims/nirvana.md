# Nirvāṇa — མྱ་ངན་འདས། (gone beyond sorrow)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation cites a
> raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never cites a
> commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather note:** `nirvana` is a global cross-cutting topic, not one of the 21
> registered Tārā-verse spine slots, so there is no `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map
> packet for it. The claims below were located by a direct search of every
> commentary's `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/<id>.md` file, first for the literal
> string མྱ་ངན་འདས (16 hits across 12 files), then — because the compound most often
> appears as མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས or མྱང་འདས (with ལས, or the archaic contraction) — a second,
> broader pass for the bare root མྱ་ངན, which surfaced genuine nirvāṇa content in all
> sixteen files. The broader pass also surfaced two unrelated senses of the same root
> that were excluded as different lexical items (see "Claims reviewed, not separately
> cited," below): "the sorrowless tree" (མྱ་ངན་མེད་པའི་ཤིང, an iconographic name for the
> aśoka plant) and ordinary "grief/sorrow" (མྱ་ངན་གྱིས་ནོན, "overcome by grief," in
> narrative anecdotes) — neither of which is the doctrinal compound མྱ་ངན་(ལས་)འདས་པ.
> Every quotation on this page is copied character-for-character from that search,
> never retyped from memory.
>
> **Cross-reference note:** `2-RAILS/Claims/suffering.md` (duḥkha, consolidated
> earlier the same session) treats the identical "blissful, virtuous, peaceful,
> [nirvāṇa]" epithet-quartet from suffering's side — what it means for the *result*
> to be free of suffering. This page treats the same quartet from nirvāṇa's side —
> what the fourth term itself denotes (cessation, dharmakāya, the non-abiding state).
> The two pages necessarily cite some of the same claim IDs for the same quartet
> (e.g. `sungrab-tulku:c-18-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-3`,
> `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-3`) but analyse them for different content, per each page's
> own scope; this is not double-consolidation. The bodhisattva vow not to pass into
> nirvāṇa while beings drown in "the ocean of suffering" is fully consolidated on
> `suffering.md` (its own facet, `pema-namgyal:c-1-6-2` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-1-6-1`)
> and is only cross-referenced, not re-argued, here.

## Questions asked

1. How does the corpus gloss the "mya ngan 'das" (nirvāṇa) term of the recurring "blissful, virtuous, peaceful, [her domain is] nirvāṇa" epithet-quartet, and what specifically is exhausted or abandoned to attain it?
2. Does any commentary use the specific technical term "non-abiding nirvāṇa" (mi gnas pa'i mya ngan las 'das pa), and how consistently is it attested across independent commentaries?
3. Do commentaries diverge on whether the bliss/virtue/peace/nirvāṇa quartet describes the goddess's own inherent qualities or what she bestows on, or establishes in, others?
4. Does any commentary apply a graduated hermeneutical schema (word-meaning / general meaning / hidden meaning / ultimate meaning) to unpack nirvāṇa's peace across generation- and completion-stage tantric practice, and is any single layer corroborated by more than one commentary?
5. Does any commentary supply an independent scholastic or etymological analysis of the term "mya ngan" (sorrow) itself, distinguishing levels of nirvāṇa — e.g. a "natural nirvāṇa" of the basis versus a nirvāṇa attained on the path?
6. Does the corpus address the doctrine that a buddha does not truly pass into nirvāṇa but only displays doing so for the sake of disciples?
7. How does the corpus's treatment of the bodhisattva vow not to pass into nirvāṇa (already consolidated in suffering.md) bear on this topic, and should it be re-argued here or only cross-referenced?
8. Are there attested homage epithets built on "dispelling sorrow" (mya ngan sel ba) that are lexically adjacent to, but distinct from, the nirvāṇa compound mya ngan (las) 'das pa, and how should they be treated?
9. Which commentaries are silent on nirvāṇa as an independent topic in their tree-guided extraction, and is that silence explicable?

## The epithet-quartet's fourth term — nirvāṇa as her domain

### Consensus

Thirteen of the sixteen commentaries, glossing the root verse's fourth epithet
(after "blissful," "virtuous," "peaceful"), quote or paraphrase the line naming
nirvāṇa's peace as her domain of activity:

— attested (root-verse quotation): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-0-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-18-2`, `taranatha:c-15-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-50` (11 commentaries)

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-3`, quoting what it calls "the tantra" (རྒྱུད་ལས), gives the
line in its bare form: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བདེ་མ་དགེ་མ་ཞི་མ། ། མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཞི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" — "Homage to
her who is Bliss, Virtue, and Peace; whose very domain is the peace of nirvāṇa."
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-2` renders the same line with a variant spelling: "མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཤིང་
སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" ("passed beyond sorrow, whose very domain [it is]").

Of these, seven commentaries go on to state explicitly *what* is exhausted or
abandoned to attain that domain: the two obscurations (afflictive and cognitive,
ཉོན་སྒྲིབ་དང་ཤེས་སྒྲིབ) together with their habitual imprints, with the result named as
cessation-truth (འགོག་བདེན) and the dharmakāya:

— attested (etymology: two obscurations exhausted → cessation, dharmakāya): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-16-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-18-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-15-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-51` (7 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-18-4` gives the fullest chain: "...ཉོན་སྒྲིབ་དང་ཤེས་སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་བག་ཆགས་
དང་བཅས་པ་མ་ལུས་པ་ཡོངས་སུ་སྤངས་པས་འགོག་པ་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་ཤིང་ཞི་བ་ཆོས་སྐུ་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་དང་
ལྡན་པ་ནི་རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉིད་ལའོ།" — "because the propensities of both the afflictive
and cognitive obscurations are, without exception, thoroughly abandoned through
cessation, she has passed beyond sorrow and is peaceful, possessing the dharmakāya
as her directly manifest domain: this is venerable Tārā herself." `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2`
gives the same structure more tersely: "སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་ཞི་བ་མཆོག་གི་དབྱིངས་ལ་
མངོན་སུམ་དུ་མཉམ་པར་བཞག་པས་དེའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཉིད་མ" ("directly and evenly resting in the
supreme peaceful expanse that transcends the two obscurations' sorrow, she is its
domain"). `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-2-6` gives the same structure independently: "སྒྲིབ་
གཉིས་ཀྱི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་ཞི་བ་མཆོག་གི་དབྱིངས་ལ་རྟག་ཏུ་མཉམ་པར་འཇོག་པས་དེའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཉིད་དུ་
གྱུར་པ།" ("because she constantly abides in meditative equipoise in the expanse of
supreme peace, the nirvāṇa beyond the two obscurations, that [expanse] itself
becomes her domain"). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-51` reaches the same cessation via
"the afflictive and cognitive obscurations to be abandoned are abandoned,"
continuing at `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-52` to explain that on that basis she
"shows the peaceful path of sacred Dharma, and ultimately establishes [beings] in
the peaceful state of nirvāṇa" (མཐར་ཐུག་གི་མྱང་འདས་... ཏེ་ཞི་བའི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་བཀོད་པར་མཛད་པ),
continued and concluded at `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-53`.

Three commentaries phrase what is exhausted somewhat differently, without
contradicting the seven-commentary pattern above: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-81` glosses the
line via exhausted *conceptual thought* rather than the two obscurations by name —
"33 རྟོག་པ་ཟད་པས་མྱ་ངན་འདས། སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཞི་བས་ཞི་བདེའི་ངང་ལ་སྤྱོད་པའོ།" ("through the exhaustion
of conceptual thought she attains nirvāṇa; through the pacification of suffering
she acts within the state of peace and bliss"); `taranatha:c-15-4` names the
resting-place directly as "the expanse of the truth of cessation" (འགོག་བདེན་གི་
དབྱིངས) without naming the two obscurations as such: "མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ་མཆོག་ཏུ་ཞི་བ། །
འགོག་བདེན་གི་དབྱིངས་ལ་མཉམ་པར་འཇོག་པས་ཞི་བའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་མ་སྟེ།"; and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-3`
names *three* obscurations rather than two, adding wisdom-of-the-paths language (see
"The graduated hermeneutical unpacking," below, for its fuller context): "སྒྲིབ་པ་
གསུམ་གྱི་དྲི་མ་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་བྲལ་བ་ནི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་སྤྱོད་པའི་ཡུལ་དང་ལྡན་པ་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ཉིད་ལ།..."

Glossing the same "domain" line, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-7` explains that nirvāṇic
peace "at every level, subtle and coarse" (ཕྲ་རགས་ཐམས་ཅད) has become the goddess's own
domain, praising her both for being herself free of suffering and its causes and
for possessing the power to establish others in that same state: "དེ་ལྟ་བུའི་མྱ་ངན་
ལས་འདས་པའི་ཞི་བ་ཕྲ་རགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལྷ་མོ་རང་ཉིད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ལམ་ལོངས་སྤྱོད་དུ་གྱུར་པས་... འདི་ནི་རང་
སྡུག་བསྔལ་རྒྱུ་དང་བཅས་པ་ལས་གྲོལ་ཞིང་གཞན་ཡང་དེ་ལ་འགོད་པའི་ནུས་པ་མངའ་བའི་ཆེ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པའོ།"
This is the same "own attainment / power to establish others" pairing that
Konchok Thabkhe's commentary splits into two rival positions (see Divergence 2,
below); here the pairing is offered as a single, non-competing description rather
than as two alternatives.

## "Non-abiding nirvāṇa" (མི་གནས་པའི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ) — the specific Mahāyāna technical term

### Consensus

Four commentaries, independently glossing the same fifteenth-homage verse, reach
for the specific technical term "non-abiding nirvāṇa" (apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa) — the
state that abides in neither saṃsāra nor a quiescent, self-absorbed cessation —
rather than leaving "nirvāṇa" unqualified:

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-15-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-67` (4 commentaries)

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-15-1`: "...མི་གནས་པའི་མྱ་ངན་པས་འདས་པ་མཆོག་ཅུ་ཞི་བའི་སྦྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཉིད་དུ་
གྱུར་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ།" ("...the Tārā who has become one whose object of engagement is the
supreme, utterly peaceful non-abiding nirvāṇa"). `karma-maitri:c-1-1-15-6` gives
the same phrase independently: "མི་གནས་པའི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ་མཆོག་ཏུ་རབ་ཏུ་ཞི་བའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་
ཅན་ཉིད་དུ་འགྱུར་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ།" `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-2-2` uses the term within its own
"two obscurations" etymology (quoted above). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-67`, in a
different context — its extended excursus on generating bodhicitta — uses the same
term for the *object* of the bodhisattva's compassionate aspiration for others:
"...འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་མི་གནས་པའི་མྱང་འདས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་ལ་བདག་གིས་དགོད་པར་བྱའོ་སྙམ་པའི་སེམས་རྒྱ་
བསྐྱེད་དེ..." ("...thinking 'I shall establish beings in the non-abiding nirvāṇa of
buddhahood,' [Tārā] generates and expands this vast intention").

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — all four attestations converge on the same technical sense.

### Unique

None beyond the attestations above.

## Two internal divergences on how to read the quartet

### ⚑ Divergences

**Divergence 1 — own inherent qualities vs. a four-path mapping.** Gendun Drub's
own commentary records two competing readings of the full "blissful, virtuous,
peaceful, nirvāṇa" quartet as an explicit internal tension, not a single voice:

— `⚑ gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-2-3` — **Position 1** (attributed to "རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ,"
i.e. Drakpa Gyaltsen): the four terms are simply undefiled bliss, freedom from
afflictions, pacified suffering, and "nirvāṇa" as conceptuality-pacified —
"ཞི་མ་ནི། འབྲས་བུ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཞི་མ། རྟོག་པ་ཞི་བའི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ་ལ་སྤྱོད་པ་ཉིད་མ་ཞེས་སོ།" **Position
2** (attributed to unnamed "མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག," other scholars): the same four terms
instead map onto the four paths — accumulation, preparation, seeing, and
no-more-learning — with nirvāṇa specifically explained as the culmination on the
path of no-more-learning: "མི་སློབ་ལམ་དུ་བག་ཆགས་དང་བཅས་པ་ཞི་བའི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ་ཆོས་སྐུ་
མངོན་དུ་མཛད་མའོ།" ("on the path of no-more-learning, [she is] one who, [her afflictions']
habitual imprints having been pacified, actualizes the nirvāṇa that is the
dharmakāya").

**Divergence 2 — her own qualities vs. what she bestows on disciples**, a
structurally distinct disagreement recorded within Konchok Thabkhe's own
commentary:

— `⚑ konchok-thabkhe:c-2-15-2` — **Position 1** (earlier commentaries): "bliss/
virtue/peace" are her own inherent qualities — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བའི་ཡུལ་ཟག་མེད་ཀྱི་བདེ་བའི་
རང་བཞིན་ཅན་ཡིན་པས་བདེ་མ། སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་སྤངས་པས་དགེ་མ། རྩུབ་རེག་དང་བྲལ་བས་ཞི་མ།" **Position 2**
(this commentary's own adopted reading, following a mahāsiddha's verse): the same
terms describe what she *bestows* on practitioners — higher rebirth, liberation,
and buddhahood — with nirvāṇa correspondingly reinterpreted as the domain she
grants, the dharmakāya itself: "སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་གོ་འཕང་སྟེར་བས་ཞི་མ། མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ་ཞི་བ་མཆོག་
གི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྐུའི་བདག་ཉིད་ཅན་མ།" ("because she grants the state of buddhahood,
she is 'peaceful'; she who, as the domain of supreme, peaceful nirvāṇa, is
identical with the dharmakāya").

These two divergences are not the same disagreement: Divergence 1 turns on whether
the quartet is a static description or a path-stage mapping; Divergence 2 turns on
whether it describes the goddess's own attainment or her activity toward others.
Each divergence's two positions are recorded as internal tensions within a single
commentary, not as a split between two different commentaries.

### Unique

None beyond the two divergences above.

## The graduated hermeneutical unpacking of nirvāṇa's peace across tantric practice

### Consensus

Two commentaries, independently and in the same "ultimate meaning" (མཐར་ཐུག) slot of
a graduated word-meaning-to-ultimate-meaning exegesis, identify nirvāṇa's peace with
the last of the five wisdoms, dharmadhātu-wisdom:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1`, `taranatha:c-15-15` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-15-15` gives the minimal form: "མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཞི་ནི་ཆོས་དབྱིངས་ཡེ་ཤེས།" ("'the
peace of nirvāṇa' is dharmadhātu-wisdom"), preceded by the same mapping for the
preceding three terms at `taranatha:c-15-12` (bliss = discerning wisdom),
`taranatha:c-15-13` (virtue = mirror-wisdom), and `taranatha:c-15-14` (peace =
wisdom of equality). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1` gives the
identical four-term mapping with a fifth added (destroyer-of-sin = all-accomplishing
wisdom): "བདེ་མ་ནི་སོར་རྟོག་ཡེ་ཤེས་དང་། དགེ་མ་ནི་མེ་ལོང་ཡེ་ཤེས་དང་། ཞི་མ་ནི་མཉམ་ཉིད་ཡེ་ཤེས་དང་།
མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཞི་ནི་ཆོས་དབྱིངས་ཡེ་ཤེས་དང་། སྡིག་པ་ཆེན་པོ་འཇོམས་པ་ཉིད་མ་ནི་བྱ་གྲུབ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཏེ..."

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

**Palden Sherab** nests the "ultimate meaning" wisdom-mapping above inside a full
four-layer hermeneutical schema unique to this commentary — word-meaning, general
meaning, hidden meaning, and ultimate meaning — each layer unpacking the same
"bliss/virtue/peace/nirvāṇa" quartet differently:

- **Word-meaning** (ཚིག་གི་དོན): the four terms as the four truths — result, cause,
  path, cessation. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-1-1`: "...སྒྲིབ་གཉིས་ས་བོན་བག་ཆགས་དང་
  བཅས་པ་སྤངས་པས་འགོག་པ་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་ཤིང་ཞི་བ་ཆོས་སྐུ་ཐོབ་པའོ།" (already quoted in full
  above, under the Consensus etymology).
- **Hidden meaning** (སྦས་དོན), completion-stage-with-marks: the four terms as the
  four uncontaminated joys arising from striking the vital point of the four
  cakras. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-3-1`: "...མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཞི་ནི། རང་ལུས་དང་གཞན་ལུས་
  ཐབས་ལྡན་གྱིས་རྩ་འཁོར་ལོ་བཞི་ལ་ཐིག་ལེ་དབབ་བཟུང་ལྡོག་དགྲམ་བཞི་ཡིས་གནད་དུ་བསྣུན་པས། དགའ་བ་
  དང་། མཆོག་དགའ་བ་དང་། དགའ་བྲལ་དང་། ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་དགའ་བ་སྟེ་ཟག་མེད་ཀྱི་དགའ་བ་བཞིའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་
  ཀྱི་ངོ་བོ་བདེ་ཆེན་ལྷན་གཅིག་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཐབས་ལམ་གྱི་རྣལ་འབྱོར་བྱ་
  ཚུལ་བསྟན་པའོ།" ("'nirvāṇa's peace' [arises when], through the method of one's own
  or another's body, the four [processes] of dropping, holding, reversing, and
  arraying the drop strike the vital point of the four channel-wheels, giving rise
  to the four uncontaminated joys — this teaches the manner of the method-path
  yoga").
- **Ultimate meaning** (མཐར་ཐུག), completion-stage-without-marks: the five-wisdoms
  mapping already cited above as Consensus, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-15-4-1`.

**Pema Namgyal** independently supplies a different "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན)
mapping for the same quartet — onto the four tantric empowerments rather than the
four joys — making it a genuine alternative unpacking, not a variant of Palden
Sherab's: `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58`: "...བདེ་མ། དགེ་མ། ཞི་མ། མྱ་ངན་འདས་ཞི་བཞི་པོས།
ཤེར་དབང་གི་དབང་བཞི་དང་། དེ་ཉིད་གསུམ་གྱིས་རྣམ་ཐར་གསུམ་ལྡན་གྱི་དབང་བཞི་པ་དང་..." ("...the four
[terms] bliss/virtue/peace/nirvāṇa's-peace [correspond to] the four wisdom-
empowerments, and those same three, together, to the fourth empowerment endowed
with the three liberations..."), immediately followed by the commentary's own
methodological caveat that it declines to unpack this further because other
tantras already do so at length. Elsewhere in the same commentary, the word-meaning
layer alone (without the hidden-meaning apparatus) is given at `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-42`:
"...ཚེ་གཅིག་ལ་མྱ་ངན་འདས་པའི་ཞི་བ་ཆོས་སྐུ་དབྱིངས་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཆེན་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་དགོངས་ཀློང་མངོན་
དུ་གྱུར་པའི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ལ་བཀོད་པ་ཉིད་མ།" ("...in a single lifetime, [you attain] the peace
of nirvāṇa — established in the domain where the great mother-of-the-dharmakāya's
expanse, your own realization, Venerable Lady, becomes manifest").

**Tenga Tulku** independently develops a third, looser variant of the same
graduated pattern, mapping the quartet's culmination onto the wisdoms of the paths
of seeing and meditation, plus an unnamed "ultimate" wisdom, without naming the
canonical five wisdoms as such: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-2-3` (quoted in fuller context
above): "...སྒྲིབ་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་དྲི་མ་ཐམས་ཅད་དང་བྲལ་བ་ནི་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་སྤྱོད་པའི་ཡུལ་དང་
ལྡན་པ་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ཉིད་ལ། མཐོང་ལམ་གྱི་ཡེ་ཤེས། སྒོམ་ལམ་གྱི་ཡེ་ཤེས། མཐར་ཇི་ལྟ་ཇི་སྙེད་ཀྱི་ཡེ་ཤེས་
མངོན་དུ་གྱུར་པ་ནི་ཐུགས་ཆོས་སྐུའི་ཡོན་ཏན་དང་..." ("...free of the stains of the three
obscurations, the Venerable Lady's own domain of nirvāṇa's conduct [comprises] the
wisdom of the path of seeing, the wisdom of the path of meditation, and,
ultimately, the wisdom knowing all things as they are and in their full extent —
these are the qualities of her mind, the dharmakāya").

No two of these three graduated unpackings (Palden Sherab's four joys, Pema
Namgyal's four empowerments, Tenga Tulku's path-wisdoms) describe the same
completion-stage content; each is Unique to its own commentary, though all three
converge on locating nirvāṇa's peace within a tantric completion-stage or
wisdom-realization framework rather than treating it as a bare doctrinal label.

None — this entire excursus is unique to a single commentary.

None observed.

## The buddhas' nirvāṇa as compassionate display, not real cessation

### Consensus

None — attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

**Tenzin Dhonzang** quotes an unnamed scripture (in the context of a discussion of
why the buddhas teach non-literal emanations, citing the *Suvarṇaprabhāsottama
Sūtra* as scriptural warrant for such didactic display) stating that a buddha does
not truly pass into nirvāṇa, but only displays doing so for the sake of taming
disciples: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-15`: "སངས་རྒྱས་མྱ་ངན་མི་འདའ་ཞིང་། ། ཆོས་ཀྱང་ནུབ་པར་
མི་འགྱུར་ཏེ། ། སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ནི་གདུལ་པའི་ཕྱིར། ། མྱ་ངན་འདས་པ་ཉེ་བར་སྟོན།" ("The Buddha does
not pass into nirvāṇa, nor does the Dharma perish; for the sake of taming beings,
[the Buddha] displays passing into nirvāṇa"). This is a distinct doctrinal claim
from the bodhisattva vow treated on `suffering.md` (a petition asking the buddhas
*not* to display passing into nirvāṇa): here the point is that even an apparent
passing is itself already only a display, never an actual cessation of the
Buddha's continuum.

No other commentary in the corpus makes this claim.
