# Kṣānti — བཟོད་པ། (Patience, one of the six pāramitās)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather method note.** Per this topic's own task instructions, this packet
> was gathered manually rather than by `assemble_packet.py` or a Signal-A
> keyword-packet script (no spine slot and no Signal-A tagging run exists for
> a cross-cutting doctrinal term like this): `rg -n 'བཟོད་པ'` against all
> sixteen `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files, followed by reading each
> hit's full claim block (heading through `Cite:` line) in context, copied
> character-for-character — never retyped. Every one of the sixteen
> commentaries returned at least one hit; none is silent on this term.
>
> **Scope note — two unrelated senses excluded.** བཟོད་པ (the verbal root "to
> bear/tolerate") surfaces in this corpus in senses that have nothing to do
> with kṣānti as a doctrinal category: (1) as a plain adjective/adverb
> ("unbearably [bright]," མི་བཟོད་པར), and (2) as a ritual verb of
> forgiveness/forbearance in a confession liturgy ("please forbear/forgive,"
> བཟོད་པར་མཛད). Claims using བཟོད་པ only in these senses are logged under
> "Claims reviewed, not separately cited," never folded into a doctrinal
> facet. A third sense — "acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena"
> (མི་སྐྱེ་བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ, anutpattika-dharmakṣānti), a bodhisattva-path
> attainment narrated in Tārā's origin story — shares the same root term and
> is doctrinally adjacent to (but not identical with) the kṣānti-pāramitā;
> it is kept on this page as its own clearly separated facet rather than
> silently merged with the pāramitā material.

## Questions asked

1. How does the root verse (the "golden-blue, lotus-hand" verse) present kṣānti among the six pāramitās, and how consistently do commentaries gloss this as Tārā's iconographic/doctrinal domain (spyod yul)?
2. Do any commentaries single out kṣānti as the specific antidote to a named affliction, and do they agree on which one?
3. Do any commentaries expand the six-pāramitā list into an extended ten-pāramitā list, and where does kṣānti fall in it?
4. Do any commentaries give a "hidden" or completion-stage (sbas don) reading of kṣānti distinct from the plain six-pāramitā gloss, and if more than one does, do they agree?
5. Does any commentary define kṣānti in ultimate/non-referential (Madhyamaka) terms via a scriptural citation?
6. Does any commentary subdivide kṣānti into named types?
7. Does any commentary offer a divergent reading of the pāramitā-list passage that assigns kṣānti's slot differently from the corpus's default six-fold reading?
8. Do commentaries narrate an attainment called "acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena" (mi skye ba'i chos la bzod pa) in Tārā's origin story, and how does this technical sense of བཟོད་པ relate to the kṣānti-pāramitā sense?
9. Is any commentary silent on བཟོད་པ entirely, and does the term appear anywhere in the corpus in a sense unrelated to kṣānti (pāramitā or dharmakṣānti) that should be excluded from this page?

## Kṣānti among the six pāramitās — the root verse's list (Tārā's iconographic/doctrinal domain)

### Consensus

Fourteen of the sixteen commentaries attest, either by quoting the root verse
directly or by glossing it, that the verse describing Tārā's lotus-adorned
hand ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་གསེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱིས། ། པདྨས་ཕྱག་ནི་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ། ། སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་
འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། ། བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" — "Homage to her
golden-blue [form], born from water, whose hand is adorned with a lotus; whose
domain of activity is generosity, diligence, austerity, peace, patience, and
concentration") names kṣānti (བཟོད་པ) as one of the six pāramitās that
constitute her domain of practice (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ). The remaining two commentaries
address the same passage differently rather than being silent on it: gendun-drub
reads it as a sign of *ten* pāramitās (recorded under "Extended ten-pāramitā
list" below) and gendun-gyatso offers a self-labeled alternate ("yang na")
parse of a related passage (recorded under "Divergent alternate reading"
below) — both cited there rather than double-counted here:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-6`,
`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-6`,
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-19`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`,
`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`,
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `taranatha:c-3-4`,
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-12`
(14 commentaries)

Eight of these claims quote the root verse itself (`yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`,
`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-6` [a fragment: "བཟོད་པ་
དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་། །"], `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`,
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`); the remaining claims are commentarial prose
glosses of the same passage, restating the same six-item list in each
commentator's own words (e.g. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`: "...བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་
གཏན་སྟེ་ཐབས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ལྔས་གང་ལ་སྤྱོད་པའི་ཡུལ་ལམ...ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་
རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་མ་ཉིད་ལའོ།"). `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-19` gives the corpus's only
bare terminological gloss of the word itself: "བཟོད་པ་ནི། བཟོད་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་
ཕྱིན་པའོ།" ("'Patience' is the perfection of patience.").

### ⚑ Divergences

None on the basic identification — every commentary that discusses this verse
agrees kṣānti is one of its six named pāramitās. (A genuinely divergent
*alternate parse* of a related passage is recorded separately below, under
"Divergent alternate reading.")

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above — this facet is the corpus-wide floor every
commentary shares.

## Kṣānti as antidote to / undefiled by anger

### Consensus

Three commentaries independently pair kṣānti with anger (ཞེ་སྡང / ཁོང་ཁྲོ) as
its specific opposing affliction, within an explicit six-antidotes-to-six-faults
scheme:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` (3 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-6-5` states the fullest version of the scheme: "སེར་སྣའི་
གཉེན་པོ་སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ལེ་ལོའི་གཉེན་པོ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། ཚུལ་འཆལ་གྱི་གཉེན་པོ་
བསྲུང་དཀའ་བ་ཐུབ་པའི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས། རྨོངས་པའི་གཉེན་པོ་ཤེས་རབ། ཁོང་ཁྲོའི་གཉེན་པོ་
བཟོད་པ། རྣམ་གཡེང་གི་གཉེན་པོ་བསམ་གཏན" ("the antidote to miserliness is
generosity...the antidote to anger is patience, the antidote to distraction is
concentration"). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` states the same pairing from
the opposite direction, as an absence of the fault rather than a
possession of the antidote: "...བཟོད་པ་ལ་ཁོང་ཁྲོའི་སྐྱོན་གྱིས་མ་གོས་པ" ("...
patience undefiled by the fault of anger"), within a longer list pairing each
of the six pāramitās with the fault it is free of. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7`
gives the same pairing as an "enemy" (དགྲ་བོ) relation: "བཟོད་པའི་དགྲ་ཞེ་སྡང་
དང་། ཁོང་ཁྲོ" ("the enemy of patience is hatred and anger").

### ⚑ Divergences

None — all three name the same affliction (anger/hatred) as kṣānti's opposite,
using three different rhetorical framings (antidote / undefiled-by / enemy-of)
for what is textually the same claim.

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above.

## Extended ten-pāramitā list including kṣānti

### Consensus

Two commentaries, elsewhere in their treatment of Tārā's qualities (not the
lotus-hand verse itself), extend the six-pāramitā list to the standard
Mahāyāna ten-pāramitā list (six plus thabs/upāya, stobs/bala, smon-lam/
praṇidhāna, ye-shes/jñāna), placing kṣānti third, and map the ten pāramitās
onto the ten bodhisattva bhūmis:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8`
(2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4` gives it in full: "ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་ནི། སྦྱིན་པ་
དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་དང་།
ཐབས་དང་། སྟོབས་དང་། སྨོན་ལམ་དང་། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ནོ།" — with the ten bhūmis
also enumerated in the same claim. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` gives a parallel
version, again ten pāramitās ("སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས། བཟོད་པ། བརྩོན་འགྲུས།
བསམ་གཏན། ཤེས་རབ། ཐབས་མཁས། སྨོན་ལམ། སྟོབས། ཡེ་ཤེས") tied to victory over the
ten non-virtues, the two obscurations, and self-cherishing across the ten
bhūmis. Both commentaries put kṣānti in the same third position in the list.

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` corroborates the broader pattern of reading
Tārā's lotus-hand mudrā as a sign of *ten*, not six, pāramitās ("ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་
ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་དག་པའི་རྟགས་སོ") but does not itemize the ten by name in this
claim, so it is recorded here as a corroborating but less specific attestation
rather than as full support for the itemized list above.

### ⚑ Divergences

None — the ten-pāramitā material supplements rather than contradicts the
six-pāramitā consensus above; both counts are used by the same commentaries
(palden-sherab and tenzin-dhonzang each also appear in the six-pāramitā
consensus facet) in different sections of their own texts.

### Unique

None beyond the elaborations above.

## Hidden/secret meaning: kṣānti as equanimity toward pleasure and pain

### Consensus

Two commentaries independently give a "hidden meaning" (sbas don) reading of
the same lotus-hand verse, in which kṣānti is glossed not as the ordinary
pāramitā but specifically as equanimity toward all pleasure and pain:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`, `taranatha:c-3-7` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1` (from a "third, hidden-meaning" reading paired
with the completion-stage union of secret lotus and vajra): "...བདེ་སྡུག་
ཐམས་ཅད་སྙོམས་པའི་བཟོད་པ་དང་།" ("...and patience that is equanimity toward
all pleasure and pain"). `taranatha:c-3-7` gives near-identical wording in its
own "hidden meaning" reading of diligence, patience, and generosity: "...བདེ་
སྡུག་ཐམས་ཅད་དེའི་ངང་དུ་སྙོམས་པའི་བཟོད་པ་དང་།" ("...and patience that is
equanimity toward all pleasure and pain, in that very state").

### ⚑ Divergences

None — the two commentaries' wording is close enough to read as the same
formula, though each embeds it in its own surrounding completion-stage/hidden
frame (palden-sherab's alongside the union of secret lotus and vajra;
taranatha's alongside renunciation and generosity) rather than a fully
identical doctrinal context, so neither is folded into a single verbatim
"synthesised consensus" line — the Tibetan above is quoted claim-by-claim, not
harmonized.

### Unique

None beyond the two positions above.

## Ultimate, non-referential kṣānti (Brahmaviśeṣacintin-sūtra citation)

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None — additional doctrine unique to one commentary, not a second position on

### Unique

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2` cites the *Sūtra of the Questions of
Brahmaviśeṣacintin* (ཚངས་པ་ཁྱད་པར་སེམས་ཀྱིས་ཞུས་པའི་མདོ) to gloss each of the

པའོ། མི་བསྲུང་བ་ནི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་སོ། མི་གནས་པ་ནི་བཟོད་པའོ། མི་བརྩོན་པ་ནི་བརྩོན་
འགྲུས་སོ། མི་སེམས་པ་ནི་བསམ་གཏན་ནོ། མི་དམིགས་པ་ནི་ཤེས་རབ་བོ།" ("Non-grasping
is generosity. Non-guarding is discipline. **Non-abiding is patience.**
Non-striving is diligence. Non-thinking is meditative absorption.
Non-referencing is wisdom."). No other commentary in this packet cites this
sūtra or gives a non-referential/Madhyamaka-style definition of kṣānti.

## Threefold subdivision of kṣānti

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None — additional doctrine unique to one commentary, not a second position on
a question others also answer.

### Unique

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-6`, in a passage subdividing each of the six
pāramitās into three, gives kṣānti's threefold division as: "བཟོད་པ་ལ་སྡུག་
བསྔལ་དང་ལེན་གྱི་བཟོད་པ། གནོད་པ་ལ་ཇི་མི་སྙམ་པའི་བཟོད་པ། ཆོས་ལ་ངེས་ཐོབ་ཀྱི་
བཟོད་པ་གསུམ།" ("Patience has three: the patience of accepting suffering, the
patience of not minding harm [done to oneself], and the patience of certainty
in the Dharma."). No other commentary in this packet gives a comparable
named threefold breakdown of kṣānti specifically.

## Divergent alternate reading of the pāramitā-list passage

### Consensus

None — this entire facet is a single commentary's own alternate ("yang na")
reading of one of its own passages.

### ⚑ Divergences

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-3`, introduced explicitly with "ཡང་ན" ("or
alternatively"), offers a second reading of a pāramitā-bearing passage,
assigning it as renunciation, patience, concentration, and the wisdom whose
object is suchness — concluding the goddess "has the nature of one who has
fully perfected the six pāramitās" under this reading too: "ཡང་ན་...སྤངས་པ་
དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཉིད་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཅན་གྱི་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་
བར་གྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ཏེ། ཕར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ཡོངས་སུ་རྫོགས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་ཅན་ཡིན་ནོ་ཞེས་
པའོ།" This is presented by the commentary itself as an alternative alongside
its own immediately preceding reading (generosity/diligence/discipline/
pacification of afflictions — not independently gathered by this page's
kṣānti-only search, since that claim does not itself contain བཟོད་པ). Both
readings agree the total is six pāramitās and both keep kṣānti and dhyāna in
place; they differ on which two further terms (renunciation and
wisdom-of-suchness, vs. the ordinary generosity/diligence/discipline triad
attested elsewhere in the corpus) complete the set. No other commentary in
this packet offers a comparable second, self-labeled "alternative" parse of
the same kind.

### Unique

None beyond the divergence above.

## Related but distinct sense: "acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena" in Tārā's origin narrative

> This facet uses བཟོད་པ in a different, though doctrinally adjacent, technical
> sense from every facet above: not the kṣānti-*pāramitā* itself, but
> **anutpattika-dharmakṣānti** (མི་སྐྱེ་བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ) — the bodhisattva
> attainment of accepting/tolerating that all phenomena are unarisen. It is
> kept on this page, clearly separated, because it shares the same root term
> and appears in this corpus's own account of Tārā's origin narrative, but it
> should not be read as further attestation of the six-pāramitā material
> above.

### Consensus

Three commentaries narrate that Tārā (as a princess/queen figure, prior to her
vow), while dwelling in the royal palace and meditating on samādhi even while
skillfully enjoying the five sense-objects, attained "acceptance of the
non-arising of phenomena," immediately followed by accomplishing the samādhi
"Liberator of All Beings" (འགྲོ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲོལ་བ):

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-4`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-8` (3 commentaries)

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-4` states it most concisely: "མི་སྐྱེ་བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ་
ཐོབ་སྟེ། འགྲོ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྒྲོལ་བ་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་གྲུབ།" ("She attained
acceptance of the non-arising of phenomena, and accomplished the samādhi
called 'Liberator of All Beings.'"). `sungrab-tulku:c-2-4` gives the same
attainment with fuller narrative framing: "...འདོད་པའི་ཡོན་ཏན་ལྔ་ནི།
གཟུགས་སྒྲ་དྲི་རོ་རེག་བྱ་བཅས་ལ་ཐབས་མཁས་པས་ལོངས་སྤྱོད་བཞིན་དུ་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་
སྒོམ་པས་མི་སྐྱེ་བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ་ཐོབ་ཅིང་།" ("...skillfully partaking of the
five sense-objects — form, sound, smell, taste, touch — while cultivating
meditative absorption, she attained tolerance of the non-arising of
phenomena."). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-7` matches this narrative closely
("...ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་བསྒོམས་པས། ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་སྐྱེ་བ་མེད་པར་རྟོགས་པའི་མི་སྐྱེ་
བའི་ཆོས་ལ་བཟོད་པ་"); its immediately following claim `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-8`
continues the same passage into the "Liberator of All Beings" samādhi, though
this page's own gathered text of that claim ends mid-clause ("...བཟོད་པ་ལ་
མ་") — it is cited here as a corroborating continuation of the same
narrative, not as an independent quotation beyond what was gathered.

### ⚑ Divergences

None — all three commentaries narrate the same attainment in the same
narrative position (immediately before the "Liberator of All Beings" samādhi).

### Unique

None beyond the consensus above.
