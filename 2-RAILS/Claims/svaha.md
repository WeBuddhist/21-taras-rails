> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **On this packet's assembly:** སྭཱ་ཧཱ is a cross-cutting keyword subject, not a
> registered spine slot, so `assemble_packet.py`/spine-map does not apply, and
> (unlike `mantra.md`/`tara-mantra.md`) no Signal-A keyword-tagging packet script
> exists for it either. Per this run's explicit instruction, the gather was done
> directly: a Python script located every line in all 16 raw tree-guided files
> matching any attested orthographic variant of the syllable — སྭཱ་ཧཱ, སྭཱཧཱ, སྦཱ་ཧཱ
> (subjoined-ba spelling), སྭཱ་ཧྭ / སྭཱཧྭ (corrupted/variant final), and སྭཱ་ཧུ
> (`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-9`'s own spelling) — then mechanically located each hit
> inside its own claim block (bounded by that block's `#### c-...`/`##### c-...`
> heading, at whatever heading depth that commentary uses, through the next `#`
> line) and extracted the block **character-for-character**, never retyped. This
> found 70 distinct claim blocks across all 16 commentaries (17 further hits are
> **Grounding index** table rows quoting the mantra as reference material, not
> claims, and are correctly excluded).
>
> **Scope note — relationship to `tara-15.md`, `tara-mantra.md`, `phat.md`,
> `mantra.md`, and `hum-syllable.md`:** SVĀHĀ (like ཕཊ and ཧཱུྃ) is a generic
> tantric mantra-closing syllable used across countless deities' mantras,
> independently notable outside this text. Within this corpus its single
> heaviest concentration is the fifteenth homage's own root-verse line —
> "…སྭཱ་ཧཱ་ཨོཾ་དང་ཡང་དག་ལྡན་མ། སྡིག་པ་ཆེན་པོ་འཇོམས་པ་ཉིད་མ།" ("…genuinely endowed with
> SVĀHĀ and OṂ, she herself destroys great sin") — which is **already fully
> consolidated** as that verse's own "The mantra: OṂ and SVĀHĀ destroy great
> sin" Consensus facet in `2-RAILS/Claims/tara-15.md` (all sixteen commentaries
> attested there, including `taranatha`'s two word-gloss/doctrinal claims cited
> as Unique on that page). Re-deriving that same Consensus here, from the same
> claim IDs, would duplicate rather than add — per this run's brief, this page
> does not do so. The great majority of this packet's remaining claims are
> either (a) plain quotations of the ten-syllable formula elsewhere in the text
> (`tara-mantra.md`'s territory), (b) the PHAṬ-replaces-SVĀHĀ placement

## Etymology / meaning of the syllable SVĀHĀ

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently gloss SVĀHĀ's core semantic content as pacifying misdeeds/sin and increasing the accumulation of virtue:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-26-11`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3` (2 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-26-11` states this explicitly via the word "peace" (ཞི་བ) as SVĀHĀ's own gloss:

**བོད་ཡིག:** སྭཱ་ཧཱ་སྟེ་ཞི་བའི་དོན་ལ་གོ སྡིག་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཞི་བར་བྱེད་ཅིང་དགེ་བའི་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་འཕེལ་བར་བྱེད་པའོ།།
**English:** "SVĀHĀ is understood in the sense of peace: it pacifies all misdeeds and increases every accumulation of virtue."

`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3` (part of a syllable-by-syllable gloss of the whole mantra — OṂ, then TĀRE TUTTĀRE, then SVĀHĀ) gives the matching two-part content for SVĀHĀ specifically, without itself using the word "peace":

**བོད་ཡིག:** སྭཱ་ ཧྭ་ཞེས་ནི་ཅི་ཞིག་བརྗོད། །སྡིག་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཞི་བྱེད་ཅིང་། །དགེ་བའི་ཚོགས་ནི་འཕེལ་བར་བྱེད། །ཉེས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་འཕྲོག་བྱེད་ཅིང་། ། སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཐམས་ཅད་འཇོམས་པའི་མཆོག
**English:** "As for what is expressed by 'SVĀHĀ': it pacifies all sin, increases the accumulation of virtue, removes all faults, and is supreme in destroying all suffering."

The shared core — "pacifies sin, increases virtue" — is character-close between the two (སྡིག་པ་ ... ཞི་བ / ཞི་བྱེད and དགེ་བའི་ཚོགས་ ... འཕེལ་བ in both); `tsultrim-namdak` alone extends the gloss with two further clauses ("removes all faults," "destroys all suffering") not present in `sungrab-tulku`'s claim, so per Rule 9 the attestation above covers only the two-clause core, not the extension. `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-3` is also cited (for its OṂ/TĀRE-TUTTĀRE clauses) in `2-RAILS/Claims/tara-mantra.md`; citing its SVĀHĀ clause here as well is not a duplication, since that page's own scope excludes SVĀHĀ as an independently-notable generic syllable (see that page's own scope note).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the readings below are additional, non-corroborated glosses rather than incompatible readings of the same proposition.

### Unique

  **བོད་ཡིག:** སྭཱ་ཧུའི་ཡི་གེ་ནི་ཁྱོད་ལ་འདུད་དོ།
  **English:** "The syllable Svāhā means 'homage to you.'"

  This claim is routed to a node distinct from the fifteenth-homage slot (it is not among the claims `tara-15.md` consulted for `pema-namgyal`), so it is not a duplicate of that page's own material.

- `taranatha:c-15-9` gives a technical, activity-classification gloss — SVĀHĀ as the mantra-ending marking a specific category of ritual activity (paired elsewhere in the same commentary with TRAD/PHAṬ as the endings for repelling/slaying activity, per `phat.md`'s own citation of `taranatha:c-15-10`):

  **བོད་ཡིག:** འདིས་ཞི་བའི་ལས་ཀྱི་མཐའ་བསྒྱུར་བའི་སྔགས་སྭཱ་ཧཱ་ར་བཤད་ཅིང་།
  **English:** "By this, [Tāranātha] explains SVĀHĀ as the mantra-ending marking peaceful activity."

  This claim is already cited as a Unique attestation on `tara-15.md` (under "The mantra"). It is repeated here because it is squarely on-topic for this page's own etymology facet — gathering every attested tradition on what SVĀHĀ itself means, wherever in the vault it was first consolidated — not because it adds a claim absent from the sibling page.

## Doctrinal ("hidden meaning") reading of OṂ and SVĀHĀ

### Unique
`taranatha:c-15-17` gives a completion-stage/doctrinal reading distinct from any plain semantic gloss, reading OṂ and SVĀHĀ together as symbolizing the "indestructible speech-sound":

**བོད་ཡིག:** གསུང་གཞོམ་མེད་ཀྱི་སྒྲ་རིག་སྔགས་ཨོཾ་དང་སྭཱ་ཧཱ་ས་མཚོན་པ་མཐའ་ཡས་པར་ཤར་བས།
**English:** "The indestructible speech-sound — the vidyā-mantra symbolized by OṂ and SVĀHĀ — arises infinitely."

No other commentary in the packet corroborates this specific doctrinal reading. As with `taranatha:c-15-9` above, this claim is already cited as Unique on `tara-15.md` ("Hidden / completion-stage meaning" is instead where that page's own five-wisdoms mapping sits — `taranatha:c-15-17` is not part of that five-wisdoms set, and is its own separate Unique entry there too); it is repeated here for the same reason — this page's own doctrinal-reading facet is incomplete without it.

## SVĀHĀ used to name the whole ten-syllable mantra by its own final syllable

### Unique
`sungrab-tulku`'s closing "condensed praise verse" (a colophon-area passage distinct from the fifteenth homage) uses "SVĀHĀ" itself, rather than "the ten-syllable mantra" or a full quotation, as the name by which the verse refers to the whole mantra:

**བོད་ཡིག (verse, `sungrab-tulku:c-26-2`):** ༄༎ ཨོཾ་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་མ་འཕགས་མོ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།། ཕྱག་འཚལ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཏཱ་རེ་དཔའ་མོ།། ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཡིས་འཇིགས་ཀུན་སེལ་མ།། ཏུ་རེས་དོན་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྟེར་མ༎ སྭཱ་ཧཱ་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་ལ་རབ་འདུད།། ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ཏུ་རེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ།
**English:** "OṂ, homage to the Blessed Ārya Tārā. Homage, Tārā, Tāre, heroine, who by TUTTĀRE dispels all fear, who by TURE bestows all benefits — I bow before the ten syllables of SVĀHĀ. OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ."

`sungrab-tulku:c-26-10` re-quotes and isolates exactly this phrase as its own claim, in the immediate lead-in to the etymology (`sungrab-tulku:c-26-11`, cited above) that follows it:

**བོད་ཡིག:** སྭཱ་ཧཱ་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་ལ་རབ་འདུད༎ ཞེས་པ་སྟེ།
**English:** "'I bow before the ten syllables of SVĀHĀ' — that is:"

Read together, these two claims and `sungrab-tulku:c-26-11`'s etymology form one continuous passage in `sungrab-tulku`'s own commentary: the verse names the whole ten-syllable mantra metonymically as "SVĀHĀ," and the commentary's very next words gloss that name. No other commentary in the packet uses SVĀHĀ this way (as the name of the whole mantra rather than its final syllable); the closest parallel — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-0-4`'s "སྭཱཧཱ་ཡི་གེར་བཅས་ལ་རབ་འདུད" ("with the syllable svāhā, to her I bow down") — is a near-identical verse line but is already cited in `tara-mantra.md` for a different point (the OṂ…SVĀHĀ bracketing of the mantra's three-syllable core), so it is not re-cited here as a second witness to this same metonymic-naming point; the point stands on `sungrab-tulku`'s own claims alone.

## SVĀHĀ as a generic ritual mantra-closing syllable outside the root text's own mantra

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak` is the only commentary in this packet whose source text extends past the root twenty-one homages into an appended sādhana/ritual manual (the same fact already established in `phat.md`'s parallel facet for that commentary). Within that appended material, SVĀHĀ recurs as a generic mantra-closing syllable in formulas that have nothing to do with the root text's own Tārā mantra:

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-8` — a general offering mantra to Tārā and her retinue:

  **བོད་ཡིག:** ཨོཾ་ཨཱརྱ་ཏཱ་རེ་ས་པ་རི་ཝཱ་ར་བཛྲ་ཨགྷཾ། པཱཏྱཾ། པུསྦེ། དྷུཔེ། ཨ་ལོ་ཀེ གདྡྷེ། ནེ་ཝིདྱ། ཤཔྟ་པྲ་ཙྪ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ།
  **English:** "OṂ ĀRYA TĀRE SAPARIVĀRA VAJRA ARGHAṂ PĀDYAṂ PUṢPE DHŪPE ĀLOKE GANDHE NAIVIDYA ŚABDA PRATĪCCHA SVĀHĀ" — a general offering, made to "Noble Tārā together with her retinue."

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-7` — the mantra of a wholly different name-praise appended within the same commentary's anthology, structurally unrelated to the root text's ten-syllable formula:

  **བོད་ཡིག:** ཨོཾ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཀྲྀ་པཱ་བ་ལཾ་བ་ན། མཻ་ཏྱ་ ཏྰ་གེ་ཤྲ་པ་ཎཱི་ཡེ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ།
  **English:** "OṂ TĀRE KṚPĀ VALAMBANA MAITRYA TĀGE ŚRAPĀṆĪYE SVĀHĀ."

These two instances are the packet's clearest direct evidence for this term's own stated notability rationale — that SVĀHĀ is "a generic tantric mantra-closing syllable used across countless deities' mantras" — since neither involves the root text's own Tārā mantra at all. `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-2-3` (a further torma-offering mantra ending "…ཨོཾ་ཨཿཧཱུྃ་ཕཊ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ") is the same pattern but is already cited as the parallel witness in `phat.md`'s own "PHAṬ as a generic ritual mantra-closing syllable" facet; it is not re-cited here to avoid double-counting one ritual instance under two topic pages for the same underlying evidence. `pema-namgyal`'s cluster of distinct SVĀHĀ-terminated deity-mantras (`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-26`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-28`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-49`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-53`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-54` — OṂ TĀRE TRAD PHAṬ SVĀHĀ; OṂ TURE SVĀHĀ; OṂ TARA TARA PHAṬ / …PHAṬ SVĀHĀ; OṂ HARA HARA TUTTĀRE SVĀHĀ; and a threefold TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE form) is further corroborating evidence of the same general pattern — SVĀHĀ terminating many distinct formulas within one commentary — but every one of these is already cited by `tara-mantra.md` under its own benefit facets (destroying sorcery, destroying māras, dispelling poison, dispelling epidemic disease), so they are logged under "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below rather than re-cited as primary evidence here.
