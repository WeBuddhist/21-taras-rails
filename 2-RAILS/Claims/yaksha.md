## Verse 6 — the fourfold host (bhūta, vetāla, gandharva, yakṣa) who bow and praise Tārā

### Consensus
The verse describing Tārā's worship by the great mundane gods (Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Maheśvara/Viśveśvara) also names hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and **yakṣas** as beings who bow and praise her "from in front":

འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། །གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།
("...praised in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas.")

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-40`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-12`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`, `taranatha:c-6-12`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22` (15 commentaries)

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3` recasts the list with substitutions — "the powerful elemental spirit, the corpse-riding rākṣasa, Yama the lord of gandharvas, and the assembly of yakṣas guarding the north" — rather than the bare four terms, but occupies the same structural position (the second group of worshippers) and still names yakṣas explicitly.

No claim located by this page's own gather names pema-namgyal among the commentaries attesting this verse; see Coverage.

### ⚑ Divergences
None on whether yakṣa belongs in this fourfold list — only on who the yakṣas' chief is (next facet).

### Unique
None beyond the identity variants discussed below.

## Identity of the yakṣas in verse 6: who is their chief?

### Consensus
None. Commentaries that name a chief/ruler for the yakṣas in this verse give genuinely different figures; none states its own reading is exclusive of another's, but none restates another's specific figure either.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position 1 — chief is Vaiśravaṇa (རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས / རྣམ་ཐོས་བུ), lord of wealth, associated with the north:**
- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-43`: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས་ལ་སོགས་པའོ།" ("'Yakṣas' means [beings] such as Vaiśravaṇa.")
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་གཙོ་བོ་རྣམ་ཐོས་ཀྱི་སྲས" ("the chief of yakṣas, the son of Vaiśravaṇa").
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-4`: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས་སོགས་བྱང་ཕྱོགས་ནོར་གཏེར་བདག་པོ་ཚོགས་རྣམས" ("'yakṣas' are the hosts of the lords of wealth-treasures of the north, headed by Vaiśravaṇa").
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`: "བྱང་ཕྱོགས་སུ་གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས་སོགས་ནོར་བདག་ཚོགས" ("in the north, Vaiśravaṇa and the other wealth-lord hosts of yakṣas").
- `taranatha:c-6-12`: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་རྣམ་ཐོས་བུ་ལ་སོགས་པ་གཏེར་གྱི་བདག་པོ་རྣམས" ("Yakṣas are Vaiśravaṇa and other lords of treasure").
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22`: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་གཙོ་བོ་རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས་སོགས་ནོར་ལྷ་གཏེར་བདག" ("the yakṣa-chief Vaiśravaṇa and his retinue of wealth-gods and treasure-lords").

(6 commentaries)

**Position 2 — yakṣas as the retinue of an unnamed "ill-bodied one" (ལུས་ངན), no chief named:**
- `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`: "ལུས་ངན་གྱི་འཁོར་གནོད་སྦྱིན་སོགས" ("the yakṣas, retinue of the 'ill-bodied one'").
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "ལུས་ངན་གྱི་འཁོར་གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས" ("the assembly of yakṣas — the retinue of the ill-formed").
- `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`: "ལུས་ངན་གྱི་འཁོར་གནོད་སྦྱིན་སོགས" ("the yakṣas, retinue of the evil-bodied ones").

(3 commentaries)

**Position 3 — yakṣas headed by a named figure Rmugs-'dzin / Smug-'dzin, distinct from Vaiśravaṇa:**
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`: "རྨུགས་འཛིན་ལ་སོགས་པའི་གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཚོགས" ("the hosts of yakṣas headed by Rmugs-'dzin").
- `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-12`: "སྨུག་འཛིན་སོགས་གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཚོགས" ("hosts of yakṣas, such as Smug-'dzin").

(2 commentaries)

The page observes (this is the page's own reading, not asserted by any single claim): the three positions are not stated as mutually exclusive by their own commentaries, and Positions 1 and 3 could in principle name the same figure under different epithets, or Position 3's name could be a corrupted or alternate rendering of a Vaiśravaṇa-epithet — but no claim in this packet states any such identification, so the three are preserved as distinct rather than harmonized. Three further commentaries (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`) describe the yakṣas in this verse (guarding the north; endowed with magical power; generically) without naming any chief at all, and are not counted toward any of the three positions above.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Completion-stage equivalence: yakṣa as "conceptual thought" (rnam-par-rtog-pa)

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently give the same hidden-meaning/completion-stage equivalence for the verse-6 fourfold host, in the same order — bhūta = channel, vetāla = drop, gandharva = wind, **yakṣa = conceptual thought** — glossing the host's "praising from in front" as dissolution:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`, `taranatha:c-6-17` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ་དང་། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་དང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" ("'bhūtas' are the channels, 'vetālas' are the drop, 'gandharvas' are the wind, 'yakṣas' are conceptual thought"). `taranatha:c-6-17`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" — near-identical wording and identical ordering.

Note: this is a *different* completion-stage mapping from the one both commentaries also give for the verse-21 triad (gdon/ro-langs/gnod-sbyin = channel/drop/thought there too, but without a bhūta/wind term, since verse 21's triad has three members, not four) — see `2-RAILS/Claims/gdon.md`'s own Divergences section for that verse-21 "Reading 1." The two mappings share vocabulary (thought for yakṣa/gnod-sbyin in both) but are attached to different verses and different-length lists.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no other commentary in this packet's gather offers a competing completion-stage reading for the verse-6 host.

### Unique
None beyond the two attestations above.

## Yakṣa's recurring epithet within the triad-verse: "radiance/luster-stealer" (mdangs-'phrog-pa)

### Consensus
Within the verse-21 triad-destruction material (see previous section), five commentaries independently and specifically gloss or describe **yakṣa** — not gdon or ro-langs — as one who "steals radiance/luster" (མདངས་འཕྲོག་པ), a descriptive epithet not attached to the other two members of the triad in any claim this gather located:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-6-6`, `taranatha:c-21-1-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7` (5 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-21-1-6` gives the bare word-gloss: "གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་མདངས་འཕྲོག་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་གནོད་པ་སྟེ།" ("'Yakṣa' is the harm of the radiance-stealers"). `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-6-6`: "མདངས་འཕྲོག་པའི་གནོད་སྦྱིན་ལ་སོགས་པའི་གནོད་པའི་ཚོགས་རྣམས" ("hosts of harm-doers such as light-stealing yakṣas"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-2` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-7` each use the same or a closely matching phrase ("མདངས་འཕྲོག་པའི་གནོད་སྦྱིན...") in their own glosses of the same triad-destruction material.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no claim in this packet's gather gives yakṣa a competing epithet within the triad-verse context.

### Unique
None beyond the five attestations above.

## Yakṣa among the ten directional guardians summoned as Tārā's servants

### Consensus
A separate verse — distinct from both verse 6 and verse 21 — describes Tārā as able to summon the ten directional guardians (earth-goddess, nāga kings, Indra, and others) as servants to accomplish her activity. Several commentaries name the **yakṣa king** (usually Vaiśravaṇa) among this list, associated with the north:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-39` (5 commentaries)

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3` gives the fullest directional breakdown of the standard ten guardians, placing yakṣa explicitly in the north: "...བྱང་དུ་གནོད་སྦྱིན...." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1` both name "the yakṣa king Vaiśravaṇa" (གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྒྱལ་པོ་རྣམ་ཐོས་སྲས / གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ) explicitly in this list. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-39` name yakṣas among the ten without repeating the Vaiśravaṇa name in the quoted passage.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the five attestations above.

## Iconography: Tārā's trampling posture subdues powerful yakṣas (verse 7)

### Unique
`yama-sonam:c-3-7-5` alone describes Tārā's bodily posture — right leg bent, left extended, with a slightly wrathful cast — as treading underfoot "powerful yakṣas and the like" (གནོད་སྦྱིན་མཐུ་བོ་ཆེ་སོགས), and states that this trampling reverses the epidemic illness and hexes arising from their evil intentions. No other commentary's claims in this packet's gather describe this specific iconographic detail in connection with yakṣa.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.

## Narrative episodes involving yakṣas

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak` alone contributes two narrative anecdotes involving yakṣas, at points in its own exposition unrelated to verse 6, verse 21, or the ten-guardians verse:

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-4-11-1`: monks at a monastery in India (Bötsam Lak) are terrorized by shape-shifting apparitions — including figures "in the form of yakṣas" (གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་གཟུགས) alongside brahmins, women, tigers, and lions — causing some to faint and others to go mad; commissioning a painted image of Tārā ends the haunting, and the monastery adopts her as its yidam.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-4-15-1`: a Buddhist lay farmer in Bengal accidentally treads on a yakṣa's shrine (གནོད་སྦྱིན་གྱི་རྟེན) while going to sow his field; the enraged yakṣa hurls a thunderbolt from the sky, but the farmer's mere recollection of Tārā turns the flame-tongues into flowers, leaving him unharmed.
