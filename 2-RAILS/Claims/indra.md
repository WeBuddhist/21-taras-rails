## Questions asked

1. Who is Indra (བརྒྱ་བྱིན), and how do the commentaries identify or gloss him where he is named?
2. In what textual context does Indra first appear across the corpus, and which other gods does he appear alongside?
3. Do commentaries assign Indra a cosmological/directional role, and if so, which direction and within what scheme of guardians?
4. Which class of spirit-beings, if any, do commentaries describe as Indra's own retinue?
5. Does any commentary give a "hidden meaning" (completion-stage) reading of Indra, and if so what does he correspond to?
6. Does any commentary describe Indra as summoned or subjugated as a servant of Tārā's enlightened activity?
7. In Indra's second appearance in the corpus (the verse on the "king of the hosts of gods"), what role does he play, and how consensual is his identification there?
8. Do any commentaries use Indra, often paired with Brahmā, as a simile or benchmark for the temporal benefits of devotion to Tārā?
9. Does any commentary supply unique narrative or doctrinal material about Indra not found elsewhere in the corpus?
10. Are there two attested Tibetan spellings of Indra's name in this corpus, and does the choice of spelling track any doctrinal difference?
11. Does any commentary depict Indra in a devotional or iconographic vignette distinct from the standard worshipping-gods list?

## Identity — named in the sixth homage, glossed as king/lord of the gods

### Consensus

**Statement A — Indra appears in the corpus's sixth homage-verse, among a small set of worldly gods said to worship Tārā** (most commonly Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara together), whether the commentary quotes the verse in full, in part, or paraphrases its list:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `taranatha:c-6-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19` (16 commentaries)

The root-verse wording, where quoted in full, runs: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ། ། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་མཆོད་མ། ། འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། ། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།" (`yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`) — "Homage to her worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and Sarveśvara, and praised from in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas." Several other commentaries paraphrase the same list rather than quoting it verbatim, e.g. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`: "ལྷ་དབང་བརྒྱ་སྦྱིན། མེ་ལྷ། ཚངས་པ། རླུང་ལྷ་ལ་སོགས་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་དབང་ཕྱུག་སྟེ" ("Śakra, king of the gods; Agni; Brahmā; Vāyu; and so forth"); `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-2` gives the minimal form of this attestation, the bare word "བརྒྱ་བྱིན" glossed simply as "Indra is named among those who worship her."

**Statement B — Ten of these sixteen commentaries additionally gloss Indra explicitly as "lord/king of the gods"** (ལྷའི་དབང་པོ / ལྷ་དབང་ / ལྷའི་འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ), rather than leaving him as a bare name in the list:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-19` (10 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-3` is the clearest single-clause gloss: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ལྷའི་འགྲོ་བ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོའོ།" ("Indra is the king of the class of gods"). `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34` and `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1` both use "ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན"; `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1` and `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5` use "ལྷའི་དབང་པོ" / "ལྷ་དབང་" in the same construction; `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3` opens its exegesis "ལྷ་ཡི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན" before moving to the directional gloss (see "Directional role," below).

**Orthography.** Two spellings of Indra's name are attested across the corpus: བརྒྱ་བྱིན (the more frequent form, and the one used as this page's own title and in the article-worthiness queue) and བརྒྱ་སྦྱིན (used throughout `gendun-drub`'s raw file, e.g. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, and once each in `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-4`). `yama-sonam`'s own extraction flags this tension directly: claim `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`'s Referent field notes "this claim's own quotation spells the name བརྒྱ་སྦྱིན, not the Grounding index's chosen form བརྒྱ་བྱིན." No claim in the packet treats the two spellings as denoting different figures or ties the choice of spelling to any doctrinal difference — this page treats them as one figure throughout, following this page's own orthography (བརྒྱ་བྱིན) except inside verbatim quotations, which preserve each claim's own spelling.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed regarding Indra's basic identity. Every attestation above converges on "Indra, king/lord of the class of gods," named among the worldly gods who worship Tārā in the corpus's sixth homage. Roughly half the commentaries stop at this generic identification without adding a directional assignment (see "Directional role," below, for the commentaries that do) — this is a difference in how much detail each commentary supplies, not a conflicting position, so it is not flagged as a divergence.

### Unique

None beyond the minimal attestation already folded into Statement A above (`karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-2`).

## Directional role — guardian of the east

### Consensus

Eight commentaries place Indra within a directional-guardian scheme (of eight, ten, or fifteen guardians, depending on the commentary), consistently assigning him the eastern direction:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6` (8 commentaries)

`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "ཤར་གྱི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་" ("Indra, guardian of the eastern direction"). `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`: "ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་" (same). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-1`: "ཤར་གྱི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་" ("Indra, lord of gods, guardian of the east"). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3` gives the fullest ten-guardian scheme: "ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ་ནི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ་པོ་སྟེ་སྟེང་དུ་ཚངས་པ། ཤར་དུ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན།..." ("the 'earth-protectors' are the ten directional guardians: Brahmā above; Indra in the east; ..."). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6` cites an external liturgical source (notes on Cakrasaṃvara's subtle-drop-body maṇḍala) for an eight-guardian scheme assigning Indra the east: "ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན།..." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1` places Indra, again as "ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན," at the head of the ten directional guardians summoned as Tārā's servants (see "Summoned as servant," below).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed. No claim assigns Indra a direction other than east.

### Unique

None beyond the attestations above.

## Retinue — gandharvas as Indra's own retinue

### Consensus

Five commentaries, glossing the root verse's mention of gandharvas among Tārā's worshippers, identify the gandharvas specifically as Indra's own retinue:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7` (5 commentaries)

The wording is close to identical across all five, suggesting a shared exegetical source (re-read and confirmed: each independently pairs the same four lord–retinue assignments in the same order). `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" ("the gandharvas, retinue of Indra"). `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" (identical wording). `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" (identical wording). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-4`: "དྲི་ཟ་ནི་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཤིང་རྟ་སོགས་རྣམས་དང་" ("'gandharvas' are the various chariot[-riding] retinue of Indra, lord of gods, of the east"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`: "ཤར་ཕྱོགས་སུ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་འཁོར་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་" (same pairing, with the eastern direction added).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed within this packet (i.e., among the claims that name Indra specifically). Note that `2-RAILS/Claims/tara-06.md` records a separate divergence — Konchok Thabkhe identifies Yama, not Indra, as "lord of the gandharvas" — but that claim (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`) does not itself name Indra and so was never returned by this page's own gather search; it is out of scope here and not re-verified on this page.

### Unique

None beyond the attestations above.

## Hidden meaning — Indra as the earth element

### Consensus

Two commentaries, in a completion-stage ("hidden meaning") reading of the sixth homage, correlate the five worshipping gods with the five elements, assigning Indra to earth:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` (2 commentaries)

The wording is close to identical (re-read and confirmed): `taranatha:c-6-15`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ལྔ་ནི་འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་སྟེ། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས། མེ་ལྷ་ནི་མེ། ཚངས་པ་ནི་ཆུ། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནམ་མཁའ་འོ།" ("The hidden meaning: Indra and the other four are the five elements — Indra is earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, Vāyu is wind, and Viśveśvara is space"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས་དང་། མེ་ལྷ་མེ་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཆུ་དང་། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་དང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ནམ་མཁའ་ཡིན་པས་ན།" (same correspondence, same order). The near-verbatim match across these two commentaries suggests a shared exegetical source rather than fully independent corroboration.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

None beyond the corroborated pair above.

## Summoned as servant of Tārā's enlightened activity

### Consensus

Two commentaries, glossing a later homage on Tārā's power to summon earth- and wealth-deities, name Indra among the hosts summoned as servants of her activity:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1` (2 commentaries)

Wording is close to identical (re-read and confirmed): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1`: "...ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུའི་འཁོར་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པའི་བྲན་དུ་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་མ།" ("...she who is capable of summoning, as servants to accomplish her activity, ...Indra, lord of gods, and the retinue-hosts of the ten directional guardians"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1`: "...ལྷ་དབང་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ཀྱི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཁྱེད་ཀྱི་ཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པའི་བྲན་དུ་འགུགས་པར་ནུས་པའི་འཕགས་མ་ཁྱེད་ཉིད་མ།" ("...you, Noble Lady, are able to summon as servants to accomplish your enlightened activity...Indra lord of gods, and others"). The near-identical framing again suggests a shared exegetical source.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

## Second appearance — among the "kings of the hosts of gods" who serve Tārā

### Consensus

Thirteen of the sixteen commentaries name Indra a second time, in connection with a later homage praising Tārā as served/relied-upon by "the king of the hosts of gods" together with ordinary gods and kinnaras:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-19-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` (12 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2` gives the clearest gloss of the verse's phrase: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་རྒྱལ་པོ་དང་ཞེས་པ་འདོད་ལྷ་བརྒྱ་སྦྱིན་དང།གཟུགས་ཀྱི་ཚངས་པ་ཆེན་པོ་སྟེ་ལྷའི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་པོ་དང་..." ("'Homage to [her revered by] the king of the hosts of gods' means: Śakra, king of the desire-realm gods, and great Brahmā of the form realm — the kings of the hosts of gods..."). `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3` both give the same identification in near-parallel wording, pairing Indra and Brahmā as "ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་ཚངས་པ" / "ཚངས་པ་དང་། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1` and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50` both extend the same list to include mountain-, tree-, and water-gods and kinnara-kings alongside Indra and Brahmā. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` names this full retinue (Brahmā, Indra, nature-deities, kinnara kings) within a richer, partly-elided verse-recitation claim; `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` gives the same content as an independent gloss.

The remaining four commentaries in the packet (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `taranatha`, `tenga-tulku`) contributed no claim naming Indra in this second context — none of their raw claims files returned a hit here in this page's own gather search. This is recorded as an observed gap in this specific attestation, not as a divergence (no claim from these four commentaries takes a conflicting position; they are simply silent on Indra's presence in this later verse, in the claims this page's search located).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

None beyond the attestations above.

## Benefit-simile — Indra (with Brahmā) as a benchmark for temporal reward

### Consensus

Two commentaries, glossing a verse on the benefits of prayer to Tārā, liken the practitioner's temporal (as opposed to ultimate) reward to becoming like Indra or Brahmā:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3` (2 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`: "...གནས་སྐབས་ཀྱི་ཕན་ཡོན་ནི། མཐོ་རིས་ཀྱི་ཡོན་ཏན་དང་སྟོབས་འབྱོར་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་ཚངས་པ་ལྟ་བུ་དང་འཁོར་ལོའི་སྒྱུར་བའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལྟ་བུ་འཐོབ།" ("...the temporary benefit is attaining qualities and abundant power like a god-lord such as Indra or Brahmā, or like a wheel-turning king"). `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-3`: "...བསོད་ནམས་ཀྱི་དབང་དུ་ན་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དང་ཚངས་པ་ལྟ་བུ།..." ("...in terms of merit, like Indra or Brahmā..."), within a nearly parallel three-part structure (wheel-turning king / Indra-or-Brahmā / buddhahood) to `sungrab-tulku:c-2-11`.

## Unique narrative and citational material (Tenzin Dhonzang)

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

Tenzin Dhonzang alone supplies three distinct pieces of material about Indra not found in any other commentary in this packet:

- **A past-life correspondence naming Indra as an earlier incarnation of the elder Devadatta.** `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-21` quotes a list correlating bodhisattvas and buddhas with figures around the historical Buddha: "...ཀུན་ཏུ་བཟང་པོ་ནི་གནས་བརྟན་ཀུན་དགའ་པོར་གྱུར་ཏོ། །ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་གནས་བརྟན་ལྷས་བྱིན་ནོ། །རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཆེན་པོ་ནི། ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པར་གྱུར་ཏོ།" ("...Samantabhadra became elder Ānanda; Indra, lord of gods, became elder Devadatta; and great Vairocana became the perfectly complete Buddha Śākyamuni"). This claim is cited on this page as scriptural correspondence-material *about Indra*; it is a doctrinal/narrative aside within the commentary's own argument for teacher–tantra continuity, not itself about this text's praise.
- **Indra and Brahmā as the traditional "requesters" of the Buddha's teaching.** `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-3`: "སངས་རྒྱས་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ལ་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱིས་ཆོས་དུང་དཀར་པོ་གཡས་སུ་འཁྱིལ་བ་དང་། སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་བདག་པོ་ཚངས་པ་གཙུག་ཕུད་ཅན་གྱིས་གསེར་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་རྩིབས་སྟོང་ཅན་ཕུལ་ནས་བདེ་བཞིའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་བསྐོར་བར་སྐུལ་བ་པོ་བརྒྱལ་བྱིན་དང་། ཚངས་པ་ཡིན།" ("To the Buddha Bhagavān, Indra, lord of gods, offered a white conch-shell coiled to the right, and Brahmā...offered a golden thousand-spoked wheel, urging him to turn the dharma-wheel of the four joys — these are the requesters Indra and Brahmā"). No other commentary in this packet gives this narrative.
- **A named external liturgical citation for Indra's directional role.** `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6` (already cited under "Directional role," above) is distinctive in naming its source explicitly — a page of notes on Cakrasaṃvara's subtle-drop-body maṇḍala — rather than stating the directional-guardian scheme as the commentary's own unattributed exegesis, as the other seven commentaries in that facet do.

## Unique devotional and iconographic vignettes (Tsultrim Namdak)

### Consensus

None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

Tsultrim Namdak alone supplies two distinctive devotional vignettes involving Indra, both outside the standard "worshipping gods" list format used elsewhere in the corpus:

- **Indra bowing his jeweled hair to Tārā's lotus feet.** `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-15`: "བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ལ་སོགས་པས། །རིན་ཆེན་དོ་ཤལ་འཕྱང་བའི་རལ་པའི་རྩེས།། ཁྱོད་ཞབས་པདྨོའི་འདབ་མར་བཏུད་མཛད་པས། །ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་ལ་ཨུཏྤལ་ཆུན་པོས་རེག" ("...and Indra, and others — with the tips of their hair, hung with jeweled necklaces, they bow to the petals of your lotus feet, so that a small cluster of utpalas touches the sole of her foot"). This is a physical, embodied image of Indra's devotion, distinct from the flat enumerations found elsewhere in the corpus.
- **Indra named in a closing dedication-prayer, paired with Brahmā and Viṣṇu.** `tsultrim-namdak:c-9-10`, from the colophon of the printing project this commentary's raw file records: "...ཚངས་དང་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ཁྱབ་འཇུག་ལ་སོགས་ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་སྨན་སྤྲིན་མེ་ཏོག་མཛེས་པས་ལྗོངས་ཀུན་གཏམས..." ("...may the gods — Brahmā, Indra, Viṣṇu, and their hosts — fill every land with beautiful medicine-clouds of flowers..."). This is the only claim in the packet naming Indra alongside Viṣṇu (ཁྱབ་འཇུག) rather than only Brahmā, and the only one occurring in a colophon/dedication rather than in commentary on the root verses themselves.
