# Tārā 5 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ཧཱུྃ་ཡི་གེས། / Homage to Her of the Syllables TUTTĀRA HŪM

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Where does each commentary place this verse structurally — as the flat "fifth" of the twenty-one homages, or nested within a different, mode-of-praise taxonomy (e.g. "the fourth, praising by way of subduing what is discordant")?
2. By what name(s) or epithet(s) is this homage's deity identified, and what iconography (colour, posture, implements) accompanies each name?
3. What does the mantra TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE HŪM accomplish, and how do commentaries map its three terms ('dod/desire, phyogs/direction, nam mkha'/space) onto the three realms (desire, form, formless)?
4. Does the root verse's second couplet read "three worlds" (gsum) or "seven worlds" (bdun) pressed beneath her feet, and how do commentators that notice the variant account for it?
5. When "the seven worlds" is read, what seven categories are actually enumerated, and do commentaries agree with one another?
6. What activity is described in the verse's second couplet — pressing/treading the worlds and summoning all beings "without exception" — and is this uniformly read as an act of subjugation, or does any commentary read it differently?
7. Does any commentary offer an esoteric, completion-stage ("hidden-meaning") reading of TUTTĀRA HŪM, and do any two commentaries converge on the same specific formulation?
8. What additional benefits, etymological notes, or practice instructions do individual commentaries uniquely attach to this homage?
9. Anon-Trinle-Char argues at length that "seven worlds" is a scribal corruption and "three worlds" is the philologically correct reading, citing Nyima Sbaspa's own sādhana and Praise of Tārā as corroborating witnesses — do other commentaries in the corpus address or corroborate this specific textual-critical claim?
10. Three named scholars (Drakpa Gyaltsen, Tāranātha, Gendun Gyatso) are reported by Palden Sherab and, separately, by Gendun Gyatso's own commentary, as holding three different enumerations of "the seven worlds" — do their own commentaries, where present in this corpus, confirm the positions attributed to them?
11. Palden Sherab and Tāranātha both give a completion-stage "hidden meaning" (sbas don) reading of TUTTĀRA HŪM as caṇḍālī/tummo practice, mapping the seven worlds onto channel-cakras — do any other commentaries offer a parallel esoteric reading, or is this confined to these two?
12. Palden Sherab, Sangye Nyentrul, and (by epithet) Tsultrim Namdak identify this verse's deity as red Kurukullā holding a bow and arrow atop an utpala, rather than the "Roarer of HŪM" Tārā described by Anon-Trinle-Char (yellow, vajra posture, refuge-mudrā and lotus) — how widely is each iconographic tradition attested in the corpus?
13. Tenzin Dhonzang glosses "pressing with her feet" as an act of compassionate, non-abandoning embrace rather than domination — does any other commentary share this reading, or do the rest uniformly read it as subjugation?

## Structural placement

### Consensus
Eight commentaries mark this content with the flat ordinal "fifth" (lnga pa).

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-1`, `taranatha:c-5-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-1` (8 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Four commentaries instead nest this homage within a shared four-fold rhetorical-mode taxonomy, labelling it "the fourth: praising by way of subduing what is discordant" (མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) — the same taxonomic axis documented for verse 4 in `2-RAILS/Claims/tara-04.md` ("Structural placement" Divergences). Two of the four are verbatim-identical (anon-utpala, gendun-drub: both "བཞི་པ་མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི།"); the other two (lobsang-dawa, tenga-tulku) share the same thematic content but with genuinely different wording — lobsang-dawa's own claim gives only the bare ordinal "བཞི་པ་ནི།" ("as for the fourth"), the fuller "[subduing what is discordant]" description being an editorial gloss added by the raw-claims curator, not lobsang-dawa's own words; tenga-tulku's phrasing ("བཞི་པ་རྗེ་བཙུན་མས་མི་མཐུན་པའི་ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི།") is a paraphrase, not a verbatim match.

- Position 1 (flat "fifth of twenty-one"): the eight commentaries listed under Consensus above.
- Position 2 (nested "fourth mode of praise," within a rhetorical-mode taxonomy rather than a flat sequence number): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-1`.

This is a difference in classificatory axis (sequence-of-homages vs. mode-of-praise), not a doctrinal disagreement about the verse's content — recorded here per Rule 4 since these four commentaries' own structural claims do not label the verse "the fifth" anywhere captured in the packet.

The two axes are not, however, fully independent: Sungrab Tulku's own section marker (`sungrab-tulku:c-8-1`, listed under Consensus above for its bare numeral) reads "༼༥༽ མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ།" — "(5) Praise by way of subduing the adverse [forces]" — combining a flat numeral "(5)" with the same mode-of-praise descriptor (མི་མཐུན་ཕྱོགས་གནོན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) that Position 2 above treats as diagnostic of the separate rhetorical-mode taxonomy. This one claim is counted under Position 1 (Consensus) here because it uses a flat numeral rather than an ordinal nested in the four-fold taxonomy, but its wording shows the two classificatory languages are not wholly segregated across the corpus.

Konchok Thabkhe's own structural heading (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-1`) reads "the fifth, homage by way of subduing the three worlds" ('jig rten gsum 'dul ba'i sgo nas bstod pa) — using the exact same lexical construction ('jig rten + numeral) used everywhere else on this page for "the seven worlds" ('jig rten bdun), the count of worlds pressed underfoot by the verse's second couplet. This stands in unremarked tension with the same commentary's own root-verse quotation elsewhere in this packet, which reads "seven worlds" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, already cited under "The 'three worlds' vs. 'seven worlds' variant," below): one claim in Konchok Thabkhe's own commentary says "three," another says "seven," and neither claim resolves the discrepancy. Per Rule 6, the page does not resolve it either — it is flagged here as a genuine, unremarked internal puzzle in this commentary, parallel to (but distinct from) the cross-commentary "three vs. seven" variant discussed below. Separately, and not to be conflated with this heading: Konchok Thabkhe's *other* claim, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-2` (cited under "Mantra semantics," below), does use "khams gsum" (the desire/form/formless triad) to gloss what the mantra's light-rays fill — but that is a distinct claim about a distinct Tibetan phrase, not a gloss of c-2-5-1's own "'jig rten gsum" wording.

### Unique
None beyond the taxonomic split above.

## Deity identity and iconography

### Consensus
None on a single name or visual form — see Divergences.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two distinct identity/iconographic traditions are attested for this homage's deity:

**Position 1 — "[She Who] Roars/Proclaims the Sound HŪM" (yellow; vajra posture; refuge-granting mudrā and lotus).** The epithet is attested by three commentaries: Anon-Trinle-Char's own section heading and structural marker name her "the Venerable Lady Roarer of the Sound HŪM" (rje btsun ma hUM sgra sgrog ma); Gendun Drub states outright "she is called here 'Tārā Who Proclaims the Sound HŪM'"; Tenzin Dhonzang's heading names her "Sound-Roarer" (sgra sgrogs).

— attested (epithet): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-1` (3 commentaries)

Only Anon-Trinle-Char supplies full iconographic detail for this tradition: yellow body, seated in vajra posture on a moon seat, one face, two arms, right hand in refuge-granting mudrā, left holding a yellow lotus — corroborated by the same commentary's own citation of a verse it attributes to Nyima Sbaspa ("Upon the moon, in vajra [posture], pressing down with her feet; forming the refuge-granting [mudrā] and holding a golden lotus stem").

— attested (full iconography, 1 commentary): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-12`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-14`

Anon-Trinle-Char additionally characterises this deity's mind as "moistened with non-referential compassion" yet bearing "a slightly wrathful cast" — a nuance not echoed elsewhere in the corpus. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-4`

**Position 2 — Kurukullā, "Goddess Who Draws [All] under Her Power" / "Vidyā Goddess" (red; archer or half-cross-legged posture; utpala topped by a drawn bow and arrow).** The epithet is attested by three commentaries: Palden Sherab identifies her as "Goddess Who Draws [All] under Her Power, Kurukullā" (dbang sdud rigs byed lha mo) and, in its etymological gloss, as "red Kurukullā"; Sangye Nyentrul identifies her as "this 'Goddess of Knowledge Who Subjugates' [with a] red body"; Tsultrim Namdak identifies her as the red-hued "Dbang-sdud rig-byed lha-mo" ("Power-Gathering Vidyā Goddess").

— attested (epithet and colour): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-0-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-8` (3 commentaries)

Full iconographic detail — red body, seated half-cross-legged/in archer posture, one face, two hands, right in supreme-giving mudrā, left holding an utpala topped by a drawn bow and arrow — is given independently by two commentaries: Palden Sherab's generation-stage visualization and Sangye Nyentrul's iconography claim correspond closely (re-read and confirmed as genuinely close, not merely topical overlap): "atop the pollen-centre of an utpala, [she is] holding a drawn bow" (Palden Sherab) against "left holding an utpala atop which is a drawn bow and arrow" (Sangye Nyentrul).

— attested (full iconography, 2 commentaries): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-1-2`

Sangye Nyentrul alone adds a practice instruction that light rays radiate from the bow and arrow. `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-1-3`

**No commentary in this corpus reconciles the two traditions** or states that one supersedes the other for this homage.

### Unique
Sungrab Tulku glosses "homage" as bowing to "venerable Tārā, the goddess who generates the [mantra] families" (rigs byed lha mo) — a name sharing the root term "rigs/rig byed" with Position 2's epithet, but the claim itself gives no colour, posture, or implement, so it is logged here rather than counted toward Position 2's attestation. `sungrab-tulku:c-8-3`

## Mantra semantics: TUTTĀRA HŪM and the three realms

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries state, in some form, that the mantra-syllables TUTTĀRA/TUTTĀRE and HŪM — through their light-rays (a speech-aspect) and/or the HŪM-syllable's symbolism of emptiness-compassion (a mind-aspect) — pervade or fill the three realms: the desire realm ('dod khams), the form realm, and the formless realm.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-28`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-29`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-22`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`, `taranatha:c-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-17` (16 commentaries)

Note on the base-tier citations for two of these sixteen: Drakpa Gyaltsen's `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-28` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-29` establish that the verse's mantra-syllables and their light/power are doing the "filling" (naming the mantra, then attributing the fill to "the power and light-rays of those mantra letters"), but neither claim by itself names the three realms — that specific content is in the adjacent `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-30`, cited in the sub-list below. Likewise Anon-Utpala's `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3` and `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4` establish the speech-aspect (light-rays from the mantra-garland) and mind-aspect (the HAṂ syllable marking emptiness-compassion) of the fill, without themselves naming the three realms — that content is in `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5` through `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-7`, cited in the sub-list below. In both cases the fuller three-realms content is genuinely attested by the same commentary, just in a sibling claim rather than the one cited here.

Ten of these give the further specific word-gloss that "direction" (phyogs) denotes the form realm and "space" (nam mkha') denotes the formless realm:

— attested (specific phyogs=form / nam mkha'=formless mapping): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-7`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-30`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-17` (10 commentaries)

Tenga Tulku's own gloss adds a numeric elaboration not found elsewhere in the corpus: it specifies the form realm as having "seventeen abodes" (gnas rigs bcu bdun). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`

Anon-Utpala's word-gloss chain (already cited above) is the only one in the corpus that explicitly narrates the mantra's fivefold-plus-two-realms structure as itself "completing the seven" — see "Enumerating 'the seven worlds,'" below, where the same claims are cited again for that count. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-7`

### ⚑ Divergences
Tāranātha alone maps the verse's three terms differently from the ten-commentary standard mapping above: "space" (nam mkha') is glossed as encompassing *both* the form and formless realms' abodes together, while "direction" (phyogs) is glossed not as the form realm specifically but as "all other world-realms of the ten directions" — a reading that folds the standard form/formless distinction into "space" and reassigns "direction" to a cosmological-geographic sense rather than a realm-of-existence sense. `taranatha:c-5-2`

### Unique
Pema Namgyal frames the same mantra-fills-three-realms content within an added ritual/liturgical frame: relying on "the deity-mantra samādhi of red Tārā," activated through the syllables OṂ TUTTĀRE HŪM. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-22`

Two commentaries in the packet prefix the mantra with OṂ, against the OṂ-less form given in the base-text quotations cited elsewhere on this page: Pema Namgyal here, and Tenzin Dhonzang, whose etymological aside on "homage" also quotes the mantra as "ༀ་ཏུ་ཏྟཱ་ར་ཧུཾ" (OṂ TUTTĀRE HŪM) in the course of describing a rain of nectar falling from it (see "Unique elaborations: benefits, etymology, and practice," below). `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-22`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3`

## The "three worlds" vs. "seven worlds" variant

### Consensus (base-text reading)
Fifteen of the sixteen commentaries quote or paraphrase the verse's second couplet with "seven worlds" ('jig rten bdun po) pressed beneath her feet, without discussing a textual variant.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-2`, `taranatha:c-5-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-16` (15 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position 1 (Anon-Trinle-Char) — "three worlds" is the philologically correct reading; "seven" is a scribal corruption.** Anon-Trinle-Char's own root-verse quotation, given explicitly "from the tantra" (rgyud las), and its subsequent gloss and activity-description, all read "three worlds" ('jig rten gsum po) throughout. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-5`

The same commentary then mounts an extended textual-critical argument: it first notes that Tibetan Tārā image-captions using "seven worlds" rest on "many mutually inconsistent explanations" that circulate "without clear source" (three specific folk explanations are surveyed and rejected) and concludes this "appears to be a scribal error" (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-6`). It then identifies its source for "three worlds" as the master Nyima Sbaspa — "directly accepted [as a disciple] by the Venerable Lady herself" (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-7`) — and gives three separate citations from Nyima Sbaspa's own sādhana and Praise of Tārā, all reading "three worlds" (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-8`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-9`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-10`). It concludes that because these three citations, early and late, consistently say "three worlds" and "seven" does not occur in any of them, "three worlds" is "far more tenable," and that no other sūtra or treatise besides this praise reads "seven," nor has any commentator shown a source for it (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-11`). The same commentary raises, without resolving, a further doctrinal puzzle this reading does not by itself settle: given that Nyima Sbaspa describes her as seated in vajra posture, how a seated deity presses the worlds "underfoot" should be examined. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-13`

**Position 2 (Konchok Thabkhe) — "three worlds" is an independently-attested, non-corrupt variant, without adopting it over "seven" for this verse.** Konchok Thabkhe's own root-verse quotation in this packet retains "seven worlds" (cited under Consensus above, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`). Separately, it notes that a mahāsiddha's own treatise quotes "the three worlds pressed by her feet," and — since there is no pattern of confusing "three" with "seven" — concludes this is "evidently not a corrupted reading, nor a scribal error" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-4`). It then quotes the mahāsiddha's verse in full and notes that the phrase "subduing the three" is itself commentarial on this point, "indicating variant readings among the Sanskrit exemplars of the tantra" (`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-5`). Read on its own terms, this defends "three worlds" as a legitimate parallel transmission elsewhere in the tradition, rather than arguing (as Anon-Trinle-Char does) that "seven" is itself an error in *this* verse.

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## Enumerating "the seven worlds"

### Consensus
None — three distinct, mutually incompatible enumerations of "the seven worlds" are attested when "seven" is read (a fourth, tantric enumeration is treated separately under "Esoteric completion-stage reading," below, since it belongs to a different hermeneutical register).

### ⚑ Divergences
**Position (a) — the five classes of desire-realm migrators plus the form and formless realms, making seven.** Attested by five commentaries: Drakpa Gyaltsen ("the five classes of beings of the desire realm, the form realm as the sixth, and the formless realm as the seventh"); Karma Maitri ("the five classes of migrating beings plus the two upper realms"); Gendun Drub ("the three lower rebirths, [plus] desire-realm gods and humans... [plus] the form and formless realms" as the seven worlds); Tenga Tulku ("the three lower realms of the desire realm; the abodes of gods and humans, five abodes in all; and above these the form realm and formless realm — seven in all"); Tenzin Dhonzang ("hell-beings, hungry ghosts, and animals... together with the five continua of beings of the desire realm, humans and gods... 'the directions'... the form realm; and 'space'... the formless realm").

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-4` (5 commentaries)

Anon-Utpala's word-gloss chain builds the same structure (desire realm's five classes, plus form realm, plus formless realm "completing the seven") without stating the ordinal count in one place; it is logged here as a sixth, structurally-equivalent attestation. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-7`

**Position (b) — the six classes of migrating beings plus the intermediate state (bardo), making seven.** Attested by two commentaries in their own voice: Konchok Thabkhe ("the six rebirth classes together with the intermediate state"); Tenzin Dhonzang, offering this as an explicit alternative ("yang na," "alternatively") to its own Position (a) gloss above. Pema Namgyal independently attests the same six-classes-plus-bardo structure while describing the activity of pressing/summoning.

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-7`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23` (3 commentaries)

**Position (c) — seven classes of beings (nāga, preta, asura, human, vidyādhara, kinnara, deva).** Attested by three commentaries giving closely corresponding sevenfold lists of beings (rather than realms): Tāranātha ("nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras, and the world of the devas"); Sangye Nyentrul ("nāgas, pretas, asuras, vidyādharas, humans, kinnaras, and devas"); Tsultrim Namdak ("the worlds of nāgas, pretas, gods, asuras, humans, and vidyādhara-kinnaras and gods" — re-read and confirmed as the same seven-class set, with "gods" appearing twice in this claim's own wording).

— attested: `taranatha:c-5-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18` (3 commentaries)

**Meta-attestations — claims that report more than one of the above positions without adjudicating between them.** Two claims in the packet are themselves internally flagged (⚑) as recording multiple positions; per Rule 11 they are logged here rather than split across the position-bullets above, since each documents competing views rather than taking a side:

- Gendun Gyatso's own commentary (`gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-3`) reports two explanations: one attributed to "Jetsün Drakpa Gyaltsen" — the five classes of desire-realm beings plus the two higher realms (structurally equivalent to Position (a), and independently corroborated by Drakpa Gyaltsen's own commentary, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`, above) — and an anonymous "some say" position: the six classes of beings plus one further, unspecified item, making seven. The packet's own English gloss for this second position leaves the seventh item unspecified; it is not identified here as "bardo," since the source claim itself does not name it.
- Palden Sherab's own commentary (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-3`) reports three named positions without adjudicating: Drakpa Gyaltsen's (matching Position (a), corroborated by `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-31`); Tāranātha's (matching Position (c) — "nāgas, pretas, asuras, vidyādharas, humans, kinnaras, and gods" — independently corroborated by Tāranātha's own commentary, `taranatha:c-5-6`, above, which gives the same seven classes, though in a different sequence: Tāranātha's own list orders them "nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras, and devas," with human and vidyādhara transposed relative to Palden Sherab's report of the position); and Gendun Gyatso's ("the six classes of migrating beings together with the intermediate state [bardo]" — structurally matching Position (b), though this specific "bardo" identification of the seventh item is not confirmed by Gendun Gyatso's own commentary in this packet, which leaves the item unspecified, as noted above).

### Unique
Tāranātha, before giving its own Position (c) list, separately notes that "many differing explanations" of "seven worlds" exist — categorised as "seven vessels [environments]," "seven contents [inhabitants]," or "seven combined vessel-and-content" — a different taxonomy of alternate explanations than Anon-Trinle-Char's survey of Tibetan image-caption folk explanations (cited under "The 'three worlds' vs. 'seven worlds' variant," above); the two meta-notes are not merged, since they name different sets of alternatives. `taranatha:c-5-5`

## Activity: pressing the worlds, summoning all beings without exception

### Consensus
Sixteen commentaries state that, by pressing/treading the worlds underfoot, she is able to draw or summon all beings dwelling in them under her power, without exception (lus pa med par 'gugs par nus ma).

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-32`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-33`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-5-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-6`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-5-7`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-5-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-23`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-5`, `taranatha:c-5-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18` (16 commentaries)

Anon-Trinle-Char's attestation uses its own "three worlds" wording (see "The 'three worlds' vs. 'seven worlds' variant," above) rather than "seven"; the shared activity-structure — pressing underfoot, summoning without exception — is otherwise the same across both readings.

Drakpa Gyaltsen's word-gloss adds a specific example not found elsewhere: "able to summon" is glossed as drawing "kings and others" under her power. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-33`

Tenga Tulku's word-gloss adds a poetic elaboration unique to this commentary: the "two feet" that press the worlds are identified as "the foot of seeing emptiness" and "the foot of not abandoning others through compassion." `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-4`

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the core activity-structure (pressing + summoning without exception) — but see Unique, below, for a distinct interpretive nuance on what "pressing" means, offered by one commentary without contradicting the consensus reading elsewhere in the same commentary.

### Unique
**Tenzin Dhonzang alone reframes "pressing with her feet" as compassionate, non-abandoning embrace rather than domination.** In this same commentary's word-gloss (distinct from the standard "summon without exception" content it also attests at `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-7`, cited under Consensus above), pressing the worlds with her feet is glossed as "she holds them firmly, taking beings under her care with unwavering great compassion... these seven worlds are not cast aside or abandoned, but fully embraced with great love." It supports this reading with a quoted verse from a "Third Chapter" source: "When this field is abandoned by others, protector, what use is there in your praising it as like a white lotus?" No other commentary in the corpus offers this reframing; all others that describe the activity in interpretive terms (rather than bare paraphrase) describe it as subjugation. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-6`

## Esoteric completion-stage reading: TUTTĀRA HŪM as tummo

### Consensus
Two commentaries — Tāranātha and Palden Sherab — independently give a closely corresponding "hidden meaning" (sbas don) / completion-stage-with-marks reading of the verse as caṇḍālī (tummo) practice. Re-read and confirmed as genuinely close, not merely topical overlap, on each of the following points:

- **TUTTĀRA = the fire of tummo.** Near-verbatim: "ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ནི་གདུང་པ་སྟེ། གཏུམ་མོའི་མེས་གདུང་བའོ" (Tāranātha) against "ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་ནི་གདུང་བ་སྟེ་གཏུམ་མོའི་མེའོ" (Palden Sherab). — `taranatha:c-5-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`
- **HŪM as "indestructible" (mi shigs pa).** Both commentaries use this shared term, though each supplies a different further gloss for it: Tāranātha identifies HŪM as "the indestructible sound, arisen from tummo"; Palden Sherab identifies it as "the unchanging, indestructible, i.e. great bliss." The shared core term is attested by both; the further elaboration (sound-origin vs. bliss-identity) is not identical and is not merged. — `taranatha:c-5-8`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`
- **The three realm-terms as subtle-body correspondences.** Closely corresponding: "'desire' is the secret cakra; 'directions' is the central channel, filled by that sound; 'filling space' is the inexpressible" (Tāranātha) against "'desire' is the secret cakra... 'directions' is the central channel... filled inwardly with bliss... 'filling space'... is unutterable co-emergent wisdom" (Palden Sherab). — `taranatha:c-5-9`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`
- **The seven worlds = the five channel-cakras plus the fire- and wind-cakras.** Near-identical: "the 'seven worlds' are the five channel-cakras plus the two cakras of fire and wind" (Tāranātha) against "the seven worlds are the five channel-cakras and the two cakras of fire and wind" (Palden Sherab). — `taranatha:c-5-10`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`
- **The practice draws everything from the secret place up to the crown.** Corresponding: "all of that, without exception, is again drawn upward from the secret place to the crown of the head" (Tāranātha) against "drawing it again from the secret place up to the crown" (Palden Sherab). — `taranatha:c-5-11`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-2`

— attested (this esoteric reading as a whole): `taranatha:c-5-7`, `taranatha:c-5-8`, `taranatha:c-5-9`, `taranatha:c-5-10`, `taranatha:c-5-11`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-3-2` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None between the two commentaries above.

### Unique
**Palden Sherab's four-tier hermeneutic.** Beyond the word-meaning (cited under "Deity identity and iconography," above) and the completion-stage-with-marks reading shared with Tāranātha above, Palden Sherab gives two further tiers not found elsewhere in the corpus for this verse:
- Generation-stage visualization (also cited under "Deity identity and iconography," above, for its iconographic content): red Kurukullā on lotus-and-moon, holding a drawn bow atop an utpala, blazing light subduing the arrogance of all seven worlds, who then place the dust of her feet upon their crowns. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-2-1`
- Completion-stage-without-marks (the ultimate meaning): TUTTĀRA HŪM as the abiding-nature dharmadhātu bodhicitta, self-arisen awareness-wisdom; the five sense-doors, mental consciousness together with the afflicted mind, and the ālaya-consciousness successively self-liberate into dharmadhātu (glossed respectively as "treading" the desire, form, and formless realms underfoot); this culminates in identification with the great mother Samantabhadrī, "Jetsün Ārya Tārā, Goddess Who Draws [All] under Her Power." Tāranātha's version in this packet does not include a parallel fourth, "ultimate" dissolution-into-dharmadhātu tier. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-4-2`

## Unique elaborations: benefits, etymology, and practice

**Even the great gods can be summoned as servants.** Tāranātha: "if there is the power to summon even great gods such as Maheśvara as servants, what need is there to mention lesser ones?" `taranatha:c-5-4`

**Etymology of "homage" as extending to "offering," with a nectar-rain image.** Tenzin Dhonzang: "homage" (phyag 'tshal) extends to offering (mchod pa, Skt. pūjā, "pleasing an object"), itself of two kinds (material offering, further subdivided into offering of possessions and offering of respect, illustrated with examples; and offering through practice); from the mantra OṂ TUTTĀRE HŪM "a rain of nectar falls, the letters themselves roaring their own sound." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3`

**Five-coloured light rays accomplishing the enlightened activities.** Tenzin Dhonzang identifies this deity's chief enlightened activity as magnetizing (dbang du sdud pa), and gives a detailed generation-stage practice: white, yellow, red, dark-blue, and green light-ray masses radiate in turn from the heart mantra-wheel, each accomplishing a distinct activity (pacifying illness/obscurations/obstacles to life; increasing life/merit/wisdom; magnetizing the three realms; wrathful/forceful actions; and the "whole variety" of activities generally). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-8`

**Supporting citation from the "Legdrima."** Tenzin Dhonzang continues the same practice with a further light-ray mass ("liver-brown," stabilizing the power of activities and attainments) and quotes the "Legdrima" praise on the four unceasing enlightened activities (pacifying, increasing, magnetizing, wrathful) proceeding "like the waves of an ocean." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-9`

**Benefit: drawing beings toward higher rebirth and liberation.** Tenga Tulku: she possesses the quality of being able to summon, without exception, all beings of saṃsāra "away from the lower realms toward higher rebirth and the path of liberation." `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-5`

**Identity epithet echoing, but not confirmed as, the Kurukullā tradition.** Sungrab Tulku's epithet "goddess who generates the mantra families" (rigs byed lha mo) is cited and discussed under "Deity identity and iconography," above, where it is logged as a partial terminological echo of Position 2 rather than a full attestation, since no colour or posture is given. `sungrab-tulku:c-8-3`
