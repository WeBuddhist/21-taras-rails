# Tārā 12 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུས་དབུ་རྒྱན། / Homage to She Whose Crown Is Adorned by the Crescent Moon

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but not all mark it the same way. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the twelfth" (བཅུ་གཉིས་པ) or its numeral (12): `yama-sonam:c-3-12-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1` (node title "1.12 — བཅུ་གཉིས་པ་ནི"), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-1` ("བཅུ་གཉིས་པ་ནི"), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-1` (marked "༼༡༢༽"), `taranatha:c-12-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-1`. Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the fifth" (ལྔ་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the fifth member of a "praise by way of the crown-ornament" (དབུ་རྒྱན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1`. `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1` and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-1` are character-identical (ལྔ་པ་དབུ་རྒྱན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི།); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1` abbreviates to bare "ལྔ་པ་ནི།" without repeating the "crown-ornament" phrase; `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-1` instead frames the same fifth position via "རིགས་བདག་གིས་དབུ་བརྒྱན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས" ("by way of her head being adorned by the family-lord"), naming the family-lord rather than the crown-ornament as such. `sungrab-tulku:c-15-1` uses the identical "crown-ornament" phrasing (དབུ་རྒྱན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) shared by dharmabhadra/gendun-drub, but pairs it with the global numeral "(12)" rather than "the fifth." `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-1` combines both classifying terms under the global numeral: "twelfth, homage by way of the crown-ornament and the family-lord" (དབུ་རྒྱན་དང་རིགས་བདག་གི་སྒོ་ནས). The remaining four (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no ordinal marker within the claims themselves in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`, each flagged ⓘ in the packet) or by the node's own title numeral rather than a claim's quoted text (`sangye-nyentrul`, whose node title carries "༡༢" but whose own claims do not quote an ordinal).

Ten commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines, either as one continuous block or split across two or more adjacent claims:

— full verse quoted attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-41`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-68`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-69`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-70`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-71`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3` (10 commentaries)

`drakpa-gyaltsen`'s four claims quote and gloss one line each in sequence (c-1-68 line 1, c-1-69 line 2, c-1-70 line 3, c-1-71 line 4), together covering the full verse; `lobsang-dawa` similarly splits the verse across two claims labelled "Verse 27" (lines 1–2) and "Verse 28" (lines 3–4) — an internal verse-numbering convention of that commentary's own extraction, not evidence of disagreement about which homage this is. These quotations are close but not identical across commentaries: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-0-2` reads line 3 as རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ན (locative ན), matching most others, while `yama-sonam:c-3-12-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-2`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-41` read རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ནས (ablative ནས). [Ed: corrected 2026-08-12 — the grouping was previously reversed for yama-sonam; verified against 2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/yama-sonam.md line 1205.]

Six commentaries give only a partial, paraphrastic, or piecemeal quotation rather than a clean four-line block: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1`, `karma-maitri` (structural opener only, no block quote), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-2`–`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-3` (glossed piecemeal), `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1` (paraphrastic "ཡུལ་གང་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ན" gloss, no block quote), `taranatha:c-12-3`–`taranatha:c-12-9` (glossed line-by-line without one continuous quotation), and `pema-namgyal` (does not quote the verse in this packet at all, being routed here by claim-content match).

`yama-sonam` additionally preserves a textual-variant discussion not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-8` introduces a comparison of discrepant exemplars, citing the Dergé print Tantra volume Ca; `yama-sonam:c-3-12-9` quotes that print's reading "ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཟླ་བའི་རྩེ་མོས་དབུ་བརྒྱན།" ("crowned by the *tip* of the moon," རྩེ་མོ rather than དུམ་བུ "crescent"); `yama-sonam:c-3-12-10` quotes a further Dergé Tengyur reading ("ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུས་") and notes an internal early/later mismatch within that same print; `yama-sonam:c-3-12-11` quotes Jonangpa's detailed exposition reading "རྟག་ཏུ་རྒྱུན་མི་འཆད་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་འོད་རབ་བྱེད་མ" ("who constantly, without interruption, makes light thoroughly radiate") for the verse's final line. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-2` reads དབུ་བརྒྱན (rather than དབུ་རྒྱན) for line 1, matching the "moon-tip" print's ornament-verb rather than yama-sonam's own preferred reading — though `gendun-drub` does not itself flag this as a variant.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as disagreement about which verse this is — the ordinal-marker and quotation differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad and different exemplars, not disagreement about content or placement.

### Unique
None beyond the structural and textual-variant notes above.

## Name, epithet, and classification

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name for this homage gives one from a small set of divergent naming traditions; no single name is shared by a majority.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming traditions are attested, and one commentary (`yama-sonam`) explicitly catalogues three of them as belonging to named authorities:

1. **"Bestower of All Auspiciousness"** (བཀྲིས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྦྱིན་མ) — `yama-sonam:c-3-12-1` gives this as the section's own heading name. `yama-sonam:c-3-12-12` itself attributes this exact name to a named authority: "ཉི་སྦས་ཀྱིས་བཀྲིས་ཐམས་ཅད་སྦྱིན་མ" ("Nyisbepa calls her 'Bestower of All Auspiciousness'"). One commentary.
2. **"Source of Auspiciousness"** (བཀྲ་ཤིས་འབྱུང་གནས་མ) — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-1` gives this as its own heading name. Re-reading `yama-sonam:c-3-12-12`'s catalogue confirms a matching (spelling-variant) attribution: "ཅོ་ནེས་བཀྲིས་འབྱུང་གནས་མ" ("Co ne pa calls her 'Source of Auspiciousness'" — བཀྲིས vs tenzin-dhonzang's བཀྲ་ཤིས is the same word, differently spelled). `yama-sonam` does not itself identify tenzin-dhonzang with "Co ne pa," so this is recorded as a matching *name*, not a confirmed identity between the two commentaries.
3. **"Tārā of Auspicious Appearance"** (བཀྲ་ཤིས་སྣང་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-6` gives this as an epithet ("འདི་ལ་བཀྲ་ཤིས་སྣང་བའི་སྒྲོལ་(,མ་)ཞེས་འབྱུང་ངོ་"). Re-reading `yama-sonam:c-3-12-12`'s catalogue confirms a matching (spelling-variant) attribution: "རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ་པས་བཀྲིས་སྣང་བའི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་གསུངས" ("Rje Dge 'dun grub calls her 'Tārā, Radiance/Appearance of Auspiciousness'") — the named authority "Rje Dge 'dun grub" ("Lord Gendün Drub") shares its name with the registered commentator `gendun-drub`, and the epithet `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-6` gives matches what `yama-sonam` attributes to that name almost exactly (བཀྲིས vs བཀྲ་ཤིས spelling only). `yama-sonam` does not itself state that this "Rje Dge 'dun grub" is the author of the `gendun-drub` commentary in this corpus; the match is recorded as a naming correspondence, not a confirmed authorial identity.
4. **"Auspicious/Benefit-Accomplishing Tārā"** (བཀྲ་ཤིས་དོན་བྱེད/གྲུབ་སྒྲོལ་མ) — three commentaries share this naming family with a documented lexical variant: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-0-1` uses བཀྲ་ཤིས་དོན་བྱེད་སྒྲོལ་མ ("Auspicious, Benefit-*Accomplishing* Tārā," verb དོན་བྱེད, "who brings rain, harvest, and peace to the land"); `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-1` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-15` both instead use བཀྲ་ཤིས་དོན་གྲུབ(་མ) ("Auspicious, Benefit-*Accomplished* Tārā," verb དོན་གྲུབ) — a different root verb (བྱེད "to do/make" vs གྲུབ "to be accomplished/attained), not a spelling variant of the same word. `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-1`'s own quotation reads "འཕགས་མ་བཀྲ་ཤིས་དོན་གྲུབ" and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-15`'s own quotation reads "སྒྲོལ་མ་བཀྲ་ཤིས་དོན་གྲུབ་མ" — these two are character-identical on the shared root; `palden-sherab`'s "དོན་བྱེད" is a genuine, if closely related, lexical divergence from both. Three commentaries, two sub-readings (2 vs 1).

The thematic/functional classifications "praise by way of the crown-ornament" and "praise by way of the crown-ornament and the family-lord" are recorded above under Structural placement, not repeated here, since they classify the verse's topic rather than name the goddess.

The remaining commentaries (`dharmabhadra`, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `sungrab-tulku`) supply no proper name for this homage within the claims in this packet, beyond the generic identification "Tārā" itself (see Unique, below, for `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-2` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-6`, which do only this).

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-3` additionally glosses the word "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself etymologically — as a request for the blessing that erroneous cognition, up through the subtlety of dual appearance across the three tantric stages of appearance/increase/attainment, be easily ceased, and its unerroneous counterpart easily arise. This etymology is not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-6` through `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-12` form a single extended digression not paralleled by any other commentary: a quotation from a text called "Stacked Auspiciousness" (བཀྲ་ཤིས་བརྩིགས་པ) associating auspicious symbols with the crown, body, speech, mind, tongue, throat, hand, feet, and the eight auspicious substances (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-6`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-8`), followed by the author's own doctrinal aside that eight-goddess sets across several tantras (Cakrasaṃvara's gate-goddesses, Guhyasamāja's consort-bodhisattvas, Bhairava's wrathful deities, the eight Tathāgatas/Bodhisattvas/Bodhisattva-consorts) are not different in essence from the eight auspicious goddesses (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-9`), and a description of the specific benefits granted by the Wheel, Victory-Banner, Parasol, Glorious-Knot, Lotus, Vase, Golden-Fish, and Conch goddesses, closing with a note that supplicating "this goddess" at the outset of an undertaking, timed to one's birth-star, grants uncommon auspicious activity (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-10`–`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-12`). This entire passage elaborates the general theme of "auspiciousness" carried by this Tārā's own name (see Divergences, above) but is not itself a claim about the root verse's crown-ornament or Amitābda-in-locks content, and is not corroborated by any other commentary in the packet.

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-2` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-6` each close their respective treatments with a bare identification — "this is Tārā" (སྒྲོལ་མ་ལའོ) — without any distinguishing epithet; these are logged here rather than counted among the four naming traditions above, since they identify the subject only as "Tārā" generically.

## Iconography — the crescent-moon crown

### Consensus
Fifteen commentaries (18 claims) describe the same basic image: her crown (or the crown of her head/topknot) is adorned by a crescent moon, glossed as a half-moon, from which — together with her other ornaments — light blazes forth. Two further details recur but are not shared by the whole list, so are not folded into this statement: only six of the eighteen claims specify which lunar day's moon — five gloss it as the first day (ཚེས་གཅིག): `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-68`; `yama-sonam:c-3-12-4` alone glosses it as the *third* day (ཚེས་གསུམ) instead — the remaining claims give no day-number at all. And five of the eighteen further specify both the light's colour (white) and its effect (dispelling suffering) together: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-3` — this reading, and the other specific-effect readings alongside it, are given in full under ⚑ Divergences below, which is why several of the same claim IDs recur on both sides here.

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-68`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-69`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-36`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-3`, `taranatha:c-12-3`, `taranatha:c-12-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-42` (18 claims from 15 commentaries — `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-1` also touch this element but are counted separately below because their own claims bundle it with other content)

### ⚑ Divergences
The commentaries diverge on what precisely the crown-light's blazing accomplishes:
- **Dispels suffering / affliction generally**: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3` ("radiates suffering-dispelling white light"), `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-3` ("radiates light that utterly dispels suffering"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1` ("dazzling white light rays that dispel accumulations of suffering"), `sungrab-tulku:c-15-3` ("white light-rays that dispel all suffering"), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-3` ("suffering-dispelling white light-rays"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-42` ("dispels the suffering of all beings"). Six commentaries.
- **Cools the heat of affliction specifically** (a narrower framing than generic "suffering"): `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3` ("protects all beings tormented by the heat of the afflictions... granting them a cooling thing") — unique to this commentary; no other claim in the packet uses this heat/cooling framing.
- **Destroys the pride of wealth-lords / worldly gods**: `yama-sonam` does not use this framing for the crown-light specifically (its own reading is the "auspicious excellences" one below); this pride-destruction theme is instead attested by `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1` (which combines both suffering-dispelling and pride-destroying in the same claim), `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4`, and `taranatha:c-12-5` — treated fully under "Fruits of the light," below, since it is the more distinctive and better-attested claim about this element.
- **Causes desired auspicious excellences to come like the waxing moon**, when practitioners supplicate with an undefiled mind: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-4`, corroborated by a quotation from Nyima Sbaspa at `yama-sonam:c-3-12-5` ("to whomever supplicates [her], adorned by the crescent moon, she causes auspiciousness and virtuous excellence to increase"). Unique to `yama-sonam` among the claims in this packet (no other commentary cites this specific "waxing moon" simile).

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-3` states only that her nature "blazes with white light, more brilliantly than all her other ornaments," without naming a specific effect of the light; it is counted in the Consensus list above for the shared crown-blazing image but not claimed for any specific-effect Divergence.

### Unique
`gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1` bundles the crescent-moon crown together with a gloss identifying it as (or associating it with) the syllable TAṂ ("ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུས་ཏཾ་ཟླ་བ་ཕྱེད་པས་དབུ་བརྒྱན་ཅིང") — a seed-syllable identification not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-1`'s own structural claim names the topic as "crown-ornament and family-lord" together (see Structural placement, above); its content-bearing gloss of the crown-ornament proper is `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-2`, already counted in Consensus.

## Iconography — Amitābha amid her matted locks

### Consensus
Twelve commentaries (15 claims) describe the same basic image: (the Buddha) Amitābha dwells or abides amid her matted locks — several specify them as black, glossy, or bee-black — from whom light radiates; every one of the twelve attests the "constantly" (རྟག་) element in at least one of its cited claims. Two further elaborations recur but are not shared by the whole list, so are not folded into this statement: the epithet "family-lord" (རིགས་བདག) is used explicitly by six of the twelve — `yama-sonam:c-3-12-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5` — while the rest simply name Amitābha without the epithet; and the light's purpose is stated as "for the sake/welfare of beings" by seven of the twelve — `yama-sonam:c-3-12-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-71`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5` — while the rest state only that light radiates, without specifying its purpose.

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-70`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-71`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4`, `taranatha:c-12-7`, `taranatha:c-12-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-43` (15 claims from 12 commentaries — `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1`/`gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2`, and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-37` also describe this element but are treated separately below because their claims add distinctive content beyond the shared image)

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries specify that Amitābha is manifesting in a particular bodily form, using near-identical phrasing:
- `taranatha:c-12-6` ("ལན་འགའ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་སྐུར་བསྟན་ནས," "sometimes manifesting in the form of an ascetic") and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2` ("རེས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་སྐུར་བསྟན་ནས," "at times manifesting in the form of an ascetic") share the clause "དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་སྐུར་བསྟན་ནས" (manifesting in the form of an ascetic) character-for-character, differing only in the opening adverb (ལན་འགའ vs རེས, both meaning "sometimes/at times"). Two commentaries, independently converging on the same detail.
- No other commentary in the packet names a specific manifest form for Amitābha here; the remaining fourteen simply state that he "dwells" or "abides" without specifying a bodily aspect.
- **Symbolizes the gathering of boundless merit** through making offerings and supplications to the goddess: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-6` (already cited above in Consensus for the shared dwelling-in-locks image), corroborated by a quotation from Nyima Sbaspa at `yama-sonam:c-3-12-7` ("Meditate that, since light streams from Amitābha, she causes a blazing mass of merit-light."). Unique to `yama-sonam` among the claims in this packet — no other commentary states this specific merit-gathering symbolism for the Amitābha-in-locks element; it parallels the same commentary's "waxing moon" symbolism for the crown-light (see "Iconography — the crescent-moon crown," above).

### Unique
`gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1` identifies Amitābha as family-lord dwelling amid her locks in the same claim that describes the crescent-moon crown (see above); `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-2` separately states that "from that time on" she causes light to radiate continuously "for the sake of beings' welfare" — consistent with, but not adding to, the shared Consensus image.

`sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2` names Amitābha as dwelling "in a blissful realm" (བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང), i.e. Sukhāvatī, and describes the light he emits as specifically "the light of immeasurable compassion utterly free of the three-spheres' conceptualization" (ཤིན་ཏུ་འཁོར་གསུམ་དམིགས་པ་བྲལ་བའི་ཚད་མེད་པའི་སྙིང་རྗེའི་འོད་ཟེར) — a doctrinally more specific characterization (compassion free of subject-object-action conceptualization) not paralleled elsewhere in the packet, though `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` independently also names Amitābha as "protector of the western pure land of Sukhāvatī" (བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང་གི་མགོན་པོ) and also calls the light "the light of loving compassion" (བརྩེ་བའི་སྙིང་རྗེའི་འོད་ཟེར) — a thematic overlap (Sukhāvatī + compassion-light) between `sangye-nyentrul` and `sungrab-tulku`, though the two do not share a verbatim formula and are recorded as independent, not corroborating, attestations.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-37` uniquely specifies the light's effect as increasing "longevity, merit, good qualities, majesty, realization, and all activities toward disciples" — see "Fruits of the light," below.

## Fruits of the light: pride-destruction, prosperity, and longevity

### Consensus
Beyond the generic "for the sake/welfare of beings" language already recorded under the two iconography facets above, several commentaries specify a distinctive fruit of the light. The most widely and most verbally consistently attested is the destruction of the pride of great wealth-lords or worldly gods:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4`, `taranatha:c-12-5` (4 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-12-5` ("ཕྱུག་བདག་ཆེན་པོ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྱག་བརྒྱའི་དྲེགས་པ་འཇོམས་པ་མོ") and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1` (which contains, within a longer passage, "ཕྱུག་བདག་ཆེན་པོ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བརྒྱའི་དྲེགས་པ་འཇོམས་པ") share the specific formula "hundred million great wealth-lords' pride, destroyed" (ཕྱུག་བདག་ཆེན་པོ་བྱེ་བ...བརྒྱའི་དྲེགས་པ་འཇོམས་པ) almost character-for-character, differing only in a single syllable (taranatha's ཕྱག་བརྒྱ vs palden-sherab's ཕྲག་བརྒྱ, both idiomatic for "hundred"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-1` gives the same theme in looser language ("འཇིག་རྟེན་ན་ཆེ་བར་གྲགས་པའི་ཕྱག་བདག་ཡོངས་ཀྱི་ང་རྒྱལ་དང་དྲེགས་པ་འཇོམས", "destroys the pride and arrogance of all who are renowned as great lords in the world") without the specific "hundred million" numeral. `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` narrows the target to a named deity instead of an unspecified class of wealth-lords: "the worldly god Īśvara and others most renowned in the world" (འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་དབང་ཕྱུག་སོགས). All four share the pride-destruction theme; only `taranatha` and `palden-sherab` share the specific "hundred million wealth-lords" formula.

### ⚑ Divergences
Alongside pride-destruction, at least two further distinctive fruits are attested, not contradicting the pride-destruction theme but representing additional or alternative emphases within individual commentaries:
- **Rain of nectar/prosperity for crops and medicinal herbs**: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-1` (in the same claim as its pride-destruction content: "a rain of nectar falls, causing medicinal herbs and crops to flourish") and, independently, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-15` ("from the light-rays of her crescent-moon crown falls a rain of nectar that makes trees, crops, and flowers flourish"). Two commentaries.
- **Increases longevity, merit, and qualities**: `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-37` uniquely states the light "causes longevity, merit, good qualities, majesty, realization, and all activities toward disciples to flourish" — not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.
- **Rains down auspicious substances/whatever is desired, subduing negative interdependence**: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2` (Amitābha's compassion-light "raining down auspicious substances and signs, subduing all negative interdependence") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2` (Amitābha's compassion-light "raining down all that is desired, dispelling every fault of the animate and inanimate world") and `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` (the same claim already cited above in Consensus for its Īśvara pride-destruction content; Amitābha's compassion-light also performs "the activity of raining down everything desired"). Three commentaries share this "rains down what is desired / subdues faults of the animate-inanimate world" theme, attached to the Amitābha-light (locks) element rather than the crown-light element.

None of these divergent fruits contradicts another; they represent different commentaries emphasising different benefits of the same two iconographic elements, not incompatible readings of a single claim, and are therefore not treated as a strict ⚑ divergence in the sense of mutually exclusive positions — the flag here marks that the fruit attributed is genuinely different from commentary to commentary, per Rule 9 (a claim is not counted toward a fruit it does not itself state).

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-4` gives an unparalleled, extensive elaboration: the light dispels not only bodily illness, mental suffering, and adverse thoughts, but is mapped onto the three sufferings (of suffering, of change, and pervasive conditioned suffering), six saṃsāric faults, and eight sufferings (birth, death, sickness, old age, meeting the unpleasant, separation from the pleasant, not obtaining what is sought, and the suffering of the appropriated aggregates) — all visualized as exiting the practitioner's body as soot and black smoke while reciting the mantra oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā. No other claim in the packet gives this specific enumerated framework or ties it to mantra recitation.

## Generation-stage iconographic elaboration

### Consensus
Two commentaries move beyond the root verse's two bare elements (crescent-moon crown; Amitābha in the locks) to give a fuller generation-stage sādhana description, agreeing on several specific details: a yellow body, seated on lotus-and-moon, one face, two hands (right in supreme-giving/varada mudrā, left holding an utpala symbolizing the Three Jewels), the utpala topped with a "glorious emblem" or "auspicious endless-knot" (དཔལ་བེའུ) that itself radiates light, and a closing meditation in which an auspicious rain falls, subduing all faults of the animate and inanimate world.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-3` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-2-1` ("ཨུཏྤལ་ཟེའུའི་འབྲུའི་སྟེང་། ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པའི་དཔལ་བེའུ་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོ་བ་ཅན་བསྣམས་པ") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-2` ("ཨུཏྤལ་འཛིན་པའི་སྟེང་དཔལ་བེའུ་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོ་བ་ཅན་གྱིས") share the clause "དཔལ་བེའུ་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོ་བ" ("a glorious-knot/endless-knot emblem radiating light") almost character-for-character — a strong, specific corroboration of this detail between the two commentaries. Their closing meditation instructions likewise converge closely: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-2-1` ("...བཀྲ་ཤིས་རྫས་རྟགས་ཀྱི་ཆར་བབས་པས། བརྟན་གཡོའི་རྟེན་འབྲེལ་ལོག་དུག་ཐམས་ཅད་གཞིལ་ཏེ་སྣོད་བཅུད་ཐམས་ཅད་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པར་གྱུར་པར་བསམ་སྟེ་རིག་སྔགས་བཟླའོ") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-1-3` ("བཀྲ་ཤིས་པའི་རྫ་ས་ཀྱིས་ཆར་བབས། བརྟན་གཡོ་ཉེས་ཚོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་བཅོམ་སྟེ་དགེ་ལེགས་ཀྱི་ཁྱབ་པར་བསྒོམས་པ་ནི་དམིགས་རིམ་ཡིན") both instruct visualizing an auspicious rain that subdues all faults of the animate and inanimate world (བརྟན་གཡོ...ཐམས་ཅད in both), pervading with excellence — a shared visualization-sequence structure, independently converging.

### ⚑ Divergences
`yama-sonam` gives a markedly different generation-stage iconography for this same homage: a **golden**-coloured body, seated in vajra posture on a lotus-and-moon seat, **one face, eight arms** (not two), holding a trident, iron hook, vajra, and sword in the four right hands, and a jewel at the heart, iron hook, staff, and vase in the four left hands — corroborated by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own sādhana giving the identical implement list.

— eight-armed iconography attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-13`, `yama-sonam:c-3-12-14`, `yama-sonam:c-3-12-15` (1 commentary)

This eight-armed, golden, multi-implement iconography shares no element with `palden-sherab`'s and `sangye-nyentrul`'s two-armed, yellow, twin-mudrā iconography beyond the general fact of describing "her bodily appearance" — the two traditions describe different deity forms under the same homage-verse, and neither claim acknowledges the other's existence. This is recorded as a genuine divergence, not reconciled anywhere in the packet.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-15`'s English gloss additionally mentions a golden hue and an utpala topped with a "victory-emblem," but this content falls within an ellipsis-marked gap in the claim's own quoted Tibetan (the Bod-yig field shows "..." at exactly this point) — per Rule 12, only what is verbatim-quoted may be attributed to the claim with confidence, so this detail is noted here as unverifiable within the packet rather than counted toward either the two-armed or eight-armed position.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give closely convergent completion-stage-with-marks ("hidden meaning," སྦས་དོན) readings of the crescent-moon crown and Amitābha, reading the crown as bodhicitta stabilized at the crown, Amitābha as the essence of the red element, and their union as generating the empty-form mahāmudrā maṇḍala-wheel:

— attested: `taranatha:c-12-10`, `taranatha:c-12-11`, `taranatha:c-12-12`, `taranatha:c-12-13`, `taranatha:c-12-14`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-3-1` (2 commentaries)

The two are close in content but not verbatim-identical. `taranatha:c-12-10` ("ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུའི་དབུ་རྒྱན་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས་སྤྱི་བོར་བརྟན་པའོ") and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-3-1` ("ཟླ་བའི་དུམ་བུས་དབུ་རྒྱན་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་ཐིག་ལེ་དཀར་པོ་སྤྱི་བོར་བརྟན་པའོ") share the frame "crescent-moon crown-ornament = bodhicitta...stabilized at the crown," with palden-sherab specifying "the white bodhicitta-*drop*" (ཐིག་ལེ་དཀར་པོ) where taranatha has plain "bodhicitta" (བྱང་ཆུབ་ཀྱི་སེམས) — a close paraphrase, not a character-identical match. `taranatha:c-12-11` ("རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་གནས་ནི་གྲིབ་མའི་ཚུལ་དུ་སྤྱི་བོར་བརྟན་པ") and palden-sherab's continuation ("རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ན་ནི་ལུས་དང་གྲིབ་མའི་ཚུལ་དུ་སྤྱི་བོར་བརྟན་ཏེ་གནས་པའོ") likewise share "གྲིབ་མའི་ཚུལ་དུ་སྤྱི་བོར་བརྟན" ("stabilized at the crown in the manner of a shadow") near-verbatim, palden-sherab adding "ལུས་དང" ("body and [its shadow]"). `taranatha:c-12-12` ("འོད་དཔག་མེད་ནི་དམར་ཆའི་དངས་མ") and palden-sherab's "འོད་དཔག་མེད་ནི་ཐིག་ལེ་དམར་ཆའི་དྭངས་མའོ" ("Amitābha is the [drop-]essence of the red element") match closely, again with palden-sherab inserting ཐིག་ལེ ("drop"). `taranatha:c-12-13`–`taranatha:c-12-14` ("...སྟོང་གཟུགས་...ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོའི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མཐའ་ཡས་པར་གྱུར་པའོ") and palden-sherab's closing ("...སྟོང་གཟུགས་འོད་གསལ་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོའི་དག་སྣང་གི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མཐའ་ཡས་པ་འཆར་པར་འགྱུར") likewise share the core phrase "སྟོང་གཟུགས...ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོའི...དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་གྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མཐའ་ཡས་པ" ("the boundless maṇḍala-wheel of empty-form mahāmudrā") near-verbatim, with palden-sherab adding "འོད་གསལ" (clear-light), "དག་སྣང" (pure vision), and an explicit reference to binding the two drops "without transference in the Avadhūtī" that taranatha's extracted claims do not state outright.

### ⚑ Divergences
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-4` reports a distinct completion-stage tradition for the same two iconographic elements, attributed to "a mahāsiddha" (གྲུབ་ཆེན): the crescent-moon crown symbolizes the syllable *haṃ* at the crown, and Amitābha as family-lord is identified with the upward-moving fire-wind (གྱེན་རྒྱུ་མེའི་རླུང), together forming a hidden instruction on caṇḍālī (གཏུམ་མོ, inner heat) — the completion-stage practice of applying the channels and wheels (རྩ་འཁོར) to the fire-offering. This is a genuinely different completion-stage doctrinal framework from `taranatha`'s and `palden-sherab`'s bodhicitta-drop/red-element/mahāmudrā reading: neither the syllable *haṃ* nor the fire-wind/caṇḍālī identification appears in either of the other two commentaries' claims, and neither `taranatha` nor `palden-sherab` mentions caṇḍālī. No claim in the packet reconciles the two readings or acknowledges the other's existence.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-4-1` extends its own exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks" (མཐར་ཐུག་མཚན་མེད་རྫོགས་རིམ), mapping the verse's four phrases onto the four empowerments and the four visions of the Great Perfection (Dzogchen): the crescent-moon crown-ornament is the fourth, ultimate word-empowerment (the direct actualization of dharmatā); "all her ornaments blazing" is the vision of increasing experience; "Amitābha among the locks" is awareness reaching its full measure; "ever radiant with the utmost light" is dharmatā reaching the stage of exhaustion, whereby kāya and wisdom expand. This fourth-level reading is not paralleled by `taranatha` (whose extracted claims stop at the completion-stage-with-marks level) or by any other commentary in the packet.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58` — flagged ⚑ ambiguous in the packet itself — gives a different four-empowerments mapping, but one that is not confined to this verse: it reads "Amitābha amid the matted locks" as the vase empowerment and "crowned with a crescent moon" as the secret empowerment, while drawing further elements from other homages (^1-15's Bliss/Virtue/Peace/Pacified as the wisdom-empowerments, ^1-10's garland of light as the display of the diadem, ^1-13/^1-16's "encircled, delighting" imagery as the generation- and completion-stage aspects of principal-and-retinue and the channels/winds) into one cross-verse doctrinal synthesis. Per Rule 6, this claim is carried through here with its ambiguity explicit rather than folded into `palden-sherab`'s four-empowerments reading: the two mappings assign different empowerments to the crown and to Amitābha-in-locks (`palden-sherab` reads the crown as the *fourth* empowerment and Amitābha-in-locks as a *vision*, not an empowerment; `pema-namgyal` reads Amitābha-in-locks as the *vase* [first] empowerment and the crown as the *secret* [second] empowerment) and are not treated as corroborating one another.
