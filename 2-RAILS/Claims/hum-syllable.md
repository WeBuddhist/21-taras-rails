This page treats the bare seed-syllable ཧཱུྃ། (hūṃ) and the phrase-variant
ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ། ("the letter hūṃ") as one merged subject, per the packet's own
term set. Hūṃ is a generic tantric seed-syllable used across countless deities'
mantras and independently notable outside this text; within the Twenty-One
Homages it recurs across many separate verses (the root-guardian-summoning
homage, the earth-striking homage, the realm-pervading homages, the
three-suchness homage, and others), so the facets below are organized by verse
context and by theme rather than by a single spine slot. 86 claims were routed
to this topic across all 16 commentaries in the packet (none silent).

## Location of the syllable on Tārā's body, and the accompanying wrathful frown

### Consensus
Independent of any specific verse's benefit, several commentaries state — without
further elaboration — that the HŪṂ syllable is located at Tārā's heart, typically
alongside a wrathfully moving or furrowed brow:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-66`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-4` (4 commentaries)

(`gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-2` is flagged in its own raw claims file as an OCR-degraded
block; its heart-location content is legible, but the claim's own extraction notes
uncertainty elsewhere in the passage.)

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-5` explicitly reports a live disagreement among four named
masters over whose HŪṂ letter is meant in the earth-striking, seven-levels verse
(see "The wrathful HŪṂ that crushes the seven levels," below, for that verse's main
content):

> ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་གང་གི་ཡིན་ཞེ་ན། རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ་ཀྱིས་ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་དང་། ཅོ་ནེས་ཕྱག་གཡས་པའི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་ལ། དངུལ་ཆུས་ཞབས་ཕྱག་གི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་སྔོན་པོ་ཞེས་ཞབས་ཕྱག་གཉིས་ཀའི་མཐིལ་གྱི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ལ་མཛད་ཅིང་། ཇོ་ནང་པས་ཤངས་ནས་འབྱུང་བའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ཡིན་པར་གསུངས་སོ།

— i.e.: Rje Dge 'dun grub says it is the HŪṂ at her heart; Co ne pa says it is on
the palm of her right hand; Ngulchu speaks of a blue HŪṂ letter "on the sole and
palm," taking it as the HŪṂ on both foot-sole and hand-palm together; Jonangpa
says it is the HŪṂ arising from her nostrils. (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-5`)

Re-reading this packet's own claims against those four positions (Rule 10 of this
skill): three of the four positions are independently corroborated *in substance*
by other commentaries elsewhere in this packet, though no claim in the packet
identifies any of these other commentaries' authors with the four named masters
by name:
- **Right-palm position** — `karma-maitri:c-1-1-14-5` independently states "གཡས་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་མཐིལ་ན་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་" ("On her right palm is the letter HŪṂ"), matching Co ne pa's reported position in substance.
- **Blue HŪṂ on hand-and-foot soles** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4` independently states "ཕྱག་དང་ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་དུ་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་སྔོན་པོ་རེ་རེ་གནས་པ" ("a single blue syllable HŪM abiding in the sole of each hand and foot"), matching Ngulchu's reported position in substance, including the colour.
- **Nostrils** — both `taranatha:c-14-5` ("ཤངས་ནས་ ... འཇིགས་པའི་སྒྲ་བསྒྲགས་པས," uttering HŪM through her nostrils) and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41` ("ཤངས་ནས་སྲིད་གསུམ་གཡོ་བའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུྃ་གི་སྒྲས," from her nostrils the syllable HŪṂ resounds) independently place a HŪṂ-utterance at the nostrils, matching Jonangpa's reported position in substance.

The fourth position (heart, attributed to Rje Dge 'dun grub) is directly
corroborated by the registered commentary `gendun-drub`'s own claims on this same
earth-striking verse — see `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3` under "The wrathful HŪṂ
that crushes the seven levels," below, which locates the crushing HŪṂ explicitly
at the heart.

One further data point complicates rather than resolves the picture: this
packet's claims from `anon-utpala` — whose commentary's own frontmatter names its
author as Ngulchu Dharmabhadra — consistently place the HŪṂ at the *heart*
(its heart-location claim is already cited above under Consensus; its
three-suchness claim `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-6-3` under "The three suchness syllables
OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ at the three places," below, likewise places HŪṂ at the heart), not
at the "sole and palm" position anon-trinle-char reports.
Whether `anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-5`'s "Ngulchu" and this vault's registered
`anon-utpala`/Ngulchu Dharmabhadra are the same author, discuss different verses
of the root text, or simply diverge is not resolvable from the claims in this
packet; it is recorded here as an open question rather than silently harmonized.

Separately, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-9` records its own correction against an
unnamed group of "other commentaries": on the iron-hook homage's line
"ཁྲོ་གཉེར་གཡོ་བའི་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་གིས" ("by the letter HŪM of the quivering, frowning
[brow]"), it states that other commentaries construe "quivering" as modifying the
frown itself, but that in its own reading "quivering" must instead modify the
HŪM-essence — i.e. it is the *syllable* that quivers, not the brow. No other
commentary's claims in this packet are cited by name as holding the position
`anon-trinle-char` argues against, so this is recorded as a single commentary's
own reported divergence rather than a two-sided disagreement between two
registered commentaries in this packet.

None beyond the positions catalogued above.

## The wrathful HŪṂ that summons wealth and liberates from destitution (the "iron hook" homage)

### Consensus
On the homage praising her as able to summon all the earth-protecting hosts, whose
quivering, wrathfully-frowning HŪṂ thoroughly liberates all the destitute, a wide
consensus holds that light-rays from this heart-HŪṂ liberate beings from poverty
and destitution:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-7`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-35`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-40` (9 commentaries)

Only one of the nine elaborates the specific mechanism in detail: `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-2` alone states that the light-rays "draw in the wealth and resources of gods, nāgas, humans and others, raining all desired things down." The other eight state the general liberation-from-destitution outcome without this specific gods/nāgas/humans imagery; it is not extended to them here.

Several of these quote the root verse itself before or alongside their gloss:

— quoted: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2` (4 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as doctrinal disagreement.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-13` gives this homage its own distinctive two-part
synthesis, pairing it with a separately-discussed "iron hook" epithet: the iron
hook clears away adverse conditions, while the essence — the letter HŪM —
accomplishes favourable conditions. This two-part framing (adversity-clearing
instrument vs. favourable-condition-accomplishing essence) is not paralleled by
any other commentary's claims in this packet.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4` gives a distinctive extension: toward the powerful
spirits who do not keep the commands given them, the wrathful HŪṂ at her heart
emanates countless further HŪṂ-syllables and hosts of minor wrathful deities,
thereby placing all beings to be tamed — who are destitute of emptiness and
compassion and engage in bad karma, suffering, and sin — into the state of the
omniscient buddha. No other commentary's claims in this packet extend the
wealth-summoning HŪṂ into an emanation of subsidiary wrathful deities or a
buddhahood-conferring function in this way.

## The wrathful HŪṂ that crushes the seven levels beneath the earth

### Consensus
On the homage describing her striking the earth's surface with palm and foot,
wrathful-browed, crushing all seven levels with the syllable HŪṂ, a wide
consensus holds that light, sound, or fire radiating from this HŪṂ destroys or
subdues the malicious spirits (nāgas, asuras, rākṣasas, demons, and the like)
dwelling in the seven levels beneath the earth:

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-14-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-41`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-14-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-48` (6 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3` is routed to this node but does not itself support the "destroys malicious spirits" statement above: its own text states only that the HŪM at the heart "crushes — that is, dries up — the ocean of existence of the world's seven levels" (སྲིད་མཚོ་འགེམས་མ), a more abstract samsāra-image with no mention of spirits, light, sound, or fire. It is not counted in the Consensus attestation; see its own Unique entry below for what it does say — the claim still corroborates the heart-location of the crushing HŪM (see "Location of the syllable," above).

Several of these quote the root verse itself before or alongside their gloss:

— quoted: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-17-2` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-14-4-2` gives a distinct, explicitly doctrinal reading of the
same verse line, read together with palden-sherab's own literal-crushing claim
(cited above under Consensus): the "seven levels crushed" are, on this
reading, allegorized as the six consciousness-collections (the five
sense-consciousnesses, the afflicted mind, and mental consciousness) together
with the ālaya-consciousness as their basis — seven in all — which dissolve into
the primordial, primordially-pure dharmadhātu "like clouds dissolving in the
sky," whereby Tārā actualizes the dharmakāya state of Samantabhadrī. No other
commentary's claims in this packet offer this allegorical reading of the seven
levels; it is recorded as a second, doctrinal layer that `palden-sherab` itself
adds on top of (not instead of) its own literal reading, without displacing it.

### Unique
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-7-3` gives its own, more abstract reading, not paralleled by any other commentary's claims in this packet: the heart's HŪM syllable "crushes — that is, dries up — the ocean of existence of the world's seven levels" — an image of drying up saṃsāric existence rather than destroying spirit-inhabitants by light, sound, or fire.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-14-4` complicates the plain "crushes all spirits" picture
with a two-step mechanism not stated elsewhere: when Tārā strikes the earth, she
first summons — largely as allies to accomplish her own activity — the gods,
nāgas, and yakṣas dwelling below the earth who harm the doctrine and beings; only
those among them *not* thus summoned are then crushed without exception by light
rays from the heart-HŪṂ penetrating the seven levels. No other commentary's
claims in this packet distinguish summoned allies from crushed holdouts in this
way.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-76` glosses the same root-verse line with a distinctive
variant: rather than light radiating from a heart-HŪṂ, it states that by
*uttering* HŪM aloud, light rays radiate from the vajra held in Tārā's right
hand, filling the abodes of the seven classes of beings and thereby clearing —
rather than crushing or destroying — all their obstacles. This differs from the
majority account in two respects: the stated source of the light (a hand-held
vajra rather than the heart) and the stated effect (obstacles of the seven
classes of beings cleared, rather than hostile spirits in the seven underground
levels crushed/destroyed). See also "Location of the syllable on Tārā's body,"
above, for the broader pattern of commentaries locating HŪṂ or its light at sites
other than the heart.

## HŪṂ (with TUTTĀRE) filling and pervading the three realms

### Consensus
On the homage(s) describing her filling the desire realm, the directions (the
form realm), and space (the formless realm), a consensus holds that this
pervasion is accomplished by the combined sound of TUTTĀRE and the syllable HŪṂ:

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-4`, `taranatha:c-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-4` (6 commentaries)

Several of these quote the root verse itself before or alongside their gloss:

— quoted: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-8-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-5-2` (3 commentaries)

Within this consensus, four commentaries additionally converge on a specific,
near-identical doctrinal gloss of the HŪṂ syllable itself as symbolizing
emptiness and compassion (mind), using closely matching Tibetan phrasing:
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-4-3` ("ཐུགས་གའི་ཧཱུཾ་ཡིག་ནི། སྟོང་ཉིད་སྙིང་རྗེ་མཚོན་པས,"
the HŪṂ letter at her heart, since it symbolizes emptiness and compassion),
`sungrab-tulku:c-8-4` ("ཐུགས་སྟོང་ཉིད་སྙིང་རྗེ་མཚོན་པའི་ཧུཾ་ཡི་གི་ཡེ་གེའི་སྒྲ་སྐད་,"

## The three suchness syllables OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ at the three places

### Consensus
On the homage describing the three syllables OṂ (crown, body), ĀḤ (throat,
speech), and HŪṂ (heart, mind) arrayed at the three places, a consensus holds
that this arrangement pacifies afflictions or obstacles (of the three doors —
body, speech, mind):

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-6-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-21-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-21-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-1` (5 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-9` gives a related but functionally distinct account of
the same three syllables in a completion-stage context: rather than pacifying
obstacles, the resonance of HŪṂ at the heart-seed is said to radiate outward and
emanate the mandala of Tārā, the resonance of OṂ to draw the mandala back in and
dissolve it into the heart-seed together with the Venerable Mother, and the
resonance of ĀḤ to mark its abiding — a mantra-recitation and mandala
emanation/reabsorption practice, not stated as pacifying afflictions. No other
commentary's claims in this packet describe this emanation/reabsorption function
for the three syllables, so it is recorded as a distinct elaboration rather than
folded into the pacification consensus above.

## HŪṂ as the seed-syllable of wrathful Tārā, and its relation to the ten-syllable mantra

### Consensus
Several commentaries state that wrathful Tārā (or her epithet "Swift One"/TURE)
is generated from, or arises from, the seed-syllable HŪṂ:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-45`, `taranatha:c-16-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-3` (4 commentaries)

`sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-1` is routed here but does not itself state a generation/arising claim: its own text presupposes an *existing* heart-HŪM and describes Tārā stamping both soles on the earth and radiating further HŪM-shaped light-rays from that heart-HŪM — a radiation from an existing syllable, not an origination of Tārā from it. It is not counted in this Consensus.

Separately, several commentaries relate the ten-syllable wrathful mantra
(oṃ nama tāre namo hāre hūṃ hāre svāhā) to the HŪṂ syllable — as arising from a
"wheel" of HŪṂ, as arrayed with HŪṂ at the heart, or simply as containing/naming
HŪṂ within it:

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-5` (4 commentaries)

Two further claims routed to this node do not belong in the list above:
`sangye-nyentrul:c-17-0-2` quotes a ten-syllable mantra, but it is the text's
*peaceful*-aspect mantra (oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā — no HŪM syllable at all),
not the wrathful ten-syllable mantra this facet concerns; conflating the two
would misidentify which mantra is being discussed. `taranatha:c-17-8` is a
wrong-homage citation: its own text ("'In the form of HŪM' means HŪM-KĀRA, that
is, the syllable HŪM") is a bare word-gloss embedded in taranatha's exegesis of
the *Meru/Mandara/Vibhedaka mountain-shaking homage* (see "HŪṂ's power shaking or
piercing Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vibhedaka," above) — a different homage from
taranatha's own ten-syllable-mantra homage (`taranatha:c-16-6`, `taranatha:c-16-7`,
cited elsewhere on this page) — and says nothing about the ten-syllable mantra.

`sungrab-tulku:c-19-2` additionally quotes the corresponding root verse in full
("... the knowledge-mantra arranged with the ten-syllabled speech, from HŪṂ, she
liberates ...").

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-9` reports its own internal exegetical debate over the
placement of "the ten-syllable mantra" relative to HŪṂ, citing two readings
before stating its own judgment of the tantra's intent:
- **Position A** ("some commentaries"): the ten-syllable mantra (oṃ namaḥ tāre namo hāre hūṃ hāre svāhā, the vidyā-mantra of HŪṂ) is arrayed at the heart and recited aloud.
- **Position B** ("others"): the ten-syllable mantra (oṃ tāre and so forth) is arrayed at TĀṂ, and HŪṂ itself is encircled by the vidyā-mantra HŪṂ syllables "oṃ namaḥ" and so on, radiating light from that.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-9` itself judges that taking HŪṂ as encircled by the
ten mantra-letters — with light rays radiating from that encirclement to dispel
the darkness of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa — "appears to be the tantra's own intent,"
i.e. it favours a reading closer to Position B. This is a single commentary's own
report of a debate between unnamed prior readings, not a disagreement between two
registered commentaries in this packet; no other commentary's claims in the
packet explicitly take a side between Position A and Position B as stated here.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-5` continues the same discussion with the two
readings' respective benefits: meditating that one is encircled by the
mantra-letters is said to yield the future benefit of being encircled by one's
own steady mind, while meditating that light rays radiate from the HŪṂ letter is
said to yield the future benefit of dispelling the darkness of saṃsāra and
nirvāṇa.

### Unique
None beyond the material catalogued above.

## HŪṂ's power shaking or piercing Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vibhedaka

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently state that light or power from the HŪṂ syllable
shakes or pierces the named mountains — Meru, Mandara, and Vibhedaka/Vindhya (a
mountain outside the trichiliocosm):

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-86`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` (3 commentaries)

Only `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2` itself states the thief-subduing effect: swift
Tārā's feet trample while light from the seed-syllable HŪṂ (or its form) causes
Meru, Mandara, Vibhedaka and other mountains to emanate and shake, "shaking the
three worlds" and subduing bands of thieves — and adds that "both the paṇḍita and
the siddha find this section readily applicable to protection from thieves on the
road," a practical application not stated by the other two commentaries.
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-86` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4` state only the
mountain-piercing/shaking content itself — neither mentions thieves, enemies, or
any effect beyond the mountains shaking — so the "subduing thieves" effect is not
extended to them here.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond `konchok-thabkhe`'s thief-protection application noted above.

## Esoteric and completion-stage symbolism of HŪṂ

### Consensus
None — this facet is attested almost entirely by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as a cross-commentary disagreement.

### Unique
`taranatha` alone, in this packet, gives a cluster of esoteric/completion-stage
readings of HŪṂ not paralleled by any other commentary: the five parts of HŪṂ
symbolize the five wisdoms (`taranatha:c-16-13`); HŪṂ is glossed as "the vidyā —
that is, the lamp arisen from the vidyā-mantra syllable HŪM," a personified
figure ("she") whose body blazes with light-rays (`taranatha:c-16-6` — the
blazing-body quality belongs to this vidyā/deity-figure arisen from HŪM, not to
the bare syllable itself); HŪṂ's hidden sign is the vajra-mind channel
avadhūtī, into which the seed-potency of all channels dissolves
(`taranatha:c-17-16`); HŪṂ is the indestructible sound arisen from tummo
(`taranatha:c-5-8`); and, in a correspondence-list to the generation-stage ritual
and the four empowerments, "the syllable HŪM" is said to indicate the
mantra-empowerment specifically (`taranatha:c-21-4-17`).

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-5` gives a related but independently-stated
completion-stage reading, not verbally identical to and not cross-referenced
against any of taranatha's claims above: the essence in which the enlightened
activity of all buddhas is gathered arises in the aspect of the syllable HŪM,
which itself arises in the form of the guru, Venerable Tārā; furthermore, the
wisdom of great bliss arising from the yogi's own winds entering, abiding, and
dissolving in the central channel is meditated as arising in the form of
Venerable Tārā. This is thematically adjacent to taranatha's tummo/avadhūtī
material (both invoke completion-stage subtle-body practice) but states a
distinct content (guru-yoga and great-bliss wisdom, rather than the avadhūtī
channel or tummo specifically) and is not treated here as corroborating
taranatha's claims, per this skill's Rule 10.
