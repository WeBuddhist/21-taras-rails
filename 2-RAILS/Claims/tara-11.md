## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but they do not all mark it the same way. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the eleventh" (བཅུ་གཅིག་པ) or its numeral, across ten claims (karma-maitri and taranatha each cite it twice): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-1` (marked "༼༡༡༽"), `taranatha:c-11-1`, `taranatha:c-11-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-1` (8 commentaries).

Four instead give a *local* ordinal, "the fourth" (བཞི་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the fourth member of a "praise by way of accomplishing activity through the ten directional guardians" (ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུས་འཕྲིན་ལས་སྒྲུབ་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`. `sungrab-tulku:c-14-1` also uses this "ten directional guardians" phrasing but pairs it with the global numeral "(11)" rather than "the fourth" alone — the same double-marking pattern its counterpart used at homage 9.

The remaining three (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within the claims in this packet — their content was routed to this slot by claim-content match. `sangye-nyentrul` likewise states no ordinal within its own claim text, though its node's own title carries the numeral ༡༡.

Nine commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines, either as one continuous block or split across adjacent claims:

— full verse quoted attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-38`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-14`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-64`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-65`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-66`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-67`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3` (9 commentaries)

These quotations are not textually uniform. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2` quotes the heart-syllable as **ཧཾ** (HAṂ) rather than the root text's ཧཱུཾ (HŪṂ) found in every other quotation in this packet — a genuine variant, not a typo the packet silently corrects; oddly, `anon-utpala`'s own exegesis claim (`anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4`, cited below under "The wrathful frown and heart-syllable") reverts to ཧཱུཾ, so this is an internal inconsistency within that commentary's own extraction rather than a stable alternative reading. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-2` drops the genitive particle found in the root and most other quotations (གཡོ་བ་ཡི་གེ་ rather than གཡོ་བའི་ཡི་གེ་). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`'s own quotation carries internal ellipsis marks ("…") between each line, copied as they appear in the packet. `drakpa-gyaltsen` quotes the verse split one line per claim — `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-64` (line 1), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-65` (line 2), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-66` (line 3), `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-67` (line 4) — each introduced as a lemma before its own gloss, rather than as one continuous quotation; `lobsang-dawa` splits it in two, labelling the halves "verse 25" (`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`) and "verse 26" (`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`).

The remaining seven commentaries give only a partial or paraphrastic opening in this packet: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-1` (OCR-degraded), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-2` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-1` and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-2`, `taranatha:c-11-1` and `taranatha:c-11-2` (which explicitly abbreviates with "སོགས", "etc."), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-34`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1`, and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-2` (a lemma-by-lemma word-gloss quoting only short root-text fragments — e.g. ཕྱག་འཚལ་ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ, ཚོགས་རྣམས — rather than a continuous or fully line-split verbatim quote; it does not reach line 4 of the verse at all, so it was moved out of the "full verse" group above). None of these seven quote the full verse directly; they are routed here by claim-content match.

### ⚑ Divergences
None regarding *which* verse this is — the ordinal-marker differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad, not disagreement. The HAṂ/HŪṂ variant noted above is a textual, not doctrinal, divergence; it is not elaborated into a position by any commentary and is recorded here rather than as a ⚑ divergence proper.

### Unique
None beyond the structural and textual notes above.

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name for this homage gives a **different** exact wording, though several converge on the same underlying theme (dispelling destitution / bestowing wealth). This facet is a set of divergent namings, not a shared consensus statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least five distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"Dispeller of destitution / bestower of wealth,"** with six commentaries each giving their own exact wording: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-1` ("the Venerable Lady Who Delivers All from Destitution," རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་ཕོངས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་ཀྱང་རྣམ་པར་སྒྲོལ་མ), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-0-1` ("Jetsün Ārya Tārā Who Dispels Poverty and Bestows Wealth," དབུལ་སེལ་ནོར་སྟེར་རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ), `sungrab-tulku:c-14-3` ("venerable Tārā, Dispeller of All Destitution," རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཕོངས་པ་ཀུན་སེལ་མ), `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-1-1` ("Wealth-Bestowing Tārā Who Dispels Poverty," དབུལ་ཕོངས་སེལ་བའི་ནོར་སྟེར་སྒྲོལ་མ), `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-1` ("She Who Dispels All Poverty," ཕོངས་པ་ཀུན་སེལ), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-14` ("Furrow-Browed Dispeller of Poverty, Bestower of Wealth," སྒྲོལ་མ་དབུལ་སེལ་ནོར་སྟེར་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ཅན). Six commentaries, six distinct wordings of the same theme.
2. **"Tārā Who Summons"** (དགུག་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-4`. Unique to this commentary; names her by the summoning power itself rather than by the destitution-dispelling benefit.
3. **"[She] of the earth"** (ས་གཞི) — `taranatha:c-11-1`. Unique; names her by the earth-element theme of the verse's opening line rather than by its closing benefit.
4. **Functional classification by mechanism, "praise by way of accomplishing activity through the ten directional guardians"** — `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-1` (already cited above under "Structural placement").
5. **Functional classification by benefit, "homage by way of dispelling destitution"** (ཕོངས་བ་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས) — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-1`. Distinct from group 4: this classifies the verse by its result (removing poverty) rather than by its mechanism (the directional guardians).

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage within the claims in this packet.

### Unique
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-4` and `taranatha:c-11-1` are each unique to their own commentary (already detailed above).

## Who is summoned — the identity of the "earth-protectors"

### Consensus
Seven commentaries identify the "earth-protectors" (ས་གཞི་སྐྱོང་བ) of the root verse specifically as **the ten directional guardians** (ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་བཅུ), summoned as servants to accomplish her enlightened activity:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` (7 commentaries)

Two of these give a full named list of the ten, and the lists are not identical: `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3` names Brahmā (above), Indra (east), Agni (southeast), Yama (south), Rākṣasa (southwest), Vāyu (northwest), Kubera (north), Īśāna (northeast), and the earth-lord (below); `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-3` instead names the fire-god, Brahmā, the wind-god, Yama, elemental spirits, yakṣas, rākṣasas, Īśāna, the water-god, and the earth-goddess. Both agree on the ten-guardian *framework* and share several members (Brahmā, Yama, Īśāna/dbang-ldan), but the two lists are not the same enumeration — this is recorded as a shared structure with divergent membership, not flattened into one list.

### ⚑ Divergences
1. **"Earth-guardian" glossed instead as the eight planets** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-2` ("she is able to summon the entire assembly of the eight planets"), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-4` (attributing this reading to "a mahāsiddha['s]" own sādhana, in which she is "surrounded by the eight planets," summoned "with the iron hook in her hand"), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-5` (giving the astrological reasoning for the equation). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-8` independently cites the same eight-planets identification via a different text, the "Sādhana of the Twenty-One Tārās": "from the eight directions, the eight great planets are drawn in by the iron hook held in her right hand" — this citation corroborates konchok-thabkhe's reading. `anon-trinle-char`'s *own* gloss at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-5`, however, identifies "earth-protectors" broadly as "all gods and humans," and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-4` names the eight planets only as one class among several ("…all classes of non-human beings, such as the eight great planets, and hosts of earth-protectors, such as renegade kings…") rather than equating "earth-protector" exclusively with "planet" — so only the cited sādhana passage (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-8`), not anon-trinle-char's own position, corroborates konchok-thabkhe's exclusive eight-planets reading. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-6`, a further quote from the "Sādhana of the Praise in Twenty-One Homages to Tārā" ("The glorious earth-protectors of the directions, and those beyond direction too — she draws them all in without exception"), attests the general drawing-in theme but does not itself specify planets versus directional guardians, so it is not counted toward either side of this divergence.
2. **"Drawing in" as forceful subjugation, not summoning to benign service** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-11` states explicitly: "Although some commentaries explain the meaning of 'drawing in' as summoning the ten directional guardians to accomplish [their] activities, since these earth-protectors obstruct the doctrine and beings, it is clearly meant in the sense of being severely subdued by force." The "some commentaries" it describes are corroborated by the Consensus attestation list above, all seven of which frame the ten guardians as servants "accomplishing her activity" rather than as adversaries being forcibly subdued. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-11` stands alone in this packet on the forceful-subjugation side.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-1`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-38`+`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-39` each broaden the summoned host beyond the bare "ten directional guardians," naming the earth-goddess Sthāvarā (or "the earth-goddess"), specific nāga kings (Nanda and, in palden-sherab, also Takṣaka), the yakṣa king Vaiśravaṇa (named by palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul), and Indra (named by palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul) alongside the ten guardians. These three do not contradict the ten-guardians reading — the ten guardians are explicitly named in palden-sherab's and tsultrim-namdak's lists — they simply enumerate a richer retinue; sangye-nyentrul's own list (earth-goddess, nāga king Nanda, yakṣa king, Indra) does not separately name the ten directional guardians, closing instead with a general "and others" (སོགས), so it broadens the retinue without itself re-confirming the ten-guardian framework. They are recorded as elaborations, not as a fourth divergent position.

`taranatha:c-11-3` and `taranatha:c-11-4` give the broadest reading in the packet: the goddess's mantra summons not only "the host of kings who protect the earth, or the earth-goddesses" but also subjugates "nāgas beneath the earth, asuras, vidyādharas, and hosts of ḍākinīs" — a retinue not paralleled by any other single claim.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-65` describes the ten guardians as acting specifically as her "messengers" (ཕོ་ཉ), "obeying whatever she commands" — a nuance close to, but not identical in wording to, the "servants accomplishing activity" framing of the Consensus above. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-1` (OCR-uncertain) gives a similar "messenger" framing — earth-guardian deities "throughout the ten directions" who "behold her and carry out her command" — paralleling drakpa-gyaltsen's nuance, though the OCR degradation of this claim keeps it from being treated as a firm second attestation.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-34` gives a third and distinct mechanism for the summoning: she draws in "the earth-guarding beings and, in particular, the assembled kings of men" *through their own faith in her* (ཁྱོད་ལ་དད་ནས), rather than through command, service, or force. No other claim in the packet frames the summoning this way.

## The wrathful frown and heart-syllable — mechanism of summoning and liberation

### Consensus
Twelve commentaries describe the wrathful frown itself as moving or quivering (ཁྲོ་གཉེར་གཡོ་བ), together with the heart-syllable HŪṂ, as the agent of summoning and/or liberation:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-66`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-4`, `taranatha:c-11-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-40` (12 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-9` explicitly rejects this reading: "Although in [other] commentaries, 'the quivering of the wrathful frown' is explained to mean the frown itself quivering, here it must instead be taken to mean the quivering of the essence, the letter HŪṂ." The twelve claims in the Consensus list above are exactly the "other commentaries'" reading this claim describes and rejects. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-10`, quoting Nyima Sbaspa's own sādhana ("Meditate that, by the intense quivering of that very essence, she dispels all destitution"), gives supporting scriptural authority for this same HŪṂ-essence-quivers reading — `anon-trinle-char` stands alone in this packet on the HŪṂ-essence-quivers side, but its own position is internally corroborated by this second citation.

A related but distinct divergence concerns the heart-syllable's own identity. Most quotations and glosses give HŪṂ (ཧཱུཾ/ཧཱུྃ). `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`'s own root-verse quotation instead gives HAṂ (ཧཾ) — see "Structural placement" above; its own exegesis claim `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4` — already counted once above in the Consensus list, not re-counted here, and not itself a divergent position — reverts to HŪṂ, so this is an internal inconsistency rather than a sustained alternative. (This backreference is why the claim ID appears in both this section's Consensus and Divergences text; it attests only the Consensus mechanism, not any divergent reading.) `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3` names the light-emitting heart-syllable as **TĀṂ** (ཏཱཾ) in its exegesis of who is summoned, even though its own root-verse quotation (`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-2`) gives HŪṂ — again an internal variation within one commentary's own claims, not corroborated by any other commentary in the packet, and not resolved here.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char` treats the **iron hook** as a literal iconographic implement distinct from the HŪṂ-syllable, dividing the verse's action in two: "the iron hook clears away adverse conditions, while the essence, the letter HŪṂ, accomplishes favourable conditions" (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-13`; see also `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-7`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-4`, and the iconography claims `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-12`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-15`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-16`). `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-4` independently mentions an iron hook in the same connection (a mahāsiddha's "ritual for summoning [the eight planets] with the iron hook in her hand"), which corroborates that some strand of the tradition gives this Tārā a literal iron hook — matching `anon-trinle-char`'s iconography — though no other commentary in the packet mentions a hook implement at all; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-4` instead uses "like a hook" (ལྕགས་ཀྱུ་ལྟ་བུ) only as a simile for the light-rays themselves, not as a separate implement.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-4` gives a distinct soteriological extension not paralleled elsewhere: the wrathful brow, stirring "like clouds," subdues unruly spirits who do not obey her command, and thereby "places all beings to be tamed... into the state of the omniscient buddha" — the verse's action culminating in full buddhahood for those subdued, not merely in their service or in the alleviation of poverty.

## Iconography

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is a direct two-tradition divergence, paralleling the pattern found at other homages in this corpus.

### ⚑ Divergences
**Dark body, one face, two arms, iron hook and lasso, standing in an extended-leg stance** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-14` ("her body colour is dark; she stands atop a lotus seat upon a sun maṇḍala, right leg extended and left bent; she has one face and two arms"), `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-15` ("she abides holding an iron hook in her right hand and a lasso in her left"), supported by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa's own Twenty-One Homages sādhana at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-11-16` ("Abiding on lotus and sun, right leg extended in a dancing stance; dark-coloured, one face, two arms; the right holding the iron hook that draws in the planets, [the left] holding the binding lasso"). One commentary (`anon-trinle-char`, internally consistent across three of its own claims plus a supporting citation).

**Reddish-yellow/red-gold body, peaceful-wrathful, one face, two hands, right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding an utpala topped by a treasure vase** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-2-1` ("reddish-yellow, majestic yet attractive, at once peaceful and wrathful, both legs in archer's posture, one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving, left holding at ear-level, atop the open pollen-centre of an utpala, a treasure-vase"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-1-1` ("red-gold body, resplendent with a mingled peaceful-wrathful expression") + `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-1-2` ("seated half-cross-legged on lotus-and-moon; one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving mudra, left holding an utpala atop which a treasure vase"). Two commentaries.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-14` partially corroborates this second tradition, describing the body as "reddish-yellow" and the left hand as holding a treasure-vase, without stating an explicit posture or arm-count — consistent with, but not a full third independent attestation of, the complete iconographic set.

Despite the colour and implement divergence, both traditions share a similar right-leg-extended stance: `anon-trinle-char`'s "right leg extended, left bent" (dancing/standing) is structurally close to `palden-sherab`'s "archer's posture" (also a right-leg-extended stance), though applied to figures of different colour and holding different implements — a cross-cutting observation, not a point of full agreement.

No other commentary in the packet states an explicit body colour, hand-implement set, or arm-count for this Tārā; the remaining twelve describe only the wrathful frown and heart-syllable (see above) without addressing full iconography.

### Unique
None beyond the two traditions above.

## Benefits — dispelling poverty and destitution

### Consensus
Ten commentaries state, in their own wording, that this homage liberates beings from the suffering of poverty and destitution:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-67`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-11-5`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-4-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-14-4`, `taranatha:c-11-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-40`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-4` (10 commentaries)

A narrower group of four additionally elaborates this into a positive image of wealth and desired resources being drawn in and, in most cases, raining down to fill the need — a distinct, additional proposition, not attested by all ten above:

— wealth/resources rain down attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-2-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-12-0-2`, `taranatha:c-11-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-5` (4 commentaries — palden-sherab cited twice; taranatha's own claim describes resources "flourishing" rather than literally "raining down," and tenzin-dhonzang's uses a magnet-and-overflow image rather than rain, so only palden-sherab and sangye-nyentrul use the rain image itself)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary in the packet contests that this homage removes poverty/destitution; the divergence, if any, is only in how much further elaboration is added (see Unique below).

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-35` gives a distinctive four-fold enumeration of "destitution" not paralleled elsewhere: liberation from destitution "of dharma, of material wealth, of form, and of the sense-faculties."

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-11-2` (OCR-uncertain) adds that this Tārā "brings about a special bliss even amid [states like] hell" — extending the benefit into infernal realms, a detail not found in any other claim in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-11-7` cites an otherwise-unidentified text, the "Legdrima," praising this Tārā's "protecting all from poverty" — a distinct scriptural attribution not paralleled by any other claim.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
`taranatha` and `palden-sherab` independently give a closely parallel completion-stage-with-marks reading of the verse, in five/six matching moves: (1) "protecting the earth" as the pure essence of earth-element particles, drawn and stabilized from the crown down to (or up from) the jewel-tip; (2) the "wrathful frown" and the sound of HŪṂ identified with the moon's own essence; (3) a drop travelling via the brow-point and stabilizing at the crown; (4) this process accomplishing samādhis such as "Treasury of Space," thereby liberating from destitution; (5) this being explained as the special quality of a bliss-emptiness samādhi-branch in which the white and red elements' movement ceases above and below. The wording is close but not identical between the two — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-3-1` gives the fuller and more continuous account (e.g. it specifies the drop's path runs "from the secret place" through the brow-point, which `taranatha`'s corresponding claim does not state), while `taranatha`'s claims (`taranatha:c-11-8` through `taranatha:c-11-12`) break the same five moves into shorter, separately-numbered units.

— attested: `taranatha:c-11-8`, `taranatha:c-11-9`, `taranatha:c-11-10`, `taranatha:c-11-11`, `taranatha:c-11-12`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-3-1` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the additional readings below are independent elaborations, not incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-11-4-1` extends the exegesis to a fourth level, "completion-stage-without-marks": ignorance — not recognizing awareness-wisdom's own face — is itself "the hosts of earth-guardians"; the instant awareness is realized, self-arisen wisdom's HŪṂ-radiance dispels all destitution of existence and peace. This further level is not paralleled by `taranatha` or any other claim in the corpus.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-11-6` gives a related but differently-framed doctrinal extension of its own "eight planets" reading (not labelled སྦས་དོན in the claim itself): the interdependence of a person's inner channels, drops, and winds gives rise externally to the rising and setting of planets and stars, and "by this outer-inner correspondence one should also understand how all destitution of saṃsāra and peace is dispelled." This shares the general completion-stage register (channels/winds, outer-inner correspondence) with the taranatha/palden-sherab reading above but is not the same specific doctrine and is not corroborated by either.
