# ཕཊ། — The Mantra-Closing Syllable PHAṬ

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **On this packet's assembly:** ཕཊ is a cross-cutting keyword subject, not a
> registered spine slot, so `assemble_packet.py`/spine-map does not apply. Unlike
> the sibling pages `hum-syllable.md`, `mantra.md`, and `tara-mantra.md` (each
> assembled via a Signal-A keyword-tagging packet script), no such packet exists
> for ཕཊ at the time of writing. Per this run's explicit instruction, the packet
> was instead built directly: `rg -n 'ཕཊ'` was run across all 16 raw tree-guided
> files, every hit was located inside its own claim block (bounded by that
> block's own `#### c-...` heading through its `**Cite:**` line), and each block
> was mechanically extracted **character-for-character** by script from the
> commentary's own raw claims file — never retyped. One false positive was
> caught and excluded during this process: `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-1-3` does not
> itself mention ཕཊ; the term only appeared in this packet's first sweep because
> that claim's block boundary (next-heading-to-next-heading) swept in an
> intervening node-title line ("༧ ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏྲཌ་ཅེས་བྱ་དང་ཕཊ་ཀྱིས") that belongs to
> the *following* node, not to `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-1-3`'s own content — a second, narrower pass
> restricted to each block's own བོད་ཡིག/English fields removed it.

**Scope note — relationship to `hum-syllable.md`, `mantra.md`, and
`tara-mantra.md`:** ཕཊ (like ཧཱུྃ) is a generic tantric seed-syllable used across
countless deities' mantras and independently notable outside this text. Within
this corpus, nearly every attestation of ཕཊ occurs as part of one of two root
verses' mantra formulas — the "Destroyer of Others' Power" homage (TRAD/TRAṬ
and PHAṬ destroying the enemy's magical devices) and the poison-dispelling
homage (TĀRA twice + PHAṬ) — both already substantially covered, as plain
mantra-recitation and benefit content, by `tara-mantra.md`'s "Dispelling
poison" facet. Per this run's brief, this page does not re-derive that
consensus. It instead gathers every claim in the packet that says something
**about the syllable PHAṬ itself** — its word-gloss, its distinct function
alongside TRAD/TRAṬ, doctrinal/completion-stage/esoteric readings of it, a
reported exegetical divergence over its exact placement in the poison-mantra,
and its appearance as a bare mantra-closing syllable in ritual formulas outside
the root text's own Tārā mantra. Sixty claim blocks across all 16 commentaries
mention ཕཊ; roughly half restate the plain "TRAD/PHAṬ destroys magical
devices" or "TĀRA-TĀRA-PHAṬ dispels poison" content already established
elsewhere and are logged under "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" with a
cross-reference rather than re-cited here.

## TRAD/TRAṬ and PHAṬ, the wrathful mouth-sounds that destroy the enemy's magical devices

### Consensus
A wide consensus, spanning fourteen of the sixteen commentaries, glosses the
root verse "ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཏྲད་ཅེས་བྱ་དང་ཕཊ་ཀྱིས། ། ཕ་རོལ་འཁྲུལ་འཁོར་རབ་ཏུ་འཇོམས་མ།" as:
the two wrathful mouth-sounds TRAD/TRAṬ and PHAṬ, uttered from the goddess's
own mouth, utterly destroy the enemy's harmful magical device (and, in several
glosses, evil mantras, sorcery, and hail/thunderbolt weapons more broadly):

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-7-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-7-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-6-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-45`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-6-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-7-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-7-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-7-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-7-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-8-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-10-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-10-4`, `taranatha:c-7-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-7-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-24`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-10` (14 commentaries)

Only `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-6-4` glosses PHAṬ as a word in its own right,
rather than simply pairing it with TRAD/TRAṬ:

**བོད་ཡིག:** དང་ཕཊ་(འགས་པ་)ཅེས་བྱ་བའི་རིག་སྔགས་ཀྱི་ང་རོ་
**English:** — and "phaṭ," meaning "to burst": the sound of this vidyā-mantra.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-10` alone additionally derives from this verse the
goddess's epithet at this homage, "Gtum-mo gzhan-gyis-mi-thub-ma" ("Fierce One
Unconquerable by Others"), and extends the destroyed target to "war, hail, and
battle-machinery" plus "harmful afflictions, dön-spirits, and demons" — a
broader target list than the plain "enemy's magical device" most other
commentaries name; no other commentary in the packet corroborates this
specific extension.

### ⚑ Divergences
`taranatha:c-15-10` and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-26` assign TRAD and PHAṬ opposite,
mutually exclusive specific functions. Taranatha:

**བོད་ཡིག:** གོང་དུ་ཟློག་པ་ལ་ཏྲཊ་དང་། བསད་པ་ལ་ཕཊ་དུ་གསུངས་པས།
**English:** As stated above, TRAD [is used] for repelling (zlog pa) and PHAṬ for slaying (bsad pa).
(`taranatha:c-15-10`)

Pema Namgyal, glossing the same pairing in a deity-mantra context (OṂ TĀRE
TRAD PHAṬ SVĀHĀ):

**བོད་ཡིག:** བསད་སྔགས་ཏྲད་ཅེས་བྱ་དང་ཟློག་སྔགས་ཕཊ་ཀྱིས
**English:** through the killing-syllable (bsad) TRAD and the averting-syllable (zlog) PHAṬ
(`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-26`)

The two assignments are a direct reversal of each other — Taranatha holds
TRAD = repelling / PHAṬ = slaying; Pema Namgyal holds TRAD = slaying / PHAṬ =
repelling/averting. Neither claim names or responds to the other commentary,
so this is recorded as two independently-held, mutually incompatible readings
rather than a debate either author frames as such. No other commentary in the
packet assigns TRAD and PHAṬ separately-named functions at all — every other
attestation above treats the pair jointly as a single destructive utterance.

### Unique
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-7-4` — reports (and endorses, over an alternative he
  says occurred to him but did not write) a mahāsiddha's phowa (transference of
  consciousness) reading of this same verse: "tad" is, per an unnamed
  purification tantra, a mantra-syllable destroying lower rebirths, and "phaṭ"
  is "a sign propelling consciousness upward" (རྣམ་ཤེས་གོང་དུ་འཕེན་པའི་བརྡ):

  **བོད་ཡིག:** གྲུབ་ཆེན་གྱིས་འདིའི་ལས་ཚོགས་ལ་འཕོ་བ་སྦྱར་འདུག་པ་ཊད་ཅེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་ངན་སོང་འཇོམས་པའི་སྔགས་སུ་སྦྱོང་རྒྱུད་ལས་གསུངས་པ་དང་། ཕཊ་ནི་རྣམ་ཤེས་གོང་དུ་འཕེན་པའི་བརྡ་ཡིན་པས་འདིའི་དགོངས་པར་བྱས་པ་ཡིན་འདྲ་བས།
  **English:** A mahāsiddha applies the transference of consciousness (phowa) to this section: "tad," per the purification tantra, is a mantra-syllable destroying lower rebirths; "phaṭ" is a sign propelling consciousness upward — this is taken as the verse's [hidden] intent.

  This is a distinctly esoteric reading, structurally analogous to but
  independent from the plain "destroys magical devices" consensus above; no
  other commentary in the packet corroborates a phowa-based reading of PHAṬ.

- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-7-3-1` — gives a completion-stage-with-marks (rtsa
  rlung) reading: TRAṬ ("rending") and PHAṬ ("cutting or shattering") mark the
  karmic and afflictive winds of the twelve links of dependent origination
  ceasing within the central channel, paired with the bent right Rasanā and
  extended left Lalanā channels.
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-7-4-1` — gives a further, completion-stage-without-marks
  (mtshan med) reading of the same syllables: TRAṬ and PHAṬ mark all dualistic
  grasping — hope, fear, subject-object clinging regarding saṃsāra and
  nirvāṇa — self-liberating like clouds dissolving into naked, empty awareness.
  These two readings (with-marks and without-marks) are presented by
  `palden-sherab` as a graduated pair of esoteric readings of the same verse,
  not corroborated by any other commentary in the packet.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-7-3` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-7-4` — give, in sequence,
  first an etymological gloss of TRAT as "liberating," i.e. destroying (rather
  than PHAṬ specifically), roared as a fierce cry from the Venerable Mother's
  mouth, and then, after restating the plain destructive sense, an explicit
  alternative gloss offered for PHAṬ itself ("yang na," "alternatively"):

  **བོད་ཡིག:** ཡང་ན་ཕཊ་ཀྱིས་ཉོན་མོངས་དང་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཆོམས་ཤིག་ཅེས་པའམ། སྟོང་ཉིད་རྟོགས་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་དང་། བདེ་བ་ཆེན་པོའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་གཉིས་ངོ་བོ་དབྱེར་མེད་དུ་སྦྱོར་བ་མཚོན་པའི་དོན་རེད།
  **English:** Alternatively, PHAṬ means "destroy all afflictions and suffering!" or signifies uniting the wisdom realizing emptiness and the wisdom of great bliss inseparably in essence.

  No other commentary in the packet offers either of these two alternative
  glosses of PHAṬ.

## Divergence over PHAṬ's exact placement in the poison-dispelling mantra

### ⚑ Divergences
On the "holds a deer-marked moon" homage's mantra ("ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེས།
དུག་རྣམས་མ་ལུས་པར་ནི་སེལ་མ།"), `yama-sonam:c-3-18-6` reports a live
disagreement among four named masters over exactly how PHAṬ attaches to the
ten-syllable root mantra:

**བོད་ཡིག:** ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་ཅན་གྱི་རིག་སྔགས་ལ། ཨོཾ་ན་མཿ ཏཱ་རེ་ན་མོ་ཏཱ་རེ་ཧཱུྃ་ཧ་རེ་སྭཱཧཱ། ཞེས་པ་འདི་དང་། མཐར་རྟེན་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེ་སརྦ་བི་ཛཿ ཤནྟིངྐུ་རུ་ཕཊ་ཅེས་པ་འདི་ཡིན་པར་ཇོ་ནང་པས་གསུངས། དངུལ་ཆུས་ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཀྱིས་མཚོན་པའི་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པའི་སྭཱཧཱའི་གནས་སུ་ཕཊ་ཅེས་སྦྱར་བའི་ཡི་གེ་བཟླས་པ་ལ་བྱེད་པར་གསུངས། ཅོ་ནེས་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པ་དང་བཅས་པའི་ཏཱ་ར་ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཅིང་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེས་ཞེས་ཏཱ་ར་ལན་གཉིས་བརྗོད་པ་དང་། མཐའ་རྟེན་ཕཊ་ཡིག་ཀྱང་ཕཊ་ཡིག་རྐྱང་པ་རང་ལ་མཛད་སྣང་ཡང་། རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ་པས་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཚིག་ཟུར་ལྟར། ངག་ཏུ་ཏཱ་ར་གཉིས་བརྗོད་ཕཊ་ཀྱི་ཡི་གེས། །ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལྟར་ཏཱ་ར་ཏཱ་ར་ཕཊ་ཅེས་ཏཱ་ར་བཟླས་ཏེ་ལན་གཉིས་ཀྱི་མཐར་ཕཊ་ཅེས་བཏགས་པའི་རིག་སྔགས་ལ་བྱེད་ན་རྒྱུད་ཚིག་དང་མཐུན་ནོ།
**English:** As for the vidyā-mantra containing "tāra" twice: Jonangpa states it is "OṂ NAMAḤ TĀRE NAMO TĀRE HŪṂ HARE SVĀHĀ," with the final-support syllable PHAṬ being part of "SARVA VIJAḤ ŚĀNTIṄ KURU PHAṬ." Ngulchu states it should be applied to reciting the ten-syllable [mantra] symbolised by the double "tāra," with PHAṬ attached in place of SVĀHĀ. Co ne pa appears to take it as the ten syllables together with the double "TĀRA TĀRA," with "by the letter PHAṬ" as a bare single PHAṬ serving as final support. Rje Dge 'dun grub, however, following the tantra's own marginal wording, states it as "TĀRA TĀRA PHAṬ," reciting "tāra" twice and appending PHAṬ at the end; applying the vidyā-mantra this way accords with the tantra's own wording.
(`yama-sonam:c-3-18-6`)

Re-reading this packet's own claims against those four reported positions
(per Rule 10 of this skill): two of the four are independently corroborated
in this packet, one by the same author's own commentary directly, one by the
matching registered commentary's own gloss; the other two are not
corroborated by any claim in this packet.

- **Ngulchu's position** ("PHAṬ attached in place of SVĀHĀ") is directly
  corroborated by `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-3-5` — and `dharmabhadra`'s own
  frontmatter names its author as Ngulchu Dharmabhadra, i.e. this is the same
  author's own commentary independently stating the position
  `yama-sonam` attributes to him:

## PHAṬ as a generic ritual mantra-closing syllable outside the root text's own mantra

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak` is the only commentary in this packet whose source text
extends past the root twenty-one homages into an appended sādhana/ritual
manual (a fact independently visible from its own claims, e.g. its five-part
practice-manual node structure). Within that appended material, PHAṬ recurs
three further times as a generic mantra-closing syllable in ritual formulas
that have nothing to do with the root text's own Tārā mantra:

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-2-1` — blessing the offering-substances with a
  wrathful nectar-swirl mantra ending in PHAṬ:

  **བོད་ཡིག:** ཨོཾ་བཛྲ་ཨ་མྲི་ཏ་ཀུནྜ་ལི་ཧ་ན་ཧན་ཧཱུྃཕཊ།
  **English:** OṂ VAJRA AMṚTA KUṆḌALI HANA HANA HŪṂ PHAṬ — the wrathful nectar-swirl mantra, blessing the offering-substances.

- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-2-1` — the same wrathful nectar-swirl mantra
  ("OṂ VAJRA AMṚTA KUṆḌALĪ HANA HANA HŪṂ PHAṬ"), reused to sprinkle and bless
  the torma offering and clear obstructors, before the torma-substance is
  separately purified into emptiness with a different, PHAṬ-free mantra.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-2-3` — a further torma-offering mantra to the
  retinue and guests ending in PHAṬ:

  **བོད་ཡིག:** ཨོཾ་ཨ་ཀཱ་རོ་མུ་ཁཾ་སརྦ་དྷརྨ་ཎཾ་ཨདྱ་ནུཏྤནྣ་དྷ། ཨོཾ་ཨཿཧཱུྃ་ཕཊ་སྭཱ་ཧཱ
  **English:** "OṂ AKĀRO MUKHAṂ SARVA DHARMĀṆĀM ĀDYANUTPANNATVĀT OṂ ĀḤ HŪṂ PHAṬ SVĀHĀ," recited while offering to the retinue and guests.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-7-1-3` restates the same offering-blessing mantra again at
a later node, explicitly cross-referenced in its own gloss to the verses just
cited above ("cross-referenced to the identical verses of work 5"), so it is
not cited separately here (see "Claims reviewed, not separately cited").
Taken together, these three instances are the packet's clearest direct
evidence for this term's own stated notability rationale — that PHAṬ is "a
generic tantric mantra-closing seed-syllable used across countless deities'
mantras" — since none of them involve the Tārā mantra at all.
