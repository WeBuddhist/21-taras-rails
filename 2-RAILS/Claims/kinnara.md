## Questions asked

1. Does each commentary name kinnaras together with gods among those who rely upon and serve Tārā in the "armor of joy dispels quarrels and bad dreams" verse, whether by direct quotation or by gloss/paraphrase — and how does each commentary's own tree number that verse?
2. Do multiple commentaries identify a specific named exemplar or king of the kinnaras, and is that naming consistent across them?
3. anon-utpala, lobsang-dawa, and tenzin-dhonzang each qualify the kinnara exemplar as "horse-headed" (རྟ་མགོ་ཅན) — do gendun-drub's and drakpa-gyaltsen's parallel namings of "the Tree" (ལྗོན་པ) corroborate this as the same figure, or name a different one?
4. Do commentaries that enumerate "the seven worlds" (a separate doctrinal context tied to the TUTTĀRA HŪṂ subjugation verse) include kinnara among the seven, and do they agree on the full enumeration?

## Textual attestation — kinnara among those who rely upon and serve Tārā (the "armor of joy" verse)

### Consensus
A verse of homage — "Homage to her who is relied upon by the king of the hosts of gods, by gods and kinnaras; who, by the splendour of joyful armor on every side, dispels quarrels and bad dreams" — is quoted, paraphrased, or glossed by every one of the sixteen commentaries in this packet. The standard form of the verse:

ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་རྒྱལ་པོ།། ལྷ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི་ཡིས་བསྟེན་མ།། ཀུན་ནས་གོ་ཆ་དགའ་བའི་བརྗིད་ཀྱིས།། རྩོད་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་སེལ་མ།།

Quoted (root-verse form): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` (7 commentaries quote the verse itself; tsultrim-namdak quotes it twice, in two different sections)

Glossed/paraphrased (identifying kinnara as among those who serve/rely upon Tārā in this same verse, without independently re-quoting the full stanza): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-91`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3`, `taranatha:c-19-5`, `taranatha:c-19-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` (15 further attestations)

— attested overall: 15 of 16 commentaries (all except pema-namgyal, whose related but textually distinct claim is recorded separately below).

**Note on verse numbering:** most commentaries in this packet place this verse at their own tree's "19" (e.g. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `taranatha:c-19-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`). Two commentaries number the equivalent content "20" in their own trees (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, "Verse 20 quoted and glossed"; `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, node `6.1`/"20"), and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`'s own claim heading reads "Verse 41" (likely a folio- or line-based count internal to that commentary's own apparatus rather than a stanza count). This is recorded as an observed difference in each commentary's own enumeration scheme, not a doctrinal disagreement about the verse's content — no commentary disputes what the verse says.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on whether kinnara belongs in this verse's fourfold-plus-host list of reverers — only on further details (exemplar naming, seven-worlds enumeration) treated in the facets below.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50` gives a related but textually distinct identification: "even the kings of the assembled gods of the desire and form realms — Brahmā, Indra, and the like — together with the gods, humans, and kinnara-kings belonging to their retinues... revere even the mere dust-particle of her feet." This shares the general motif (gods and kinnaras revering Tārā) but lacks the "armor of joy / dispels quarrels and bad dreams" language of the verse quoted above and additionally names "humans" among the reverers, so it is not folded into the Consensus attestation list above as though it were the same verse citation (Rule 9/10) — it may reflect this commentary's own restructuring of the surrounding verses rather than an independent quotation of the same stanza.

## Named exemplar of the kinnaras — "the Tree" (ལྗོན་པ), sometimes qualified "horse-headed" (རྟ་མགོ་ཅན)

### Consensus
Five commentaries, glossing the same "gods and kinnaras" verse above, go further and name a specific figure as exemplar or king of the kinnaras, called ལྗོན་པ ("the Tree") or ལྗོན་པ་ཆེན་པོ ("the Great Tree"):

- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`: "ལྷ་དང་མིའམ་ཅིའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལྗོན་པ་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པས" ("the great tree-king of the gods and kinnaras and so forth").
- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-91`: "ལྷ་ཚོགས་དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་དཔོན་ནམ་མི་འམ་ཅི་ཡི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ལྗོན་པ་ལ་སོགས་པས" ("the lord of that host of gods, or the king of the kinnaras, [named] the Tree, and so on").
- `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-3`: "མིའམ་ཅི་ལྗོན་པ་རྟ་མགོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས" ("kinnaras — [such as] the Tree, the horse-headed ones, and so forth").
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`: "མིའམ་ཅི་སྟེ་ལྗོན་པ་རྟ་མགོ་ལ་སོགས་པ" ("kinnaras, i.e., the Tree, the horse-headed ones, and so on").
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`: "མི་འམ་ཅི་ཡིས་ལྗོན་པའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་རྟ་མགོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས" ("kinnaras — the tree-king Horse-Head, and the like").

(5 commentaries.)

Three of the five (anon-utpala, lobsang-dawa, tenzin-dhonzang) additionally qualify this same named exemplar as རྟ་མགོ(་ཅན) ("horse-headed"), listing "the Tree" and "horse-headed" side by side as descriptors of the kinnara host's exemplar rather than as two different figures; two of the five (gendun-drub, drakpa-gyaltsen) name only "the [Great] Tree" without the horse-headed qualifier. No commentary states that these are two different beings, and no commentary contests another's naming — read together, these five claims converge on one named figure under two overlapping descriptions, so this is recorded as Consensus rather than Divergence (Rule 4 in reverse: this is a case where apparent variation is not a genuine disagreement).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — see the harmonization note above.

### Unique
None beyond the five claims above.

## Kinnara among enumerations of "the seven worlds" (a separate doctrinal context)

A different verse in this praise — the one built on the syllables TUTTĀRA HŪṂ, describing "the seven worlds trodden underfoot" and subjugated by mantra-power — occasions a second, structurally separate set of claims about what "the seven worlds" comprise. Several commentaries include kinnara in this sevenfold list; others, per palden-sherab's own report, do not.

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently give near-identical sevenfold enumerations that include kinnara:

- `taranatha:c-5-6`: "ཀླུ་དང་ཡི་དྭགས་དང་ལྷ་མ་ཡིན་དང་། །མི་དང་རིག་པ་འཛིན་པ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི་དང་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་ནོ།" ("nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādharas, kinnaras, and the world of the devas").
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`: "ཀླུ། ཡི་དྭགས། ལྷ་མ་ཡིན།རིག་འཛིན། མི། མིའམ་ཅི། ལྷ་སྟེ་བདུན་པོ" ("nāgas, pretas, asuras, vidyādharas, humans, kinnaras, and devas — seven").
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18`: "ཀླུ་དང་ ཡི་དྭགས་ལྷ་མ་ཡིན། མི་དང་རིག་པ་འཛིན་པ་མིའམ་ཅི་དང་ལྷའི་འཇིག་རྟེན་རྣམས" ("nāgas, pretas, asuras, humans, vidyādhara-kinnaras, and the worlds of the gods").

— attested: `taranatha:c-5-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-6-0-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-18` (3 commentaries; re-read together, all three name the same seven classes — nāga, preta, asura, human, vidyādhara, kinnara, deva — in near-identical order, a genuine independent corroboration, not merely three commentaries each choosing "kinnara" while differing on the rest).

### ⚑ Divergences
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-3` (an internal-tension claim already flagged ⚑ within that commentary's own raw file) reports three named scholars giving three *different* enumerations of "the seven worlds," only one of which names kinnara:

- Position 1 (per palden-sherab's report of Drakpa Gyaltsen): "the five classes of migrating beings together with the two higher realms, form and formless — thus seven" — no kinnara named. (This is consistent with the fact that Drakpa Gyaltsen's own raw claims file in this packet contains no independent "seven worlds" enumeration of its own.)
- Position 2 (per palden-sherab's report of Tāranātha): "nāgas, pretas, asuras, vidyādharas, humans, kinnaras, and gods — seven" — kinnara named. Re-reading Tāranātha's own raw file (see Tāranātha's own enumeration under Consensus above) confirms this report: Tāranātha's own claim independently matches the same seven classes (order: nāga/preta/asura/human/vidyādhara/kinnara/deva vs. palden-sherab's paraphrase order nāga/preta/asura/vidyādhara/human/kinnara/deva — same seven members, human and vidyādhara transposed).
- Position 3 (per palden-sherab's report of Gendun Gyatso): "the six classes of migrating beings together with the intermediate state (bardo)" — no kinnara named.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-5-1-3` (1 commentary reporting on three named scholars; two of the three reported positions, per this same claim, do not include kinnara at all, so "the seven worlds" is not a fixed formula across the tradition and its kinnara-inclusive form is one enumeration among several, not the only one).

Separately, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-6` — a textual-critical note on captions reading "seven worlds" found on Tibetan images of Tārā — reports that "many mutually inconsistent explanations circulate without clear source" for what the seven worlds are, listing as one of several candidate explanations "preta, nāga, human, vidyādhara, kinnara, asura, and the deva-world, making seven," but concludes that the "seven worlds" caption tradition itself "appears to be a scribal error" (ཡིག་ནོར). This is the packet's most skeptical voice on the entire "seven worlds" question, bearing on all the enumerations above: even where a kinnara-inclusive sevenfold list is independently corroborated (Consensus, above), one commentary in this same packet regards the underlying "seven worlds" framing as possibly a textual corruption rather than an authoritative doctrinal count.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Tantric completion-stage symbolism: kinnara (with deva) as the channels Rasanā and Lalanā

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-3-1` gives a completion-stage-with-marks ("hidden meaning") reading of the "gods and kinnaras" verse: "ལྷའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ནི་རྩ་དབུ་མ། ལྷ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི་ནི་རོ་རྐྱང་གཉིས།" ("'the king of the divine hosts' is the central channel; 'gods and kinnaras' are the two, Rasanā and Lalanā"). The same commentary's further completion-stage-without-marks ("ultimate") reading, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-4-1`, glosses "served by gods and kinnaras" as "the great two accumulations, merit and wisdom, perfected in that state." No other commentary in this packet gives a tantric symbolic equivalence for kinnara specifically — this is distinct from the separate fourfold bhūta/vetāla/gandharva/yakṣa completion-stage mapping (gandharva = wind) recorded in `2-RAILS/Claims/gandharva.md`'s "Tantric completion-stage symbolism" facet, which belongs to a different verse and a different fourfold list.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — single-commentary claim.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim.

## Kinnara-harm within a broader taxonomy of harmful spirits (shared with gandharva)

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-6`, quoting the White Parasol (Sitatapātra) dhāraṇī rather than the root praise itself, lists kinnara-harm (མིའམ་ཅིའི་[གདོན]) as one of twenty-eight named classes of harmful spirit — alongside deva-, nāga-, asura-, wind-deity-, garuḍa-, gandharva-, great-serpent-, yakṣa-, rākṣasa-, and preta-harms, among others — from which the practitioner seeks protection. This is the same claim block already cited in `2-RAILS/Claims/gandharva.md`'s "Gandharva within a broader taxonomy of harmful spirits" facet for the gandharva-harm half of the identical list; see that page for the full quotation and context rather than repeating it here. No other commentary's claims in this packet situate kinnara within this broader taxonomy.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Consensus
None — single-commentary claim (shared block, cross-referenced above).
