## Textual attestation — bhūta among the fourfold host who praise Tārā (verse 6)

### Consensus
The verse describing Tārā's worship by the great mundane gods (Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, Maheśvara/Sarveśvara) also names hosts of **bhūta**, vetāla, gandharva, and yakṣa as beings who bow and praise her "from in front":

འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། །གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།
("...praised in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas.")

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-40`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-9`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`, `taranatha:c-6-9`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21` (15 commentaries)

## Identity of bhūta — four converging and diverging namings

### Consensus
None as a single shared statement — every commentary that goes beyond bare textual attestation to explain what a bhūta actually *is* gives one of four positions below; several converge closely within a position, but no single wording is shared across all of them.

### ⚑ Divergences

**Position A — "lord of hosts" (ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ) heading a class of bgegs (obstructing spirits) — cross-referenced to `2-RAILS/Claims/obstacle.md`'s own facet, not re-derived here:**
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-2`: "བགེགས་སོགས་འབྱུང་པོ" ("the obstructing-spirit elementals").
- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-2`: "འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་... བགེགས་དང་ལོག་འདྲེན" ("'bhūtas' are ... obstructing and misleading spirits").
- `taranatha:c-6-9`: "འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པ་བགེགས་དང་ལོགས་པར་འདྲེན་པ་རྣམས་སོ།" ("Bhūtas are the 'lord of hosts' and others — obstructors who lead [beings] astray").
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21`: "འབྱུང་པོ་ཞེས་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་གཙོས་པའི་བགེགས་ཀྱི་རིགས" ("the class of obstructing spirits headed by the lord of the bhūta hosts").

(4 commentaries — see `obstacle.md`'s "བགེགས as a technical gloss for 'byung po (bhūta)" facet for the full analysis of this position.)

**Position B — the same "lord of hosts" epithet, explicitly named as Gaṇapati (ཚོགས་བདག / ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ), without the word bgegs:**
- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-40`: "འབྱུང་པོ་ནི་ཚོགས་བདག་ལ་སོགས་པའོ།" ("'bhūtas' means [beings] such as Gaṇapati").
- `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "འབྱུང་པོའི་གཙོ་བོ་ཚོགས་བདག" ("Gaṇapati, chief of the bhūtas").
- `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-2`: "ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པའི་འབྱུང་པོ་རྣམས" ("the hosts of spirits headed by Gaṇapati").
- `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-25`: "ཚོགས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པའི་འབྱུང་པོ" ("spirits such as Gaṇapati" — from the benefit-of-prostration verse, not the fourfold-host verse itself).

(4 commentaries.)

Positions A and B share the identical Tibetan epithet ("lord of hosts") for bhūta's chief, naming him either as Gaṇapati by ordinary gloss (Position B) or, in the same breath, as head of the bgegs-class (Position A). The page observes (this is the page's own reading, not asserted by any single claim) that Gaṇapati is conventionally treated in Tibetan tantric literature as "Lord of Obstacles" as well as "Lord of Hosts," so these may be one tradition read two ways rather than a genuine doctrinal split — but no claim in this packet states that harmonization itself, so the two remain distinct positions per Rule 4.

**Position C — bhūta as retinue of, or directly equated with, "dbang ldan" (the Powerful One, i.e. Viṣṇu):**
- `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-5`: "དབང་ལྡན་གྱི་འཁོར་འབྱུང་པོ་རྣམས" ("the bhūtas, retinue of 'the powerful one'").
- `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "དབང་ལྡན་གྱི་འཁོར་འབྱུང་པོ" ("the bhūtas — retinue of Vishnu (dbang ldan)").
- `sungrab-tulku:c-9-7`: "དབང་ལྡན་གྱི་འཁོར་འབྱུང་པོ" ("the bhūta spirits, retinue of Īśvara").
- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-3`: "འབྱུང་བོ་སྟེ་དབང་ལྡན" ("the powerful elemental spirit," i.e. bhūta equated directly with dbang ldan rather than as its retinue — a variant of the same naming).

(4 commentaries.)

**Position D — bhūta as a preta-class being wandering the sky, tormented by fear and suffering — a doctrinal/descriptive characterization rather than a named exemplar:**
- `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`: "འཇིགས་སྐྲག་སྡུག་བསྔལ་གྱིས་དུས་རྟག་ཏུ་མནར་བའི་མཁའ་ལ་རྒྱུ་བའི་ཡི་དྭགས་ཀྱི་རིགས་སུ་གྱུར་པའི་འབྱུང་པོ" ("Spirits (bhūta) of the preta class who wander the sky, forever tormented by fear and suffering").

(1 commentary.) No other commentary in this packet gives this same characterization; it is not stated as contradicting Positions A–C (a preta-class being could in principle still be led by Gaṇapati or dbang ldan), but no claim states that harmonization either.

**No identification given, bare listing only:** `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-6`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-9`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4`.

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## Tantric completion-stage symbolism: bhūta as the channels (རྩ)

### Consensus
Two commentaries give an identical fourfold completion-stage ("hidden meaning") mapping of the same fourfold host onto inner elements, agreeing precisely on bhūta = channel:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`, `taranatha:c-6-17` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-2`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ་དང་། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་དང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" ("'bhūtas' are the channels, 'vetālas' are the drop, 'gandharvas' are the wind, 'yakṣas' are conceptual thought"). `taranatha:c-6-17`: "འབྱུང་པོ་རྩ། རོ་ལངས་ཐིག་ལེ། དྲི་ཟ་རླུང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་རྣམ་རྟོག་སྟེ།" ("bhūta is the channel; vetāla is the drop; gandharva is the wind; yakṣa is conceptual thought").
