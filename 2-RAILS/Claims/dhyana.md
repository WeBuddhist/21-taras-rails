## Questions asked

1. Where and how is concentration (བསམ་གཏན, dhyāna) named among the six pāramitās in the commentarial tradition on this verse, and do all commentaries attest it?
2. Do any commentaries frame concentration as the antidote to a specific mental fault?
3. Do any commentaries give a completion-stage / secret-meaning reading of concentration distinct from the plain six-pāramitā listing?
4. Do any commentaries situate concentration within an extended ten-pāramitā / ten-bhūmi scheme, rather than the six-pāramitā scheme?
5. Do any commentaries subdivide concentration into further types?
6. What canonical/scriptural sources, outside this verse, do commentaries cite to define or elaborate concentration?
7. Do any commentaries use the term བསམ་གཏན in a sense broader than the six-pāramitā doctrinal category — e.g. generic meditative absorption?

## Position among the six pāramitās

### Consensus
Every one of the sixteen commentaries names བསམ་གཏན (dhyāna, "concentration") as one of the six pāramitās (ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག — generosity, diligence, discipline ["austerity," དཀའ་ཐུབ], wisdom ["peace," ཞི་བ], patience, and concentration) that the root verse for this homage presents as Tārā's own domain of conduct (སྤྱོད་ཡུལ), either by quoting the root verse directly — "སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། །བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" — or by paraphrasing/glossing the same six-item list in their own exegesis.
— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-20`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `taranatha:c-3-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6` (16 commentaries)

Palden Sherab additionally restates the same list, with the six pāramitās explicitly tied to "the ten powers" (དབང་བཅུ) of a bodhisattva ocean-like in conduct — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`.

Drakpa Gyaltsen's gloss is the minimal, circular form of this same attestation: "བསམ་གཏན་ ནི་ 8 བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའོ།" ("Concentration is the perfection of concentration") — `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-20`.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed. No commentary excludes concentration from the six-item list or substitutes a different term for it.

### Unique
None — this facet is universally attested (see Consensus).

## Concentration as antidote to mental distraction

### Consensus
Sungrab Tulku and Tenga Tulku both frame each of the six pāramitās as the specific antidote (གཉེན་པོ) to a specific fault, with concentration named as the antidote to distraction/scatteredness (རྣམ་གཡེང):

Sungrab Tulku: "རྣམ་གཡེང་གི་གཉེན་པོ་བསམ་གཏན་ཏེ་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་སྤྱོད་..." ("concentration, the antidote to distraction, [is one of] the six pāramitās practiced...") — `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`.

Tenga Tulku, in an extended discussion of the six pāramitās as each being "unstained" (མ་གོས་པ) by its corresponding fault: "བསམ་གཏན་ལ་རྣམ་གཡེང་གི་སྐྱོན་གྱིས་མ་གོས་པ།" ("concentration is unstained by the fault of distraction") — `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`.

Tenzin Dhonzang independently reaches the same identification of concentration's opposing fault, framed as an "enemy" (དགྲ་བོ) rather than a fault the pāramitā avoids — see Unique, below.
— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-6-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7` (3 commentaries, on the shared point that distraction is concentration's opposing fault)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Tenzin Dhonzang elaborates this into a fuller doctrinal statement, naming concentration's "enemies" as agitation and dullness/distraction together, drawing the parallel to the other five pāramitās' own named enemies (miserliness for generosity, immorality for discipline, anger for patience, laziness for diligence): "བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་དགྲ་རྒོད་པ་དང་། རྣམ་གཡེང་། བྱིང་བའོ།" ("concentration's enemies are agitation, distraction, and dullness/laxity") — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-7`.

## Completion-stage / secret-meaning: concentration as bliss

### Consensus
Two commentaries — independently, in their treatment of the same root verse's "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན/གསང་དོན) — read the union of lotus and vajra in the verse's iconography as encoding the six pāramitās in completion-stage terms, with concentration identified as the essence of bliss (བདེ་བ):

Palden Sherab: "...བདེ་བའི་ངོ་བོ་བསམ་གཏན་དང་། བདེ་གསལ་སྟོང་ཉིད་རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པའི་ཤེས་རབ་དང་..." — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`.

Tāranātha: "...བདེ་པའི་བསམ་གཏན་དང་། སྟོང་ཉིད་རྟོགས་པས་རྣམ་རྟོག་ཞི་བའི་ཤེས་རབ་དང་..." — `taranatha:c-3-6`.

Both pass the same content: concentration paired with bliss, immediately followed in both by wisdom paired with the pacification of conceptuality through realizing emptiness — confirming this is a shared, not merely coincidentally similar, esoteric reading.
— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`, `taranatha:c-3-6` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed. This esoteric reading does not contradict the plain six-pāramitā reading in the first facet above; both commentaries present it as an additional layer of meaning (སྦས་དོན / གསང་དོན) over the same verse, not a replacement for the exoteric reading.

### Unique
None beyond the two-commentary consensus above.

## Extended ten-pāramitā / ten-bhūmi correlation

### Consensus
Two commentaries, in commentary on a different verse of the praise (the homage to Uṣṇīṣavijayā), extend the six-pāramitā scheme to the standard Mahāyāna ten pāramitās mapped onto the ten bodhisattva bhūmis, with concentration as the fifth listed:

Palden Sherab: "ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་ནི། སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དང་། བཟོད་པ་དང་། བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་དང་། ཐབས་དང་། སྟོབས་དང་། སྨོན་ལམ་དང་། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་ནོ།" — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4`.

Tenzin Dhonzang: "...སྦྱིན་པ་དང་། ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས། བཟོད་པ། བརྩོན་འགྲུས། བསམ་གཏན། ཤེས་རབ། ཐབས་མཁས། སྨོན་ལམ། སྟོབས། ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་ཕར་ཕྱིན་..." — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8`.
— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed between the two full ten-item attestations.

### Unique
Anon (Trinle Char)'s etymology gloss for the same root verse discussed in the first facet names the domain of conduct "the ten pāramitās" (ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ) while explicitly enumerating only the standard six items (generosity, diligence, discipline, wisdom, patience, concentration): "...བཟོད་པ་དང་། བསམ་གཏན་བཅས་ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་དུ་བྱས་ནས་..." — `yama-sonam:c-3-3-6`. This is flagged, not resolved: the claim's own wording says "ten" while listing six items, and no other commentary corroborates a ten-item list at this particular verse (the two genuine ten-item attestations above are commenting on a different verse). The page reports this discrepancy as it stands in the source rather than silently correcting "བཅུ" to "དྲུག" or silently treating it as a corroboration of the ten-pāramitā facet.

## Threefold subdivision of concentration

### Consensus
Not applicable — single-commentary attestation only.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Tenzin Dhonzang alone subdivides concentration (alongside each of the other five pāramitās) into three types: "བསམ་གཏན་ལ་ཚེ་འདི་ལ་ལུས་སེམས་བདེ་བར་གནས་པར་བྱེད་པའི་བསམ་གཏན། ཡོན་ཏན་མངོན་པར་སྒྲུབ་པའི་བསམ་གཏན། སེམས་ཅན་དོན་བྱེད་པའི་བསམ་གཏན་དང་གསུམ་རྣམས་སོ།" ("concentration that brings physical and mental ease in this life; concentration that actualizes qualities; and concentration that benefits sentient beings — these three") — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-6`.

## Canonical citations elaborating concentration

### Consensus
Not applicable — each citation below is drawn from a different scriptural source by a different single commentary; none is corroborated by a second commentary citing the same source for the same point.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are complementary elaborations, not competing definitions.

### Unique
Tenzin Dhonzang cites three separate canonical/scriptural sources on concentration, outside the root verse itself:

- The *Lamrim Nyams Gur* ("Songs of Experience on the Stages of the Path") on śamatha as sovereign over the mind: "བསམ་གཏན་སེམས་ལ་དབང་བསྒྱུར་རྒྱལ་པོ་སྟེ། །བཞག་ན་གཡོ་མེད་རི་ཡི་དབང་པོ་བཞིན་། །བཏང་ན་དགེ་བའི་དམིགས་པ་ཀུན་ལ་འཇུག །ལུས་སེམས་ལས་སུ་རུང་བའི་བདེ་ཆེན་འདྲེན།" — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-12`, with a continuation warning that one-pointed concentration alone cannot cut samsara's root without being united with insight: "རྩེ་གཅིག་བསམ་གཏན་ཙམ་ལ་འཁོར་བ་ཡི། །རྩ་བ་གཅོད་པའི་ནུས་པ་མ་མཐོང་ཞིང་།" — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-13`.
- The *Abhidharmakośa* on the sequence from ethical discipline through hearing to meditation: "ཚུལ་གནས་ཐོས་དང་བསམ་གཏན་པས། །བསྒོམ་པ་ནི་རབ་ཏུ་སྦྱོར།" — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-30`.
- The *Guru Puja* (བླ་མཆོད) on one-pointed equipoise in emptiness as the completion of the pāramitā of concentration: "རྩེ་གཅིག་མཉམ་པར་འཇོགས་པའི་ཏིང་འཛིན་གྱིས། །བསམ་གཏན་ཕར་ཕྱིན་རྫོགས་པར་བྱིན་གྱིས་རློབས།" — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-13-10`.

Palden Sherab cites the *Sūtra of the Questions of Brahmaviśeṣacintin* (ཚངས་པ་ཁྱད་པར་སེམས་ཀྱིས་ཞུས་པའི་མདོ), which defines each of the six pāramitās by way of an absence: "མི་སེམས་པ་ནི་བསམ་གཏན་ནོ།" ("non-conceptualizing/not-thinking is concentration") — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-2`.

## Broader, non-pāramitā uses of the term

### Consensus
Not applicable — each use below is a single-commentary attestation, and the two commentators use the term in different senses from one another.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are not competing claims but two distinct, non-conflicting extended uses of the same word outside the six-pāramitā doctrinal sense that is the topic's main subject.

### Unique
Tāranātha uses བསམ་གཏན to name the practitioner's own generation-stage meditative practice of self-generating as Tārā, placing the three seed-syllables at the three places: "སྒྲོལ་མའི་སྐུར་བསྐྱེད་པའི་བསམ་གཏན་ལ། གནས་གསུམ་དུ་ཡི་གེ་འབྲུ་གསུམ་གི་དེ་ཉིད་བཀོད་པས།..." — `taranatha:c-21-2-2`. Elsewhere Tāranātha also uses "བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་ཡན་ལག" ("branch of concentration") for a stage of mantra-recitation practice that destroys the body of impure appearance: "དགྲ་སྟེ་མ་དག་པའི་སྣང་བའི་ལུས་འཇོམས་པར་བྱེད་དོ། །ཅེས་པ་བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་ཡན་ལག་དང་།" — `taranatha:c-16-9`.

Tsultrim Namdak uses the term in its generic sense of "meditative absorption" in a poetic verse of iconographic praise: an accomplished sage's meditative absorption is said to be stirred by the mere sight of Tārā's toenails — "མཐོང་བ་ཙམ་གྱིས་བསམ་གཏན་དཀྲོགས་མཛད་མ།" — `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-11`.

## Claims reviewed, not separately cited

Not cited a second time to avoid double-counting one commentary's attestation of the same facet.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-5-3-11` — the colophon, which names the hermitage "བསམ་གཏན་ལྡིང་ཁང" ("Samten Ding," lit. "concentration-perch") where the commentary was composed. A place-name built from the word, not a doctrinal usage of the term; excluded from all facets above.
- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5` — "her arising from the practice of the six perfections," a paraphrase of the same root verse already covered by this commentary's direct-quote citation `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-2` in "Position among the six pāramitās." Not cited a second time to avoid double-counting one commentary's attestation of the same facet.
- `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-12` — an earlier, general-overview restatement of the same six-pāramitā list ("...བཟོད་པ། བསམ་གཏན་ཏེ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག...") already represented by this commentary's verse-level citation `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6` in "Position among the six pāramitās." Not cited a second time to avoid double-counting one commentary's attestation of the same facet.
