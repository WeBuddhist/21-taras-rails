# Conqueror — རྒྱལ་བ།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that
> pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and *conqueror as a
> buddha-epithet* is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead, like the sibling
> pages `mantra`, `gdon`, `mara`, `vetala`, `wheel`, `pacification`,
> `three-jewels`, `ushnisha`, `hum-syllable`, and `tara-mantra`. No deterministic
> assembly script exists for this topic, so the gather step was done directly:
> `rg "རྒྱལ་བ"` against all sixteen `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files,
> followed by reading each hit's full claim block (heading through the next
> heading) to judge relevance, copying **བོད་ཡིག** text character-for-character
> from the raw file — never retyped or paraphrased from memory.
>
> **Scope note — one term, two grammatical senses.** རྒྱལ་བ is common in this
> corpus in two distinct functions: (1) a **substantive epithet** for buddhas
> ("the Conqueror(s)," Skt. *jina*), standing alone or in compounds such as
> རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ ("mother of the Victors"), རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས ("sons/heirs of the
> Victors"), རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན ("seven hundred million Victors"), and
> རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔ ("the five Victor-families"); and (2) a **verb** ("to be
> victorious, to triumph") describing the praised goddess's *own* conduct or
> forming part of her *own* name-epithets, as in རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ ("She
> Who Acts Victorious [over the limitless]") or the sixth homage's title
> "Victorious over the Three Worlds." This page is scoped to sense (1) only —
> the substantive buddha-epithet. Sense (2) is recorded in a dedicated
> "Excluded as a different sense" section below rather than silently dropped,
> per the gather instructions. A handful of claims are genuinely ambiguous
> between the two senses (a name built on the verb root, applied to a being who
> is *also* discussed as buddha-like); these are flagged inline where they
> occur rather than silently assigned to either side.

## Questions asked

1. What does "the Victors" (རྒྱལ་བ) mean as a collective epithet for buddhas in this corpus, and in what recurring compounds does it appear?
2. How do commentaries gloss the praised goddess's identity as "mother of the Victors," and is this the same claim as her being "mother of the bodhisattva sons of the Victors"?
3. Who do commentaries identify as "the sons/heirs of the Victors" (རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས), and what do they do in relation to the goddess?
4. Does any commentary widen the root verse's "sons of the Victors" formula to include the Victors themselves, or other classes of noble being, as also relying on her?
5. How do commentaries gloss the root text's "seven hundred million Victors" who swiftly grant empowerment, and how uniform is this benefit-verse language across the corpus?
6. What do commentaries mean by "the five Victor-families," and in what contexts — iconographic, completion-stage, devotional — does the phrase appear?
7. Is Vajradhara ever named "the Victor" or teacher of "the Victors' tantra," and how does that usage relate to the epithet's application to buddhas generally?
8. Does any commentary use "the Victor" to name the historical Buddha Śākyamuni specifically, distinct from the generic plural "Victors" for buddhas collectively?
9. Does any commentary use "the Victor(s)" as a generic buddha-epithet in a quotation from a text other than this praise itself, and what does that show about the term's currency beyond this corpus?
10. When རྒྱལ་བ / རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ appears as a verb ("to be victorious") describing the goddess's own conduct or name, is this the same sense as the substantive epithet "the Conqueror(s)" applied to buddhas, and how should the two be distinguished in this packet?

## "Mother of the Victors" — the goddess as source of the buddhas

### Consensus
Several commentaries open their exposition by identifying the praised goddess directly as "mother of the Victors" (རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ / རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་ཡུམ / རྒྱལ་ཡུམ), usually in the opening homage or its immediate gloss:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-1-3`, `gendun-drub:c-0-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-0-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-5` (6 commentaries)

`anon-utpala:c-1-1-3` states simply, "རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་གྱི་མཆོག་ཏུ་གྱུར་ཅིང" — "she has become the supreme mother of the Victors." `gendun-drub:c-0-3` opens its whole commentary with the line "རྒྱལ་བའི་ཡུམ་གྱུར་བཅོམ་ལྡན་སྒྲོལ་མ" — "to Bhagavatī Tārā, who is the mother of the Victors." `konchok-thabkhe:c-0-3` names her "རྒྱལ་ཡུམ་འཕྲིན་ལས་ལྷ་མོ" — "mother of the Victors, goddess of [buddha-]activity." `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-4` glosses the fifth homage's uṣṇīṣa imagery through the same identity: "ལྷ་མོ་ཁྱོད་ནི་རྒྱལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས" — "because you, goddess, are the mother of all the Victors" — and gives this as the very reason she is borne as the Tathāgatas' crown-ornament. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-3` derives her epithet "Ārya" from the same claim: "རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་ཡུམ་མཆོག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པས...འཕགས་མ" — "because she is the supreme mother of all the Victors, [she is] 'Noble.'" `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-5` gives the fullest form, in verse: "དུས་གསུམ་རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་ཡུམ་གྱུར་མ" — "she who is the mother of all Victors of the three times."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every claim in this facet agrees on the identity, only varying in how much surrounding elaboration it attaches.

### Unique
`sungrab-tulku:c-1-9` gives a related but distinctly-framed opening epithet, not reducible to the "mother of" formula above: "རབ་འབྱམས་རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་གྱི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི། གཅིག་བསྡུས་ཐུགས་རྗེའི་གཏེར་ཆེན་རྗེ་བཙུན་མ" — Jetsünma as "the single condensation of the activity of all infinite Victors, the great treasury of compassion." Here she embodies or condenses the buddhas' collective activity rather than being called their mother; per the full-statement-support rule this is kept separate from the Consensus statement rather than padding its count.

`sungrab-tulku:c-7-6` gives a second, doctrinally distinct route to the same underlying idea (the goddess as source of the buddhas): she is identified as the very essence of Prajñāpāramitā, and "སངས་རྒྱས་ཐམས་ཅད་ནི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་འཁྲུངས་པ" — "all buddhas are born in dependence on the Perfection of Wisdom." The claim's own wording never uses the phrase "mother of the Victors," so it is recorded here as a related-but-distinct doctrinal route to the same conclusion, not folded into the Consensus count.

## Bodhisattvas as "sons/heirs of the Victors," and who is said to rely on the goddess

### Consensus
**Statement A — several commentaries state that the goddess, having attained the ten bhūmis and ten perfections, became mother of the bodhisattva "sons of the Victors":**

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-5`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-21`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (4 commentaries)

`karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-5` states plainly, "རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དུ་གྱུར་པས" — "she has become the mother of all the great bodhisattva sons of the Victors." `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-3` ties this explicitly to her attainment: "ས་བཅུ་མ་ལུས་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་པས་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དུ་གྱུར་བས" — "having attained the perfections of all ten grounds, she became the mother of all the bodhisattva sons of the Victors." `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-21` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` give the same structure with additional detail on what she nurtures in them (wisdoms of the paths of seeing and meditation, in pema-namgyal's case).

**Statement B — several commentaries state that the bodhisattva sons of the Victors, having attained the ten bhūmis and perfections, rely upon or venerate the goddess, without themselves stating that she is their mother:**

— attested: `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-4`, `taranatha:c-4-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2-3` (3 commentaries)

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-4` quotes the root verse itself: "ས་བཅུ་མ་ལུས་པའི་ངོ་བོ་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་ཐོབ་པའི། རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བསྟེན་མ" — "she who is deeply relied upon by the Victor's heirs who have attained the ten perfections, the essence of all ten bhūmis." `taranatha:c-4-7` names the reliant bodhisattvas more specifically as "ས་བཅུའི་དབང་ཕྱུག" — "sovereigns of the ten bhūmis" — who "rely on her and worship her with devotion." `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2-3` gives the same picture in exegetical prose.

### ⚑ Divergences
**How wide is the class denoted by "sons of the Victors"?** Most claims above (in both statements) treat "sons of the Victors" as coextensive with bodhisattvas specifically. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-2` gives a wider reading: its own continuation (the same node's next block, not separately cited here) names "སྲས་བཞི" — "the four [types of noble] offspring" — as śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, and buddhas themselves, all born in dependence on the goddess as mother Prajñāpāramitā. This is a broader class than the bodhisattvas-only gloss the commentaries above give, attributed here to palden-sherab alone within this packet.

**Does the root verse's own referent include the Victors themselves, not only their heirs?** The root verse's own wording (quoted identically across the corpus, e.g. in `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2-2` above) names only "རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས" — "the sons of the Victors" — as the party relying on the goddess. Two commentaries, however, restate this same passage's own topic under a wider heading: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-1` labels the section "རྒྱལ་བ་དང་། རྒྱལ་སྲས་ཀྱིས་གུས་པས་བསྟེན་པའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ" — "praise by way of being reverently relied upon by **the Victors and their heirs**" — and `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-3` states outright that "རྒྱལ་བ་སྲས་དང་བཅས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་གུས་པས...བསྟེན་པའི" — "**the Victorious Ones together with their heirs** rely on you...with the utmost reverence." Both claims thus widen the reliant party beyond the root verse's own single-referent phrase to include the buddhas themselves. Whether this reflects a genuine doctrinal claim that buddhas too revere the goddess, or is simply loose paraphrase carried over from the parallel construction "Victors and Victor-heirs" used elsewhere in the same commentaries' own structural titles, is not settled by any claim in the packet — recorded here as a real divergence in framing between these two commentaries and the root-verse-literal wording of the others, not adjudicated by this page.

### Unique
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-26` is the only claim in the packet that gives a bare definitional word-gloss of the compound itself, independent of any mother- or reliance-claim: "རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བརྟེན་མ...རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཐམས་ཅད་དོ" — "'greatly relied upon by the sons of the Victors' — 'sons of the Victors' means all bodhisattvas."

## "Seven hundred million Victors" swiftly grant empowerment

### Consensus
Nearly every commentary in the packet glosses the root text's closing benefit-verse line naming "seven hundred million Victors" (རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན) as the agents who swiftly grant empowerment to the reciter, leading toward buddhahood — the single most widely and uniformly attested use of the epithet in the whole corpus:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-3-1`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-6`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-2-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (11 commentaries)

The wording is close to identical across all eleven: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-6` glosses "རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས" as bestowing this "without interruption"; `sungrab-tulku:c-2-10` quotes the verse whole, "རྒྱལ་བ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྲག་བདུན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། མྱུར་དུ་དབང་ནི་བསྐུར་བར་འགྱུར" — "the seventy million Victors will swiftly grant empowerment"; `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-1` and `pema-namgyal:c-3-2` both extend the same line into the full benefit sequence — common attainments in this very life, and the supreme attainment of buddhahood itself. `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-5` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` each embed the same line within a longer quoted benefits-passage (pacification of sin, destruction of lower rebirths, poison and epidemic cured, wish-fulfillment on repeated recitation) without altering the "seven hundred million Victors" formula itself.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — this is the packet's most stable, formulaic attestation, with no commentary contesting the number, the agents, or the benefit.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-22-1-20` attests a related but distinguishable claim, not folded into the Consensus count above per the full-statement-support rule: rather than glossing the "seven hundred million Victors grant empowerment" line directly, it describes an intermediate step — "བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་མ་འདི་ཉིད་དང་ལྷན་ཅིག་ཏུ་གཤེགས་ཤིང་བཞུགས་པའི་རྒྱལ་བ་སངས་རྒྱས་སྤྲུལ་པའི་སྐུ་བྱེ་བ་ཕྱག་བདུན་རྣམས་ཀྱིས... ཞལ...མཐོང་པར་འགྱུར" — "one swiftly comes to **see the faces of** the millions of emanation-body Victor-Buddhas who go together with the Bhagavatī" — with the empowerment itself following in the commentary's next block (not separately cited here). The claim thus attests the same "seven hundred million Victors" figure but supports only the seeing-them half of the benefit, not empowerment directly.

## "Five Victor-families"

### Consensus
None — every claim in this facet is a single commentary's own use of the phrase, in different contexts (iconographic, completion-stage, devotional); no two claims restate an identical statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-4` uses the phrase iconographically, describing the sixth homage's crown-ornament: "དབུ་ལ་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔའམ་དེའི་མཚོན་དོན་དུ་རིན་པོ་ཆེའི་དབུ་རྒྱན" — "on her head [she wears] the five Victor-families, or symbolically a jewelled crown."

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-4-2` uses the phrase in a completion-stage-without-marks doctrinal passage, mapping the five aggregates onto the five Victor-families within a self-arisen maṇḍala: "ཕུང་པོ་ལྔ་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔ" — "the five aggregates are the five Victor-families" — as part of purifying all ordinary grasping into the expanse.

`palden-sherab:c-0-20` uses the same phrase again, in the commentary's own devotional preamble rather than in root-text exegesis: "ཀུན་བཟང་རྡོར་འཆང་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔ་ཡིས... གདམས་ཟབ་བདུད་རྩིའི་རྒྱུན" — the profound nectar-stream of secret-mantra instruction flowing "from Samantabhadra-Vajradhara and the five-family Victors" down through Akaniṣṭha to Padmasambhava.

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20` uses the phrase to build a further epithet for the goddess herself: "ཕྱོགས་བཅུའི་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔའི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་གཙུག་གཏོར་གྱི་ནོར་བུ...ན་རྒྱལ་བ་ལས་ཀྱང་ལྷག་པ་ལྟ་བུའི་ཕྱོགས་ཀུན་ལས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ" — she is "the crown-jewel of the assembled tathāgatas, the Victors of the five families throughout the ten directions," and — since consecrated as mistress even superior to them — is named "Victorious in All Directions." This last clause shades into the verbal sense of རྒྱལ་བ (see "Excluded as a different sense," below); it is kept here because the claim's own first half is a clean substantive use of "the five Victor-families."

## Vajradhara, Śākyamuni, and the "Victor" outside this text's own goddess

### Consensus
None — each claim in this facet names a different figure as "the Victor," from a different commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are not competing claims about the same referent, only distinct instances of the epithet applied to different figures.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-0-2` opens the commentary with homage to Vajradhara under this very epithet: "རྒྱལ་བ་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་སྟོན་པ་རྡོ་རྗེ་འཆང" — "Vajradhara, teacher of the Victors' tantra" — paired with a description of him as "the secret lord who gathers the lineage of all the bodhisattva heirs." Here "the Victors" names the tantra's own authorship-lineage of buddhas, with Vajradhara as their teacher, distinct from the goddess who is the commentary's main subject.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-1` uses the singular "the Victor" (རྒྱལ་བ) to name the historical Buddha Śākyamuni specifically, in an analogy to textual history: "རྒྱལ་བ་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་མདོ་ཕྱོགས་ལ་བཀའ་བསྡུ་རིམ་པ་གསུམ་བྱུང་བ་ལྟར" — "just as, after **the Victor's** passing into nirvāṇa, three successive councils occurred on the sūtra side." This is the packet's only claim naming a single historical buddha as "the Victor" rather than the plural "Victors" for buddhas collectively.

## The Victor(s) in canonical prayer-quotations beyond this praise

### Consensus
None — each claim below quotes a different external text; no two claims quote the same passage.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-61` quotes the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* on bodhicitta, using "the Victor's body" as the transformed state a practitioner attains: "མི་གཙང་ལུས་འདི་བླངས་ནས་རྒྱལ་བའི་སྐུ...བསྒྱུར་པས" — "having taken up this impure body, [bodhicitta] transforms it into the priceless jewel of **a Victor's body**."

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-3-95` quotes the *Bhadracaryāpraṇidhāna* describing the dharmadhātu as filled with buddhas under this same epithet: "ཐམས་ཅད་རྒྱལ་བ་དག་གིས་གང་བར་མོས" — "I believe the entire dharmadhātu, without exception, is filled by **all the Victors**."

`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-4-2-2-2-1` quotes the same *Bhadracaryāpraṇidhāna* in a different passage, embedded in the commentary's own seven-branch liturgy, as an offering formula: "དེ་དག་རྒྱལ་བ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་ཡང་མོས...རྒྱལ་བ་ཀུན་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་མཆོད་པར་བགྱི" — "I hold [these offerings] as offered to **every Victor**...I prostrate and make offering to **all the Victors**."

Read together, these three quotations (per Q9) show that when this corpus's commentaries reach for a citation using "the Victor(s)" as a bare, generic buddha-epithet outside their own text's homage-verses, they draw on the same small set of pan-Mahāyāna liturgical sources (Bodhicaryāvatāra, Bhadracaryāpraṇidhāna) rather than coining new usage — a page-level observation, not stated by any claim itself.

## Excluded as a different sense

Per the gather instructions, the following pattern is recorded explicitly rather than silently included in or excluded from the facets above. རྒྱལ་བ / རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ appears very frequently in this corpus as a **verb** ("to be victorious, to triumph") describing the praised goddess's *own* conduct, or as an element of her *own* name-epithets — a different grammatical and semantic function from the substantive noun "the Conqueror(s)" applied to buddhas that this page treats. Representative instances, kept out of the facets above:

- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-5` — etymology of "མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ་སྤྱོད་མ" ("She Who Acts Victorious over the Limitless") as the goddess overturning obscurations herself — a verbal quality of hers, not a noun naming a buddha.
- `anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-1`–`anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-4` — the sixth homage's own section title, "རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གསུམ་ལས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བ" ("the Venerable Lady Victorious over the Three Worlds"), naming the goddess herself, verbally, not a buddha.
- `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6`'s closing clause and `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20`'s closing clause (both cited above under other facets for their genuine substantive-epithet content) each end by shading into this same verbal sense — "རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི་ཚུལ་དུ་སྤྱོད་ཅིང་བཞུགས་མ" / "རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ" — flagged here as the ambiguous edge of those two claims rather than silently resolved either way.

Also excluded, as clearly different words or referents rather than ambiguous senses of the same one: the goddess-name element རྣམ་(པར་)རྒྱལ་མ (Vijayā, e.g. Uṣṇīṣavijayā, a distinct deity named in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-4` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7`'s own iconographic labels); the anthology title རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བའི་བ་དན ("Banner of Victory," the name of a different text within `tsultrim-namdak`'s source volume, not a claim about this praise); the compound རྒྱལ་བློན ("king and ministers," an unrelated word that only shares the substring རྒྱལ་བ); and the several commentators' own personal names built on the same root (e.g. རྒྱལ་བ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ, "Gyalwa Gendun Drub" — the honorific "Gyalwa" in an author's name, not a textual claim).
