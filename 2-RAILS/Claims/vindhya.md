## Naming — third of the three mountains shaken by Tārā's foot-stamping

### Consensus
Thirteen commentaries name Vindhya (འབིགས་བྱེད, variously spelled — see "Orthographic variants" below) as the third mountain, after Meru and Mandara, shaken by Tārā's foot-stamping in the verse glossed at homage seventeen. **This is the same verse and the same naming already fully treated on `2-RAILS/Claims/meru.md`'s "Identity" facet** (its Consensus paragraph and attestation list). It is not re-argued here; only the attestation list is reproduced, re-verified directly against the raw files for this page's own purposes:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-17-4`, `yama-sonam:c-3-17-8`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-2-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-17-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-47`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-0-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-58`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-59` (12 commentaries)

Representative wording (re-quoted here character-for-character from the raw files): `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-5` — "རིའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་རི་རབ་དང་། རི་མནྟཱ་ར་དང་། རི་ནག་པོ་འབིགས་བྱེད་ལ་སོགས་པ...གཡོ་བར་མཛད་པ" ("she causes the king of mountains Meru, Mount Mandara, the black mountain Vindhya, and so forth...to shake"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2` names the alternative reading in the same breath: "རིའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་རི་རབ་དང་། རི་མནྡཱ་ར་དང་། རི་འབིགས་བྱེད་དམ་གངས་ཅན་གྱི་རི" ("Mount Meru, king of mountains, and Mount Mandara, and Mount Vindhya, or the mountain of snow [Himalaya]") — this "or" is the same manuscript-variant question treated in full below and on `meru.md`.

### ⚑ Divergences
See "Textual identity of the third mountain" and "Is འབིགས་བྱེད a name or a verb?", below — both broken out as their own facets rather than folded into this one, since each raises a distinct question about Vindhya specifically.

## Is འབིགས་བྱེད a name or a verb? — a divergence not carried on `meru.md`

### Consensus
The thirteen-commentary majority above reads འབིགས་བྱེད as a proper name — the third of three distinct mountains (Meru, Mandara, Vindhya) named in series and each shaken.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries instead read འབིགས་བྱེད as a verb — "to split apart" or "to be pierced" — describing an action that happens to a single combined "Meru Mandara," not as the name of an independent third peak:

- `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-86` — "རི་རབ་མན་དྷཱ་ར་དང་འབིགས་བྱེད། ། ཞེས་པ་ནི། སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་ཕྱི་རོལ་ན་རི་རབ་མན་དཱ་ར་བ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ཡང་ཡི་གེ་ཧཱུཾ་གི་འོད་ཟེར་གྱིས་འབིགས་པར་བྱེད་པའོ" ("'Meru Mandara and Vibhedaka' — outside the trichiliocosm there is [a mountain] called Meru Mandara, which too **is pierced** by the light rays of the letter HŪM"). Here "Meru Mandara" (རི་རབ་མན་དཱ་ར་བ) is treated as the name of one single mountain outside the trichiliocosm, and འབིགས་པར་བྱེད་པ is the predicate describing what the HŪM-light does to it — not a third mountain's name at all.
- `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4` — "རི་རབ་མནྡ་ར་ཡང་རབ་ཏུ་གཡོ་ཞིང་འབིགས་པར་བྱེད་པ" ("Mount Meru and Mandara are also greatly shaken and **split apart**"). Again "Meru [and] Mandara" is the subject, and འབིགས་པར་བྱེད་པ ("split apart") is the verb phrase applied to it — no third mountain is separately named in this exegetical restatement, even though this same commentary's own root-verse quotation two claims earlier (`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-2`) had quoted "རི་རབ་མནྡ་ར་དང་འབིགས་བ�ྱེད" with all three terms in series.

Both readings still acknowledge the same four syllables in the root verse; the divergence is grammatical, not lexical — whether འབིགས་བྱེད functions as a name (nominal, "Vindhya/Vibhedaka") or as the predicate of the sentence (verbal, "is split/pierced"). `meru.md`'s own divergence (a), "what acts upon Meru," cites `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-86` for a related but distinct question (feet vs. light as cause); it does not note this second, grammatical dimension of the same claim, and does not cite `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-2-4` for it at all. This facet is this page's own independent contribution.

### Unique
None beyond the two attestations above.

## Textual identity of the third mountain — Vindhya or Kailāśa/Himalaya (fully treated on `meru.md`)

### Consensus
None — this is a live manuscript-variant question, not a settled consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
**This entire divergence is already recorded in full on `2-RAILS/Claims/meru.md`, "Identity" ⚑ Divergences, item (b), including `taranatha`'s explicit manuscript-comparison note weighing "Vibhedaka" against a Sanskrit "Kailāśa" reading, `karma-maitri`'s "Meru Vibhedaka" reading (treating the name as a further form of Meru itself rather than an independent peak), and `palden-sherab`'s side-by-side "Vindhya, or the mountain of snow" phrasing.** It is not re-derived here. For completeness, the attestation list (re-verified against the raw files):

— attested: `taranatha:c-17-5`, `taranatha:c-17-7`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-17-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-1-2` (3 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-17-7` gives the fullest statement of the textual question, quoted here again verbatim per this page's own verification: "བོད་དཔེ་ཕལ་ཆེར་ལས། འབིགས་བྱེད་ཅེས་འབྱུང་བ་ལྟར་ན། རིའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་འབིགས་བྱེད་དེ། གཞུང་འདིའི་རྒྱ་སྐད་མཐོང་པ་རྣམས་ན། ཀཻ་ལ་ཤ་ཅེས་པར་སྣང་སྟེ། དེ་ལྟར་ན་གངས་རི་ཞེས་འདོན་པ་དང་མཐུན་མོད། །དཔེ་སྣ་ཚོགས་པ་སྣང་བའི་ཕྱིར། །གཅིག་ཏུ་ངེས་བཟུང་བ་དཀའ་བས་གཉིས་ཀ་ལྟར་མི་འགལ་བར་བྱའོ། །" ("according to most Tibetan copies, which read 'Vibhedaka' — the king of mountains, Vibhedaka; those who have seen the Sanskrit of this text find it reads 'Kailāśa' — thus it accords with reading it as 'the snow mountain' [Himalaya]; since various readings appear, and it is hard to determine one with certainty, both should be taken as not contradicting one another"). This confirms, independently re-read for this page, that `meru.md`'s summary of the passage is accurate: taranatha treats "Vindhya/Vibhedaka" and "Kailāśa/Himalaya" as two textually attested names for the same third mountain, without adjudicating between them.

### Unique
None beyond the attestations above.

## Descriptive epithet — "the black mountain" (fully treated on `meru.md`)

### Consensus
Several commentaries pair Vindhya's name with the descriptive epithet "black mountain" (རི་ནག་པོ). **This epithet is already quoted in full on `meru.md`'s "Identity" Consensus paragraph** via `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`. Re-verified for this page, two further attestations of the same epithet appear that `meru.md` does not separately quote:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-2-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-20-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4` (4 commentaries)

`lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-2-4` — "དེའི་ཞབས་བརྡབས་པས་ནི་རི་རབ་དང་རི་མནྡཱ་ར་དང་རི་ནག་པོ་འབིགས་བྱེད་སོགས" ("by her feet stamping, Meru, Mount Mandara, and the black mountain Vindhya and so forth [are shaken]") — the same "black mountain" epithet, independently attested but not itself quoted on `meru.md` (which cites this claim ID only for the general Identity consensus, not for its epithet wording specifically).

Separately, `tsultrim-namdak`'s own Places register glosses the name's sense rather than repeating the color-epithet: PLC-4 describes Vindhya as "a piercing/cleaving mountain shaken alongside Meru and Mandāra" — an interpretive gloss of what the name འབིགས་བྱེད itself means (from འབིགས་པ, "to pierce/split"), consonant with the verbal reading recorded above rather than the "black mountain" epithet.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed beyond the name/verb divergence already recorded above.

### Unique
None beyond the attestations above.

## Vindhya in a generation-stage sādhana visualization — new context, not on `meru.md`

### Consensus
None — attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab`, in the "general meaning, according to the generation-stage" (སྤྱི་དོན་བསྐྱེད་རིམ) portion of its fourfold word/general/hidden/ultimate exegesis of homage seventeen, places Vindhya inside a full sādhana visualization of "Immeasurably Subduing Tārā" (སྒྲོལ་མ་དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ) — a context `meru.md` does not carry at all, since its own citation of this same commentary's fourfold structure stops at the "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) facet (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-3-1`) and does not reach this "general meaning" claim: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-17-2-1` — "གཉིས་པ་སྤྱི་དོན་བསྐྱེད་རིམ་ལྟར་ན། པདྨ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་གདན་གྱི་སྟེང་དུ། རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་དཔག་མེད་གནོན་མ་དམར་སེར་མཚམས་སྤྲིན་གསར་པའི་མདོག་ཅན། ཞབས་གཉིས་རྟ་བབས་སྐྱིལ་ཀྲུང་། ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས། ཕྱག་གཡས་མཆོག་སྦྱིན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ་དང་། གཡོན་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་ཨུཏྤལ་སྙན་ཐད་དུ་གྲོལ་བའི་ཟེའུ་འབྲུའི་དབུས་མཆོད་རྟེན་འཛིན་པ། ཐུགས་ཀའི་ཧཱུྃ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་ཧཱུྃ་ཡིག་གྲངས་མེད་དང་། ཞབས་མཐིལ་ས་ལ་བརྡབ་པས། རི་རབ་དང་། མནྡཱ་ར་དང་། འབིགས་བྱེད་དང་། ཁམས་གསུམ་གཡོ་ཞིང་ལྡིག་པས་དྲག་ཤུལ་སྟོབས་ཀྱིས་དམིགས་བྱ་རྣམས་ཞབས་འོག་ཏུ་མནན་པར་བསམ་ལ་རིག་སྔགས་བཟླའོ" ("as for the second, the general meaning, according to the generation-stage: upon a lotus-and-moon seat, Jetsün Ārya Tārā 'Immeasurably Subduing,' reddish-yellow like a fresh storm-cloud, both legs in archer's posture, one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving mudrā, left, symbolizing the Three Jewels, holding a stūpa at the pollen-centre of an open utpala at ear-level; from her heart-HŪṂ, countless HŪṂ-syllable light-rays [radiate], and by stamping her foot-sole upon the earth, Meru, Mandara, Vindhya, and the three realms shake and quake; contemplate, through wrathful power, the intended objects being subdued underfoot, and recite the vidyā-mantra"). This is the only place in the packet where Vindhya appears as an element of a specific iconographic sādhana visualization (with named colour, mudrās, and hand-implement) rather than purely as a cosmological landmark struck by the verse's stamping.

## Orthographic variants

Vindhya's name appears in the corpus under at least three spellings: འབིགས་བྱེད (the majority form), འབིག་བྱེད (`karma-maitri`, one ག instead of གས — its own Places register: "PLC-2 | རི་རབ་འབིག་བྱེད | Mount Meru Vibhedaka"), and འབིབས་བྱེད (`sangye-nyentrul:c-18-1-3`, a variant with བ for ག — flagged in that commentary's own Places register as "a variant orthography not literally containing this registered string"). No divergence in meaning attaches to the spelling variance; all three are read as the same mountain name (or, for `drakpa-gyaltsen`/`tenga-tulku`, the same verb) by their respective commentaries.
