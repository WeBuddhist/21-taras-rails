## Questions asked

1. What does the root text's own third homage verse say about a lotus in Tārā's hand, and how do commentaries gloss the flower's identity, colour, and how it is held?
2. Do commentaries distinguish padma (lotus, generic) from utpala (blue lotus / water-lily), or treat the terms as denoting the same flower when glossing this verse?
3. What narrative origin do commentaries give for Tārā's own arising via a lotus connected to Avalokiteśvara, and do they agree the lotus grew specifically from his tears?
4. Beyond the root text's homage verses, how do generation-stage / sādhana visualizations in this corpus use the lotus as a deity's seat, and does its role or colour vary by form?
5. In these same visualizations, what is placed on the lotus or utpala held in the hand, and is this pattern constant across every subsidiary form described?
6. Does any commentary give a hidden-meaning or completion-stage symbolic reading of the lotus distinct from its plain iconographic description?
7. Beyond the hand-implement and the seat, what other epithets or similes use the lotus to describe parts of Tārā's body?
8. Does any commentary treat "the Lotus" as one deity within a set of auspicious-symbol goddesses, distinct from Tārā's own iconography?
9. Does any commentary attest the lotus in material outside the twenty-one-homage root text's own verses — an embedded praise to a different form, or a refuge visualization?
10. Does any commentary offer a folk-etymology of padma/utpala as meaning "born from water" (chu-skyes)?

## The root text's own hand-lotus (homage verse 3) and the padma/utpala equation

### Consensus
Homage verse 3 of the root praise identifies Tārā by the water-born lotus adorning her hand — quoted or glossed in near-identical wording across the corpus, e.g. `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་གསེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི།། པདྨས་ཕྱག་ན་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ༎ ..." ("Homage to her of golden-blue [hue], born from water, her hand adorned with a lotus..."). Commentaries agree the implement is held in the **left** hand, at ear-level, its petals opened — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3` add the further nuance that the flower is held **at her heart** (ཐུགས་ཀར), the blossom opening toward the ear: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14` states plainly "ཕྱག་གཡོན་ན་པདྨ་བསྣམས་པའོ།" ("she holds the lotus in her left hand"; a stray OCR artifact "7 " preceding this clause in the raw claim's Tibetan is elided here); `yama-sonam`'s independent iconographic strand converges on the same detail, e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-3-7`: "སྲིན་མཛུབ་དང་མཐེ་བོང་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པད་མའི་ཡུ་བའི་སྣེ་ནས་ཟིན་ཞིང་རྩེ་མོ་སྙན་ཐད་ནས་ཁ་ཕྱེ་ཞིང...". Body colour is glossed as golden or golden-blue: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1` — "སྐུ་མདོག་འཛམ་བུའི་ཆུ་བོའི་གསེར་ལྟར་སེར་ལ་མཛེས་སྡུག་དང་ལྡན་པ་... ཨུཏྤལ་སྔོན་པོ་ཆུའི་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་པདྨས་ཕྱག་གཡོན་ན་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་སྟེ་འཛིན་མ་ཁྱེད།" ("...whose left hand is adorned, holding a blue utpala lotus born from within water").
— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-18`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `taranatha:c-3-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-1`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-7` (23 commentary-claims across all 16 commentaries in the packet — `yama-sonam` contributes the single verse-3 claim `yama-sonam:c-3-3-7`, quoted above; its remaining 37 claims belong to the seat/implement facet below).

**padma = utpala, in this verse.** Wherever this cluster glosses the verse's own word པདྨ, it identifies the specific flower as an **utpala** (blue lotus / water-lily), not a generic lotus: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` — "ཆུ་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་པདྨ་སྟེ་ཨཏྤལའི་ཡུ་བས་..." ("[a] water-born padma, that is, [an] utpala..."); `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4` — "ཆུ་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའམ་མེ་ཏོག་ཨུཏྤ་ལའི་ཡུ་བས་..." ("the stem of a water-born flower padma, or [the same as] the flower utpala..."); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` — "ཁྱེད་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་ནི་ཆུ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པ་ནི་པདྨའམ་མེ་ཏོག་ཡིན་པས་ཨུཏྤ་ལའི་ མེ་ཏོག་གིས་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ་སྟེ" ("...since what is born from water is padma or a flower, [your hand] is adorned with an utpala flower"); `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` — "ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་པདྨ་སྟེ་ཨུཏྤ་ལས་ཐུགས་ཀར་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་པ" ("a water-born padma — that is, an utpala — adorning her heart"). No claim in the packet glosses this verse's own hand-implement as any lotus species other than utpala.
— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` (4 commentaries; consistent with, not contradicted by, the wider identity across the rest of this facet's claims, which use the two terms side by side without objection).

**Folk-etymology: "born from water" (chu-skyes).** Every gloss in this facet that touches the flower's identity does so via the same standing formula, "ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་པ" / "ཆུ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པ" (born from/in water) — functioning across the corpus as the flower's defining epithet rather than a technical etymology of the Sanskrit *padma* itself; no claim in the packet analyses the word པདྨ into its own morphological parts.

### ⚑ Divergences
No commentary in this facet contests the left-hand placement or the padma-utpala identification. ⚑ The **body-colour gloss**, however, is not uniform: most claims read the verse's གསེར་སྔོ as golden or golden with a bluish cast (as quoted under Consensus above), but `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` glosses the colour as **green** — "སྔོན་པོ་གཉིས་འདྲེས་པ་ནི་ལྗང་གུ་ཡིན་པས་སྐུ་མདོག་ལྗང་གུ་དང་ལྡན་ པ" ("[gold] and blue mixed together are green — so, possessed of a green body-colour") — and `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1`'s raw gloss reads "ཅུང་ཟད་སྡེ་བའི་ཁ་དོག་ཅན" ("somewhat reddish in hue," per the raw claim's own English rendering). ⚑ The reading སྡེ་བ in `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1` is possibly an OCR corruption of སྔོ་བ ("bluish"); this is flagged for a human source-check against the scan rather than resolved here.

Both divergent claims are also cited under Consensus above, and the overlap is deliberate for the same reason as in the origin facet below: each claim attests the shared hand-lotus statement *and* a colour gloss that does not match the others. The Consensus records what they agree on; the ⚑ records where the same claims part company.

### Unique
Several claims specify the *finger-grip* on the stem, rather than simply "the left hand": `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4` name the thumb and ring-finger (མཐེབ་སྲིན), `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4` the same grip (མཐེ་སྲིན), `yama-sonam:c-3-3-7` the ring finger and thumb (སྲིན་མཛུབ་དང་མཐེ་བོང་), and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` the ring-finger (སྲིན་ལག) — while `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1` states only that the left hand holds the flower, naming no finger. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` is the only claim in this facet to map the gesture onto the **ten pure pāramitās** specifically: "བཟུངས་ནས་སྙན་གྱི་ཐད་ཀར་ཁ་བྱེ་བ་ནི། ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་དག་པའི་རྟགས་སོ། །" ("held so, its opening facing toward her ear — this is the sign of the ten pure perfections"). The sign-reading as such is not unique to it: `yama-sonam:c-3-3-7` (quoted in the Consensus above) reads the same gesture as a sign that the perfections have been brought to completion and her intelligence fully expanded (མཐར་ཕྱིན་...རྟགས་སུ), and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3` reads it more weakly as showing "the qualities of the body" — the ten-pāramitās mapping, not the sign-reading, is gendun-drub's unique contribution.

## Origin narrative: Tārā's emanation from a lotus connected to Avalokiteśvara

### Consensus
Seven commentaries narrate Tārā's own arising as connected to a water-born lotus (padma) associated with Avalokiteśvara. In six of them her emanation is described as arising from that lotus's pollen; `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` instead has her arise from the tears of great compassion **directly** — "སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོའི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་འཕགས་མ" ("the Ārya arisen from the tears of great compassion") — its lotus imagery confined to similes for Avalokiteśvara's face and eyes ("ཆུ་སྐྱེས་མེ་ཏོག་ལྟ་བུའི་ཞལ་གྱི་པདྨའི་སྤྱན་ཟུང་"), with no tears→lotus→pollen chain stated. `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9`: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཞལ་གྱི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་ནི་ཉེ་བར་བྱུང་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལའོ།" ("She arose from the myriad pollen of the lotus mouth-flower of Avalokiteshvara — that is Tara").
— attested: `taranatha:c-1-9`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-9`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` (7 commentaries).

### ⚑ Divergences
Within this shared narrative, commentaries diverge on **whether the lotus specifically grew from Avalokiteśvara's tears**, or whether "the water-born [lotus] of his face" is a standing epithet for his face itself (pollen simply issuing from it), with no tear-event stated.

- **Tear-origin, stated explicitly:** `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8` — "དེའི་ཞལ་གྱི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ལ་གནས་པའི་སྤྱན་གྱི་ཆུ་སྒྱན་ཆབ་ལས་སྒྱེས་པའི་པདྨའི་གསེར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མ་དེ་བྱུང་བའོ།" ("From the golden pollen of the lotus born from the tear-water present in the maṇḍala of his face, that Tārā arose"); `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-9` — "དེའི་ཞལ་གྱི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ལ་གནས་པའི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་ཁ་བྱེ་བ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་..." ("...arisen from the opening pollen of a lotus flower grown from the tears abiding in the maṇḍala of his face"); `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14` — "སྤྱན་ཆབ་དེ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་ཁ་ཕྱེ་བ་ལས་..." ("From those tears a lotus was born; from the opened stamens of that lotus flower..."); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6` gives the fullest narrative, explaining *why* he wept — seeing that countless beings he had already freed from saṃsāra had not diminished in number — before the tear-water grows the lotus; `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` also states the tears explicitly, but as the goddess's own **direct** origin ("སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོའི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་འཕགས་མ") — it does not narrate a lotus growing from them (see Consensus above), so it attests the tear-event without the tears→lotus→pollen chain.
- **No tear-event stated — the "water-born [lotus]" is his face's own quality:** `taranatha:c-1-9` — "སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཞལ་རས་ཀྱི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་པདྨ་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྤུལ་ཞིང་བྱུང་བའོ།" ("...emanated and arose from a myriad of lotus-pollen born of the water-born [lotus, i.e.] face of Avalokiteśvara") and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9` (quoted above) both describe the pollen as issuing from Avalokiteśvara's "lotus face" without narrating tears; neither commentary's raw file contains the word སྤྱན་ཆབ ("tears") anywhere.

This page treats the divergence as a difference in what these two commentaries' own captured claims *state*, not as a positive denial of the tear-narrative on their part — both remain compatible with a fuller tear-account appearing elsewhere in their own text, outside the claims tagged with this term. The two readings are nonetheless genuinely different in what they assert here: a narrated event (tears fall → lotus grows → pollen emanates) versus a standing poetic epithet (his face itself is "water-born," i.e. lotus-like; pollen from it emanates).

— tears stated explicitly: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-1-9`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6` (tears → lotus → pollen), and `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` (tears as her direct origin, no lotus-growth step) (5 commentaries).

— face-epithet side: `taranatha:c-1-9`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9` (2 commentaries).

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above — every claim in this facet participates in one side or the other.

## Generation-stage / sādhana visualization: the lotus as universal seat, the utpala as implement-platform

### Consensus
Independently of the root text's own homage verses, three commentaries each attest an extended generation-stage or sādhana section describing a set of subsidiary Tārā forms (peaceful and wrathful), in which every form is seated upon a lotus. The seat is paired with a **moon** disc throughout the `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul` cycles, while several of `yama-sonam`'s seats pair the lotus with a **sun** disc instead (e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-3-9`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-7`, `yama-sonam:c-3-7-7`). The further pattern — an **utpala** held in the left hand whose pollen-centre or open blossom serves as the platform for a form-specific object — is attested as a constant of the `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul` cycles only: in `yama-sonam`'s cycle the hand-implements vary freely by form with no utpala-platform stated (sword/arrow/wheel/bow — `yama-sonam:c-3-13-8`; wrathful implements — `yama-sonam:c-3-14-6`; a moon-disc held at the heart — `yama-sonam:c-3-17-11`; a fire-vessel — `yama-sonam:c-3-19-9`; a vase — `yama-sonam:c-3-20-8`; rosary and trident — `yama-sonam:c-3-21-7`).

- **`yama-sonam`** (38 claims) attributes its iconographic sequence to "Nyisbepa['s] sādhana" and confirms nearly every form with a matching verse citation from "Nyima Sbaspa" (ཉི་མ་སྦས་པ). The lotus-seat colour varies by form: yellow (`yama-sonam:c-3-4-9` — "པདྨ་སེར་པོ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་ན་... ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་བཞི་མ") vs. red (`yama-sonam:c-3-6-7` — "པདྨ་དམར་པོ་དང་ཉི་མའི་གདན་ལ་...") vs. white (`yama-sonam:c-3-15-9`) vs. multicoloured (`yama-sonam:c-3-3-9` — "སྣ་ཚོགས་པདྨའི་སྟེང་གི་ཉི་མ་ལ་གནས་མ"). In several forms the padma also appears as one specific hand-implement among several rather than the identifying utpala alone, e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-3-11` — "གསུམ་པ་ན་པདྨ" ("the third [left hand holds] a lotus") within a five-implement left hand. Each iconographic prose claim is paired with a direct verse-citation from Nyima Sbaspa confirming the same details, e.g. `yama-sonam:c-3-5-14` — "ཟླ་སྟེང་རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཞབས་གནོན་ཅིང་། ། སྐྱབས་སྦྱིན་གསེར་གྱི་པད་ལྕུག་འཛིན།" ("Upon the moon, in vajra [posture]... holding a golden lotus stem").
- **`palden-sherab`** (20 claims of this pattern; 21 total generation-stage claims in the packet, one of which — c-3-1-2-2-1 — visualizes a non-Tārā deity, Dorje Yangchenma, atop the same lotus-and-moon seat) frames each visualization as "Mahāyoga"-style generation-stage practice; the opening claim sets the template — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`: "སྟོང་པའི་ངང་ལས་པདྨ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་གདན་གྱི་སྟེང་དུ་རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་མྱུར་མ་དཔའ་མོ་..." ("from a state of emptiness, upon a lotus-and-moon seat, [is] Jetsün Ārya Tārā 'Swift Heroine'..."), and every subsequent form in the series (each corresponding to one of the remaining homage verses) repeats the phrase "པདྨ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་གདན་གྱི་སྟེང་དུ" ("upon a lotus-and-moon seat") verbatim or near-verbatim before naming the form, e.g. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` (Uṣṇīṣavijayā, golden, holding an utpala topped with a life-vase).
- **`sangye-nyentrul`** (21 claims) uses the same template, opening each of its 21 entries "པད་ཟླའི་སྟེང་དུ་ཞབས་ཕྱེད་སྐྱིལ" ("seated half-cross-legged on lotus-and-moon") followed by the hand-implements, e.g. `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-2` — "...ཨུཏྤལ་རབ་ཏུ་རྒྱས་པའི་སྟེང་དུ་དུང་གཡས་འཁྱིལ" ("...atop a fully-blossomed blue utpala, a right-coiling white conch") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-1-2` — "...གཡོན་ཨུཏྤལ་འཛིན་པའི་སྟེང་ཡིད་བཞིན་གྱི་ནོར་བུ་..." ("...left holding an utpala atop which sits a wish-fulfilling jewel...").

Across all three commentaries the lotus functions as the **constant seat** (moon-paired in `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul`, moon- or sun-paired in `yama-sonam`). The utpala-as-platform — the form-specific implement or symbol resting on the utpala held in the left hand, changing verse by verse (weapon, vase, wheel, banner, stūpa, parasol, and so on) while the utpala itself does not — is constant within the `palden-sherab` and `sangye-nyentrul` cycles but is not a feature of `yama-sonam`'s cycle.
— attested (representative citations only; the remaining claims of this pattern are listed by ID in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below, each logged as a duplicate of this same attested pattern): `yama-sonam:c-3-1-21`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-9`, `yama-sonam:c-3-3-11`, `yama-sonam:c-3-4-9`, `yama-sonam:c-3-5-14`, `yama-sonam:c-3-6-7`, `yama-sonam:c-3-15-9`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-1-2` (3 commentaries; representative of 79 further claims in this same pattern across the same three commentaries, all logged below).

### ⚑ Divergences
The three commentaries never name the same form with the same epithet at the same position in their respective 21-form (or comparable) sequences — `yama-sonam`'s forms are identified only by iconographic description (no names given in the captured claims), and no `sangye-nyentrul` claim captured in this packet names its form either (its per-form c-N-1-2 iconography claims describe without naming); only `palden-sherab` names each form individually in the captured claims (e.g. "Swift Heroine" — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`, Uṣṇīṣavijayā — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1`). This page does not treat the three sequences as depicting the same 21 forms in the same order — only the shared *structural* role of the lotus (seat) and utpala (implement-platform) is attested as common ground; the specific correspondences between the three naming systems are not addressed by any claim in this packet and are not asserted here. (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` are cited on both sides of this facet by design: they attest the shared seat-and-platform pattern *and* are the two claims that show palden-sherab naming its forms, which is precisely what the other two cycles' claims do not do.)

### Unique
None beyond what is already noted above as facet-internal variation (seat colour; padma as one implement among several in `yama-sonam`'s multi-armed forms).

## Hidden-meaning / completion-stage symbolism of the lotus

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab` alone, within its exegesis of homage verse 3, moves from the plain iconographic reading of the lotus to two further layers of tantric symbolism not found elsewhere in the packet:

- **Completion-stage-with-marks:** the union of the water-born "secret lotus" with the "vajra" hand is read as the six pāramitās embodied in sequence — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་གསང་བའི་པདྨ་དང་། ཕྱག་སྒྲ་ནི་རྡོ་རྗེ་སྟེ་མཉམ་པར་སྦྱར་བ་ལས་འཕོ་བ་མེད་པའི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་དང་། བདེ་བའི་ངོ་བོ་བསམ་གཏན་དང་། ..." ("the water-born secret lotus and the 'hand' as vajra, [their] union without transference, is discipline-austerity; the essence of bliss is meditative absorption...").
- **Completion-stage-without-marks:** the lotus itself becomes a simile for self-purifying primordial wisdom — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-1`: "པདྨ་ལྟར་གཟུང་འཛིན་དྲི་མ་རང་གར་སངས་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཇི་བཞིན་པའི་རང་བྱུང་ཡེ་ཤེས་ངང་ལ...." ("within the state of self-arisen wisdom — just like a lotus, whose stains of subject-object grasping are cleared by their very own [nature]...").

No other commentary's claims in this packet offer a comparable hidden-meaning reading of the lotus; this typology is unique to `palden-sherab` within the packet.

## Lotus as epithet and simile for Tārā's feet and face

### Consensus
No merged consensus statement is drawn, but each of the facet's two images is corroborated by two commentaries: the **lotus-feet homage** by `pema-namgyal:c-1-1-3` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-15`, and the **lotus-like-face-with-frown simile** by `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-29` and `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-6`. The four claims are quoted individually under Unique below because each glosses its own verse and none states it is quoting or following another.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the "lotus feet" and "lotus-like face" images are complementary similes, not competing claims.

### Unique
- **"Lotus feet":** `pema-namgyal:c-1-1-3` opens its commentary with an epithet-homage: "ལྷ་དང་ལྷ་མིན་ཅོད་པན་གྱིས། ། ཞབས་ཀྱི་པདྨ་ལ་བཏུད་དེ།" ("With the crowns of gods and demigods bowing to her lotus feet..."). `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-15` elaborates the same image with more physical specificity, describing the jeweled hair-tips of Indra and others touching "ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་ལ་ ཨུཏྤལ་ཆུན་པོས་རེག" ("a small cluster of utpalas touches the sole of her foot") as they bow to "ཁྱོད་ཞབས་པདྨོའི་འདབ་མར" ("the petals of your lotus feet").
- **"Lotus-like face":** `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-29` — "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་པདྨ་རྒྱས་འདྲའི་ཞལ་ནི་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ལྡན་པར་མཛད་པས...." ("Her lotus-blossom-like face bears a wrathful frown..."), read together with the frown's effect (effortlessly slaying the practitioner's enemies). `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-6` uses the identical simile for a different verse's iconography: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་པདྨ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟར་མཛེས་པའི་ཞལ་རས་ནི་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་དང་ལྡན་པའི་..." ("Her face, beautiful like a fully blossoming lotus, bears a wrathful expression"). The near-identical Tibetan phrasing across two different commentaries (independently glossing two different homage verses) suggests a shared idiom for pairing lotus-beauty with a wrathful expression, though each claim is retained here as a single-commentary attestation on its own verse rather than merged into a Consensus statement, since no claim states it is quoting or following the other.

## The lotus in material beyond the root text's own twenty-one homages

### Consensus

## Claims reviewed, not separately cited

The following claims were located and read in full but are not separately quoted above because they repeat, without adding a new fact, the pattern already attested by the representative citations in "Generation-stage / sādhana visualization" — each is a per-form (or per-verse) instance of the same lotus-seat template (moon- or sun-paired), differing only in the specific colour, name, or implement of that one form.
