## Questions asked

1. What does the root text's own third homage verse say about a lotus in Tārā's hand, and how do commentaries gloss the flower's identity, colour, and how it is held?
2. Do commentaries distinguish padma (lotus, generic) from utpala (blue lotus / water-lily), or treat the terms as denoting the same flower when glossing this verse?
3. What narrative origin do commentaries give for Tārā's own arising via a lotus connected to Avalokiteśvara, and do they agree the lotus grew specifically from his tears?
4. Beyond the root text's homage verses, how do generation-stage / sādhana visualizations in this corpus use the lotus as a deity's seat, and does its role or colour vary by form?
5. In these same visualizations, what is placed on the lotus or utpala held in the hand, and is this pattern constant across every subsidiary form described?
6. Does any commentary give a hidden-meaning or completion-stage symbolic reading of the lotus distinct from its plain iconographic description?
7. Beyond the hand-implement and the seat, what other epithets or similes use the lotus to describe parts of Tārā's body?
8. Does any commentary treat "the Lotus" as one deity within a set of auspicious-symbol goddesses, distinct from Tārā's own iconography?
9. Does any commentary attest the lotus in material outside the twenty-one-homage root text's own verses — an embedded praise to a different form, or a refuge visualization?
10. Does any commentary offer a folk-etymology of padma/utpala as meaning "born from water" (chu-skyes)?

## The root text's own hand-lotus (homage verse 3) and the padma/utpala equation

### Consensus
Homage verse 3 of the root praise identifies Tārā by the water-born lotus adorning her hand — quoted or glossed in near-identical wording across the corpus, e.g. `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་གསེར་སྔོ་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི།། པདྨས་ཕྱག་ན་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ༎ ..." ("Homage to her of golden-blue [hue], born from water, her hand adorned with a lotus..."). Commentaries agree the implement is held in the **left** hand, at ear-level, its petals opened: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14` states plainly "ཕྱག་གཡོན་ན་པདྨ་བསྣམས་པའོ།" ("she holds the lotus in her left hand"); `anon-trinle-char`'s independent iconographic strand converges on the same detail, e.g. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-7`: "སྲིན་མཛུབ་དང་མཐེ་བོང་གཉིས་ཀྱིས་ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པད་མའི་ཡུ་བའི་སྣེ་ནས་ཟིན་ཞིང་རྩེ་མོ་སྙན་ཐད་ནས་ཁ་ཕྱེ་ཞིང...". Body colour is glossed as golden or golden-blue: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1` — "སྐུ་མདོག་འཛམ་བུའི་ཆུ་བོའི་གསེར་ལྟར་སེར་ལ་མཛེས་སྡུག་དང་ལྡན་པ་... ཨུཏྤལ་སྔོན་པོ་ཆུའི་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་པདྨས་ཕྱག་གཡོན་ན་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་སྟེ་འཛིན་མ་ཁྱེད།" ("...whose left hand is adorned, holding a blue utpala lotus born from within water").
— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-14`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-18`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `taranatha:c-3-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-10`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-1` (22 commentary-claims, 15 of the 16 commentaries — every commentary in the packet except `anon-trinle-char`, whose own claims on this verse were not captured by the Signal-A padma tag and are instead folded into the seat/implement facet below).

**padma = utpala, in this verse.** Wherever this cluster glosses the verse's own word པདྨ, it identifies the specific flower as an **utpala** (blue lotus / water-lily), not a generic lotus: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4` — "ཆུ་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་པདྨ་སྟེ་ཨཏྤལའི་ཡུ་བས་..." ("[a] water-born padma, that is, [an] utpala..."); `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4` — "ཆུ་ནང་ནས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའམ་མེ་ཏོག་ཨུཏྤ་ལའི་ཡུ་བས་..." ("the stem of a water-born flower padma, or [the same as] the flower utpala..."); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` — "ཁྱེད་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་ནི་ཆུ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པ་ནི་པདྨའམ་མེ་ཏོག་ཡིན་པས་ཨུཏྤ་ལའི་ མེ་ཏོག་གིས་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ་སྟེ" ("...since what is born from water is padma or a flower, [your hand] is adorned with an utpala flower"). No claim in the packet glosses this verse's own hand-implement as any lotus species other than utpala.
— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (3 commentaries; consistent with, not contradicted by, the wider identity across the rest of this facet's claims, which use the two terms side by side without objection).

**Folk-etymology: "born from water" (chu-skyes).** Every gloss in this facet that touches the flower's identity does so via the same standing formula, "ཆུ་ནས་སྐྱེས་པ" / "ཆུ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པ" (born from/in water) — functioning across the corpus as the flower's defining epithet rather than a technical etymology of the Sanskrit *padma* itself; no claim in the packet analyses the word པདྨ into its own morphological parts.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary in this facet contests the left-hand placement, the golden/golden-blue body colour, or the padma-utpala identification.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-1` and `sungrab-tulku:c-6-4` are the only claims to specify the *stem* is held at the ring finger and thumb specifically (rather than simply "the left hand"); `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` is the only claim in this facet to read the gesture itself — lotus held open at ear-level — as a **sign of the ten pure pāramitās** rather than merely an iconographic detail: "བཟུངས་ནས་སྙན་གྱི་ཐད་ཀར་ཁ་བྱེ་བ་ནི། ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་བཅུ་དག་པའི་རྟགས་སོ། །" ("held so, its opening facing toward her ear — this is the sign of the ten pure perfections").

## Origin narrative: Tārā's emanation from a lotus connected to Avalokiteśvara

### Consensus
Seven commentaries narrate Tārā's own arising as connected to a water-born lotus (padma) associated with Avalokiteśvara — her emanation described as arising from that lotus's pollen. `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9`: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཞལ་གྱི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་ནི་ཉེ་བར་བྱུང་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལའོ།" ("She arose from the myriad pollen of the lotus mouth-flower of Avalokiteshvara — that is Tara").
— attested: `taranatha:c-1-9`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-9`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` (7 commentaries).

### ⚑ Divergences
Within this shared narrative, commentaries diverge on **whether the lotus specifically grew from Avalokiteśvara's tears**, or whether "the water-born [lotus] of his face" is a standing epithet for his face itself (pollen simply issuing from it), with no tear-event stated.

- **Tear-origin, stated explicitly:** `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8` — "དེའི་ཞལ་གྱི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ལ་གནས་པའི་སྤྱན་གྱི་ཆུ་སྒྱན་ཆབ་ལས་སྒྱེས་པའི་པདྨའི་གསེར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མ་དེ་བྱུང་བའོ།" ("From the golden pollen of the lotus born from the tear-water present in the maṇḍala of his face, that Tārā arose"); `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-9` — "དེའི་ཞལ་གྱི་དཀྱིལ་འཁོར་ལ་གནས་པའི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་ཁ་བྱེ་བ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་..." ("...arisen from the opening pollen of a lotus flower grown from the tears abiding in the maṇḍala of his face"); `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14` — "སྤྱན་ཆབ་དེ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་མེ་ཏོག་པདྨའི་གེ་སར་ཁ་ཕྱེ་བ་ལས་..." ("From those tears a lotus was born; from the opened stamens of that lotus flower..."); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6` gives the fullest narrative, explaining *why* he wept — seeing that countless beings he had already freed from saṃsāra had not diminished in number — before the tear-water grows the lotus; `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` likewise: "སྙིང་རྗེ་ཆེན་པོའི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་..." ("...arisen from the tears of great compassion...").
- **No tear-event stated — the "water-born [lotus]" is his face's own quality:** `taranatha:c-1-9` — "སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཞལ་རས་ཀྱི་ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་པདྨ་གེ་སར་བྱེ་བ་ལས་སྤུལ་ཞིང་བྱུང་བའོ།" ("...emanated and arose from a myriad of lotus-pollen born of the water-born [lotus, i.e.] face of Avalokiteśvara") and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9` (quoted above) both describe the pollen as issuing from Avalokiteśvara's "lotus face" without narrating tears; neither commentary's raw file contains the word སྤྱན་ཆབ ("tears") anywhere.

This page treats the divergence as a difference in what these two commentaries' own captured claims *state*, not as a positive denial of the tear-narrative on their part — both remain compatible with a fuller tear-account appearing elsewhere in their own text, outside the claims tagged with this term. The two readings are nonetheless genuinely different in what they assert here: a narrated event (tears fall → lotus grows → pollen emanates) versus a standing poetic epithet (his face itself is "water-born," i.e. lotus-like; pollen from it emanates).

— tear-origin side: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-1-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-1-9`, `sungrab-tulku:c-4-14`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-6`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-2-0-5` (5 commentaries).

— face-epithet side: `taranatha:c-1-9`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-1-9` (2 commentaries).

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above — every claim in this facet participates in one side or the other.

## Generation-stage / sādhana visualization: the lotus as universal seat, the utpala as implement-platform

### Consensus
Independently of the root text's own homage verses, three commentaries each attest an extended generation-stage or sādhana section describing a set of subsidiary Tārā forms (peaceful and wrathful), in which **every** form, regardless of colour, activity, or hand-implement, is seated upon a lotus — almost always paired with a moon disc — and holds, in her left hand, an **utpala** whose pollen-centre or open blossom serves as the platform for a form-specific object.

- **`anon-trinle-char`** (38 claims) attributes its iconographic sequence to "Nyisbepa['s] sādhana" and confirms nearly every form with a matching verse citation from "Nyima Sbaspa" (ཉི་མ་སྦས་པ). The lotus-seat colour varies by form: yellow (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-9` — "པདྨ་སེར་པོ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་དཀྱིལ་ན་... ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་བཞི་མ") vs. red (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-6-7` — "པདྨ་དམར་པོ་དང་ཉི་མའི་གདན་ལ་...") vs. white (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-15-9`) vs. multicoloured (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-9` — "སྣ་ཚོགས་པདྨའི་སྟེང་གི་ཉི་མ་ལ་གནས་མ"). In several forms the padma also appears as one specific hand-implement among several rather than the identifying utpala alone, e.g. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-3-11` — "གསུམ་པ་ན་པདྨ" ("the third [left hand holds] a lotus") within a five-implement left hand. Each iconographic prose claim is paired with a direct verse-citation from Nyima Sbaspa confirming the same details, e.g. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-5-14` — "ཟླ་སྟེང་རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཞབས་གནོན་ཅིང་། ། སྐྱབས་སྦྱིན་གསེར་གྱི་པད་ལྕུག་འཛིན།" ("Upon the moon, in vajra [posture]... holding a golden lotus stem").
- **`palden-sherab`** (20 claims of this pattern; 21 total generation-stage claims in the packet, one of which — c-3-1-2-2-1 — visualizes a non-Tārā deity, Dorje Yangchenma, atop the same lotus-and-moon seat) frames each visualization as "Mahāyoga"-style generation-stage practice; the opening claim sets the template — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-1`: "སྟོང་པའི་ངང་ལས་པདྨ་དང་ཟླ་བའི་གདན་གྱི་སྟེང་དུ་རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་མྱུར་མ་དཔའ་མོ་..." ## Hidden-meaning / completion-stage symbolism of the lotus

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab` alone, within its exegesis of homage verse 3, moves from the plain iconographic reading of the lotus to two further layers of tantric symbolism not found elsewhere in the packet:

- **Completion-stage-with-marks:** the union of the "secret lotus" (water-born, i.e. the female sexual organ) with the "vajra" hand is read as the six pāramitās embodied in sequence — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1`: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་གསང་བའི་པདྨ་དང་། ཕྱག་སྒྲ་ནི་རྡོ་རྗེ་སྟེ་མཉམ་པར་སྦྱར་བ་ལས་འཕོ་བ་མེད་པའི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་དང་། བདེ་བའི་ངོ་བོ་བསམ་གཏན་དང་། ..." ("the water-born secret lotus and the 'hand' as vajra, [their] union without transference, is discipline-austerity; the essence of bliss is meditative absorption...").
- **Completion-stage-without-marks:** the lotus itself becomes a simile for self-purifying primordial wisdom — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-4-1`: "པདྨ་ལྟར་གཟུང་འཛིན་དྲི་མ་རང་གར་སངས་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཇི་བཞིན་པའི་རང་བྱུང་ཡེ་ཤེས་ངང་ལ...." ("within the state of self-arisen wisdom — just like a lotus, whose stains of subject-object grasping are cleared by their very own [nature]...").

No other commentary's claims in this packet offer a comparable hidden-meaning reading of the lotus; this typology is unique to `palden-sherab` within the packet.

## Lotus as epithet and simile for Tārā's feet and face

### Consensus
None — the four claims in this facet split evenly between two different body-parts and are each attested by only one commentary apiece; no shared statement is corroborated by more than one commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the "lotus feet" and "lotus-like face" images are complementary similes, not competing claims.

### Unique
- **"Lotus feet":** `pema-namgyal:c-1-1-3` opens its commentary with an epithet-homage: "ལྷ་དང་ལྷ་མིན་ཅོད་པན་གྱིས། ། ཞབས་ཀྱི་པདྨ་ལ་བཏུད་དེ།" ("With the crowns of gods and demigods bowing to her lotus feet..."). `tsultrim-namdak:c-1-15` elaborates the same image with more physical specificity, describing the jeweled hair-tips of Indra and others touching "ཞབས་ཀྱི་མཐིལ་ལ་ ཨུཏྤལ་ཆུན་པོས་རེག" ("a small cluster of utpalas touches the sole of her foot") as they bow to "ཁྱོད་ཞབས་པདྨོའི་འདབ་མར" ("the petals of your lotus feet").
- **"Lotus-like face":** `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-29` — "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་པདྨ་རྒྱས་འདྲའི་ཞལ་ནི་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་ལྡན་པར་མཛད་པས...." ("Her lotus-blossom-like face bears a wrathful frown..."), read together with the frown's effect (effortlessly slaying the practitioner's enemies). `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-1-6` uses the identical simile for a different verse's iconography: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་ཏེ་པདྨ་རྒྱས་པ་ལྟར་མཛེས་པའི་ཞལ་རས་ནི་ཁྲོ་གཉེར་དང་ལྡན་པའི་..." ("Her face, beautiful like a fully blossoming lotus, bears a wrathful expression"). The near-identical Tibetan phrasing across two different commentaries (independently glossing two different homage verses) suggests a shared idiom for pairing lotus-beauty with a wrathful expression, though each claim is retained here as a single-commentary attestation on its own verse rather than merged into a Consensus statement, since no claim states it is quoting or following the other.

## The Lotus goddess among a set of auspicious-symbol deities

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang` alone attests a passage naming "the Lotus [goddess]" (པདྨའི་ལྷ་མོ) as one deity within a set of auspicious-symbol goddesses — distinct from any of Tārā's own iconographic forms discussed elsewhere in this packet. This system is not cross-referenced by any other claim in the packet and does not overlap with the lotus's role as Tārā's own seat or hand-implement discussed above.
