# Īśvara/Maheśvara — དབང་ཕྱུག།

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather note:** `ishvara` is a global cross-cutting topic, not one of the 21
> registered Tārā-verse spine slots, so there is no `assemble_packet.py` /
> spine-map packet for it. The claims below were located by running
> `rg -n 'དབང་ཕྱུག'` directly across all sixteen commentaries'
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/<id>.md` files, then reading every hit's full
> claim block in context. Every quotation on this page is copied
> character-for-character from that read, never retyped from memory.
>
> **Scope boundary — two judgment calls, made explicitly rather than by
> conflation (per the task brief and Rule 6).** The string དབང་ཕྱུག appears in
> the corpus in several senses that are **not** this page's subject:
> - **དགའ་རབ་དབང་ཕྱུག / བདུད་དགའ་རབ་དབང་ཕྱུག ("Kāmadeva Rabga Wangchuk")** —
>   glossed consistently across the commentaries that name him (`yama-sonam`,
>   `dharmabhadra`, `lobsang-dawa`, `palden-sherab`, `sungrab-tulku`,
>   `tenzin-dhonzang`) as **Māra Paranirmitavaśavartin, "the bodiless lord of
>   the desire realm,"** drawn under Tārā's power by her mantra's laughter
>   (TUTTĀRE) in a verse distinct from the sixth homage's worshipper-list.
>   `sungrab-tulku`'s own Grounding index keeps this figure (FIG-12) entirely
>   separate from its own worshipper-list entries FIG-10 (སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག,
>   Viśveśvara) and FIG-11 (bare དབང་ཕྱུག, Īśvara). No claim anywhere in the
>   packet identifies Kāmadeva with the worshipper-list Sarveśvara/Maheśvara, or
>   treats the two names as interchangeable. **Judged: a genuinely distinct
>   figure, out of scope for this page.** Representative citation, not
>   exhaustively logged: `sungrab-tulku:c-13-4`.
> - **འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག / སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་དབང་ཕྱུག ("Lokeśvara")** — a standing
>   epithet of **Avalokiteśvara** (from whose compassionate tears Tārā is said
>   to arise), attested in `gendun-drub`, `tenga-tulku`, `tenzin-dhonzang`,
>   `palden-sherab`, `sungrab-tulku`, and `tsultrim-namdak`. This is a specific,
>   already-identified deity distinct from Sarveśvara/Maheśvara — the word
>   དབང་ཕྱུག here functions as a generic honorific ("lord/sovereign") inside a
>   different deity's name, exactly as `deva.md` excluded generic ལྷ-compounds
>   naming Tārā herself. **Judged: excluded.** Representative citation:
>   `palden-sherab`'s own Grounding index, FIG-5.
> - **Minor generic/abstract uses**, also excluded as off-topic rather than
>   silently dropped: `taranatha:c-0-9` ("འཁོར་ལོའི་དབང་ཕྱུག་མ," an epithet of
>   **Tārā herself**, not the class); `taranatha:c-4-7` ("ས་བཅུའི་དབང་ཕྱུག,"
>   generic "lords of the ten bhūmis" applied to **bodhisattvas**); and
>   `taranatha:c-22-0-5` ("མི་འཇིགས་པའི་གནས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཕུན་སུམ་ཚོགས་པ," the abstract
>   noun "mastery/sovereignty," not a deity at all).

## Identity — named as the fifth worshipping deity in the sixth homage-verse

### Consensus

**All sixteen commentaries attest the fifth worshipping deity of the sixth
homage-verse** (alongside Indra, Agni, Brahmā, and Vāyu), whether by quoting
the root verse in full, paraphrasing its list, or glossing the bare term:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`,
`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-34`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`,
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-6`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`,
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `taranatha:c-6-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21` (16 commentaries)

The root-verse wording, where quoted in full, runs: "ཕྱག་འཚལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མེ་ལྷ་ཚངས་པ།
། རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་མཆོད་མ། ། འབྱུང་པོ་རོ་ལངས་དྲི་ཟ་རྣམས་དང་། ། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཚོགས་ཀྱིས་
མདུན་ནས་བསྟོད་མ།" (`yama-sonam:c-3-6-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`,
`sungrab-tulku:c-9-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-2`,
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-2`) — "Homage to her worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā,
Vāyu, and Sarveśvara, and praised from in front by hosts of bhūtas, vetālas,
gandharvas, and yakṣas." Two commentaries (`gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`,
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-6`) explicitly equate the term with **Maheśvara**
(དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ) in the same breath as naming it: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1`:
"སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་སྟེ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་སྟེ" ("Sarveśvara, that is, Maheśvara");
`karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-6`: "སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཏེ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་དག" ("Sarveśvara,
that is, the 'great lords'"). `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2` glosses the term
directly as a compound of two lesser deities rather than one figure (see
"Divergence," below).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed regarding the deity's *presence* in the worshipper-list — every
attestation above places him fifth, alongside Indra, Agni, Brahmā, and Vāyu.
Commentaries diverge sharply, however, on **what/whom he specifically is** —
see "Specific identification," below, for that divergence.

### Unique

`sungrab-tulku` alone gives the **bare, unmodified word** དབང་ཕྱུག (Īśvara,
not the compound སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག) as a separately registered figure in its own
Grounding index (FIG-11: "Īśvara, worldly god whose pride and arrogance Tārā
destroys"), distinct from its own FIG-10 (སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག, Viśveśvara, the
worshipper-list member). See "Pride-destruction," below, for the content this
distinct entry carries.

## Etymology — "Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara" as a composite term for several creator-gods

### Consensus

Three commentaries, independently, gloss "Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara" (literally
"lord of all" or "the various lords") not as one single deity's proper name
but as a **composite term covering several renowned creator-gods** — Rudra,
Kāma, and others:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4` (3 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-7`: "དྲག་པོ་དང་གཏོགས་འདོད་དང་། །དྲང་སྲོང་ཉི་མའི་མདོག་དང་། །ཕྱྭ་ལ་སོགས་
པ་བྱེད་པོའམ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཏུ་གྲགས་པ་སྣ་ཚོགས་པ་ནི། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་སྟེ།" ("Rudra, Kāma,
the sun-colored sage, Bhaga, and others variously renowned as creators or
lords — these are 'Viśveśvara,' the various lords"). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-21`
gives an independent but structurally parallel list, glossing the same root
phrase: "གཞན་རླུང་ལྷ་དང་། དྲག་པོ་དང་། གཏོགས་འདོད་ལ་སོགས་པའི་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་དབང་ཕྱུག་སྟེ"
("furthermore, Vāyu, the fierce one [Rudra], and the various lords such as
Kāma"). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-4` gives a shorter version of the same reading:
"རླུང་ལྷ།སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་གཟུགས་ཅན་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་སོགས་འཇིག་རྟེན་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་
ཀྱང་མཆོད་པར་བྱེད་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ" ("worshipped also by Vāyu and the great mundane
gods of manifold forms, such as Maheśvara"). All three treat the fifth
worshipper-slot as naming a *class* exemplified by Maheśvara/Rudra, not a
single deity with one fixed identity — read together, this may be why the
remaining thirteen commentaries feel free to leave the term unglossed beyond
"the great lord[s]" (see "Identity," above) or to disagree about which single
figure it names (see "Specific identification," below): on this reading there
may be no single figure to identify.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed among these three; each independently gives a composite-class
reading rather than conflicting on its content. Whether a composite-class
reading is itself compatible with the single-figure identifications given by
other commentaries (below) is not addressed by any claim in the packet — this
page does not adjudicate the tension, only records both readings as attested.

### Unique

None beyond the corroborated three above.

## Specific identification — three divergent single-figure readings

### Consensus

None — see Divergences, immediately below. What is shared is only the
underlying fact that the worshipper-list slot is treated as identifiable with
*some* other named figure — attested independently by at least one
commentary on each of three different identifications.

### ⚑ Divergences

Three commentaries name Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara as **Yama**, lord of the south:

- `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-1-3`: "སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་གཤིན་རྗེ་དང་" ("Viśveśvara
  is Yama of the south").
- `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-5-4`: "དེ་དག་གིས་མཚོན་པའི་གཤིན་རྗེ་དང་ཀླུ་སོགས་སྡེ་རིས་
  སྣ་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་དབང་ཕྱུག" ("Sarveśvara — the chief lords of various classes,
  exemplified by these: Yama, the nāgas, and so forth").
- `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-0-1`: "ལྷོ་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབ༵ང་ཕྱག་སྟེ་གཤིན་རྗེ" ("Sarveśvara
  of the south, that is, Yama"). **Orthography note:** both this claim's
  quotation and the root-verse quotation earlier in this same raw file spell
  the term "དབང་ཕྱག," not the corpus's more common "དབང་ཕྱུག" — verified
  directly against `1-SOURCES/Commentaries/རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉི་ཤུ་
  རྩ་གཅིག་གི་ཚིག་འགྲེལ་དང་དམིགས་རིམ་ཉུང་ངུར་བཀོད་པ་འཕགས་མའི་བྱིན་རླབས་གྲུ་ཆར་
  བཞུགས།།.md#^0-27 and #^0-28, both of which read "དབང་ཕྱག" in the source
  itself. This spelling is treated here as an attested orthographic variant
  present in this commentary's own source transmission (see "Orthographic
  variants," below), not as a different word or a different referent — the
  content (Yama, south) is otherwise identical to `palden-sherab`'s and
  `dharmabhadra`'s.

One commentary names him **Īśāna**, in the northeast, citing an external
liturgical source, and explicitly equates that figure with Maheśvara:

- `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-6`, quoting notes on Cakrasaṃvara's subtle-drop-body
  maṇḍala: "...བྱང་ཤར་དུ་དབང་ལྡན། འདི་དང་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་གཉིས་དོན་གཅིག་སྟེ" ("...in
  the northeast, Īśāna (དབང་ལྡན) — this and Maheśvara being one and the
  same"). This is an eight-guardian scheme (east Indra, south Yama, west
  water-god, north Vaiśravaṇa, southeast Agni, southwest rākṣasa, northwest
  Vāyu, northeast Īśāna/Maheśvara) — the *same* claim already cited on
  `indra.md`'s "Directional role" facet for Indra's own eastern placement, but
  its Maheśvara-identifying clause was not drawn on there.

One commentary treats the term as a **combined name for two separate lesser
deities** (the water-god and the earth-god), not a single powerful lord at
all:

- `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-2`: "སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་སྟེ་ཆུ་ལྷ་དང་ས་ལྷ་གཉིས།" ("Sarveśvara,
  that is, the two of them, the water-god and the earth-god"), elaborated at
  `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-6-5`: "ནུབ་ཀྱི་ཆུ་ལྷས་དབང་སྡུད་ཀྱི་ཞགས་པ་བཟུང་བས་...སའི་ལྷ་མོ་
  བསྟན་མ་གླིང་བཞིའི་ས་ལ་དབང་བར་རྒྱུད་སྡེ་ལས་གསུངས་པས་ན་སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཅེས་པ་དེ་
  གཉིས་ལ་སྦྱར" ("because the western water-god holds the power-drawing noose,
  and the tantra class states the earth-goddess Tenma has power over the four
  continents, 'Sarveśvara' is applied to these two").

The remaining twelve commentaries in the packet leave the fifth worshipper
unidentified beyond "the great lord[s]" (see "Identity," above) — this is
recorded as an observed gap, not a fourth position, since none of the twelve
takes an affirmative stance that conflicts with any of the three named
readings.

### Unique

None beyond the three positions above, each already singly or doubly attested.

## Vetāla-chief — Maheśvara as lord of the rolangs (zombie/vetāla) class

### Consensus

Three commentaries, glossing the *second* half of the same root verse (the
list of spirit-classes who praise Tārā "from in front" — bhūtas, vetālas,
gandharvas, yakṣas), independently identify **Maheśvara specifically as chief
of the vetālas (རོ་ལངས)** — a role distinct from his appearance as a
worshipping deity in the verse's first half:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-41`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`,
`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22` (3 commentaries)

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-41`: "རོ་ལངས་ནི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པའོ།" ("'Vetālas' means
[beings] such as Maheśvara"). `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-5-3`: "རོ་ལངས་ཀྱི་
གཙོ་བོ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ" ("Maheśvara, chief of the vetālas"). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-22`:
"རོ་ལངས་ནི་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པ" ("'vetālas' [are ruled by] Maheśvara and
others") — identical wording to `drakpa-gyaltsen`'s, suggesting a shared
exegetical source rather than fully independent corroboration.

### Unique

None beyond the corroborated three above.

## Hierarchical framing — Maheśvara as "lord" or "father" of the other four gods

### Consensus

None — this reading is attested by only two commentaries, each independently,
but their framings differ (lord-of vs. father-of), so they are recorded as
two related but distinct Unique claims rather than merged into one Consensus
statement.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — the two framings are complementary, not conflicting.

### Unique

- **`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-38`** frames Maheśvara as **lord** of the preceding
  four gods: "དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བདག་པོར་གྱུར་པ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོས་ཞབས་ལ་མཆོད་པ་འབུལ་བའོ།"
  ("Maheśvara, who has become the lord of these [four gods, i.e. Indra, Agni,
  Brahmā, Vāyu], offers worship at her feet").
- **`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-24`** frames the Maheśvara-class gods as **father** to
  the other great lords: "...སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་མིང་ཅན་མགོན་པོ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཡང་ཡབ་ཏུ་
  གྱུར་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་མཚོན་ཏེ" ("...exemplified by the great
  Maheśvara-class gods who are father even to these great, variously-named
  protectors").

## Hidden meaning — Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara as the space element

### Consensus

Two commentaries, in a completion-stage ("hidden meaning") reading of the
sixth homage that correlates the five worshipping gods with the five
elements, assign Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara to space — the same reading already
attested for the other four worshippers on `indra.md`, `brahma.md`, `agni.md`,
and `vayu.md`:

— attested: `taranatha:c-6-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-6-15`: "སྦས་དོན་ནི་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་སོགས་ལྔ་ནི་འབྱུང་བ་ལྔ་སྟེ། བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ནི་ས།
མེ་ལྷ་ནི་མེ། ཚངས་པ་ནི་ཆུ། རླུང་ལྷ་ནི་རླུང་། སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནམ་མཁའ་འོ། །" ("The
hidden meaning: Indra and the other four are the five elements — Indra is
earth, Agni is fire, Brahmā is water, Vāyu is wind, and Viśveśvara is
space"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-3-1`: "སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག་ནི་ནམ་མཁའ་ཡིན་པས་ན།"
("...since Viśveśvara is space..."), within the same five-fold correspondence
in the same order. The near-verbatim match suggests a shared exegetical
source rather than fully independent corroboration (as already noted on the
sibling pages for the other four worshippers).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

None beyond the corroborated pair above.

## Summoned as servant of Tārā's mantra-power

### Consensus

None — attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

`taranatha:c-5-4` names Maheśvara as the example figure for the claim that
Tārā's blessed mantra has the power to summon even the greatest worldly gods
as servants: "ཁྱོད་ཀྱིས་བྱིན་གྱིས་བརླབས་པའི་རིག་སྔགས་ཀྱི་མཐུས། དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་
སོགས་པའི་ལྷ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ཀྱང་བྲན་དུ་འགུགས་པའི་ནུས་མཐུ་ཡོད་ན། ཕྲ་མོ་གཞན་ལྟ་སྨོས་ཀྱང་
ཅི་དགོས།" ("By the power of the vidyā-mantra you have blessed, if there is
the power to summon even great gods such as Maheśvara as servants, what need
is there to mention lesser ones?"). This parallels the "summoned as servant"
material already attested for Indra on `indra.md`, though it is a separate
claim about a different figure, not a shared corroboration.

## Devotional narrative — Īśvara among the gods prayed to (ineffectually) by shipwrecked merchants

### Consensus

None — attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

`tsultrim-namdak:c-4-8-1` tells of merchants shipwrecked in a storm south of
India who first pray to Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Īśvara (Śiva) without effect,
before a Buddhist layman among them recites Tārā's ten-syllable mantra and
the storm instantly calms: "...ཚོང་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་ཚངས་པ་དང་། ཁྱབ་འཇུག །དབང་ཕྱུག་
སོགས་ལ་གསོལ་བ་བཏབ་ཀྱང་མ་ཕན...སྒྲོལ་མ་དྲན་ནས་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པ་སྒྲོལ་མའི་སྔགས་བཟླས་ཤིང་
གསོལ་བ་བཏབས་པས་དེ་མ་ཐག་རླུང་རྣམས་ཞི།" ("...the merchants prayed to Brahmā,
Viṣṇu, Īśvara, and others, without effect...[a devotee] recalled Tārā and
recited her ten-syllable mantra and prayed to her; the wind instantly
calmed"). This claim's own Referent field explicitly glosses this occurrence
of དབང་ཕྱུག as "Śiva." The same narrative is drawn on by `2-RAILS/Claims/mantra.md`'s
own "example story" facet, but for the mantra's power rather than for what it
says about Īśvara himself — this page cites it independently for its Īśvara
content specifically.

## Orthographic and naming variants

### Consensus

The corpus attests several distinct surface forms for this figure, none of
which any claim treats as denoting a different referent from the others:

- **སྣ་ཚོགས་དབང་ཕྱུག** (Sarveśvara/Viśveśvara, "the various/all-encompassing
  lord") — the most frequent form, used in the great majority of root-verse
  quotations and glosses cited above.
- **དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ** (Maheśvara, "the great lord") — used interchangeably with
  the above by `gendun-gyatso:c-1-6-1` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-6-6` (see
  "Identity," above), and used independently for the vetāla-chief role (see
  "Vetāla-chief," above).
- **Bare དབང་ཕྱུག** (Īśvara) — registered as its own figure by `sungrab-tulku`
  (FIG-11, distinct from its own FIG-10), carrying the "pride-destruction"
  content (see above), and also used generically by `tsultrim-namdak:c-4-8-1`
  (Śiva, in the shipwreck narrative).
- **དྲག་པོ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ** ("the Great Wrathful Lord") — `yama-sonam`'s
  own variant naming for the fifth worshipper, attested at
  `yama-sonam:c-3-6-5`: "...ནུབ་བྱང་གི་ཕྱོགས་སྐྱོང་རླུང་ལྷ་སྣ་ཚོགས་ཏེ་སྣ་ཚོགས་
  རྟ་ཅན་དང་། དྲག་པོ་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ་དང་།" ("...Vāyu of manifold aspect,
  'Possessor of Manifold Mounts'; and the Great Wrathful Lord; and...") — no
  other commentary in the packet uses this wrathful-form epithet for the
  fifth worshipper.
- **དབང་ཕྱག** — an orthographic variant of དབང་ཕྱུག attested in `sangye-nyentrul`'s
  own source transmission, present in both its root-verse quotation and its
  gloss (see "Specific identification," above, for the full citation and the
  direct `1-SOURCES/` verification). This is the reason `sangye-nyentrul` did
  not surface in a plain `rg 'དབང་ཕྱུག'` search of the raw claims files — its
  content was located only by inspecting the sixth-homage claim block
  directly once its presence was suspected from context.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — no claim treats any of these spellings or epithets as
denoting a figure different from the others in the worshipper-list role,
though the *further* identification each commentary attaches (Yama, Īśāna,
water+earth, or none) is a real divergence, already recorded above under
"Specific identification."

### Unique

None beyond the individual variants catalogued above.
