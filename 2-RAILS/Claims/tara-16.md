# Tārā 16 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་རབ་དགའ་བའི། / Homage to She Who, Greatly Joyful, Is Encircled on All Sides

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

> Note on the root text: the root verse itself (`1-SOURCES/Text/སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉེར་གཅིག་གི་བསྟོད་པ།.md#^1-16`) reads "རིག་པ་ཧཱུྃ་ལས་སྒྲོན་མ་ཉིད་མ།" (…"is the lamp herself, [arisen] from the vidyā HŪṂ") — སྒྲོན་མ, "lamp." Every commentary quotation in this packet instead gives "སྒྲོལ་མ་ཉིད་མ" (…"is Tārā herself") at that position. This is a root-text/commentary-tradition spelling divergence noted here for transparency; it is not itself a claim in the packet and is not further analysed below.

## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but mark its place in their own outline in two different ways. Eight give an explicit global ordinal, "the sixteenth" (བཅུ་དྲུག་པ) or a numeral marker:

— global-ordinal attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-1` (marked "༼༡༦༽"), `taranatha:c-16-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-1` (8 commentaries)

Three instead give only a *local* ordinal, "the first" (དང་པོ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the first of a six-member "praise by way of activity" (འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) sub-group covering homages 16–21, rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly:

— local-ordinal ("first of six activity-homages") attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-1` ("third [major division], praise by way of activity has six homages, of which the first is the activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras"), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-1` ("of the six kinds of homage praising by way of activity... the first, praising by way of teaching the activity of the root mantra"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-2` ("as for the first [the peaceful/wrathful mantra's activity]") (3 commentaries)

`gendun-drub`'s and `tenga-tulku`'s openers are close in structure and content (both explicitly enumerate "six" activity-homages and name this one "first"), though not character-identical; `lobsang-dawa`'s is terser but uses the same "first" marker for the same content. `sangye-nyentrul`'s own node title embeds a Tibetan numeral "༡༦" alongside its node number "17" (an internal offset, likely from a preceding introductory node), but no claim in this packet states an ordinal word for it. `anon-utpala` and `tsultrim-namdak` are also routed here by claim-content match rather than by a node-title claim, and neither has a structural-opener claim in this packet stating an ordinal.

Nine commentaries quote all four lines of the root verse, either as one continuous block or split across adjacent claims:

— full verse quoted attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-2` (its own claim title anomalously labels this "Verse 17," an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's extraction, not a disagreement about which verse this is), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-54`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-3` + `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-4` + `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-5` (three lines across three claims, labelled "Verse 35"/"Verse 36" in its own numbering) (9 commentaries)

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-13` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-14` additionally cite an independent source, "the Sādhana of the Twenty-One Praises to Tārā," quoting its matching lines ("utterly crushing the armies of enemies... with the ten-syllable mantra arrayed, herself the deliveress from the vidyā-syllable HŪṂ") as a second textual witness to the same content — this is the page's own observation that the sādhana's wording echoes the tantra verse, not a claim made explicitly by `anon-trinle-char` itself.

The remaining commentaries give only partial or paraphrastic quotation in this packet: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-83` and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-84` quote only the verse's third and fourth lines (the two mantra-lines); `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1` is explicitly flagged "[OCR uncertain]" in the packet and gives only a garbled paraphrase; `karma-maitri`, `konchok-thabkhe`, `sangye-nyentrul`, `pema-namgyal` paraphrase without a clean four-line quotation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the ordinal-marker differences above reflect different levels of each commentary's own sa-bcad (global numbering of twenty-one homages vs. local numbering within a six-member activity sub-group), not disagreement about which verse this is.

### Unique
None beyond the structural notes above.

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None as a single statement — every commentary that supplies a proper name or functional label gives one from a set of several distinct traditions.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"Destroyer of Enemies"** (དགྲ་འཇོམས་མ) — the section-heading name used by `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-1` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-2` ("the sixteenth homage, to the venerable Lady Destroyer of Enemies"). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-15` additionally reports that this is one of two names Nyima Sbaspa himself gives this Tārā, the other being "Tārā Who Destroys Attachment," and that "Rje Dge 'dun grub follows the former [Destroys Attachment]." This report is independently corroborated within the packet: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-5` itself concludes, after glossing how the two mantras crush self-grasping and "mine"-grasping, "she is called here 'ཆགས་པ་འཇོམས་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ' [Tārā Who Overcomes Attachment]" — the identical Tibetan phrase anon-trinle-char attributes to Gedun Drub. This is a strong, verbatim cross-corroboration: `gendun-drub`'s own commentary independently bears out `anon-trinle-char`'s report about it.
2. **"Destroyer of the Power of Enemies' and Sorcerers' Vidyā-Mantras"** (དགྲ་དང་བྱད་མའི་རིག་སྔགས་སྟོབས་གཞོམ) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-0-1` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-1-1` (which names her, in its own words, "this 'Tārā Who Destroys the Power of Enemies' and Sorcerers' Mantras'") give near-identical names, both foregrounding sorcery (བྱད་མ) specifically rather than enemies (དགྲ) alone. Two commentaries.
3. **"[She who] liberates through the vidyā-mantra HŪM" / "Deliveress from the Vidyā HŪM"** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-1` titles its own section "homage to Tārā Liberating Through the Awareness-Mantra HŪM" (རིག་པ་ཧཱུཾ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ་བསྟོད་པ). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-15` separately reports that "Co ne pa calls her 'Tārā, the Deliveress from the Vidyā HŪM'" (རིག་པ་ཧཱུཾ་ལས་སྒྲོལ་མ) — near-identical wording to `tenzin-dhonzang`'s own title, though this is `anon-trinle-char` reporting a third exegete's naming convention, not an independent claim by `tenzin-dhonzang` itself citing Co ne pa; the two are recorded as converging, not treated as one attestation of the other.
4. **"Blazing-Light One"** (སྒྲོལ་མ་འབར་བའི་འོད་ཅན) — `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-19`. Unique to this commentary within the packet.

Five commentaries instead give a *functional/thematic* classification rather than a proper name for this Tārā: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-1` labels the verse "homage by way of bestowing wisdom" (ཤེས་རབ་སྩོལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ); `sungrab-tulku:c-19-1` labels it "praise by way of the activity of destroying döns [harm-causing spirits] and so forth" (གདོན་སོགས་འཇོམས་པའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས); `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-1`, and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-2` (already cited under Structural placement) classify it as "the activity of the peaceful and wrathful mantras" / "the activity of the root mantra" — three distinct functional labels among five commentaries, none matching each other exactly.

The remaining commentaries (`anon-utpala`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha`) supply no proper name or functional classification for this homage within the claims in this packet — `taranatha:c-16-1` gives only the verse's own opening words as its section heading ("sixteenth, homage to [she] surrounded on all sides").

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-19`'s "Blazing-Light One" (above) is unique in the packet.

## "Encircled on all sides" and the surrounding retinue

### Consensus
None as a single statement — this facet is itself the subject of an explicit divergence report by one commentary, though a majority position can be identified.

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-7` itself frames this as a live exegetical disagreement about the root verse's opening phrase "ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར་རབ" (encircled on all sides): "some commentaries [ཨགྲེལ་པ་འགའ་ཞིག] take it to mean turning the wheel of Dharma, and others explain it to mean being encircled by mantra-letters; but it means being encircled on all sides by one's own wise disciples" (རང་ལ་ཤེས་རབ་ཅན་གྱི་འཁོར་སློབ་ཀྱིས་ཕྱོགས་ཀུན་བསྐོར་བའི་དོན་ཡིན་ཏེ).

1. **Turning the wheel of dharma** — the reading `anon-trinle-char` attributes to "some commentaries" is independently attested in the packet: `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-3` ("delighting the disciples by universally turning the wheel of dharma, thoroughly crushes the bodies of outer and inner enemies") and `sungrab-tulku:c-19-3` ("turning the wheel of dharma from every door of venerable Tārā's body, speech, and mind, she thoroughly crushes and destroys the bodies of the outer and inner enemies"). Two commentaries.
2. **Encircled by one's own wise retinue** — `anon-trinle-char`'s own preferred reading is echoed, in substance if not identical wording, by the generic-retinue descriptions given independently by `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-1` ("surrounded by the hosts of mantra- and truth-speech-accomplished ṛṣis and vidyādharas, and by the incalculable host of great bodhisattvas"), `taranatha:c-16-3` ("surrounded on all sides by accomplished vidyādharas and bodhisattvas endowed with samādhi and hearts of great joy"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-0-1` ("surrounded on all sides by hosts of sages, vidyādharas, and bodhisattvas"), `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-44` ("delighting in being surrounded by accomplished vidyādharas and the retinue of bodhisattva sons of the victors"), and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-54` ("surrounded entirely by gods, vidyādharas, and buddhas..."). Five commentaries converge on a generic wise/accomplished retinue (ṛṣis, vidyādharas, bodhisattvas, sages, gods), though none uses `anon-trinle-char`'s own specific phrase "one's own wise disciples" (rang gi shes rab can gyi 'khor slob).
3. **Encircled by mantra-letters** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-7` reports this as a third reading held by unnamed other commentaries, but no claim elsewhere in this packet independently attests this specific reading of "encircled on all sides" (as distinct from the separate heart-cakra mantra-letter visualizations discussed under "The two mantras," below, which describe what surrounds a syllable at the *practitioner's own heart*, not what is said to encircle Tārā in the root verse's first line). This reading is recorded as reported-but-unattested elsewhere in the packet.

Two commentaries independently name **four specific retinue goddesses**, rather than a generic retinue — a distinct fourth position, not overlapping with 1–3 above: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-8` ("one should meditate on the four goddesses — Steady Mind, Recollection, Wisdom, and Growing Intelligence — at one's own right, left, front, and back") and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-2` ("she is surrounded by four retinue goddesses — Firm-Mindedness, Mindfulness, Wisdom, and Increasing Intellect," Referent: four separate figure IDs FIG-17–20). Both cite what is, apart from the closing particle, a **character-identical** four-line verse — "ཡིད་གཞུངས་དྲན་སོགས་ཀུན་ནས་བསྐོར། །མི་ཤེས་དགྲ་དཔུང་རབ་འཇོམས་མ། །བློ་གྲོས་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་བཀོད་པས། །ཧཱུཾ་ལས་སྲིད་ཞིའི་མུན་སྒྲོལ་བསྒོམ།" — with `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-6` attributing it by name to "ཉི་མ་སྦས་པ" (Nyima Sbaspa), and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-2` attributing it to "གྲུབ་ཆེན" (the mahāsiddha), after noting that "པཎ་གྲུབ་གཉིས་ཀས" (both the paṇḍita and the siddha) treat this verse as being about increasing wisdom. The verbatim match strongly suggests a shared source (very plausibly the same figure, differently titled), though the packet does not state this identity outright, so it is reported here as a textual match rather than asserted as a confirmed identity.

— four-goddesses reading attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-6`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-8`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-2` (2 commentaries)

### Unique
None beyond the positions above.

## The two mantras: one complex, or two forms?

### Consensus
The *text* of the peaceful ten-syllable mantra, OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ, arranged around the syllable TAṂ at the heart, is attested with only minor spelling variants across commentaries:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-83`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56` (10 commentaries)

Note: several of these same claim IDs recur below under ⚑ Divergences. This is deliberate, not a mis-sort: the Consensus statement above attests only the mantra's literal *text*; the Divergences below ask a different question of the same claims — whether that text and the vidyā HŪM belong to one mantra-complex or two separate mantras. A claim can settle the first question while taking a side on the second without being double-counted for the same proposition.

### ⚑ Divergences
Beneath this shared mantra-*text*, commentaries diverge sharply on whether this ten-syllable mantra and the "vidyā HŪM" of the verse's fourth line belong to **one single mantra-complex and one deity**, or to **two separate mantras and two respective forms (peaceful and wrathful)** of Tārā:

1. **Two separate mantras, two forms (peaceful ten-syllable at TAṂ; wrathful, longer vidyā-mantra at HŪM)** — the majority reading. `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-4` + `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-5` explicitly separates "in the peaceful [form]... the ten-syllable speech" from "in the wrathful [form]... the vidyā-mantra" surrounding a HAṂ; `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-83` ("the peaceful mantra") + `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-84` ("the wrathful mantra"); `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-3` + `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-4` ("the syllabic mantra" / "the wrathful mantra"); `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-2` + `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-3` ("the peaceful mantra" / a mantra that "liberates the malicious"); `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-5` + `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-6`; `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-4` + `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-5` (explicitly "Peaceful Tārā's heart mantra" / "the Wrathful female's heart mantra"); `sungrab-tulku:c-19-4` (both given together, explicitly "peaceful" and "wrathful" hearts); `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-3` ("of the two, peaceful and wrathful..."); `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-4` + `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-5` (explicitly "peaceful Tara" / "wrathful Tara," with a distinct recitation time for each — see "Practice and timing," below); `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56` (gives both mantra-texts together, the second "the wrathful mantra"). Ten commentaries.
2. **One single mantra-complex (HŪM encircled by the ten syllables), one deity** — `anon-trinle-char`'s own resolved position. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-4` describes a single visualization: "the supreme vidyā-mantra heart-essence syllable HŪṂ... encircled by the ten-syllable mantra beginning OṂ TVA RE." `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-9` explicitly surveys the exegetical field and states its own conclusion: some commentaries place the ten-syllable mantra at the heart with the vidyā-HŪM mantra "recited from the mouth" separately; others (matching position 1, above) place the ten-syllable mantra at TAṂ and a separate vidyā-mantra encircling HŪM; "but taking HŪM itself as encircled by the ten mantra-letters, from which light rays radiate that dispel the darkness of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, appears to be the tantra's own intent" (ཧཱུཾ་ལ་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པའི་སྔགས་ཡིག་གི་བསྐོར་བ་ལས་འོད་ཟེར་འཕྲོས་པས་སྲིད་ཞིའི་མུན་པ་སེལ་བའི་དོན་དུ་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་དགོངས་པར་སྣང་སྟེ). `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-10` cites a further Nyima Sbaspa verse in support of this single-complex reading. `taranatha:c-16-7` reaches a related but not identical conclusion by a different route: "by this, it is taught that wrathful Tārā's seed-syllable HŪM and her approach-mantra are the ten syllables" (འདིས་ནི་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཁྲོ་མོ་ལ་ས་བོན་ཧཱུྃ་དང་བསྙེན་པའི་སྔགས་ཡི་གེ་བཅུ་པར་བསྟན་ནོ) — assigning the ten-syllable mantra to the *wrathful* form alone, rather than splitting it between a separate peaceful and wrathful pair. Two commentaries hold a single-complex/single-deity reading, though by two different routes; they are not identical positions and are not merged into one statement.
3. **Single deity, single mantra given, no second (wrathful) mantra text appearing in the packet's claims** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-2` gives only the ten-syllable mantra, recited "while clearly visualizing the wrathful goddess's generation-stage" — i.e. a single, already-wrathfully-named deity ("Destroyer of Sorcerers' Vidyā-Mantras," see Name/classification) recites the ten-syllable mantra; no separate longer vidyā-mantra for a second, wrathful form appears among `palden-sherab`'s claims in this packet. `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-0-2` similarly gives only the ten-syllable mantra for its single, already-red-bodied "Destroyer of Enemies'/Sorcerers' Mantras" deity, with no second mantra text in this packet. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-45` matches the same shape: "in the generation-and-recitation practice of wrathful Tārā, the ten-syllable formula is arrayed as her speech; she herself, Tārā, is generated from the seed-syllable HŪṂ, the heart-essence of the awareness-mantra" — again a single, already-wrathful deity whose own speech is the ten-syllable formula and whose seed-syllable is HŪṂ, with no separate wrathful mantra-text given in this packet's claims. Three commentaries. This is recorded as weaker evidence than position 2: it may simply reflect what these commentaries' extractions captured for this slot, not a stated denial that a separate wrathful mantra exists elsewhere in their own texts.

`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-3` gives the wrathful mantra as "OṂ TĀRE HŪṂ HARA HARA HŪṂ" — a text substantially different from every other commentary's wrathful-mantra wording in this packet (all of which give a longer "OṂ NAMA(Ḥ) TĀRE NAMO HĀRE/HARE HŪṂ HĀRA/HARE SVĀHĀ" pattern, with only minor spelling variants among themselves: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-84`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-6`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56`). No other commentary in the packet shares `tenga-tulku`'s specific wording; it stands as an isolated textual variant.

### Unique
`tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-3`'s divergent wrathful-mantra text (above) is unique in the packet.

## Activity: crushing enemies through mantra-light

### Consensus
Fourteen commentaries attest, in their own words, that light radiating from the heart-mantra(s) crushes or destroys the bodies of enemies (most specifying "outer and inner" enemies, some naming these as sorcerers, māras, or afflictions):

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-4`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-1-6`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-16-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-1-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-44`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-1-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-19-4`, `taranatha:c-16-4`, `taranatha:c-16-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-1-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-54`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-55`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-57` (14 commentaries)

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1` is explicitly flagged in the packet as "[OCR uncertain]" at the very point that would attest this activity (the garbled clause "...དགུའི་ནི་རབ་ཏུ་པར་མཛད་པའི་སྨོ་ལམ"), and is therefore not counted in the attestation list above, though its general iconography content is used elsewhere.

`drakpa-gyaltsen`'s two claims in this packet (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-83`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-84`) quote only the verse's mantra-lines and do not themselves state the crushing-activity content, even though the root verse's own second line (quoted by others) contains it; `drakpa-gyaltsen` is therefore not counted in the attestation list above.

### ⚑ Divergences
Four claim IDs recur here from the Consensus list above (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-55`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56`) — again deliberately, not a mis-sort: each supports the general "light crushes enemies" statement above *and* offers a more specific doctrinal gloss of what "enemy" means, recorded here. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-1-5` reads "enemy" doctrinally rather than literally: the two mantras "crush the enemy of liberation — grasping at 'self' within the body and grasping at 'mine' externally." `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-55` gives a similar inner-affliction gloss ("outer enemies — māras, obstructors, and the class of döns; inner enemies — self-grasping and grasping at 'mine'..."), continuing at `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-56` ("the afflictions of desire and hatred"). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-6` gives a related but distinct doctrinal gloss: "the body accumulated through sin is 'the enemy's body'... by reciting the awareness-mantra HUM with the body accumulated through virtue, all the power of others' harmful mantras is cut off." These doctrinal/inner readings are not presented as contradicting the more literal "outer and inner enemies" readings above (most of the same commentaries also use literal-enemy language elsewhere in the same claims), so they are not treated as a full divergence against the Consensus statement, but are recorded here as additional, more specific glosses not shared by every commentary in the Consensus list.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-16-4` uniquely names a specific enemy: "Pāpīyas, lord of the Paranirmitavaśavartin devas" (i.e. Māra), an identification not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

## Iconography — body colour, arms, and implements

### Consensus
None as a single statement — the packet attests at least two distinct iconographic traditions for this Tārā's own body/hand-implements, plus separate heart-cakra visualization content that most commentaries share.

Most commentaries that give any heart-visualization content agree that a wheel (or wheel-hub) at the heart, marked with a seed-syllable and encircled by the mantra's letters, is the source of the light: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-16-1` (flagged OCR-uncertain), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-16-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-16-4`. This is recorded here rather than folded fully into "The two mantras" facet because it concerns the visualized *support* (wheel/hub) rather than the mantra-text itself.

### ⚑ Divergences
On this Tārā's own body colour, arm-count, and hand-implements, two traditions are attested:

1. **Red, one face, three eyes, two arms; right hand holds a trident at the heart, left forms a mudrā "born with the essence-mantra"** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-11` alone gives this description in its own voice.
2. **Red, one face, two arms; right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left holding an utpala topped by a crossed vajra (or crossed weapon)** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-2-1` ("red-coloured, both legs in archer's posture, one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving mudrā, left, symbolizing the Three Jewels, holding at the pollen-centre of an utpala, a crossed vajra") and `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-1-1` + `sangye-nyentrul:c-17-1-2` ("red body"; "one face, two hands — right in supreme-giving mudra, left holding an utpala atop which a crossed double-vajra") give closely matching descriptions. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-19` corroborates the same tradition in substance ("red-hued, holding a crossed-weapon that repels sorcery upon an utpala"), though it does not itself state a specific mudrā for the right hand or an explicit arm-count. Three commentaries.

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-12` immediately follows its own claim c-3-16-11 by quoting Nyima Sbaspa "in confirmation" — but the quoted verse describes different content: "coral-coloured; benefiting singly, granting accomplishments; holding a fruit-bearing branch, brandishing the threatening gesture" (འབྲས་བཅས་ཤིང་བསྣམས་སྡིག་མཛུབ་འཕྱར). This names a fruit-bearing branch and the threatening (tarjanī) finger-gesture, neither of which matches c-3-16-11's own trident-and-mudrā description. The packet does not explain or resolve this mismatch; it is recorded here as an internal tension in what `anon-trinle-char` presents as a confirming citation, not resolved by this page.

No other commentary in the packet states an explicit body colour or arm-count for this Tārā; the remaining commentaries describe only the mantras and their activity (see above) without addressing overall iconography.

### Unique
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-11` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-16-12` (including the unreconciled mismatch between them, above) are unique to that commentary.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
None — this facet is a set of independent, non-overlapping doctrinal elaborations rather than a shared statement. Two commentaries each offer an extended hidden-meaning reading of this verse, using two different frameworks that do not overlap in content.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the two schemes below are independent elaborations, not incompatible readings of the same specific point; they are recorded separately rather than merged.

### Unique
`palden-sherab` gives a four-level exegesis (word-meaning, general/generation-stage meaning, completion-stage-with-marks, completion-stage-without-marks), of which the completion-stage levels read this verse through a **channels-and-winds** framework: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-3-1` ("the chief ordinary karmic wind of the Rasanā is expelled outward... the pure essence of the five wisdoms is held within the vase-like gentle wind"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-3-2` ("the ten winds ripen into the essence of wisdom... the great vajra life-force, the deity of wind and mantra, is precisely Jetsün Ārya Tārā herself"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-3-3` (enumerating the five root winds and five elemental winds by name and colour), continuing at the "completion-stage-without-marks" level with `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-1` ("self-arisen awareness-wisdom, HŪM... is Ārya Tārā"), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-2` (quoting the Kunjed Gyalpo, a Great Perfection tantra, at length on the maṇḍala of the heart being the unborn dharmadhātu), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-3` (self-liberation of self-grasping through realizing empty-luminous self-arisen awareness), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-4` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-16-4-5` (quoting Orgyen the Second Buddha's commentary on the Mañjuśrī-nāmasaṃgīti and teaching the Great Perfection view that all appearance/sound/awareness is primordially the display of the maṇḍala of body-speech-mind).

`taranatha` gives a separate "definitive meaning" (ངེས་དོན) reading built around a **ten-signs-and-five-wisdoms / individual-withdrawal (pratyāhāra)** framework, not overlapping with `palden-sherab`'s channels-and-winds or Dzogchen material: `taranatha:c-16-8` ("surrounded by the light-rays of the empty-form mahāmudrā, 'great joy,' that is, unwavering bliss"), `taranatha:c-16-9` ("destroys the enemy — the body of impure appearance — the branch of meditative concentration"), `taranatha:c-16-10` ("the ten syllables are the ten signs attained from the cessation of the portions of the ten maṇḍalas of the right and left [channels]"), `taranatha:c-16-11` ("the syllable symbolizes wind, and the syllable's sound is the unchanging akṣara... the ten unchanging appearances of the dharmadhātu"), `taranatha:c-16-12` ("or, the ten unchanging portions of bliss arisen from seeing that"), `taranatha:c-16-13` ("the five parts of HŪM symbolize the five wisdoms"), `taranatha:c-16-14` ("the drop of the nature of the five wisdoms, together with the reflected image of the three realms"), `taranatha:c-16-15` ("these are posited as the nature of the ten signs, arisen from filling the central channel with wind"), `taranatha:c-16-16` ("resting evenly in that is the ultimate great vidyā-mantra; the lamp is the clear appearance of empty-form"), `taranatha:c-16-17` ("this is the yoga of individual withdrawal [pratyāhāra]").

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58` is a ⚑ **ambiguous-fit** claim, flagged as such in the packet itself: a "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) passage reading iconographic elements from *several* homages together as the four empowerments and the two stages, naming elements from verses 12, 15, 10, and 13/16 in one synthesis that, per the packet's own routing note, "does not belong to any single verse." It is carried through here, flagged, rather than silently dropped or silently treated as this verse's own hidden-meaning content: its only element specific to verse 16 is the phrase "encircled on all sides... right leg extended, left bent" (already covered under "Encircled on all sides," above, and "Iconography," above, respectively — not iconographic detail beyond what those facets already record), read by the claim as indicating "the generation-stage aspect of principal-and-retinue and the completion-stage aspect of the channels and winds." It does not overlap in content with either `palden-sherab`'s or `taranatha`'s hidden-meaning schemes and is not merged with either.
