# Mudrā — ཕྱག་རྒྱ། (Ritual Hand-Gesture)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather method note.** Unlike the spine-mapped topic pages in this folder,
> this packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py`, nor by the
> `assemble_keyword_packet.py` script used for `mantra.md`, `gdon.md`, and the
> other Signal-A-tagged global topics — no Signal-A tagging run exists for
> ཕྱག་རྒྱ. Per this topic's own task instructions, the packet was gathered
> manually: `rg`/`grep -n "ཕྱག་རྒྱ"` against all sixteen
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files, followed by `Read` of each hit's
> full claim block (heading through `Cite:` line), copied character-for-character
> — never retyped. Every one of the sixteen commentaries returned at least one
> hit; none is silent on this topic.
>
> **Scope note.** ཕྱག་རྒྱ (*phyag rgya*, mudrā) is used in this corpus in two
> unrelated senses: (1) the ritual hand-gesture this page treats — principally
> the two-handed gesture-pair of homage 9 (a Three-Jewels-symbolizing mudrā on
> the left hand, a supreme-giving mudrā on the right) and its repetition as an
> iconographic template elsewhere; and (2) ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (*mahāmudrā*, "Great
> Seal"), an unrelated completion-stage/dzogrim doctrinal term for the union of
> bliss and emptiness or the nature of mind. Claims using ཕྱག་རྒྱ only in the
> second sense are listed under "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" and are
> never folded into a hand-gesture facet. This page is also deliberately
> narrower than 2-RAILS/Claims/mantra.md's "armor" facet, which it cross-references
> rather than re-argues.

## Homage 9's mudrā identified as symbolizing the Three Jewels

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries gloss root verse 9 — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི། སོར་མོས་ཐུགས་ཀར་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ" ("Homage to her whose heart is adorned by the fingers of the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels") — as naming a specific hand-gesture (ཕྱག་རྒྱ) that marks or symbolizes (མཚོན་པ) the Three Jewels (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ):

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-2`, `taranatha:c-9-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32` (16 commentaries)

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-3` quotes the verse "from the tantra" itself. `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1` both gloss the object of homage directly as "དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ" ("the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels"). `tsultrim-namdak` attests the same verse twice independently — once in its interlinear commentary (`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32`) and again in a separate appendix praise-cycle that also quotes this line verbatim (`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12`, logged below as a corroborating but not separately counted instance).

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the basic identification — every commentary that discusses this verse agrees the gesture symbolizes the Three Jewels. (Divergences on the gesture's exact *configuration* are recorded in the next facet; divergences on the gesture's *deeper doctrinal status* are recorded further below.)

### Unique
None beyond the consensus above — this facet is definitionally the corpus-wide floor every commentary shares.

## Hand-configuration: thumb and ring finger hold the flower, three fingers raised

### Consensus
Fourteen commentaries specify the same physical configuration: the left thumb and ring finger join to hold the stem of an utpala (blue lotus) flower, while the index, middle, and little fingers are raised toward the heart:

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (14 commentaries)

`anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-5` states it most fully: "her left thumb and ring finger hold the lotus stem, while her index, middle, and little fingers, raised skyward, form the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1` use near-identical wording for the same configuration ("index, middle, and little fingers raised skyward; the thumb and ring finger bent, their tips joined"), suggesting a shared source or convention between these two commentaries. `drakpa-gyaltsen` gives the configuration across two adjacent claims: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55` (thumb and ring finger hold the lotus at the heart) and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56` (the three extended fingers are "the sign symbolizing the Three Jewels").

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries diverge from the fourteen-way consensus above:

- **Taranatha's wholly different configuration** — `taranatha:c-9-4` describes not a three-raised-fingers gesture at all, but "palms joined [in añjali], with the middle fingers together, supported by the index fingers, thumbs pressed side by side and arranged at the fingertips, placed at the heart." This is a two-handed prayer-gesture, structurally unlike every other commentary's one-handed thumb-and-ring-finger-plus-three-raised-fingers description. Taranatha's broader distinct tradition on this mudrā is treated as its own facet below ("Taranatha's distinct tradition").
- **Sungrab-tulku's thumb-and-middle-finger variant** — `sungrab-tulku:c-12-4` states the left thumb and *middle* finger (མཐེ་མོ་དང་གུང་མོ) hold the utpala stem, with the index, ring, and little fingers (མཛུབ་མོ་དང་སྲིན་ལག་མཐེའུ་ཆུང) raised — the same general gesture-type as the fourteen-commentary consensus, but with the pairing shifted by one finger (thumb+middle instead of thumb+ring). This is recorded as a minor divergence rather than folded into the consensus list, since it does not match the specific finger-pairing the other fourteen share.

### Unique
None beyond the two divergences above.

## The paired right-hand supreme-giving mudrā and its wheel-marked palm

### Consensus
The same verse pairs the left-hand Three-Jewels mudrā with a right-hand gesture — the supreme-giving mudrā (མཆོག་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) — whose palm several commentaries further describe as marked or adorned with a wheel, from which light radiates:

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2` (7 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` gives the fullest description: the right hand's supreme-giving mudrā, its palm bearing "the design of a thousand-spoked wheel," radiates light that dispels the eight and sixteen fears. `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6` (the latter not separately counted above, elaborating the same claim) state the palm "is adorned with a wheel" from which "all light rays of every direction radiate." `taranatha:c-9-7` gives a differently-scoped gloss of the same "wheel of every direction" phrase — reading it as the ten directions' buddha-fields pervaded by her light rather than describing the right-hand palm-mark itself — which is why it is not counted among the seven above; it is the same divergence the sibling `wheel.md` page treats in full, so it is logged there as consulted-but-not-separately-argued here rather than re-analyzed on this page.

**This page does not re-litigate the wheel itself** — whether "the wheel of every direction" (མ་ལུས་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ) names a physical palm-mark or the cosmological totality of the ten directions is the sibling topic page `2-RAILS/Claims/wheel.md`'s own subject, including its own citations from `taranatha`, `anon-trinle-char`, `konchok-thabkhe`, and `pema-namgyal` for the "directional totality" reading (not repeated here to avoid duplicate attestation across two pages). This facet is scoped only to the mudrā-pairing itself — that a supreme-giving right-hand gesture accompanies the left-hand Three-Jewels mudrā in the same verse.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the mudrā-pairing itself (see `wheel.md` for the divergence on what the wheel-mark denotes).

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## Doctrinal reasoning: why the mudrā symbolizes the Three Jewels

### Consensus
None — three commentaries each give their own, non-overlapping reasoning for the symbolism; no two independently state the same explanation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None — these are complementary elaborations, not competing claims, so they are recorded as Unique rather than as opposed positions.

### Unique
- **Cause-and-activity reasoning** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-6` explains that the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels has its "cause arising from the two jewels of Buddha and Dharma," while its "activity shows the meaning of the Saṅgha carrying [the teaching] onward" — a structural rather than merely numerical account of why three raised fingers correspond to three jewels. The same claim's companion citation, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-7`, quotes master Nyima Sbaspa's own praise in support: "well born from Buddha and Dharma; great, loving one who nurtures the entire Saṅgha."
- **Three-times-buddhas reasoning** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5` gives a different numerical correspondence: the three raised fingers signify Tārā as "the embodiment of the union of all buddhas of the three times" — emanation of past buddhas, source of future buddhas, regent of present buddhas — rather than Buddha/Dharma/Saṅgha directly.
- **Body-speech-mind and three-roots reasoning** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` gives a third, cumulative reasoning in the same commentary: Tārā's body is the Saṅgha, speech the Dharma, mind the Buddha (union of the Three Jewels); body is emanation-body, speech enjoyment-body, mind dharma-body (union of the three kāyas); body is guru, speech yidam, mind ḍākinī (union of the three roots) — with the mudrā itself, formed immediately after this three-layered identification, said to mark all of it at once.
- **Refuge-and-buddhas-of-three-times reasoning, attributed to Gendün Drup** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4` quotes the First Dalai Lama Gendün Drup's own praise to White Tārā, glossing her left-hand utpala mudrā of refuge-giving: the utpala "symbolizes the buddhas of the three times," while "the mudrā itself shows the nature of all Three Jewels gathered as one" — a fourth distinct account (utpala = buddhas of three times; mudrā-as-such = Three Jewels unity), attributed to a named source outside konchok-thabkhe's own voice.

No two of these four accounts restate each other, and none contradicts another — they are recorded as four independent doctrinal elaborations on the same base fact (facet 1's consensus), not as a divergence.

## Divergence on the source of the radiating light

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is a divergence recorded within a single commentary's own survey of others.

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-8` reports, without resolving, that "some commentaries state that [the light rays in this verse] are the radiance of the wheel-mark on the palm of her right hand, while others state that they are the radiance of her body — the positions differ." Anon-trinle-char's own claims elsewhere in this cluster (`anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-9`, citing Nyima Sbaspa's praise "self-arisen light, contemplated as pervading all," and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-9-10`, arguing "since it is stated to be light arisen from herself, it is not stated to be the radiance of a hand-implement") show anon-trinle-char siding with the "body radiance" position, while attributing the "wheel-mark radiance" position to unnamed other commentaries it does not identify by name.

This divergence is recorded as reported-but-unattributed within this corpus: no other commentary among the sixteen in this packet is independently on record taking the "wheel-mark radiance" side by name (several, per the facet above, describe the wheel-marked palm without stating whether the verse's own light-imagery originates there specifically or from the body generally — this is a finer distinction than those claims themselves draw).

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## Taranatha's distinct tradition: the mudrā as Tārā's own samaya-mudrā

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None beyond the hand-configuration divergence already recorded above; the material below is additional doctrine unique to taranatha, not a second position on a question other commentaries also answer.

### Unique
Taranatha gives an extended, doctrinally distinct treatment of this mudrā across a continuous run of claims (`taranatha:c-9-3` through `taranatha:c-9-8`), found nowhere else in the packet:

- **Identified as Tārā's samaya-mudrā** — `taranatha:c-9-3`: "The utpala-mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels is Tārā's samaya-mudrā [dam tshig gi phyag rgya]" — naming the gesture not merely as a symbolic hand-shape but as the goddess's own tantric pledge-seal.
- **Followers should bind the same mudrā** — `taranatha:c-9-5`: "If followers also correctly form that same samaya-mudrā —"
- **The Bhagavatī herself appears bound in this mudrā's form** — `taranatha:c-9-6`: "The blessing of the vidyā-mantra deity occurs beforehand, and Bhagavatī herself [appears in] the bodily aspect of binding that same mudrā" — the goddess's own iconographic body is described as itself performing the mudrā, not merely as a gesture practitioners direct toward her.
- **The mudrā invites light from the ten directions for the practitioner** — `taranatha:c-9-8`: "For the follower, when invited by that mudrā, light rays move from the buddha-fields of the ten directions and actually arrive" — the mudrā functions as an active invitational ritual instrument, not solely a passive iconographic marker.

No other commentary in this packet identifies this mudrā as a samaya-mudrā, states that the goddess's own body performs it, or describes it as functioning to invite light for the practitioner — this entire strand of doctrine (name, function, and configuration alike) is unique to taranatha within this corpus.

## Completion-stage "hidden meaning" readings of the mudrā

### Consensus
None — two commentaries both give a completion-stage (rdzogs rim) "hidden meaning" (sbas don / gsang don) reading of this same mudrā, but assign different subtle-body referents to its three raised fingers, so no shared statement is recorded as consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
- **Taranatha: the three substances** — `taranatha:c-9-9` and `taranatha:c-9-10` read "the Three Jewels" as symbolizing "the three [substances] — seminal fluid, [menstrual] essence, and wind" (ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ), with the three-finger mudrā representing these three gathered and bound at the central navel-drop, producing "the wisdom-body accomplished from the light-rays of tummo, pervading all directions."
- **Palden-sherab: drop, subtle-drop, and wind at the channel-wheels** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` gives a parallel but distinct completion-stage reading: "'symbolizing the Three Jewels' is [symbolizing] the three of essence-drop, subtle-drop, and wind; 'fingers' is the mudrā uniting these three as one; 'heart' is the central hub of each channel-wheel of these" — with the resulting light read as "wisdom's actual-body tummo... within the experiential realization of the hundredfold light of co-emergent bliss-emptiness wisdom."

Both commentaries agree on the general strategy — read "Three Jewels" as three subtle-body substances/drops bound at a central point, and the resulting light as tummo/co-emergent-wisdom radiance — but taranatha's triad (seminal fluid, essence, wind) and palden-sherab's triad (essence-drop, subtle-drop, wind) name the three substances differently (taranatha's "seminal fluid" and "essence" split what palden-sherab calls simply "essence-drop" and "subtle-drop"). This page records the two as a divergence in specifics under a shared general strategy, per Rule 11 — each claim is cited on only the one side it actually states.

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## Mantra-and-mudrā as protective "armor" (སྔགས་དང་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་གོ་ཆ)

### Consensus
Five commentaries pair mantra and mudrā together as a protective "armor" (གོ་ཆ) that the practitioner dons or is blessed by, generating joy and dispelling quarrels, curses, and bad dream-signs:

— attested: `taranatha:c-19-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-66` (5 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-19-6` states the practitioner is "fully bound by the armor of the mantra and mudrā you [the goddess] have blessed." `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2` (glossing the White Umbrella/Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā verse rather than Tārā's own generic form) both attribute to this mantra-and-mudrā armor the dispelling of "quarrels" and "bad dream-signs" through the practitioner's "three doors" (body, speech, mind). `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-66` gives the same benefit in near-identical terms.

## The supreme-giving/refuge-giving mudrā-pair as a recurring iconographic template

### Consensus
None — this facet records a *pattern* across many individually unique claims, not a single shared statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Three commentaries reuse the same basic two-handed mudrā-pair described in homage 9 (right hand: supreme-giving/mchog-sbyin; left hand: a Three-Jewels-symbolizing or refuge-giving/skyabs-sbyin mudrā, typically holding an utpala with a form-specific implement or emblem at its pollen-centre) as a template applied repeatedly across generation-stage visualizations of *other* deity-forms, well beyond the homage-9 verse itself:

- **Palden-sherab's cycle of the twenty-one individual Tārā emanations** — this commentary's `3.1.1` through `3.1.21` sub-sections each give a "general meaning" (སྤྱི་དོན) generation-stage visualization for one of the praise's twenty-one homages, and each pairs a form-specific right-hand supreme-giving gesture with a left-hand Three-Jewels/refuge-giving mudrā holding an utpala topped by a different implement (a conch for "Swift Heroine," a life-vase for Uṣṇīṣavijayā, a drawn bow for Kurukullā, a vajra-dagger for the Great Terrifying One, a sword for Tummo the Invincible, a victory-banner for "Victorious over the Three Worlds," a vajra with upturned horns for "Victorious in Battle," a stūpa for "Immeasurably Subduing," a pestle for the Wrathful-Browed form, a nectar-vase for the Utterly-Peaceful form, a crossed-vajra for the Destroyer of Sorcery, the full moon marked with a deer for the Great Peacock, a white parasol for Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā, a nectar-filled casket for the Forest-Dweller, and a golden fish for the Radiant Goddess). Representative instances: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-3` (Swift Heroine) and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-1` (the Śiṃśapā-Forest form belonging to homage 9's own appendix cycle).
- **Tsultrim-namdak's parallel cycle** — a structurally similar, independent twenty-one-verse praise (distinct from the root text, quoted in full at `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-4`) that this commentary also comments on verse-by-verse, using the same supreme-giving/Three-Jewels mudrā-pair template for each named Tārā form. Representative instances: `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4` (Tārā Myur-ma dpa'-mo) and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12` (the Khadira-Forest Tārā, corresponding to the same homage-9 slot as palden-sherab's Śiṃśapā-Forest form). The same commentary's self-generation sādhana (`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-4`) and ritual instructions (`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-12`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-7-1-5`) reuse the identical right-hand supreme-giving mudrā, there shown transforming into a refuge-granting mudrā placed over the practitioner's crown.
- **Anon-trinle-char's ritual-appendix forms** — a smaller set of similar visualizations for deity-forms outside the homage sequence proper, each opening with the same "mudrā of great joy" (རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) two-handed gesture before other implements are added. Representative instances: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-1-22` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-10-10`.

This pattern is recorded here as a single Unique facet rather than as dozens of separate iconographic facts, because the individual claims do not add new information about mudrā *as a category* beyond confirming that this specific gesture-pair became this corpus's standard iconographic shorthand for "a benevolent, Tārā-lineage deity-form." Every remaining claim instantiating this same pattern is logged in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below, with this paragraph as its shared reason.
