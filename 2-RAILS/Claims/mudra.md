# Mudrā — ཕྱག་རྒྱ། (Ritual Hand-Gesture)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> **Gather method note.** Unlike the spine-mapped topic pages in this folder,
> this packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py`, nor by the
> `assemble_keyword_packet.py` script used for `mantra.md`, `gdon.md`, and the
> other Signal-A-tagged global topics — no Signal-A tagging run exists for
> ཕྱག་རྒྱ. Per this topic's own task instructions, the packet was gathered
> manually: `rg`/`grep -n "ཕྱག་རྒྱ"` against all sixteen
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files, followed by `Read` of each hit's
> full claim block (heading through `Cite:` line), copied character-for-character
> — never retyped. Every one of the sixteen commentaries returned at least one
> hit; none is silent on this topic.
>
> **Scope note.** ཕྱག་རྒྱ (*phyag rgya*, mudrā) is used in this corpus in two
> unrelated senses: (1) the ritual hand-gesture this page treats — principally
> the two-handed gesture-pair of homage 9 (a Three-Jewels-symbolizing mudrā on
> the left hand, a supreme-giving mudrā on the right) and its repetition as an
> iconographic template elsewhere; and (2) ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (*mahāmudrā*, "Great
> Seal"), an unrelated completion-stage/dzogrim doctrinal term for the union of
> bliss and emptiness or the nature of mind. Claims using ཕྱག་རྒྱ only in the
> second sense are listed under "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" and are
> never folded into a hand-gesture facet. This page is also deliberately
> narrower than 2-RAILS/Claims/mantra.md's "armor" facet, which it cross-references
> rather than re-argues.

## Homage 9's mudrā identified as symbolizing the Three Jewels

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries gloss root verse 9 — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི། སོར་མོས་ཐུགས་ཀར་རྣམ་པར་བརྒྱན་མ" ("Homage to her whose heart is adorned by the fingers of the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels") — as naming a specific hand-gesture (ཕྱག་རྒྱ) that marks or symbolizes (མཚོན་པ) the Three Jewels (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ):

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-9-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-2`, `taranatha:c-9-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32` (16 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-9-3` quotes the verse "from the tantra" itself. `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1` glosses the object of homage directly as "དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ" ("the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels"), and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1` glosses it with the same phrase in its unexpanded form, "དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ" (without པའི). `tsultrim-namdak` attests the same verse twice independently — once in its interlinear commentary (`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-32`) and again in a separate appendix praise-cycle that also quotes this line verbatim (`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12`, logged below as a corroborating but not separately counted instance).

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the basic identification — every commentary that discusses this verse agrees the gesture symbolizes the Three Jewels. (Divergences on the gesture's exact *configuration* are recorded in the next facet; divergences on the gesture's *deeper doctrinal status* are recorded further below.)

### Unique
None beyond the consensus above — this facet is definitionally the corpus-wide floor every commentary shares.

## Hand-configuration: thumb and ring finger hold the flower, three fingers raised

### Consensus
Fourteen commentaries specify the same physical configuration: the left thumb and ring finger join to hold the stem of an utpala (blue lotus) flower, while the index, middle, and little fingers are raised toward the heart:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-9-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-30`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (14 commentaries)

`yama-sonam:c-3-9-5` states it most fully: "her left thumb and ring finger hold the lotus stem, while her index, middle, and little fingers, raised skyward, form the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels." `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-0-1` states "the index, middle, and little fingers raised, the thumb and ring finger bent with their tips touching," and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-1`'s Tibetan gives the same configuration in near-identical wording, suggesting a shared source or convention between these two commentaries. `drakpa-gyaltsen` gives the configuration across two adjacent claims: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-55` (thumb and ring finger hold the lotus at the heart) and `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-56` (the three extended fingers are "the sign symbolizing the Three Jewels").

Three of the fourteen support only part of this composite statement, not all of it: `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-2` states only that the left hand's three fingers (index, middle, little) symbolize the Three Jewels, with no stem-holding detail; `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-1` states only the three raised fingers, with no thumb-and-ring stem-hold; and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-2` gives the thumb-and-ring-joined, three-raised configuration but does not mention the utpala stem.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two commentaries diverge from the fourteen-way consensus above:

- **Taranatha's wholly different configuration** — `taranatha:c-9-4` describes not a three-raised-fingers gesture at all, but "palms joined [in añjali], with the middle fingers together, supported by the index fingers, thumbs pressed side by side and arranged at the fingertips, placed at the heart." This is a two-handed prayer-gesture, structurally unlike every other commentary's one-handed thumb-and-ring-finger-plus-three-raised-fingers description. Taranatha's broader distinct tradition on this mudrā is treated as its own facet below ("Taranatha's distinct tradition").
- **Sungrab-tulku's thumb-and-middle-finger variant** — `sungrab-tulku:c-12-4` states the left thumb and *middle* finger (མཐེ་མོ་དང་གུང་མོ) hold the utpala stem, with the index, ring, and little fingers (མཛུབ་མོ་དང་སྲིན་ལག མཐེའུ་ཆུང) raised — the same general gesture-type as the fourteen-commentary consensus, but with the pairing shifted by one finger (thumb+middle instead of thumb+ring). This is recorded as a minor divergence rather than folded into the consensus list, since it does not match the specific finger-pairing the other fourteen share.

### Unique
None beyond the two divergences above.

## The paired right-hand supreme-giving mudrā and its wheel-marked palm

### Consensus
The same verse pairs the left-hand Three-Jewels mudrā with a right-hand gesture — the supreme-giving mudrā (མཆོག་སྦྱིན་གྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ) — whose palm several commentaries further describe as marked or adorned with a wheel, from which light radiates:

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-9-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-33` (8 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` gives the fullest description: the right hand's supreme-giving mudrā, its palm bearing "the design of a thousand-spoked wheel," radiates light that dispels the eight and sixteen fears. `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-5` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6` (the latter not separately counted above, elaborating the same claim) state the palm "is adorned with a wheel" from which "all light rays of every direction radiate." `taranatha:c-9-7` gives a differently-scoped gloss of the same "wheel of every direction" phrase — reading it as the ten directions' buddha-fields pervaded by light arising from her own body, rather than describing the right-hand palm-mark itself — which is why it is not counted among the eight above. That difference is the subject of its own facet below ("Divergence on the source of the radiating light"); the sibling `wheel.md` page treats the same divergence from the wheel's side.

**This page does not re-litigate the wheel itself** — whether "the wheel of every direction" (མ་ལུས་ཕྱོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ) names a physical palm-mark or the cosmological totality of the ten directions is the sibling topic page `2-RAILS/Claims/wheel.md`'s own subject, including its own citations from `taranatha`, `yama-sonam`, `konchok-thabkhe`, and `pema-namgyal` for the "directional totality" reading (not repeated here to avoid duplicate attestation across two pages). This facet is scoped only to the mudrā-pairing itself — that a supreme-giving right-hand gesture accompanies the left-hand Three-Jewels mudrā in the same verse.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the mudrā-pairing itself (see `wheel.md` for the divergence on what the wheel-mark denotes).

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations above.

## Doctrinal reasoning: why the mudrā symbolizes the Three Jewels

### Consensus
None — three commentaries each give their own, non-overlapping reasoning for the symbolism; no two independently state the same explanation.

### ⚑ Divergences
None — these are complementary elaborations, not competing claims, so they are recorded as Unique rather than as opposed positions.

### Unique
- **Cause-and-activity reasoning** — `yama-sonam:c-3-9-6` explains that the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels has its "cause arising from the two jewels of Buddha and Dharma," while its "activity shows the meaning of the Saṅgha carrying [the teaching] onward" — a structural rather than merely numerical account of why three raised fingers correspond to three jewels. The same claim's companion citation, `yama-sonam:c-3-9-7`, quotes master Nyima Sbaspa's own praise in support: "well born from Buddha and Dharma; great, loving one who nurtures the entire Saṅgha."
- **Three-times-buddhas reasoning** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-5` gives a different numerical correspondence: the three raised fingers signify Tārā as "the embodiment of the union of all buddhas of the three times" — emanation of past buddhas, source of future buddhas, regent of present buddhas — rather than Buddha/Dharma/Saṅgha directly.
- **Body-speech-mind and three-roots reasoning** — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-4` gives a third, cumulative reasoning in the same commentary: Tārā's body is the Saṅgha, speech the Dharma, mind the Buddha (union of the Three Jewels); body is emanation-body, speech enjoyment-body, mind dharma-body (union of the three kāyas); body is guru, speech yidam, mind ḍākinī (union of the three roots) — with the mudrā itself, formed immediately after this three-layered identification, said to mark all of it at once.
- **Refuge-and-buddhas-of-three-times reasoning, attributed to Gendün Drup** — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-9-4` quotes Gendün Drup's own praise to White Tārā (the claim names him རྗེ་དགེ་འདུན་གྲུབ and no more; the identification with the First Dalai Lama is not in the source), glossing her left-hand utpala mudrā of refuge-giving: the utpala "symbolizes the buddhas of the three times," while "the mudrā itself shows the nature of all Three Jewels gathered as one" — a fourth distinct account (utpala = buddhas of three times; mudrā-as-such = Three Jewels unity), attributed to a named source outside konchok-thabkhe's own voice.

No two of these four accounts restate each other, and none contradicts another — they are recorded as four independent doctrinal elaborations on the same base fact (facet 1's consensus), not as a divergence.

## Divergence on the source of the radiating light

### Consensus
None — the commentaries divide on where the verse's light originates, and both sides are held within this packet.

### ⚑ Divergences
`yama-sonam:c-3-9-8` reports, without resolving, that "some commentaries state that [the light rays in this verse] are the radiance of the wheel-mark on the palm of her right hand, while others state that they are the radiance of her body — the positions differ." Anon-yama-sonam's own claims elsewhere in this cluster (`yama-sonam:c-3-9-9`, citing Nyima Sbaspa's praise "self-arisen light, contemplated as pervading all," and `yama-sonam:c-3-9-10`, arguing "since it is stated to be light arisen from herself, it is not stated to be the radiance of a hand-implement") show yama-sonam siding with the "body radiance" position, while attributing the "wheel-mark radiance" position to other commentaries it does not identify by name.

**The divergence yama-sonam reports plays out inside this packet, on both sides:**

- **"The light radiates from the wheel-marked palm"** — stated in the commentaries' own wording, not merely implied: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` ("her own mass of mutually agitated light-rays **radiating from the wheel adorning the palm** of her right, boon-granting hand"); `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6` ("all light rays of every direction radiate **from the wheel-adorned part of that mudrā's palm**"); `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2` ("**from the wheel-marked palm** of her right hand in the mudrā of supreme giving, radiating light that fills every direction"); `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4` and `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` (the thousand-spoked wheel on the palm, "whose beautiful light-rays dispel the eight and sixteen fears").
- **"The light is her body's own radiance"** — `taranatha:c-9-7`: the ten directions' buddha-fields are pervaded "by the agitated, radiating mass of light-rays **arising from her own body**" (རང་གི་སྐུ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་འོད་ཟེར); and `yama-sonam:c-3-9-9` / `yama-sonam:c-3-9-10`, per its argument above.

— attested, wheel-mark side: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-9-6`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-2-4`, `sungrab-tulku:c-12-5` (5 commentaries)

— attested, body-radiance side: `taranatha:c-9-7`, `yama-sonam:c-3-9-9`, `yama-sonam:c-3-9-10` (2 commentaries)

An earlier version of this page recorded the divergence as "reported but unattributed," on the reading that the wheel-palm claims describe the mark without saying where the verse's light comes from. That reading does not survive the claims' own wording: five of them state the provenance explicitly. The remaining commentaries in the packet are silent on the question and are not counted on either side.

### Unique
None beyond the divergence above.

## Taranatha's distinct tradition: the mudrā as Tārā's own samaya-mudrā

### Consensus
None — this entire facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None beyond the hand-configuration divergence already recorded above; the material below is additional doctrine unique to taranatha, not a second position on a question other commentaries also answer.

### Unique
Taranatha gives an extended, doctrinally distinct treatment of this mudrā across a continuous run of claims (`taranatha:c-9-3` through `taranatha:c-9-8`), found nowhere else in the packet:

- **Identified as Tārā's samaya-mudrā** — `taranatha:c-9-3`: "The utpala-mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels is Tārā's samaya-mudrā [dam tshig gi phyag rgya]" — naming the gesture not merely as a symbolic hand-shape but as the goddess's own tantric pledge-seal.
- **If followers bind the same mudrā** — `taranatha:c-9-5` states this conditionally, not as an instruction: "If followers also correctly form that same samaya-mudrā —"
- **The Bhagavatī herself appears bound in this mudrā's form** — `taranatha:c-9-6`: "The blessing of the vidyā-mantra deity occurs beforehand, and Bhagavatī herself [appears in] the bodily aspect of binding that same mudrā" — the goddess's own iconographic body is described as itself performing the mudrā, not merely as a gesture practitioners direct toward her.
- **The mudrā invites light from the ten directions for the practitioner** — `taranatha:c-9-8`: "For the follower, when invited by that mudrā, light rays move from the buddha-fields of the ten directions and actually arrive" — the mudrā functions as an active invitational ritual instrument, not solely a passive iconographic marker.

No other commentary in this packet identifies this mudrā as a samaya-mudrā, states that the goddess's own body performs it, or describes it as functioning to invite light for the practitioner — this entire strand of doctrine (name, function, and configuration alike) is unique to taranatha within this corpus.

## Completion-stage "hidden meaning" readings of the mudrā

### Consensus
Two commentaries give a completion-stage (rdzogs rim) "hidden meaning" (sbas don / gsang don) reading of this mudrā, and they give **the same one, in the same words**: both gloss "symbolizing the Three Jewels" with the identical Tibetan phrase ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ, read the three raised fingers as a mudrā uniting those three as one, and place them at the central hub of the channel-wheel at the heart, with the verse's light read as tummo radiance:

— attested: `taranatha:c-9-9`, `taranatha:c-9-10`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-9-9` — "སྦས་དོན་ནི། དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ནི་ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ་སྟེ།"; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` — "གསུམ་པ་སྦས་དོན་རྫོགས་རིམ་མཚན་བཅས་ལྟར་ན། དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན་ནི་ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ་སྟེ། སོར་མོ་ནི་དེ་གསུམ་གཅིག་ཏུ་འདུས་པའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ།". The clause the two share is word-for-word identical. Their statements of the locus are also near-identical — `taranatha:c-9-10` "རྩ་འཁོར་ལོ་སོ་སོའི་ཐུགས་ཀ་སྟེ་དབུས་ཀྱི་ལྟེ་བའི་ཐིག་ལེར་ཡོངས་སུ་བཅིངས་པས" against palden-sherab's "ཐུགས་ཀར་ནི་དེ་རྣམས་རྩ་འཁོར་སོ་སོའི་ཐུགས་དབུས་ཀྱི་ལྟེ་བར་རོ།" — as are their readings of the resulting light as tummo radiance. Palden-sherab additionally frames the whole gloss as the third of its four-level commentarial method and continues into "the hundredfold light of co-emergent bliss-emptiness wisdom"; taranatha ends at "the wisdom-body accomplished from the light-rays of tummo, pervading all directions." Neither states it is quoting the other, and this page does not assert a direction of dependence — only that the two readings, as attested, agree verbatim on the referents.

⚑ **Raw-file gloss conflict, flagged for a human — not a doctrinal divergence.** The two raw claims render the *same* Tibetan phrase ཁུ་རྡུལ་རླུང་གསུམ into English differently: `taranatha:c-9-9` as "seminal fluid, [menstrual] essence, and wind", `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-3-1` as "essence-drop, subtle-drop, and wind". An earlier version of this page read that difference as a divergence in what the two commentators assert; it is not — it is a difference between two English glosses of one identical Tibetan string, and the Tibetan is what either commentary actually says. Which English rendering of ཁུ་རྡུལ is correct is a question for a domain specialist, and it should be resolved in the raw files rather than here.

### ⚑ Divergences
None on the hidden-meaning reading itself: the two commentaries that give one give the same one.

### Unique
None — no third commentary in the packet offers a completion-stage reading of this mudrā.

## Mantra-and-mudrā as protective "armor" (སྔགས་དང་ཕྱག་རྒྱའི་གོ་ཆ)

### Consensus
Five commentaries pair mantra and mudrā together as a protective "armor" (གོ་ཆ) that the practitioner dons or is blessed by, generating joy and dispelling quarrels, curses, and bad dream-signs:

— attested: `taranatha:c-19-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-66` (5 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-19-6` states the practitioner is "fully bound by the armor of the mantra and mudrā you [the goddess] have blessed." `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4` and `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2` both attribute to this mantra-and-mudrā armor the dispelling of "quarrels" and "bad dream-signs" through the practitioner's "three doors" (body, speech, mind) — `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2` glossing the White Umbrella (Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā) verse, while `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4` attributes the same armor to **Tārā herself** (རྗེ་བཙུན་སྒྲོལ་མ) and does not mention Sitātapatrā at all. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-66` gives the same benefit in near-identical terms.

**This page treats the armor-pairing only from mudrā's side, briefly.** The fuller treatment of this same facet — including `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-4` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-4`, which name the armor without an independent ཕྱག་རྒྱ hit in this packet's search — belongs to `2-RAILS/Claims/mantra.md`'s own "Mantra as protective armor" facet, which this page cross-references rather than duplicates in full.

## The supreme-giving/refuge-giving mudrā-pair as a recurring iconographic template

### Consensus
None — this facet records a *pattern* across many individually unique claims, not a single shared statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
Three commentaries each open every form in an extended generation-stage cycle with a fixed two-handed gesture, applying it as a template well beyond the homage-9 verse itself. In two of the three the gesture is the mudrā-pair described in homage 9 itself (right hand: supreme-giving/mchog-sbyin; left hand: a Three-Jewels-symbolizing or refuge-giving/skyabs-sbyin mudrā, typically holding an utpala with a form-specific implement or emblem at its pollen-centre); in the third it is a different gesture used the same way (see the ⚑ on the yama-sonam bullet below):

- **Palden-sherab's cycle of the twenty-one individual Tārā emanations** — this commentary's `3.1.1` through `3.1.21` sub-sections each give a "general meaning" (སྤྱི་དོན) generation-stage visualization for one of the praise's twenty-one homages, and each pairs a form-specific right-hand supreme-giving gesture with a left-hand Three-Jewels/refuge-giving mudrā holding an utpala topped by a different implement (a conch for "Swift Heroine," a life-vase for Uṣṇīṣavijayā, a drawn bow for Kurukullā, a vajra-dagger for the Great Terrifying One, a sword for Tummo the Invincible, a victory-banner for "Victorious over the Three Worlds," a vajra with upturned horns for "Victorious in Battle," a stūpa for "Immeasurably Subduing," a pestle for the Wrathful-Browed form, a nectar-vase for the Utterly-Peaceful form, a crossed-vajra for the Destroyer of Sorcery, the full moon marked with a deer for the Great Peacock, a white parasol for Uṣṇīṣasitātapatrā, a nectar-filled casket for the Forest-Dweller, and a golden fish for the Radiant Goddess). Representative instances: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-1-2-3` (Swift Heroine) and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-2-1` (the Śiṃśapā-Forest form belonging to homage 9's own appendix cycle).
- **Tsultrim-namdak's second, iconographic pass over the same twenty-one homages** — a separate section of this same commentary that goes through the root praise's own twenty-one verses a second time, giving each a named Tārā form with a generation-stage description, and using the same supreme-giving/Three-Jewels mudrā-pair template for each. Representative instances: `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-4` (Tārā Myur-ma dpa'-mo, on root verse 1) and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-12` (the Khadira-Forest Tārā, on root verse 9 — the same slot as palden-sherab's Śiṃśapā-Forest form). This is **not** a different text's praise: `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-4`, which reproduces the twenty-one homages in full as a continuous recitation text, is the **root praise itself** ("the complete root praise… concluding 'This is the praise together with the root mantra, and the twenty-one homages'"), not an independent parallel composition. The same commentary's self-generation sādhana (`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-4`) and ritual instructions (`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-1-12`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-7-1-5`) reuse the identical right-hand supreme-giving mudrā, there shown transforming into a refuge-granting mudrā placed over the practitioner's crown.
- **Anon-yama-sonam's ritual-appendix forms** — a smaller set of similar visualizations for deity-forms outside the homage sequence proper. ⚑ These are counted here as instances of the *template habit* (a fixed two-handed gesture-pair opening each form's description), **not** as instances of the same gesture-pair: the gesture yama-sonam's appendix forms open with is the "mudrā of great joy" (རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བའི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ), a different gesture from the supreme-giving/Three-Jewels pair the other two cycles reuse. Representative instances: `yama-sonam:c-3-1-22` and `yama-sonam:c-3-10-10`.

This pattern is recorded here as a single Unique facet rather than as dozens of separate iconographic facts, because the individual claims do not add new information about mudrā *as a category* beyond confirming that this specific gesture-pair became this corpus's standard iconographic shorthand for "a benevolent, Tārā-lineage deity-form." Every remaining claim instantiating this same pattern is logged in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below, with this paragraph as its shared reason.

## Scope note: ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (mahāmudrā) is a distinct term, out of scope

Several claims in this packet use ཕྱག་རྒྱ in the sense of ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ (mahāmudrā, "Great Seal") — a completion-stage/dzogrim doctrinal term for the union of bliss and emptiness, entirely unrelated to the ritual hand-gesture this page otherwise treats.
