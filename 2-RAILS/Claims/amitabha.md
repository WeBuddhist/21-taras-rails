# འོད་དཔག་མེད། — Amitābha

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This packet was **not** assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that
> pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and *Amitābha as a
> named buddha* is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead, like the sibling
> pages `mantra`, `gdon`, `mara`, `vetala`, `wheel`, `pacification`,
> `three-jewels`, `ushnisha`, `hum-syllable`, `tara-mantra`, `indra`,
> `conqueror`, `mudra`, `lotus`, `three-worlds`, `brahma`, `suffering`, `eon`,
> `agni`, `gandharva`, `tathagata`, and `yaksha`. No deterministic assembly
> script exists for this topic, so the gather step was done directly:
> `rg "འོད་དཔག་མེད|འོད་དཔག་ཏུ་མེད"` against all sixteen
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` files, followed by reading each hit's
> full claim block (heading through the next heading) to judge relevance,
> copying **བོད་ཡིག** text character-for-character from the raw file — never
> retyped or paraphrased from memory.
>
> **Relationship to `ushnisha.md` and `tathagata.md`.** Amitābha appears in this
> corpus in a *different* verse from the uṣṇīṣa-deity homage: the uṣṇīṣa page
> concerns the root text's **fourth** homage (Uṣṇīṣavijayā, associated in the
> corpus chiefly with the Tathāgata Vairocana), while every claim on this page
> concerns the **twelfth** homage — the crescent-moon crown-ornament verse, in
> which Amitābha (not Vairocana) is named as dwelling amid Tārā's matted locks.
> The two topics do not share a referent-verse and are not merged.
> `tathagata.md`'s "Named individual Tathāgatas: Amitābha within her matted
> locks" facet already cites `taranatha:c-12-7` — the packet's only claim whose
> own བོད་ཡིག uses the term དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ ("Tathāgata") together with Amitābha's
> name — and that citation is repeated here (Amitābha is this page's own
> subject, so the claim belongs here regardless of which epithet accompanies
> it there); this page is the fuller, dedicated treatment of Amitābha himself,
> covering his identity, iconographic role, and every tantric-correspondence
> reading the corpus gives him, not only the one claim that happens to name him
> "Tathāgata."

## Questions asked

1. What text do commentaries quote or paraphrase for the root verse (homage twelve) naming Amitābha, and how consistent is the wording across the corpus?
2. How do commentaries identify Amitābha's relation to Tārā — as family-lord (རིགས་བདག) dwelling amid her matted locks, a crown-ornament, or otherwise — and do they converge on the same relation?
3. What specific effect or benefit does Amitābha's radiating light produce, according to each commentary, and do commentaries converge on one effect or diverge into several?
4. Do any commentaries specify Amitābha's own pure land or epithet (e.g. "protector of the blissful realm," Sukhāvatī), and is he ever named alongside — or confused with — the distinct figure Amitāyus?
5. Does any commentary read "Amitābha amid the matted locks" through completion-stage, empowerment-correspondence, or subtle-body doctrine, and if so, what specific referent does each assign him?
6. Do any commentaries connect Amitābha to Tārā's own family lineage or origin — e.g. as head of her lotus family, alongside Avalokiteśvara?
7. Is there a genuine divergence in what tantric or doctrinal referent different commentaries assign to "Amitābha," or do these represent complementary, non-contradictory schemes?

## The root verse (homage twelve): Amitābha within Tārā's matted locks

### Consensus

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-12-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-12-6`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-70`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-71`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-12-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-12-4`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-37`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4`, `taranatha:c-12-7`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-2-5-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-41`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-43` (16 commentaries)

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-2-5-5` likewise.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — every attestation agrees on the same identification; they differ only in whether the verse is quoted in full, quoted from "the tantra" as a citation, or given as word-commentary paraphrase, and in whether the "family-lord" term is used.

None beyond the above.

## Effects of Amitābha's radiance

### Consensus

Beyond the shared claim that light "constantly" (རྟག་པར / དུས་རྟག་པར) radiates from Amitābha (already attested in the facet above), the commentaries specify differing beneficiaries and modes for this radiance rather than converging on one further statement — recorded here as Unique elaborations rather than a single consensus proposition, per Rule 9 (a bundled statement may not be padded with claims that support only part of it).

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — the elaborations below are complementary specifics, not competing claims about what the same light does.

### Unique

`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-37` gives the most specific elaboration in the packet: the light "རྟག་པར་ཤིན་ཏུ་ཚེ་བསོད་ནམས་ཡོན་ཏན་མངའ་ཐང་ཉམས་རྟོགས་གདུལ་བྱ་ཕྲིན་ལས་ཐམས་ཅད་རྒྱས་པའི་འོད" — "causes longevity, merit, good qualities, majesty, realization, and all activities toward disciples to flourish."

`tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-43` specifies a darkness-dispelling function: the light "སེམས་ཅན་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཀྲ་མི་ཤིས་པའི་མུན་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསལ་ནས་བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཀྱི་སྣང་བས་ཁྱབ་པར་བྱེད་པ" — "dispels all beings' darkness of misfortune and pervades them with auspicious light."

`sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5` both frame the radiance as an expression of compassion, in near-identical language: sungrab-tulku's claim states the light is "ཤིན་ཏུ་བརྩེ་བའི་སྙིང་རྗེའི་འོད་ཟེར" ("the light-ray of loving compassion"), while tenzin-dhonzang's parallel claim (part of the same passage-family, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-5`) does not use this exact compassion-clause but instead emphasizes the light's constancy and abundance ("ཤིན་ཏུ་འོད་ཟེར་གྱི་ཚོགས་ནི་འགྱེད་པར"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2` independently uses a matching compassion-framing: "ཤིན་ཏུ་འཁོར་གསུམ་དམིགས་པ་བྲལ་བའི་ཚད་མེད་པའི་སྙིང་རྗེའི་འོད་ཟེར" — "the light-ray of immeasurable compassion, utterly free of the three-spheres' conceptualization" — additionally specifying it is free of the three-spheres' conceptual reference (a distinct qualifier sangye-nyentrul alone adds).

`yama-sonam:c-3-12-6` and `yama-sonam:c-3-12-7` (the latter citing an outside verse from "Nyima Sbaspa") both frame the radiance's purpose as merit-generation for the supplicant rather than beings generally: "ལྷ་མོ་འདི་ལ་མཆོད་པ་དང་གསོལ་བར་འདེབས་ན་བསོད་ནམས་ཚད་མེད་པར་བསྡུ་བའི་དོན་བསྟན་པ" — "this shows that making offerings and supplications to this goddess gathers boundless merit"; the Nyima Sbaspa citation confirms, "འོད་དཔག་མེད་ལས་འོད་འཛགས་པས། བསོད་ནམས་འོད་ཕུང་འབར་མཛད་སྒོམས" — "meditate that, since light streams from Amitābha, she causes a blazing mass of merit-light."

## Amitābha's own identity: the blissful realm, and the distinct figure Amitāyus

### Consensus

Three commentaries specify Amitābha's own domain as a "blissful realm" (བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང), i.e. Sukhāvatī, rather than leaving him an unlocated buddha:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-0-5`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` (3 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-0-5` (in this commentary's own front-matter homage sequence, not the verse-12 exegesis) states plainly: "བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང་མཆོག་མགོན་པོ་འོད་དཔག་མེད" — "Protector Amitābha of the supreme blissful realm." `sangye-nyentrul:c-13-0-2` names him "བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང་མགོན་འོད་དཔག་ཏུ་མེད་པ" — "the protector Amitābha [dwelling in] a blissful realm." `sungrab-tulku:c-15-4` specifies the direction as well: "ནུབ་ཕྱོགས་བདེ་ལྡན་ཞིང་གི་མགོན་པོ་རྒྱལ་བ་འོད་དཔག་མེད" — "the protector of the western pure land of Sukhāvatī, the Victor Amitābha."

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed.

### Unique

`palden-sherab:c-0-4`, in the same front-matter homage sequence immediately preceding `palden-sherab:c-0-5` above, pays homage to a distinct figure under a distinct name — Amitāyus (ཚེ་དཔག་མེད), not Amitābha (འོད་དཔག་མེད): "རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པའི་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཆེ།...འོད་མཚན་སྟོང་འབར་ཚེ་དཔག་མེད་མགོན་འདུད" — "...homage to protector Amitāyus, blazing with a thousand marks of light." This claim's own བོད་ཡིག does not contain the term འོད་དཔག་མེད and is not part of this page's Amitābha attestations; it is logged here only as a boundary note, since the two names sit adjacent in the same commentary's homage sequence and are easily conflated. No commentary in the packet identifies Amitābha and Amitāyus as the same figure or states a doctrinal relation between them.

## Amitābha and Tārā's family lineage

### Consensus

None — two commentaries each independently connect Amitābha to Tārā's own lineage, but from different parts of their commentaries and in different specific wording; recorded as Unique below rather than merged, since neither claim corroborates the other's specific framing closely enough to state one shared proposition.

### ⚑ Divergences

None observed — both claims agree in substance (Tārā belongs to Amitābha's lotus family and is connected to Avalokiteśvara) without conflicting.

### Unique

`tsultrim-namdak:c-5-2-7-3-2-1`, from this commentary's closing praise-and-offering section (not the verse-12 exegesis), quotes and glosses a separate praise-verse identifying Tārā's family origin: "ཨོཾ་སྣང་བ་མཐའ་ཡས་པདྨའི་རིགས། འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཞལ་ལས་བྱུང་། སངས་རྒྱས་ཀུན་གྱི་ཕྲིན་ལས་བདག འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བསྟོད།" — "Of the lotus family of boundless light [Amitābha], arisen from the face of the world-lord [Avalokiteśvara], embodiment of the activity of all buddhas — to Noble Tārā I prostrate and praise," glossed as "སྣང་བ་མཐའ་ཡས་ཏེ་སངས་རྒྱས་འོད་དཔག་མེད་གསུང་པདྨའི་རིགས...སྤྱན་རས་གཟིགས་ཀྱི་ཞལ་གྱི་སྤྱན་ཆབ་ལས་བྱུང་བ" — "of the lotus family of the buddha Amitābha [Boundless Light], arisen from the tears of...Avalokiteśvara."

`palden-sherab:c-0-12`, also from this commentary's own front-matter homage sequence, gives a related but distinct pairing — Tārā's birth from Avalokiteśvara's compassionate gaze, with Amitābha located in her crown rather than named as head of a "family": "འཇིག་རྟེན་དབང་ཕྱུག་ཐུགས་རྗེའི་སྤྱན་ལས་འཁྲུངས། མཐོན་མཐིང་རལ་གསེབ་འོད་དཔག་མེད་པ་བཞུགས" — "Born from the compassionate gaze of Avalokiteśvara — amid her blue-black locks Amitābha dwells." This claim was located while reading the surrounding context of `palden-sherab:c-0-5` rather than by the initial search on its own line, and is logged here as directly relevant rather than silently omitted.

## Completion-stage and empowerment-correspondence readings of "Amitābha"

### Consensus

Two commentaries, independently, identify Amitābha with the same specific completion-stage referent — the essence of the "red element" or red bodhicitta-drop:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-3-1`, `taranatha:c-12-12` (2 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-3-1` (the "hidden meaning," completion-stage-with-marks tier of this commentary's own fourfold exegesis of verse 12) states: "འོད་དཔག་མེད་ནི་ཐིག་ལེ་དམར་ཆའི་དྭངས་མའོ" — "Amitābha is the pure essence of the red-portion drop." `taranatha:c-12-12` (this commentary's own "hidden meaning," སྦས་དོན, of the same verse) states, in near-identical wording: "འོད་དཔག་མེད་ནི་དམར་ཆའི་དངས་མ" — "Amitābha is the essence of the red element." Both claims are explicitly framed by their own commentaries as a "hidden" doctrinal reading layered atop the verse's plain sense, not as a competing literal interpretation.

### ⚑ Divergences

Three further, non-reconciled readings assign "Amitābha amid the matted locks" to three different specific referents within empowerment-correspondence or subtle-body schemes, none of which restate or corroborate each other or the red-drop-essence reading above:

**Vase empowerment:** `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-58` (this commentary's own "hidden meaning," སྦས་དོན) states: "རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ན་འོད་དཔག་མེད་ཀྱིས་བུམ་དབང་" — "'Amitābha amid the matted locks' indicates the vase empowerment."

**Wisdom empowerment:** `taranatha:c-21-4-17`, in a separate empowerment-correspondence list from the same commentary's benefit-verse exegesis (distinct from this same commentary's hidden-meaning reading of verse 12 itself, cited under Consensus above), states: "རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ལས་འོད་དཔག་མེད་ཀྱིས་ཤེར་དབང་" — "'Amitābha within the matted locks' indicates the wisdom-empowerment." Because this is the same commentary's own second, differently-scoped correspondence scheme rather than a second commentary, it is recorded as a distinct reading rather than treated as contradicting that Consensus attestation.

**Upward fire-wind (caṇḍālī, inner heat):** `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-12-4` reports a mahāsiddha's reading in which "རིགས་བདག་འོད་དཔག་མེད་གྱེན་རྒྱུ་མེའི་རླུང་གི་ངོ་བོ་ཡིན་པས" — "the family-lord Amitābha is of the nature of the upward-moving fire-wind" — together with the crescent-moon crown symbolizing the syllable HAṂ, forming (per this claim's own framing) "a hidden instruction symbolizing caṇḍālī, the definitive meaning of the completion-stage fire-offering."

No commentary in the packet reconciles these three readings, or the red-drop-essence reading above, with one another.

### Unique

`palden-sherab` gives the fullest stratified exegesis of verse 12 in the packet, of which the red-drop-essence claim above (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-3-1`) is only the third of four tiers. The other three, all exegesis of the same verse but not themselves naming Amitābha through a completion-stage or empowerment lens:
- Word-meaning (literal) tier: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2` — Amitābha, "at times manifesting in the form of an ascetic" (རེས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་སྐུར་བསྟན་ནས), dwells within Tārā's locks radiating "unreferenced compassion" (དམིགས་མེད་སྙིང་རྗེའི་འོད་ཟེར) for beings.
- General-meaning tier (generation-stage visualization): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-2-1` — describes Amitābha's body, together with the goddess's own body and ornaments, as the source of light-rays raining auspicious substances during sādhana visualization.
- Ultimate-meaning tier (completion-stage-without-marks): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-4-1` — "རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ན་འོད་དཔག་མེད་པ་ནི། རིག་པ་ཚད་ལ་ཕེབས་པ" — "'Amitābha among the locks' is awareness reaching its full measure," read as the third of the four visions of the Great Perfection's completion-stage-without-marks.

No other commentary in the packet extends any reading of Amitābha to this word-meaning/general-meaning/ultimate-meaning stratification; only the third (hidden-meaning) tier is independently corroborated, by taranatha's claim cited under Consensus above.
