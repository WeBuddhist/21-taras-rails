## Questions asked

1. What is "Tathāgata" (དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ) as an epithet in this corpus, and how do commentaries etymologize the term itself?
2. How does the root text's fourth homage verse use "Tathāgata," and how uniformly do commentaries in the corpus gloss or quote it?

## Etymology of "Tathāgata"

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently etymologize the name itself, and both turn the derivation on the same pivot — "suchness" (དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད, *tathatā*) plus "gone" (གཤེགས་པ, *gata*):

— attested: `taranatha:c-4-9`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-3` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-4-9` gives the etymology by citation: "དཔལ་ལྡན་དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་གཤེགས་ཤིང་། ། དེ་བཞིན་སླར་ཡང་གཤེགས་པ་ཉིད། ཅེས་པ་ལྟར་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའོ།" — "As it is said, 'Glorious one, gone to suchness, and likewise come again' — accordingly she is a Tathāgata (thus-gone-one)." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-3` derives the same term compositionally rather than by citation: "ཆོས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཆོས་ཉིད་དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་ལ་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱིས་དེ་དང་རྗེས་སུ་མཐུན་པར་གཤེགས་ཤིང་མཉམ་པར་བཞག་པས་ན། དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་མཚན་གསོལ་བ་ཡིན།" — "Because wisdom goes to, and rests in equipoise concordant with, the dharmatā — the suchness-expanse of emptiness that is the nature of phenomena — the name 'Tathāgata' ('Thus-Gone') is given." Both claims thus support the identical proposition that the name analyzes as "gone" (or "abiding in accordance with") "suchness," differing only in method (citation vs. compositional gloss).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the two etymologies are complementary derivations of the same analysis, not competing ones.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-4` gives this same commentary's own second, alternate etymology, drawn from a different source and turning on a different half of the compound: "ཡང་ན། མཚན་བརྗོད་ལས། ཇི་སྐད་སྨྲས་པ་དེ་བཞིན་བྱེད། ། ཅེས་པ་ལྟར།" — "Alternatively, from the Nāmasaṃgīti: 'acts just as has been spoken' — accordingly." Here the "thus" (དེ་བཞིན) of the name is read through action matching speech, not through suchness-realization; per the full-statement-support rule this is kept separate from the Consensus statement above rather than padding its count.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-5` continues the same commentary's etymological discussion, this time isolating "gone" (གཤེགས་པ) alone, glossed through a different register — bliss rather than suchness: "གཤེགས་པ་ནི་བདེ་བ་ནས་བདེ་བར་འགྲོ་བའི་དོན་" — "'Gone' (gshegs pa) means going from bliss to bliss." This claim was located while reading the surrounding context of `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-3`/`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-4` rather than by the initial `rg` search (it does not itself contain the literal string དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ), and is logged here as a directly relevant continuation of the same etymological passage rather than silently omitted.

## The fourth homage: she is the crown-ornament of / emanation from the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa

### Consensus
Every one of the sixteen commentaries quotes or glosses the root text's fourth homage verse, whose opening line addresses the goddess in relation to the uṣṇīṣa (crown-protuberance) of the Tathāgatas (ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར) — this is the single most uniform use of the term in the whole corpus:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-4-3`, `yama-sonam:c-3-4-4`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-23`, `gendun-drub:c-0-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-4`, `taranatha:c-4-2`, `taranatha:c-4-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-2-8`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (16 commentaries)

The bare verse quotation is close to identical across the corpus: `sungrab-tulku:c-7-2` gives it in full, "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར། མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ༎ མ་ལུས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་པའི།། རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བསྟེན་མ།། ཞེས་པ་སྟེ" — "'Homage to the crown-ornament of the tathāgatas, whose conduct is endlessly triumphant, who is intently relied upon by the victors' offspring who have attained all perfections without exception' — that is." Several commentaries instead paraphrase or gloss rather than quote: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-1` states she is "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང། གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་མཆོད་པའི་གནས་སུ་སྒྱུར་ཅིང" — "made by all the Tathāgatas into an object of worship like the crown-protuberance"; `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-2` gives the same idea more tersely, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་མཆོད་པའི་གནས་སུ་གྱུར་ཅིང" — "she has become an object of worship comparable to the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas"; `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-23` gives the same simile, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟ་བུའོ" — "she is like the crown-protuberance of all the Tathāgatas."

A recurring sub-current within this same consensus adds an explicit causal reason — she dwells at / is borne as the Tathāgatas' crown-ornament *because* she is their mother or is honored by them: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3` states, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས་དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་གྱི་རྩེ་ན" — "Because she is the mother of all Tathāgatas, [she dwells] at the very crown of the uṣṇīṣa on their heads"; `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3` gives the identical causal structure, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་པས་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་འཁུར་ཞིང" — "since she is the mother of all Tathāgatas, she is borne like the crown-ornament atop the head"; and `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2` frames the same identification the other direction, as an honor bestowed rather than a family bond stated: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱིས་ཀྱང་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལྟར་བཀུར་བའི་འོས་སུ་གྱུར་པ" — "worthy of being honored even by all Tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance." `gendun-drub:c-0-5` supplies independent corroboration for the "mother of all Tathāgatas" identification at the level of the whole corpus's source-tantra: it names the tantra itself "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་སྒྲོལ་མ་ལ་བསྟོད་པ" — "Praise to Tārā, Mother of all Tathāgatas."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — every claim in this facet agrees on the identification, varying only in whether it quotes the verse bare, glosses it by simile, or adds the causal "mother of all Tathāgatas" clause.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20` gives a variant of the same identification scaled to a specific cosmological framing rather than the plain "all Tathāgatas": "ཕྱོགས་བཅུའི་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔའི་ཚོགས་ཀྱི་གཙུག་གཏོར་གྱི་ནོར་བུ" — "the crown-jewel of the assembled tathāgatas, the Victors of the five families throughout the ten directions." This claim also appears in `conqueror.md`'s "Five Victor-families" facet for its རྒྱལ་བ་རིགས་ལྔ content; here it is cited for its independent use of དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ to name the same class of buddhas.

This facet is folded into the Consensus discussion above rather than treated as a separate heading, per Rule 9: the "mother of all Tathāgatas" clause never appears in the packet as a bare, freestanding claim — every instance of it is the causal half of a claim whose other half is the uṣṇīṣa-identification already cited there (`dharmabhadra:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `gendun-drub:c-0-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2`). ## Named individual Tathāgatas: Vairocana as source-teacher of the praise

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently name the Tathāgata Vairocana (རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད) as the source or teacher behind the praise's fourth-homage uṣṇīṣa material, or as speaker of the twenty-one homages as a whole:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-4-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-3` (2 commentaries directly cited here; a third, `taranatha:c-4-3`, makes the same identification but is cited above under the uṣṇīṣa-consensus facet rather than re-cited here, per the no-double-counting practice)

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-3` states plainly, "འདིར་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱི་ཞལ་ལས་ གསུངས་པའི་བསྟོད་པ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཉེར་གཅིག་དེ་ཉིད" — "Here, the praise of the twenty-one homages spoken by the Tathāgata Vairocana himself." `taranatha:c-4-3` (already attested above) locates the fourth homage specifically "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པར་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱི་གཙུག་གཏོར་ལས" — "From the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana." `yama-sonam:c-3-4-7` reports the same identification, but explicitly as one named tradition's reading rather than the commentary's own assertion — per Rule 14 (epistemic strength copied, not upgraded), this is recorded as an attribution, not an endorsement: "ཇོ་ནང་པས་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྣམ་པ་སྣང་མཛད་ཀྱི་དབུའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ལས་རིག་སྔགས་ཀྱི་ཡེ་ཤེས་སྤྲུལ་པ་ལྷ་མོ་འདིས་བདག་གིར་མཛད་ཅིང" — "Jonangpa explains it thus: this goddess has taken possession of the wisdom-emanation of the vidyā-mantra arising from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana." The same claim's continuation notes that other Geluk masters (Ngulchu and others) instead read the verse as referring to the generic uṣṇīṣa of all Tathāgatas rather than Vairocana's specifically — recorded in the raw file as `yama-sonam:c-3-4-8`, not independently cited here since it does not itself contain the term དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ, but relevant context for why this identification is not universal.

### ⚑ Divergences
The `yama-sonam` claim already cited above under Consensus (and its uncited continuation, logged in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited" below) together record that within its own commentarial tradition, whether the fourth-homage uṣṇīṣa names Vairocana specifically or all Tathāgatas generically is unsettled — the Jonangpa reading favors Vairocana, "Ngulchu and other Geluk scholars" read it generically. This is a real internal divergence reported by a single commentary about the wider tradition, not a divergence between two commentaries in this packet, so no claim ID is re-cited on this side.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Named individual Tathāgatas: Drumsound / Dundhubhisvara in the goddess's origin-story

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently narrate the same origin episode — a past-life buddha named Tathāgata Drumsound (Dundhubhisvara, རྔ་སྒྲ), before whom the princess Yeshe Dawa (the goddess's past-life identity) first generated bodhicitta and received a prophecy:

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-2-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-6`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-9` (3 commentaries)

`sungrab-tulku:c-2-1` opens the narrative, "སྔོན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྔ་སྒྲ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་བྱུང་སྟེ" — "In a former age there was a Blessed Tathāgata called Drumsound (Nga-dra)." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-1` gives the same opening with a named world-realm added: "སྔོན་ཐོག་མ་མེད་པའི་དུས་ཤིག་ན། འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས་སྣ་ཚོགས་པའི་འོད་ཅེས་བྱ་བར། དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྔ་སྒྲ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་བྱུང་སྟེ" — "In a beginningless former time, in the world-realm called 'Various Lights,' there arose the Tathāgata called Dundhubhisvara (Drum-Sound)." The prophecy that closes the episode is likewise attested by all three: `sungrab-tulku:c-2-6`, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྔ་སྒྲས་ཀྱང་ཇི་སྲིད་བླ་ན་མེད་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་མངོན་དུ་བྱས་པ་དེ་སྲིད་དུ་མིང་ལྷ་མོ་སྒྲོལ་མའི་མཚན་འབའ་ཞིག་ཏུ་འགྱུར་བར་ལུང་བསྟན་ཏོ" — "The Tathāgata Drumsound also prophesied that for as long as she has not manifestly attained unsurpassed enlightenment, she would bear solely the name 'Goddess Tārā'"; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-1-6` gives the same prophecy nearly verbatim; `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-1-9` embeds the identical prophecy inside its own etymology-of-"Tārā" claim, quoting "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་རྔ་སྒྲས་ཀྱང་། ཇི་ སྲིད་བླ་ན་མེད་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་མ་ཐོབ་བར་དང་། ཐོབ་ཀྱང་མིང་སྒྲོལ་མ" — "The Tathāgata Dundhubhisvara likewise [prophesied]: 'Until you attain unsurpassed enlightenment, and even after attaining it, you shall bear the name Tārā.'"

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the three commentaries' narratives agree on the buddha's name, epithet, and the content of the prophecy; they differ only in incidental narrative detail (e.g. `tenzin-dhonzang` alone names the world-realm), which is not a doctrinal disagreement.

## "Tathāgata(s)" as a plural class-term in tantric symbolism

### Consensus
None — each claim below uses the plural "Tathāgatas" in a different symbolic register (empowerment correspondence, deity-class equivalence, canonical citation, seed-syllable placement), from four different commentaries; no two claims restate the same statement.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are not competing claims, only distinct symbolic uses of the same plural class-term.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-21-4-16` reads a phrase of the root text's benefit-verses as an encoded generation-stage correspondence: among a list of such correspondences, "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ཞེས་པས་ལྷ་དབང་བསྐུར་བ" — "'the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa' indicates the deity-empowerment."

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-12-9` places "the eight Tathāgatas" (དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་བརྒྱད) within a list of tantric deity-octads (Cakrasaṃvara's gate-goddesses, Guhyasamāja's bodhisattva-consorts, Bhairava's wrathful deities, eight bodhisattvas, eight auspicious goddesses), concluding they are "ཐ་དད་མེད་པ" — "not different in essence" from the buddhas despite differing appearance and name.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-17-14` quotes the *Sūtra of Inconceivable Secrets* on the Tathāgata's omniscient vision: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་གྱིས་ཀྱང་གཟིགས་སོ། ། དེ་བཞིན་མཛོད་སྤུས་ཀྱང་གཟིགས་སོ། ། དེ་ལྟར་མཚན་རེ་རེས་ཀྱང་གཅིག་ཅར་ཕྱོགས་བཅུ་རྣམས་སུ་འཇིག་རྟེན་གྱི་ཁམས་རབ་འབྱམས་ཐམས་ཅད་གཟིགས་སོ" — "The Tathāgata sees also with his crown-protuberance; likewise he sees also with his urṇā-hair. Thus, with each and every mark, he simultaneously sees all the boundless world-realms in the ten directions."

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` places seed-syllables of "all Tathāgatas'" body, speech, and mind at three centers of the twenty-first Tārā's iconographic form: "སྤྱི་བོ། མགྲིན་པ། སྙིང་ག གསུམ་ལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་སྐུ་གསུང་ཐུགས་ཀྱི་ས་ བོན" — "at the three [places] — crown, throat, heart — the seed-syllables of the body, speech, and mind of all Tathāgatas."

## Completion-stage / Atiyoga doctrinal readings of "Tathāgata"

### Consensus
None — both claims come from the same single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-3-1` reads the fourth homage's "Tathāgata" through a completion-stage-with-marks lens, identifying it with the wisdom of the four joys drawn to the crown: "ཟབ་དོན་གསང་ལམ་ལྟར་ན་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ནི་དགའ་བཞི་ཡེ་ཤེས།...གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ། ཕར་ཕྱིན་བཅུ་སྟེ་རླུང་བཅུ་གནས་དག་པས་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཆོས་སྐུ་ནི་རྒྱལ་བ་སྲས་བཅས་ཡུམ་ཆེན་ཡིན་ནོ" — "According to the profound secret path, the Tathāgata is the wisdom of the four joys...[this is] Uṣṇīṣavijayā; the ten perfections — the ten winds — once purified, [become] dharmakāya wisdom, the great mother of the victors and their heirs." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-4-1` gives the same commentary's further, "ultimate" completion-stage-without-marks reading: "དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར་ནི་ཐེག་པ་རིམ་དགུའི་རྩེ་མོ་རྫོགས་ཆེན་ཨ་ཏི་ཡོ་ག་ལྟ་བ...ཞེས་སོ" — "The Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa is the peak of the nine successive vehicles, the view of the Great Perfection Atiyoga," concluding that the great rainbow body itself "ནི་...དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར" — "is the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa." Both claims explicitly frame themselves as a "hidden" or "ultimate" doctrinal reading layered atop the verse's plain sense (already attested in the Consensus facet above via this same commentary's own `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-1`), not as a competing literal interpretation — a page-level observation about how this single commentary itself stratifies its exegesis, not a claim made by either cited passage on its own.
