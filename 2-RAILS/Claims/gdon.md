# གདོན། — Harm-Causing Spirits (gdon)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> Unlike the spine-slot topic pages in this folder, this packet was not assembled by `assemble_packet.py` / spine-map — that pipeline is for registered spine slots (verses/chapters), and གདོན། is a cross-cutting keyword subject instead. It was assembled deterministically by a parallel script, `assemble_keyword_packet.py`, which routes via the keyword-extraction pipeline's Signal-A per-commentary claim-tagging (`0-INBOX/AI_translation/keyword-extraction/output/signal_a/<id>.json`, recording which claim IDs are "about" this term, per commentary); the guarantee is the same as `assemble_packet.py`'s — every claim block in the packet is copied character-for-character from the commentary's own raw claims file.

The term གདོན། (*gdon*) surfaces across this corpus in five recurring contexts: the root verse (21) whose third line names a triad of harmful classes destroyed by Ture; a set of enumerations of how many classes of *gdon* there are; the closing benefits verse pairing *gdon* with epidemic disease (*rims*) and poison (*dug*); a second root verse (6) whose own epithet centers on destroying *gdon*; and a scatter of other verses, mantra-glosses, and closing prayers. These are treated as separate facets below rather than folded into one, since the claims themselves address different textual loci.

## Questions asked

## Verse 21 — Ture destroys the triad of gdon, ro-langs, and gnod-sbyin

### Consensus
The great majority of commentaries in this packet quote or gloss the root verse's third line — that Ture/Tārā destroys the hosts of *gdon* (harm-spirits), *ro-langs* (zombies/vetāla), and *gnod-sbyin* (yakṣas) — as the verse's central statement:

— attested: `yama-sonam:c-3-21-3`, `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-6-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-99`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-21-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-21-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-21-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-24-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-6-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-6-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-71` (11 commentaries)

Of these, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-74` continues the same commentary's own gloss (from `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-71`, above), restating that the triad's evil intentions are destroyed and adding an etymology of "Ture" (mind extremely swift, tenderly compassionate, active in all six periods of day and night) not paralleled by any other commentary's claims in this packet.

A narrower group additionally specifies the mechanism: light-rays radiating from the three syllables/places (OṂ-ĀḤ-HŪṂ at crown, throat, and heart) are what destroy the triad's malicious intent —

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-6-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-2-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-24-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` (4 commentaries)

Within that group, two commentaries further specify that the light also instantly recalls the stolen life-force (*bla-tshe*) of the sick, before destroying the spirits' malicious intent:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-1-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-2-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-24` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Not a doctrinal disagreement, but a genuine divergence from the literal reading above: two commentaries each append a pair of completion-stage tantric allegorical readings of the same triad, matching one another in order and largely in wording — a correspondence worth flagging explicitly rather than treating as two isolated Unique claims:

- **Reading 1 (subtle-body correspondence).** *gdon* = the channels (*rtsa*), *ro-langs* = the drops (*thig-le*), *gnod-sbyin* = conceptual thought (*rnam-par rtog-pa*), all destroyed into the expanse of clear light. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-3-2`: "གདོན་ནི་རྩ་དང་། རོ་ལངས་ནི་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་རྣམ་པར་རྟོག་པ་སྟེ་དེ་རྣམས་འོད་གསལ་དབྱིངས་སུ་བཅོམ་པས།" `taranatha:c-21-3-4`: "གདོན་ཏེ་རྩ་དང་། རོ་ལངས་ཏེ་ཐིག་ལེ་དང་། །གནོད་སྦྱིན་ཏེ་རྣམ་པར་རྟོག་པ་རྣམས་འོད་གསལ་གྱི་དབྱིངས་སུ་བཅོམ་པ་ལས།" The two are near-identical in wording (channel/drop/thought mapped to gdon/ro-langs/gnod-sbyin in the same order, dissolved into the same "expanse of clear light").
- **Reading 2 (afflictive/karmic correspondence).** *gdon* = suffering (*sdug-bsngal*), *ro-langs* = karma/action (*las*), *gnod-sbyin* = affliction (*nyon-mongs*). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-21-4-2` states this as direct identity ("གདོན་ནི་སྡུག་བསྔལ། རོ་ལངས་ནི་ལས་དང་། གནོད་སྦྱིན་ནི་ཉོན་མོངས་…"). `taranatha:c-21-4-5` gives the same three-part correspondence in the same order, but phrases it as simile rather than identity — "suffering *similar to* the harm of gdon... action *similar to* ro-langs-activity... afflictions *similar to* gnod-sbyin-harm" (མཚུངས་པའི་) — a softer epistemic framing than palden-sherab's flat equation, preserved here rather than flattened to a shared "X is Y" statement.

Both commentaries place Reading 1 before Reading 2 at the same point in their gloss of verse 21 — the "peaceful power" clause first, then the gdon/ro-langs/gnod-sbyin triad-mapping, and only then the "swift" [Ture] clause — a structural correspondence between the two commentaries' expositions, not observed for any other pair in this packet.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-21-2-3` notes only that the verse "also teaches the pith instruction for protecting against harm from gdon," without elaborating what that instruction is; no other claim in the packet develops this further.

## Enumeration of gdon-classes

### Consensus
Five commentaries independently gloss "gdon" as comprising eighteen classes:

— attested: `dharmabhadra:c-1-2-3-6-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-6-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-55`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-2`, `taranatha:c-21-1-4` (5 commentaries)

Four of the five explicitly cite the "boat" spirit (*nam-gru*) as the exemplary member of the set. `sangye-nyentrul:c-22-0-2` gives only "the eighteen classes of spirits **and the like**" (སོགས), without naming *nam-gru* or any other specific member.

### ⚑ Divergences
No commentary in the packet reconciles the eighteen-class count with two other enumerations that appear once each, both from tenzin-dhonzang:

- **Fifteen child-afflicting spirits** (*byis pa'i gdon chen bco lnga*) — named individually (the Gentle One, Deer-King, Thirst-Causer, Speech-Causer, Fist-Bearer, Mamo, Jamika, the Lustful One, the Boat-of-the-Sky, the Guardian, the Displeasure-Causer, the Bird-faced, the Yoke-Handed, the Face-Ornamented, and the Dangling-Eyed): `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-5`. This list is scoped specifically to harm against children, not a general restatement of the eighteen.
- **Twenty-eight harmful spirits**, attributed to the *White Parasol* (Sitātapatrā) dhāraṇī rather than to tenzin-dhonzang's own exposition: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-21-6`. The claim's own citation marks this as a quotation from a separate scriptural source (TXT-44), not a restatement or correction of the eighteen-class count.

Since all three counts (eighteen, fifteen, twenty-eight) appear in claims routed to the same verse-21 gloss without any claim explicitly relating one count to another, this page records them as three unreconciled enumerations rather than assuming any hierarchy or subset relation between them.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-55` gives, beyond the "eighteen" headline count, its own extended list of zombie (*ro-langs*) sub-classes not paralleled elsewhere in the packet: flesh-zombie, blood-zombie, skin-zombie, birthmark-zombie, sinew-zombie, and bone-zombie.

## The benefits verse: suffering from gdon, epidemic disease, and poison abandoned

### Consensus
A separate verse — from the root text's closing benefits section, distinct from verse 21 — states that the mass of suffering caused by being afflicted by *gdon*, epidemic disease (*rims*), and poison (*dug*) is entirely abandoned. This verse (quoted in whole or part, or glossed) is attested widely:

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-2-12`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-3-3-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-2-13`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-2-2-4-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-3-3`, `taranatha:c-22-1-33`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-11`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-3-3-12`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-5` (9 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary contests this verse's plain sense.

### Unique
`pema-namgyal:c-3-3` alone conditions this benefit on prior accomplishment: only after having already completed "a hundred-thousand or more" recitations of praise to Tārā does later mere recollection of the mantra clear the affliction (and even "transform poison into nectar"); no other commentary's claims in this packet state this prerequisite.

`konchok-thabkhe:c-3-6` frames this verse specifically as teaching "the benefits of this present life," a framing label not given by any other commentary's claims here.

## Verse 6 — the wrathful "Destroyer of Gdon-Classes" epithet and sādhana

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently identify a distinct root verse (verse 6 in their own numbering) whose own epithet for Tārā centers on *gdon*:

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` (3 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-0-1` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` name her "the Great Terrifying One who utterly destroys every kind of spirit" (*gdon rigs rnam par 'joms pa'i 'jigs byed chen mo*, from *'joms pa*, "to destroy"). `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-1-1` names a closely-spelled but verbally distinct epithet — "Tārā Great Terrifier who **gathers** the hosts of spirits" (*gdon tshogs 'dzoms pa'i sgrol ma 'jigs byed chen mo*, from *'dzoms pa*, "to assemble/gather," not "to destroy"). The raw claims file itself flags this name as not identical to the registered epithet used elsewhere in the corpus. Whether this is a genuine alternate epithet or an orthographic slip in the underlying source is not resolved here; the divergence is preserved rather than folded into the other two's "destroys" reading.

Two of these further describe, in closely matching terms, the sādhana visualization associated with this verse: a dark-red wrathful form destroys spirit-obstructors (variously named — confusion-causer, madness-demon, forgetfulness-causer for palden-sherab; madness-causer, forgetfulness-causer, paralysis-causer for sangye-nyentrul) so that they are "prevented from rising again" (*slar ldang du med par*):

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-7-1-3` (2 commentaries)

The two claims' phrasing is close enough to note directly: palden-sherab's "...ཀྱི་གདོན་བགེགས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་མགོ་བོ་རབ་ཏུ་བཀས་ཞིང་ཆོས་ཉིད་ངང་དུ་བརྒྱལ་ཞིང་སླར་ལྡང་དུ་མེད་པར་བསམ་" and sangye-nyentrul's "...ཀྱི་གདོན་བགེགས་རྣམས་བཅོམ་ཞིང་སླར་ལྡང་དུ་མེད་པར་བསྒོམས་པ་" share both the term *gdon bgegs* ("spirit-obstructors") and the closing clause *slar ldang du med par* ("unable/prevented from rising again"), though their specific lists of spirit-types are not identical (confusion/madness/forgetfulness vs. madness/forgetfulness/paralysis).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-6-5` states that this deity's "chief enlightened activity is pacifying harm from elemental spirits" ('byung po'i gdon) at a structurally similar point in its own exposition (a fortiori reasoning: if great gods venerate her, humans need not be mentioned) — consistent in substance with the "Destroyer of Gdon-Classes" identification above but without naming the epithet itself, so it is recorded here as a related but distinct data point rather than folded into the Consensus count.

`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-9` uniquely adds that this form is worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, and Vāyu, and specifies her iconography (dark-red body, holding a phurba radiating HŪṂ-sparks) and a broader target list — obstructing spirits, misleading forces, rākṣasas, zombies, piśācas, yakṣas, and lunatic-demon kings — not paralleled in full by either other commentary in this facet.

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-6-2-1` uniquely details the generation-stage iconography further: lotus-and-moon seat, archer's stance, one face and two hands in supreme-giving and refuge-giving mudrās, and a vajra-dagger at the centre of an utpala's pollen.

## The gdon-fear among the sixteen fears (verse 9)

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently give closely matching enumerations of sixteen fears (in two sets of eight), in which one item — "the demon-fear of deceit and forgetfulness" (*g.yo sgyu brjed ngas gdon gyi 'jigs pa*, or *g.yo sgyu brjed ngan gdon gyi 'jigs pa*) — is specifically a *gdon*-fear, distinct from the other fifteen fears in the same list (king's-punishment, enemy, leprosy, loss of friends, poverty, anger/jealousy, and loss of purpose through laziness):

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-9-1-5`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-10-1-5` (2 commentaries)

The two lists match item-for-item in content and largely in wording (both enumerate exactly these two sets of eight, in the same order), though sangye-nyentrul's claim gives only the second set of eight (numbered 10–16) while palden-sherab's claim gives the same second set introduced as "the second [inner] eight."

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the shared attestation above.

## Gdon in other verses, mantra-contexts, and closing prayers

### Consensus
None — every claim below is attested by only one commentary in this packet, addressing a different verse or textual locus from one another.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — these are independent, non-overlapping mentions rather than conflicting accounts.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-7-11` states that Yama and other *gdon* flee (in the context of a different homage than verses 21, 6, or 9); not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

`taranatha:c-22-1-16` glosses "sin" as its own fruit — illness, *gdon*, and misfortune — all pacified; this is from the benefits section but is a different verse from the "gdon/rims/dug" verse cited under "The benefits verse," above, so it is recorded separately rather than folded into that facet's attestation count.

`tenzin-dhonzang` names the external praise "the Legdrima" as its source at two separate points in its own exposition, each in a list of dangers Tārā protects from that includes *gdon* or *'byung po'i gdon* ("elemental-spirit harm"): `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-8-8` (protection from disease, epidemics, *gdon*-obstructors, untimely death, and bad dreams/omens) and `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-10-10` (the "eight great terrifying sufferings and harm from elemental spirits") both open with "ལེགས་བྲིས་མ་ལས།" ("From the Legdrima") and are both tagged as citing that same external text. A third claim, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-9-8` (harm from *gdon* and planets/constellations, untimely death, sinful dreams, poison/weapon/fire/water), sits between them and covers the same danger-list pattern, but carries no source name of its own in the raw claim — it is recorded here as the same commentary's recurring citation practice, not asserted to be the same named external source as the other two.

`tsultrim-namdak` similarly returns to *gdon* at three separate, non-verse-21/6/9/benefits-verse points: `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-25` (the secret wrathful mantra itself, apart from the root verse, is said to pacify illness and obstructing *gdon*), `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-8` (a closing prayer requesting protection from obstacles, *gdon*-obstructors, epidemics, untimely death, and bad dreams/omens), and `tsultrim-namdak:c-8-1-10` (a final dedication verse praying that illness, *gdon*-harm, poverty, and conflict be pacified wherever the practitioner dwells).

`pema-namgyal:c-3-2` states that recollecting Tārā blocks a bad rebirth caused, among other results, by illness, *gdon* (spirits), and misfortune (*nad gdon byur*) — from a different verse than the "gdon/rims/dug" benefits verse cited above, so recorded separately.
