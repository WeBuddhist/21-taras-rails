# དཀའ་ཐུབ — Asceticism / Austerity (Tapas)

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.
>
> This is a global cross-cutting topic, not a registered spine slot, so it was
> not assembled by `assemble_packet.py`. It was gathered directly: every
> `2-RAILS/Claims/raw/tree-guided/*.md` file was searched for the string
> དཀའ་ཐུབ, every hit was read in its full claim context, and each hit was
> triaged into a facet below or into "Claims reviewed, not separately cited."
> Every claim block quoted here is copied character-for-character from its
> commentary's own raw claims file.
>
> **Scope note:** དཀའ་ཐུབ recurs in at least four distinct senses across the
> corpus — (1) the "austerity" pāramitā (= moral discipline) in Tārā-3's root
> verse, its most frequent use by far; (2) a doctrinal-classificatory sense
> naming a tier of the nine-vehicle system; (3)–(4) two distinct completion-stage
> ("hidden meaning") tantric readings, one mapping "discipline-austerity" onto
> the union of lotus and vajra, the other etymologizing the mantra syllable
> TUTTĀRE as "burning austerity"; and (5) an iconographic sense, "in the form
> of an ascetic" (དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན), describing a figure within Tārā's crown-locks.
> These senses are kept as separate facets rather than merged, since they are
> not the same claim recurring — they are the same lexical item doing
> different doctrinal work at different loci of the text.

## Questions asked

1. How do the commentaries define/gloss དཀའ་ཐུབ (austerity) as it appears in Tārā-3's root verse, one of the six pāramitās?
2. Does any commentary depart from the standard one-to-one six-term/six-pāramitā correspondence when parsing "austerity" and "peace" in that verse?
3. Does any commentary use དཀའ་ཐུབ in a doctrinal-classificatory sense outside the six-pāramitā correspondence, e.g. a vehicle/yāna typology?
4. How do the commentaries' completion-stage ("hidden meaning") exegeses use དཀའ་ཐུབ in interpreting the union of lotus and vajra?
5. How do the commentaries' completion-stage exegeses of the mantra syllable TUTTĀRE relate it to austerity?
6. Which commentaries identify a figure "in the form of an ascetic" (དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན) within Tārā's iconography, and to whom do they attribute that identification?

## Austerity as the discipline-pāramitā (Tārā-3's six-perfections verse)

Tārā-3's root verse — quoted by nearly every commentary in the corpus — reads
"སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ། བཟོད་པ་བསམ་གཏན་སྤྱོད་ཡུལ་ཉིད་མ།" ("generosity,
diligence, austerity, peace, patience, meditation — these are your very
domain"), mapping six terms onto the six pāramitās. Answering Q1:

### Consensus
Twelve commentaries gloss དཀའ་ཐུབ (austerity) in this verse as ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས
(*tshul khrims*, moral/ethical discipline), the third of the six pāramitās
(*śīla*), keeping the standard one-to-one six-term correspondence (austerity →
discipline; peace, treated separately, → wisdom):

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-17`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `taranatha:c-3-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` (12 commentaries)

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-17` gives the gloss in its barest form: "དཀའ་ཐུབ་ ནི་ ...
ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས།" ("'Austerity' is moral discipline"). `karma-maitri:c-1-1-3-5` adds
a function to the same gloss: "དཀའ་ཐུབ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དེས་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་ཞི་བར་བྱེད་པ།"
("austerity, that is, moral discipline — by that, afflictions are pacified").
`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-2-4` embeds the same gloss ("སྦྱིན་པ་བརྩོན་འགྲུས་
དཀའ་ཐུབ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས") within a reading of the lotus-mudrā at Tārā's heart as
the sign of the ten pure perfections. `taranatha:c-3-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-1-2`,
`sangye-nyentrul:c-4-0-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-19`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-4`,
`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-2-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-3-2`,
and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-11` all restate the same discipline-gloss while
enumerating the six perfections as Tārā's "domain" or the cause of her arising
— `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-3-5` specifies it further as "བསྲུང་བའི་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཀྱི་ཚུལ་
ཁྲིམས" ("the austerity-discipline of guarding [conduct]").

### ⚑ Divergences
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-3-3` reads the same verse with a different structural
parse. Rather than treating "austerity" and "peace" as two separate terms each
mapped onto its own pāramitā (austerity → discipline, peace → wisdom, six
terms for six pāramitās), it compounds them into a single unit, "དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ",
glossed together as discipline alone ("དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཞི་བ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས"), and glosses
"བསམ་གཏན" (meditation) not as concentration itself but as "ཐབས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན" ("the
nature of method"). This yields only five listed perfections (generosity,
diligence, austerity-peace/discipline, patience, method-meditation), with
Tārā herself then identified as the sixth — the wisdom-pāramitā in person,
"the field of conduct, or basis relied upon" of the other five — rather than
wisdom being named by one of the six listed terms. No other claim in this
facet reads the verse this way.

### Unique
`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-6` restates the same discipline-gloss for དཀའ་ཐུབ
("དཀའ་ཐུབ་སྟེ་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས") but frames it within a different doctrinal category —
not the six pāramitās but "the thirty-seven factors of enlightenment"
(byang phyogs kyi chos sum cu rtsa bdun), of which the pāramitās are named as
one sub-group among several. This is a distinct structural claim (a different
enclosing category), not a further attestation of the six-pāramitā statement
above, so per the full-statement-support rule it is recorded separately
rather than folded into the Consensus count.

## Doctrinal classification: the "inner vehicles of austerity and knowledge"

Answering Q3 — whether any commentary uses དཀའ་ཐུབ outside the six-pāramitā
correspondence, in a classificatory sense:

### Consensus
None — this facet is attested by a single commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-0-31`, in a doctrinal aside on the nine vehicles taught to
disciples of three faculties, names "ནང་དཀའ་ཐུབ་རིག་བྱེད་ཀྱི་ཐེག་པ་གསུམ" ("the
three inner vehicles of austerity and knowledge-practice") as the middle
triad of the nine, alongside "the three outer vehicles of general causation"
and "the three secret vehicles of empowerment and method." Here དཀའ་ཐུབ names
a class of vehicle (the kriyā/caryā/yoga tier of the tantric doxography), not
a pāramitā gloss — a different sense of the term from the previous facet, not
a corroboration or contradiction of it. No other commentary's claims in this
gathering use དཀའ་ཐུབ this way.

## Completion-stage ("hidden meaning") reading: lotus–vajra union as "discipline-austerity"

Answering Q4, from two commentaries' "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) exegesis of
the same Tārā-3 verse, mapping the six perfections onto tantric symbolism:

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently give the same completion-stage reading: the
union, without transference, of the water-born lotus and the hand's
vajra-sign is "ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དཀའ་ཐུབ" ("the discipline that is austerity"):

— attested: `taranatha:c-3-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-3-6` reads: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་གནས་བའི་པདྨ་དང་། ཕྱག་གི་སྒས་རྡོ་རྗེ་སྟེ་མཉམ་པར་
སྤྱར་བ་ལས་འཕོ་བ་མེད་པའི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་དང་...". `palden-sherab:c-3-1-3-3-1` gives
substantially the same wording as part of a fuller sixfold completion-stage
mapping: "ཆུ་སྐྱེས་གསང་བའི་པདྨ་དང་། ཕྱག་སྒྲ་ནི་རྡོ་རྗེ་སྟེ་མཉམ་པར་སྦྱར་བ་ལས་འཕོ་བ་
མེད་པའི་ཚུལ་ཁྲིམས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་དང་...", continuing to map the other five perfections
(meditation, wisdom, diligence, patience) onto further elements of bliss and
non-conceptuality not quoted here, since they lie outside this term's scope.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the two Consensus claims above.

## Completion-stage etymology: TUTTĀRE as "burning austerity"

Answering Q5, from the same two commentaries' hidden-meaning exegesis of the
mantra syllables HARA HARA TUTTĀRE (in the Tārā-20 homage, on healing the
"wandering-drop" epidemic):

### Consensus
Two commentaries independently etymologize the mantra syllable TUTTĀRE as
"burning," glossed as austerity (དཀའ་ཐུབ), within an exegesis of dissolving
sun and moon into the changeless Avadhūtī to heal an epidemic disease:

— attested: `taranatha:c-20-14`, `taranatha:c-20-15`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-20-14` gives the etymology in its bare form: "ཏུཏྟཱ་རེ་ནི་གདུང་བ་
སྟེ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཡིན་ལ།" ("TUTTĀRE is 'burning,' that is, austerity"). `taranatha:c-20-15`
continues: "ཉི་མ་ཐུར་དུ་གནོན་པ་དང་ཟླ་བ་གཙུག་ཏོར་དུ་རེངས་ཏེ་འགྱུར་མེད་ཀྱི་དཀའ་ཐུབ་
འདིས་ནི་ཐིག་ལེ་འཕོ་བའི་རིམས་ནད་སེལ་བའོ།" ("by pressing the sun downward and
freezing the moon at the crown, this unchanging austerity removes the
epidemic disease of the moving drop"). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-20-3-2` gives
near-identical wording within its own gloss of the same two syllables: "ཏུཏྟཱ་ར་
ནི་གདུང་བ་སྟེ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཡིན་ལ། སྟོང་ཆེན་ཨ་ཝ་དྷཱུ་ཏིར་ཉི་མ་ཟུར་གནོན། ཟླ་བ་གཙུག་ཏོར་དུ་
རེངས་ཏེ་འཕོ་བྲལ་འགྱུར་མེད་ངང་དུ་བསྟིམ་པའི་དཀའ་ཐུབ་འདིས་ཐིག་ལེ་འཕོ་བའི་རིམས་ནད་
སེལ་ལོ།" — the same sun/moon/Avadhūtī imagery and the same epidemic-healing
conclusion, close enough to indicate a shared exegetical source or lineage
between these two commentaries at this locus.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the two Consensus claims above.

## Iconographic sense: manifestation "in the form of an ascetic"

Answering Q6, from the Tārā-12 homage verse (on her crescent-moon
crown-ornament, whose light-rays cause a nectar-rain):

### Consensus
Two commentaries identify a figure within Tārā's dark, bee-black locks as
manifesting, at times, "in the form of an ascetic" (དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན):

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2`, `taranatha:c-12-6` (2 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-12-6` gives the phrase in its bare form: "ལན་འགའ་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་
སྐུར་བསྟན་ནས།" ("sometimes manifesting in the form of an ascetic").
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-12-1-2` gives the same phrase, near-verbatim, within a
fuller verse-gloss that also names the figure: "རེས་དཀའ་ཐུབ་ཅན་གྱི་སྐུར་བསྟན་
ནས། དབུ་སྐྲ་བུང་བ་ལྟར་གནག་པའི་རལ་པའི་ཁྲོད་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་དཔག་མེད་བཞུགས་པ་ལས་..." ("at
times manifesting in the form of an ascetic, Buddha Amitābha ever dwells
within her bee-black locks..."). Both attribute the ascetic-form
identification to the same figure, Amitābha, dwelling amid Tārā's hair — the
near-identical phrasing suggests a shared source or lineage at this locus, as
with the TUTTĀRE facet above.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed.

### Unique
None beyond the three Consensus claims above.
