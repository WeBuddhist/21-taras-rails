## Structural placement and root-verse citation

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries in the packet route content to this slot, but mark it differently. Seven give an explicit global ordinal, "the nineteenth" (བཅུ་དགུ་པ): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-0-1`, `taranatha:c-19-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-1`. `sungrab-tulku:c-22-1` instead uses a bare parenthetical numeral, "(19)" (༼༡༩༽), rather than the spelled ordinal word. `sangye-nyentrul`'s own node title embeds the numeral "༡༩" even though its node number in that commentary's own tree is "20" (an internal offset consistent across this commentary, not a claim that this is a different verse) — cited here via `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, the claim the spine map routes as this slot's opener.

Three commentaries instead give a *local* ordinal, "the fourth" (བཞི་པ), because their own sa-bcad nests this verse as the fourth member of a "dispelling disputes and bad dreams" sub-group rather than numbering the twenty-one homages flatly: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-1` ("བཞི་པ་རྩོད་པ་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ནི།"), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-1` ("བཞི་པ་ནི།"), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-1` ("བཞི་པ་རྩོད་པ་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ་ནི།"). `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-1` shares this same node depth (1.2.3.4) and the same "praising through the activity of dispelling disputes and bad dreams" phrasing, but its own claim text in this packet does not include the word "བཞི་པ" itself — the node number implies the local ordinal, but the claim does not state it. The remaining five (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `karma-maitri` already counted above via global ordinal, `pema-namgyal`, `tsultrim-namdak`) give no explicit ordinal marker within this packet's claims — `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `pema-namgyal`, and `tsultrim-namdak` were routed here by claim-content match against an undivided or differently-segmented node (each flagged ⓘ in the packet).

Nine commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines (in one continuous claim, or split across adjacent claims):

— full verse quoted attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-91`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-92`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-93` (9 commentaries; `drakpa-gyaltsen` spans the four lines across three separate claims — lines 1–2 in `c-1-91`, line 3 in `c-1-92`, line 4 in `c-1-93`)

These quotations are close but not verbatim-identical. Line 3 shows a three-way particle split after དགའ་བ: the genitive འི (དགའ་བའི་བརྗིད), matching the root text, in `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-2`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-92`; no particle (དགའ་བ་བརྗིད) in `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-3` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`; the instrumental ས (དགའ་བས་བརྗིད) uniquely in `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-0-2`. Line 4 carries two apparent scribal/OCR slips rather than doctrinal variants: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-2` reads "སེམ་མ" where every other quotation reads "སེལ་མ" (dispels), and `sungrab-tulku:c-22-2` reads "རྨི་མལ" where every other quotation reads "རྨི་ལམ" (dream) — neither is treated as a meaningful reading difference. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`'s own claim title labels its quotation "Verse 20 quoted and glossed" even though it is routed here by the spine map as verse 19's content — an internal-numbering quirk of that commentary's own extraction (paralleled at other slots in this corpus), not evidence that gendun-drub disagrees about which verse this is.

The remaining seven commentaries give only a partial or paraphrastic opening in this packet rather than a clean four-line quotation: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-1`, `konchok-thabkhe` (no direct verse quotation among its four claims here, only glosses), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `taranatha:c-19-1`–`c-19-2`, `pema-namgyal` (no direct quotation, routed by claim-content match), `sangye-nyentrul` (no direct quotation among its five claims here, only glosses).

## Name, epithet, and classification of this homage

### Consensus
None — every commentary that supplies a proper name or classificatory label gives one from one of several distinct traditions; this facet is a set of divergent namings, not a shared consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming/classification traditions are attested:

1. **"[Tārā] Who Burns Away Suffering"** (སྡུག་བསྔལ་བསྲེག/སྲེག་...སྒྲོལ་མ) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-1` ("རྗེ་བཙུན་མ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསྲེག་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ", "the Venerable Lady Who Burns Away All Suffering") and `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-4` ("སྡུག་བསྔལ་སྲེག་བྱེད་སྒྲོལ་མ", "Tārā Who Burns Away Suffering") share the same root verb སྲེག/བསྲེག ("burn") applied to "suffering" (སྡུག་བསྔལ) as the theme of her name; `anon-trinle-char`'s version additionally specifies "all" (ཐམས་ཅད) and the honorific རྗེ་བཙུན་མ. Two commentaries.
2. **"[She Who] Dispels Suffering"** (སྡུག་བསྔལ་སེལ་མ) — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-1` ("བཅུ་དགུ་པ་སྡུག་བསྔལ་སེལ་མ་ལ་བསྟོད་པ", "Nineteenth, homage to Dispeller of Suffering"). This shares the "suffering" (སྡུག་བསྔལ) theme-word with group 1 but uses a different verb, སེལ ("dispel/clear") rather than སྲེག/བསྲེག ("burn") — a related but distinct naming, not merged with group 1. One commentary.
3. **"[Tārā,] Invincible Queen, [holder of the] White Parasol"** (སྒྲོལ་མ་མི་ཕམ་རྒྱལ་མོ་...གདུགས་དཀར) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-0-1` ("རྗེ་བཙུན་འཕགས་མ་སྒྲོལ་མ་མི་ཕམ་རྒྱལ་མོ་གདུགས་དཀར", "Jetsün Ārya Tārā, Invincible Queen with the White Parasol"), `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-1` ("འཕགས་མ་མི་ཕམ་གདུགས་དཀར་མོ", "Noble Invincible White Umbrella"), `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` ("སྒྲོལ་མ་མི་ཕམ་རྒྱལ་མོ", "Tārā, Invincible Queen"). `palden-sherab` and `tsultrim-namdak` share the identical three-word compound མི་ཕམ་རྒྱལ་མོ ("Invincible Queen"); `sangye-nyentrul` shares the element མི་ཕམ ("Invincible") but pairs it directly with གདུགས་དཀར(་མོ) ("White Parasol/Umbrella[-Lady]") rather than རྒྱལ་མོ ("Queen") as the head noun. Three commentaries, closely converging.
4. **Functional/thematic classification rather than a proper name**, using near-identical phrasing built on the verse's own final clause — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-1` ("རྩོད་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ", "homage by way of dispelling quarrels and bad dreams") and `sungrab-tulku:c-22-1` ("རྩོད་པ་དང་རྨི་ལམ་ངན་པ་སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ") are near character-identical (differing only by an added པ); `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-1` and `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-1` share the fuller phrase "...སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ" / "...སེལ་བའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པར་མཛད་པ" (activity-qualified); `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-1` uses "...སེལ་བའི་འཕྲིན་ལས" ("the activity of dispelling...") without the སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ frame. Five commentaries share this functional classification, with two sub-clusters of near-identical wording.

The remaining commentaries (`drakpa-gyaltsen`, `gendun-gyatso`, `karma-maitri`, `lobsang-dawa`, `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha`) supply no proper name or classificatory label for this homage beyond restating or paraphrasing the verse's own opening clause within the claims in this packet.

### Unique
None beyond the clusters above — every naming claim in the packet belongs to one of the four traditions listed.

## Those who serve and revere her

### Consensus
All sixteen commentaries describe this Tārā as served, relied upon, or revered by a retinue built on the root verse's own "king of the hosts of gods... gods and kinnaras" (ལྷ་ཡི་ཚོགས་རྣམས་རྒྱལ་པོ... ལྷ་དང་མིའམ་ཅི), typically naming Indra (བརྒྱ་བྱིན) and Brahmā (ཚངས་པ) as the "kings," ordinary gods (ལྷ་ཕལ་བ/ཕལ་པ), and kinnaras (མིའམ་ཅི), often including a named kinnara-king ("Druma"/Horse-Head, ལྗོན་པ་རྟ་མགོ).

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-91`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-2`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-50`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3`, `taranatha:c-19-2`, `taranatha:c-19-5`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-64`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` (16 commentaries)

Several commentaries elaborate beyond this base list without conflicting with it: `taranatha:c-19-3` additionally glosses "kings" (རྒྱལ་པོ) as worldly rulers such as cakravartins; `taranatha:c-19-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-1`, and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65` add mountain-, tree-, and water/lake-gods as a further class who benefit or harm the world; `sungrab-tulku:c-22-3` instead adds earth-, water-, fire-, and wind-gods; `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-3` specifies the six classes of desire-realm gods and the seventeen abodes of the form realm. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` also names this full retinue (Brahmā, Indra, mountain/tree/water/sky deities, kinnara kings) within its own richer, partly-elided claim.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the added classes of deity are elaborations on the same base list, not conflicting accounts.

### Unique
None beyond the elaborations noted above.

## The protective armor: practitioner's own, or the goddess's own splendor?

### Consensus
Nine commentaries read the root verse's "all-encompassing armor, [with] the splendor of joy" (ཀུན་ནས་གོ་ཆ་དགའ་བའི་བརྗིད) as the practitioner's own mantra-and-mudrā or deity-visualization armor, blessed by Tārā, whose donning dispels disputes and bad dreams:

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-4`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-3-4-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-51`, `sungrab-tulku:c-22-4`, `taranatha:c-19-6`, `taranatha:c-19-7`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-65`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-66` (9 commentaries)

`anon-utpala:c-1-2-3-4-4` and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-3-4-3` share near-identical phrasing: both describe donning "the armor of visualizing the goddess's form and reciting the mantra from every gate" (སྒོ་ཀུན་ནས་...སྐུ་བསྒོམ་པ་དང་སྔགས་བཟླ་བའི་གོ་ཆ་བགོས་པ). `taranatha:c-19-6` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-1-2` both explicitly name the "practitioner" (སྒྲུབ་པ་པོ) as the one bound by the armor. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-4` is the most elaborated instance: it frames the whole verse as yidam practice — taking the goddess as one's meditational deity, cultivating the threefold perception of deity/mantra/dharmakāya at body/speech/mind, and reciting OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ while wearing this armor.

### ⚑ Divergences
`anon-trinle-char` reads the same clause differently, and explicitly frames this as a disagreement with other commentaries: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-7` states, "In commentaries, when explaining the meaning of 'armour' in this context, it appears that some take it to mean an armour of visualizing the family-mantra and the deity's body... but it must instead be taken to mean that, through being served by the lords of gods and the rest abiding in the three realms, her mind becomes extremely delighted, and it is from such bodily splendour that disputes and other ills are dispelled" (Tibetan: "འགྲེལ་པ་རྣམས་སུ་སྐབས་འདིའི་གོ་ཆའི་དོན་འཆད་པའི་ཚེ་རིགས་སྔགས་དང་སྐུ་སྒོམ་པའི་གོ་ཆ་ལ་ལྷ་མོ་དགྱེས་པའམ་དགའ་བའི་དོན་དུ་བྱས་སྣང་ཡང་..."). On this reading the "armor" is not a practitioner's meditation practice at all, but a description of Tārā's own state — the joy she feels from being reverently served, whose splendor itself dispels disputes and bad dreams. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-5` states the same reading in its own voice before the correction, and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-8` supports it with a citation from Nyima Sbaspa (see "Attributed citations" below). This reading is unique to `anon-trinle-char` in this packet; no other commentary states outright that "armor" excludes mantra/visualization. One commentary against nine.

A number of claims are genuinely ambiguous as to whose armor is meant (Tārā's own, or the practitioner's) and are not counted on either side: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-92` ("she abides in the manner of armor formed by her divine body and mantra" — subject and possessor unclear), `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-19-6` (bare word-by-word glosses of the verse's own phrases, without specifying a subject), `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-3` (subject is "she," describing Tārā's own use of the mantra armor to liberate, rather than a practitioner's practice), `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-0-2` (addresses Tārā in the second person — "you, Noble Lady, through the splendour of your own three doors... dispel" — closer to `anon-trinle-char`'s reading in grammatical subject, yet still names a "mantra-and-mudra armor blessed by all," a hybrid this page does not force onto either side), `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-3` (generic meditation instruction, no stated subject for the armor), `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-3-4-4` (describes her protection as "like armor," a simile rather than a stated visualization practice), `taranatha:c-19-8` (a neutral word-gloss, "here, 'armor' means protection," which does not specify whose). `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-2` is also excluded from both sides: its own text is OCR-degraded ("ཀུན་ནས་སཱུ་ལྷའི་རྣམ་པ་དང་སྔགས་དགའ་བར་ཏེ..."), and the packet itself marks it uncertain.

### Unique
`konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-4` and `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-8` each cite what appears to be the same underlying verse for the "splendor... dispels [ills]" clause, under two different attributions — see "Attributed citations" below; this is reported there rather than duplicated here.

`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-93` specifies the disputes dispelled as those "of the tīrthikas" (མུ་སྟེགས་ཀྱི་རྩོད་པ, non-Buddhist extremists) — a specification not paralleled by any other claim in the packet.

`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-19-5` quotes an additional, otherwise-unidentified verse extending the benefit beyond "disputes and bad dreams": protection "from spirits, planets and constellations, untimely death and sinful dreams, harm from poison, weapons, fire and water" — not paralleled elsewhere in this packet.

**Attributed citations.** `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-8` attributes the verse "ས་གསུམ་དབང་པོས་རབ་མཆོད་ཅིང་། ། ཀུན་ནས་དགའ་བསྐྱེད་འབར་བ་ཡི། ། གཟི་ཡིས་ལྟས་ངན་སྡུག་བསྔལ་སེལ" to "Nyima Sbaspa" (ཉི་མ་སྦས་པ). `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-19-4` quotes the near-identical "ཀུན་ནས་དགའ་བསྐྱེད་འབར་བ་ཡི། ། གཟི་ཡིས་ལྟ་ངན་སྡུག་བསྔལ་སེལ" (differing only in ལྟས་ངན vs ལྟ་ངན) and attributes it to "a mahāsiddha" (གྲུབ་ཆེན), explicitly noting that no other commentary it knows of explains the verse's last two lines this way. Whether "a mahāsiddha" and "Nyima Sbaspa" are the same figure is not stated by either claim; this page notes the shared verse without asserting that identity (a page-level observation, not attributed to either claim).

## Iconography (where given)

### Consensus
Where body colour is stated, it is white (སྐུ་མདོག་དཀར་མོ) — attested across two otherwise sharply divergent iconographic traditions (see Divergences): `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-9`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` (4 commentaries).

Where face and arm count are stated, all agree on one face and two arms (ཞལ་གཅིག་ཕྱག་གཉིས) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-9`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-1` + `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-2` (jointly: white body in the first claim, one-face-two-hands in the second) (3 commentaries). `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` states white body but does not state an arm-count in the Tibetan preserved in this packet (its claim is partly elided with "…"), so it is not included in this narrower count.

### ⚑ Divergences
Beyond the shared white body colour and (where stated) two-armed, one-faced form, two distinct and non-overlapping iconographic traditions are attested for the remaining details:

1. **Fire-vessel, "Burns-Away-Suffering" form** — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-9`: seated in bodhisattva posture on a white lotus and sun maṇḍala, adorned with a garland of light rays, holding a fire-vessel at the heart with both hands (ཕྱག་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཐུགས་ཀར་མེའི་སྣོད་བསྣམས་པ). Supported within the same commentary by a citation from Nyima Sbaspa at `anon-trinle-char:c-3-19-10` ("པདྨ་དཀར་དང་ཉི་སྟེང་དུ...མེ་ཐབ་བསྣམས་ཤིང་སྡུག་བསྔལ་བསྲེག", "seated on white lotus and sun... holding a fire-hearth, she burns away suffering") — the same source, not an independent commentary. One commentary.
2. **White-Parasol / "Invincible Queen" form** — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-1` + `sangye-nyentrul:c-20-1-2` share near-identical phrasing: right hand in supreme-giving mudrā, left hand (for `palden-sherab`, forming the Three-Jewels mudrā) holding an utpala topped by a white parasol, from which vajra-fire sparks radiate (`palden-sherab`: "སྐུ་ལས་མེ་མཚོན་རྡོ་རྗེའི་ཚྭ་ཚྭ་སྤྲོས་པས"; `sangye-nyentrul`: "གདུགས་དཀར་པོ་ལས་རྡོ་རྗེའི་མེ་ཡི་ཚྭ་ཚྭ་འཕྲོས་པས") — destroying curses, sorcery, and disputants. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-22` shares the name and the white-parasol-holding element ("སྐུ་མདོག་དཀར་མོ་གདུགས་དཀར་པོ་འཛིན་པ") but does not include the vajra-fire-spark detail in the Tibetan preserved here. Three commentaries, two of them (`palden-sherab`, `sangye-nyentrul`) sharing near-verbatim wording.

These two traditions do not overlap in any implement or ornament beyond the shared body colour and (where stated) arm count; this page does not attempt to reconcile them.

### Unique
`palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-2-2` (continuing `c-3-1-19-2-1`) adds an eight-ornament protective armor (crown, earrings, armlets, bracelets, anklets, throat-ornament, belt, lower-body wrap), each linked to a specific symbolic function, said to dispel "all bad dreams caused by disturbed channels, winds, and drops" — not paralleled elsewhere in the packet.

`gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-3` (sun-and-moon eyes radiating clear light) and `gendun-gyatso:c-1-19-4` (the mantra HARA-TUTTĀRA dispelling virulent epidemic disease) are routed to this slot but describe content that matches the *root text's own verse 20* ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་ཉི་མ་ཟླ་བ་རྒྱས་པའི། སྤྱན་གཉིས་པོ་ལ་འོད་རབ་གསལ་མ...", 1-SOURCES root text ^1-20) rather than verse 19's armor/dispute imagery. No other commentary in this packet places sun-and-moon-eye or epidemic-mantra content at this slot. This page carries both claims through with this flag (per Rule 6) rather than silently dropping them; they are not folded into either iconographic tradition above, since neither tradition mentions eyes or an epidemic-dispelling mantra.

## Hidden / completion-stage meaning

### Consensus
None — two commentaries independently give "hidden meaning" readings of this verse, but the two schemes share no wording or content; each is reported as Unique to its own commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed as outright disagreement — the two schemes below are independent, non-overlapping elaborations of the same verse rather than incompatible readings of the same point.

### Unique
`taranatha:c-19-9`–`c-19-13` gives a five-part "definitive meaning" (ངེས་པའི་དོན) reading centred on subtle-body etymology: "deva," from the sound "deva," is glossed as "deha-bhava," arisen from the body — the wisdom-body (`c-19-9`); "hosts" as the wisdom-body's many pure aggregates and elements (`c-19-10`); the referent is also "the king of vidyādharas," worthy of the three worlds' worship (`c-19-11`); the cause is "the splendor of the armor that binds the assembly of drops without movement" (`c-19-12`); and the result destroys "changeable dispassion and disputes, and the ordinary appearance of bad dreams" (`c-19-13`).

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-3-1` gives a distinct "completion-stage-with-marks" reading built on the channel system: "the king of the divine hosts" is the central channel (རྩ་དབུ་མ); "gods and kinnaras" are the two channels Rasanā and Lalanā; "all-encompassing armour" is the fourfold concealment of drop-within-channel, thought-within-wisdom, bliss-within-clear-light, and clear-light-within-non-grasping; "dispelling quarrels and bad dreams" is the clearing of channel-wind-drop obstacles that results. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-19-4-1` further gives a "completion-stage-without-marks" reading: "the king of the divine hosts" is the awareness pointed out by a qualified guru; "gods and kinnaras" are the two accumulations of merit and wisdom perfected; "the armor of joy" is the primordially-liberated wisdom of the six collections; "dispelling quarrels and bad dreams" is that all conceptual thought self-pacifies and self-dispels in that state.

`taranatha`'s and `palden-sherab`'s schemes share no verbatim clause and no structural correspondence (subtle-body etymology and vidyādhara-kingship vs. central-channel/Rasanā-Lalanā and, separately, a Great-Perfection-styled awareness reading) — they are not treated as corroborating one another.
