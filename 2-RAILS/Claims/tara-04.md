# Tārā 4 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར། / Homage to the Uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas

> Consolidated from the raw claims files listed in `sources:`. Every attestation
> cites a raw claim ID; raw claims cite `1-SOURCES/` segments. This page never
> cites a commentary file directly, and regenerating it never touches `raw/`.

## Questions asked

1. Where does each commentary place this verse structurally — as "the fourth" of the twenty-one homages in flat sequence, or within a different, nested classificatory scheme?
2. What does the root verse literally say is the object of homage, and how do commentaries gloss the phrase "the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa" (crown-protuberance)?
3. Do commentaries read the uṣṇīṣa as belonging specifically to the Tathāgata Vairocana, or generically to "all Tathāgatas" — and is this an explicit point of disagreement anywhere in the corpus?
4. Do any commentaries identify this Tārā as the Perfection of Wisdom (Prajñāpāramitā) personified, source of all buddhas?
5. Do any commentaries describe her as an emanation of vidyā-mantra wisdom arising from the uṣṇīṣa, and does this wording recur closely enough across commentaries to suggest a shared source?
6. How do commentaries gloss "boundless victorious conduct" — what specifically is she said to be victorious over (māras, afflictive/cognitive obscurations, saṃsāra, or something else)?
7. Do commentaries agree on who has "attained the ten pāramitās/bhūmis without exception" in the verse's third line — the bodhisattva-heirs who then rely on her, or the goddess herself?
8. By what name(s) or epithet(s) do commentaries identify this particular Tārā, and do these names agree across the corpus?
9. Does any commentary identify her with a specialized "Deathless Life-Accomplishing" (longevity) form of Uṣṇīṣavijayā, distinct from the general form, and how many independently attest this?
10. What iconographic form (body colour, number of faces/hands, implements) do commentaries assign to this Tārā, and is there more than one iconographic tradition attested?
11. Do any commentaries read the verse according to a generation-stage/completion-stage ("hidden-meaning") hermeneutic, and if so, do any two commentaries converge on the same specific formulation?
12. What benefits, activities, or practices do commentaries associate with this homage / this form of Tārā, particularly around longevity?
13. Does any commentary bring in material from outside this specific verse (another deity, another text, a geographic simile) to elaborate on it?
14. Does any commentary use a distinct verse-numbering convention for this homage (e.g. numbering by individual quatrain-lines rather than by homage), and how does that compare with the rest of the corpus?
15. Does any commentary explicitly distinguish this verse's mode of praise from the previous verse's, and if so, on what basis?
16. Is the root verse quoted in full (all four lines) by each commentary, or only partially / split across separate glosses?

## Structural placement

### Consensus
Eight commentaries mark this passage with an explicit flat ordinal — "the fourth" (or "(4)") — identifying it as the fourth of the twenty-one homages.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-1`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-1`, `taranatha:c-4-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-1` (8 commentaries)

`palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-1` is also cited under "Naming and epithets" below, for the distinctive epithet ("Deathless Life-Accomplishing Uṣṇīṣavijayā") the same claim supplies alongside the ordinal; both statements are genuinely present in its text.

### ⚑ Divergences
Four commentaries instead nest this homage within a different classificatory scheme, labelling it "the third" rather than using the flat ordinal. Tenga Tulku's opening of the same node states explicitly that this is the third in a sequence of *praise-modes* (having already praised "by way of body-colour and hand-emblem," she is now, third, praised "as devotedly relied upon by the Victors and their offspring") — i.e. a rhetorical-mode count, not a renumbering of which of the twenty-one homages this is. The same node path and near-identical opening wording recur in three further commentaries.

- Position 1 (flat "fourth of twenty-one"): the eight commentaries listed under Consensus above.
- Position 2 (nested "third mode of praise," within a rhetorical-mode taxonomy rather than a flat sequence number): `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1`.

This is a difference in which classificatory axis is used (sequence-of-homages vs. mode-of-praise), not a doctrinal disagreement about the verse's content — but per Rule 4 it is recorded here rather than folded into the Consensus, since these four commentaries' own structural claims do not label the verse "the fourth" anywhere captured in the packet.

### Unique
Tenga Tulku additionally divides the reverent-reliance mode into two parts before glossing the verse — first, the manner in which the buddhas themselves rely on her; second, the manner in which the great bodhisattvas rely on her — a finer-grained internal structure not found elsewhere in the corpus. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-1`

Konchok Thabkhe's commentary appends its own view ("we say," ཁོ་བོ་ཅག་སྨྲའོ) explicitly distinguishing this verse from the previous one: this verse (the fourth) praises her as the mother who nurtures and generates beings of the three times as "the noble one," whereas the previous verse (the third) praised her by way of her companionship with the other five pāramitās. No other commentary in the corpus draws this specific verse-to-verse contrast. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-5`

## Root verse quoted

### Consensus
Six commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines together in one block (subject to minor orthographic variation across transmissions — shad placement, "རྒྱལ་བ་སྤྱོད་མ" vs. "རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ," and similar): "ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར། །མཐའ་ཡས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་སྤྱོད་མ། །མ་ལུས་ཕ་རོལ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་པའི། །རྒྱལ་བའི་སྲས་ཀྱིས་ཤིན་ཏུ་བསྟེན་མ།" — "Homage to her [dwelling at] the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa, whose conduct is victorious without limit, utterly relied upon by the victors' sons who have attained the perfections without exception."

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13` (6 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the verse's wording itself — no commentary disputes the four lines quoted above; the variation is orthographic only.

### Unique
Anon-Trinle-Char's quotation is uniquely prefaced "from the tantra" (རྒྱུད་ལས།) rather than presented simply as the praise's own verse — the only commentary in the corpus to attribute this particular quotation to an external tantra rather than to the praise text itself. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-3`

Gendun Drub quotes only the verse's first line ("ཕྱག་འཚལ་དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པའི་གཙུག་ཏོར།"), not the full four lines; the remainder of the verse appears in this commentary only as paraphrase, split across two separate glosses — line 2's content under "Identification of the object of homage" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3`), below, and lines 3–4's content under "Doctrinal: reliance of the pāramitā-bodhisattvas" (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-4`), below. Per Rule 9 this is recorded as a partial attestation, not folded into the full-quotation count above. `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-2`

Tenga Tulku splits the same four lines across three separate glosses, interleaving each line with its own exegesis rather than quoting the verse as one block: line 1 at `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-2`, line 2 at `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-4`, and lines 3–4 together at `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2-2`.

Lobsang Dawa likewise splits the verse, but under a distinct verse-numbering convention: it labels the three resulting fragments "Verse 9," "Verse 10," and "Verse 11" rather than treating them as lines of a single fourth homage — implying this commentary counts verses by individual quatrain-line groupings rather than by homage, a numbering convention not shared by any other commentary consulted for this topic. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2` (Verse 9), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3` (Verse 10, also cited under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct'" below), `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-4` (Verse 11, also cited under "Doctrinal: reliance of the pāramitā-bodhisattvas" below)

## Identification of the object of homage

### Consensus
Three commentaries give a closely corresponding causal explanation for why homage is paid at the Tathāgatas' uṣṇīṣa: because she is the mother of all Tathāgatas, she is symbolically borne / dwells as the crown-ornament atop their heads.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3` (3 commentaries)

Konchok Thabkhe's identification overlaps with this same "mother, hence crown-ornament" idea — it names the object of homage as "the supreme definitive-meaning mother who gives rise to all victors... worthy of being honored even by all tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance" — but frames it through the Perfection-of-Wisdom identification; it is cited in full under "Identified as the Perfection of Wisdom," below, rather than added to the attestation list above, since its own emphasis is the Prajñāpāramitā equation rather than the bare mother/crown-ornament causal link.

### ⚑ Divergences

**Is the uṣṇīṣa specifically the Tathāgata Vairocana's, or generic to "all Tathāgatas"?**

Anon-Trinle-Char's own commentary names this explicitly as a disagreement between two named parties:

- Position A (Vairocana-specific): Anon-Trinle-Char reports that "Jonangpa" explains the verse as the emanation of vidyā-mantra wisdom specifically from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-7`
- Position B (general, "all Tathāgatas," not Vairocana-specific): the same commentary reports that "Ngulchu and many other Geluk scholar-adepts" instead explain the verse "as before" — i.e. as the general uṣṇīṣa-symbolism of all Tathāgatas, not Vairocana's specifically. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-8`

Position A is independently corroborated in this corpus, not merely reported at second hand: Taranatha's own commentary opens its word-gloss with "from the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgata Vairocana" in its own voice, and Tsultrim Namdak's commentary — re-read to confirm — likewise continues its quotation of the verse directly into "gang la phyag 'tshal na de bzhin gshegs pa rnam par snang [mdzad]," naming Vairocana as the one whose uṣṇīṣa is meant.

— attested (Position A, independent of the report above): `taranatha:c-4-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-13` (2 further commentaries; 3 in total with the reported attribution)

No claim in the packet states Position B in the commentator's own voice with Vairocana named as the position being rejected — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-8` reports it only as an attribution to Ngulchu and other Geluk masters. Read together with that reported position, four further commentaries in the corpus independently gloss the mother/crown-ornament identification using unqualified "all Tathāgatas" (དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་) language, without naming Vairocana — consistent with, though not explicitly framed by these commentaries themselves as taking a side in, the general reading Position B reports:

— consistent with Position B (not an explicit statement of it): `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-1`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-23`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2`

`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-3` and `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3` are also cited above under Consensus, for the "mother, hence crown-ornament" statement both claims fully support in addition to their general "all Tathāgatas" phrasing; both statements are genuinely present in each claim's text, so the reuse is deliberate — this is not a mis-sorted citation. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2` is likewise cited above under "Identified as the Perfection of Wisdom" for its own Prajñāpāramitā-framed identification; its wording ("worthy of being honored even by all tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance," དེ་བཞིན་གཤེགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་) independently uses the same unqualified "all Tathāgatas" language, without naming Vairocana, so it belongs in this list as well.

### Unique
Palden Sherab identifies her as an emanation of vidyā-mantra wisdom arising from the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa, whose enlightened activity is to be perfectly victorious over discordant conditions unfavourable to virtue and over boundless afflictions (one continuous gloss, split across two claim entries in the raw file with other content interleaved between them). Taranatha and Tsultrim Namdak give a closely related identification using near-identical wording — cited and discussed under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct,'" below. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-3`

Sangye Nyentrul gives a simpler version of the same emanation idea, without the "vidyā-mantra" terminology: "an emanation from the crown-protuberance (uṣṇīṣa) of the Tathāgatas." `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-1`

Drakpa Gyaltsen's own word-gloss states the bare simile — "she is like the crown-protuberance of all the Tathāgatas" — without the causal "mother, therefore" reasoning found in the Consensus above; it is a narrower, sub-lexical point subsumed by the fuller identification, and is cited above (Divergences) for its "all Tathāgatas" phrasing. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-23`

Karma Maitri similarly gives the bare simile without causal reasoning: "she has become an object of worship comparable to the uṣṇīṣa of the Tathāgatas." `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-2`

Tenga Tulku's word-by-word gloss of the verse's opening phrase adds a grammatical observation not found elsewhere in the corpus: the word "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) itself functions as one of three markers (for the beginning, middle, and end of the verse), and here specifically marks the beginning. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2` (also listed under "Unique comparanda and asides," below, for cross-reference)

Tenga Tulku's bare identification — "to whom? to the Venerable Lady" — adds no further distinguishing content beyond what the fuller identifications above already establish; logged in "Claims reviewed, not separately cited," below.

## Identified as the Perfection of Wisdom (Prajñāpāramitā)

### Consensus
Three commentaries independently identify Tārā, in this homage, as the very essence or form of the Perfection of Wisdom (Prajñāpāramitā), the mother from whom all buddhas are born — Tenga Tulku's and Sungrab Tulku's formulations correspond so closely (re-read and confirmed as genuine, not merely superficial vocabulary overlap) as to suggest a shared exegetical source: "Venerable Tārā is the very essence of Mother Prajñāpāramitā; and since all buddhas are born in dependence on prajñāpāramitā... she dwells, acting in a manner triumphant over endless adverse conditions" (Sungrab Tulku); "The Venerable Lady is the very essence of the mother, the perfection of wisdom... since the Venerable Lady is the very form of the perfection of wisdom, the tathāgatas pay homage to her" (Tenga Tulku).

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-3` (2 commentaries)

Konchok Thabkhe independently gives a related identification — she is "the supreme definitive-meaning mother who gives rise to all victors — the perfection of wisdom — worthy of being honored even by all tathāgatas as their own crown-protuberance." This is not word-for-word identical to Sungrab Tulku's or Tenga Tulku's formulations (re-read and confirmed: it frames the equation through "definitive-meaning mother" rather than "very essence/form," and folds in the crown-ornament image directly), so it is recorded as an independent third attestation of the same core identification rather than merged into the two-commentary Consensus above.

— attested: `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-2` (1 further commentary; 3 in total for the shared core identification)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary in this corpus offers a competing identification of who or what she fundamentally is in this homage (the alternative identifications recorded below — vidyā-mantra-wisdom emanation, "Deathless Life-Accomplishing" longevity form — are additional characterizations layered onto this verse rather than statements that contradict the Prajñāpāramitā identification).

### Unique
`sungrab-tulku:c-7-6` is also cited under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct,'" below, for the same claim's further statement that she purifies the two obscurations preventing correct realization of prajñāpāramitā; both statements are fully supported by its text.

## Etymology of "boundless victorious conduct"

### Consensus
Four commentaries gloss the verse's second line using unspecified, generic language — she is "victorious over all/limitless discordant factors/factions" — without naming a specific target of that victory.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-3`, `taranatha:c-4-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-14` (4 commentaries)

`taranatha:c-4-4` and `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-14` share this generic "victorious over all discordant factions" clause as part of a longer sentence that is, in both commentaries, close to word-for-word identical: "...ཕན་ཡོན་མཐའ་ཡས་པ་དང་ལྡན་ཞིང་མི་མཐུན་པའི་ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་བྱེད་པའི་རིག་སྔགས་..." (Taranatha) against "...ཕན་ཡོན་མཐའ་ཡས་པ་དང་ལྡན་ཞིང་མི་མཐུན་པའི་ཕྱོགས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལས་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་བར་བྱེད་པའི་རིགས་སྔགས་..." (Tsultrim Namdak) — re-read and confirmed as a genuine near-verbatim correspondence, not a coincidental topical overlap, suggesting these two commentaries share an exegetical source for this clause. Both claims are also discussed under "Identification of the object of homage" → Unique, above, for the "vidyā-mantra wisdom emanation" identification carried by the same sentence.

### ⚑ Divergences
None of the target-specific elaborations below contradict one another or the generic statement above — a single commentator could consistently hold that she is victorious over māras, obscurations, and saṃsāra all at once. They are recorded as parallel unique elaborations rather than as competing positions.

### Unique

**Māra / adversaries as the named target.** Three commentaries converge on a closely-worded gloss naming Māra and other adversaries as the target of victory (re-read and confirmed as genuinely close, near-formulaic phrasing, not independently coined): "victorious over Māra and enemies and all other opponents" (Drakpa Gyaltsen); "triumphing completely over the endless host of māras and adversaries" (Gendun Gyatso); "victorious over māras and other limitless adversaries" (Karma Maitri).

— attested: `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-24`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-3` (3 commentaries)

**Afflictive and cognitive obscurations as the named target.** Anon-Trinle-Char glosses the same phrase as victory "over the limitless discordant factors such as afflictive and cognitive obscurations." Tenzin Dhonzang separately glosses it as victory over "discordant factors — the boundless cognitive obscurations and so forth." The two are related but not identical (re-read and confirmed: Anon-Trinle-Char names both obscuration-types explicitly, Tenzin Dhonzang's claim names only the cognitive one before trailing into "and so forth"), so they are not merged into one attestation. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-7`

**Obscurations preventing realization of prajñāpāramitā as the named target.** Sungrab Tulku and Tenga Tulku (already cited above under "Identified as the Perfection of Wisdom," for the surrounding Prajñāpāramitā identification) tie the "boundless victory" specifically to purifying "the two obscurations... that prevent realizing prajñāpāramitā just as it is." `sungrab-tulku:c-7-6`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-1-5`

**Saṃsāra (and, in one case, nirvāṇa/peace) as the named target.** Konchok Thabkhe glosses the phrase as victory "over the boundless directions of both saṃsāra and peace [nirvāṇa]" — both extremes. Sangye Nyentrul glosses it as victory "over the boundless faults of cyclic existence [saṃsāra]" — saṃsāra alone, without mentioning nirvāṇa. Re-read and confirmed these are not identical statements (only Konchok Thabkhe names the nirvāṇa/peace side), so they are cited separately rather than merged. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-3`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-2`

**"Through her" as the enabling cause, rather than a target she overcomes.** Konchok Thabkhe's doctrinal claim about the bodhisattvas' reliance (cited fully under "Doctrinal: reliance of the pāramitā-bodhisattvas," below) frames the relationship causally in the opposite direction from the majority: the ten grounds' bodhisattvas rely on her "because through her the ten perfections proper to each ground reach complete increase," rather than describing her own victory over an obstacle. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-4`

**Ten non-virtues, two obscurations, and self-cherishing, tied to the three kāyas.** Tenzin Dhonzang gives by far the fullest enumeration in the corpus: victory over the ten non-virtues (→ higher rebirth), the two obscurations (→ liberation), and self-cherishing (→ the three-kāya state), followed by a full naming of the ten bhūmis and ten pāramitās. This claim is also cited under "Doctrinal: reliance of the pāramitā-bodhisattvas," below, for its closing statement that the bodhisattvas who have attained these serve her feet with reverence; both statements are genuinely present in its text. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8`

**"Superior even to the victors" — an epithet-driven reading.** Pema Namgyal alone reads the phrase through a specific epithet rather than a target of victory: because she is consecrated as mistress of the boundless crown-ornament family, "she conducts herself as though superior even to the victors" — hence "Victorious in All Directions" (also cited under "Naming and epithets," below). `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20`

## Doctrinal: reliance of the pāramitā-bodhisattvas

### Consensus
Ten commentaries state that the bodhisattva-heirs of the Victors, having themselves attained the ten pāramitās (the nature of the ten bhūmis) without exception, intensely rely upon / venerate her.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-5`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-4`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-4`, `taranatha:c-4-7`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-8`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2-3`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-21`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-15`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` (10 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences

**Who has attained the ten pāramitās/bhūmis — the bodhisattva-heirs (Consensus above), or the goddess herself?**

Three commentaries diverge from the majority reading above by explicitly attributing the attainment of the ten pāramitās/bhūmis to the goddess herself, rather than to the bodhisattvas who then rely on her:

- Gendun Gyatso: "having attained the perfections of all ten grounds without exception, *she* became the mother of all the bodhisattva sons of the Victorious Ones, and is the Tārā whose feet are devotedly venerated by them." `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-3`
- Karma Maitri: "*she* has, in her own nature, attained the state of having completely gone to the far shore of the ten pāramitās and the ten bhūmis," and on this basis "has become the mother of all the great bodhisattva sons of the victors," relied upon "with great devotion." `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-4`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-5`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-6`
- Sangye Nyentrul: "because *she* has fully perfected abandonment and realization without exception, having abandoned the ten obscurations to be eliminated and attained the ten perfections as qualities, the victorious ones together with their heirs rely on you…" (the claim continues ", Noble Lady, with the utmost reverence") `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-3`

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-4-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-4`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-0-3` (3 commentaries; `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-5` and `karma-maitri:c-1-1-4-6` continue the same claim's reasoning without independently attesting a new position)

### Unique
Drakpa Gyaltsen's word-by-word gloss splits this content into three separate phrase-glosses, each narrower than the full Consensus statement above and each preserving the root verse's own (grammatically elided, but contextually bodhisattva-subject) reading rather than reassigning the attainment to the goddess: "having attained the ten pāramitās, the nature of all ten bhūmis without exception" (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-25`); "'sons of the Victors' means all bodhisattvas" (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-26`); "'greatly relied upon' — she is carried by them as foremost" (`drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-27`).

Palden Sherab gives an elaborate, densely doctrinal reading of the same lines that does not cleanly fit either side of the divergence above (the passage's own grammar conflates the goddess's and the bodhisattvas' attainment): complete abandonment/realization of the ten obscurations to be abandoned across the ten bhūmis and five paths, attainment of the ten wisdoms of the ten bhūmis and the ten pāramitās, and reliance by "the victors and their heirs" upon her as "the mother Prajñāpāramitā... since the four types of noble offspring are born from her," with a full naming of the ten bhūmis, ten pāramitās, and four types of offspring (śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas, bodhisattvas, buddhas). The combination of all three lists together is not given by any other commentary in this corpus for this verse — though Tenzin Dhonzang's `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8` (cited in full under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct,'" above) independently gives comparably full namings of the ten bhūmis and the ten pāramitās on its own, without a four-offspring list. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-1-4`

Konchok Thabkhe's causally-inverted reading ("through her the ten perfections... reach complete increase") is discussed in full under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct,'" above. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-4-4`

Tenzin Dhonzang's fuller enumeration (ten non-virtues, two obscurations, self-cherishing, three kāyas) is discussed in full under "Etymology of 'boundless victorious conduct,'" above; it is also cited here for its closing statement that the bodhisattvas who attained the ten bhūmis/pāramitās "serve her lotus feet with utmost reverence," which is fully consistent with (and here counted toward) the majority Consensus above. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-8`

## Naming and epithets

### Consensus
None at the level of a single shared proper name — see Divergences. All contributing commentaries agree only that the homage addresses a form of Tārā/Uṣṇīṣavijayā generically (see "Identification of the object of homage," above).

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming traditions are attested for this particular Tārā:

**Position 1 — "Tārā Adorned as the Crown-Ornament" (གཙུག་ཏོར་རྒྱན་པའི་སྒྲོལ་མ) / "Tārā Tsuktor Namgyalma":** Gendun Drub's commentary states outright, "she is called here 'Tārā Adorned as the Crown-Ornament.'" Sungrab Tulku's gloss of the word "homage" separately names her "venerable Tārā Tsuktor Namgyalma [Crown-Ornament, Victorious One]" — a related but not identical epithet (re-read and confirmed: "rgyan pa" vs. "rnam rgyal," both built on the shared "gtsug tor" crown-protuberance image but not the same compound).

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-3-5`, `sungrab-tulku:c-7-3` (2 commentaries, two related but distinct epithets)

**Position 2 — "Deathless Life-Accomplishing Uṣṇīṣavijayā" (འཆི་མེད་ཚེ་སྒྲུབ་གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་པར་རྒྱལ་མ), a specialized longevity form:** three commentaries independently give this same specific epithet, consistently paired with the golden-body/utpala-and-life-vase iconography (see "Iconographic form," below) and a longevity practice (see "Deathless-life practice tradition," below).

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (3 commentaries)

**Position 3 — "Victorious in All Directions," an epithet built on the "ten directions" / "superior to the victors" reading:** Pema Namgyal's own claim opens with the same crown-jewel/uṣṇīṣa (gtsug tor) image used in Positions 1–2 (she is "the crown-jewel of the assembled tathāgatas... throughout the ten directions"), then adds a further consecration detail not found in Positions 1–2: because she is "consecrated as mistress of the boundless crown-ornament family," she conducts herself as superior even to the victors, hence the name.

— attested: `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-20` (1 commentary)

**Position 4 — the plain, unqualified name "Uṣṇīṣavijayā" (གཙུག་ཏོར་རྣམ་རྒྱལ་མ), without either the "adorned/crown-ornament" or "deathless-life" qualifier:** Tenzin Dhonzang's heading names her this way.

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-1` (1 commentary — also cited under "Structural placement," above, for the same claim's ordinal marker)

### Unique
None beyond the four positions above.

## Iconographic form

### Consensus
None shared by all contributing commentaries — see Divergences. Only two of the sixteen commentaries in this packet give iconographic detail for this homage.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two distinct iconographic traditions are attested:

**Position A — elaborate form (one face, four arms):** seated firmly in bodhisattva posture on a yellow lotus and moon disc; one face, four arms; right hands in supreme-boon-granting mudrā and holding a rosary, left hands holding a pill/vase and a staff. Confirmed by this commentary's own citation of a further verse ("golden-coloured, marked by the lotus... splendorous as a golden mountain, beautiful with four arms") attributed to "Nyima Sbaspa."

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-9`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-10`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-4-11` (1 commentary, 3 claims)

**Position B — simple longevity form (one face, two hands, golden body):** golden-coloured body holding a vase of deathless life, attested by all three commentaries below (Sangye Nyentrul and Palden Sherab further specify: seated half-cross-legged on lotus-and-moon, right hand in supreme-giving mudrā). Two of the three specify the vase as resting atop an utpala (Sangye Nyentrul, Palden Sherab); Tsultrim Namdak's own claim names only the golden colour and the vase of deathless life, with no utpala mentioned at all. Only Palden Sherab specifies the utpala's colour as blue. Sangye Nyentrul, Tsultrim Namdak, and Palden Sherab's own generation-stage visualization (cited fully under "Deathless-life practice tradition" and "Esoteric two-stage interpretation," below) independently converge on this same longevity iconography in its shared core (golden body, vase of deathless life), paired with the "Deathless Life-Accomplishing" epithet from "Naming and epithets," above.

— attested (golden body + vase of deathless life, all three): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` (3 commentaries, 4 claims)

— attested (utpala specifically): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` (2 commentaries)

— attested (blue utpala specifically): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1` (1 commentary)

No commentary in this corpus reconciles these two traditions or states that one supersedes the other; they appear to correspond to the two distinct epithets/functions recorded under "Naming and epithets" above (Position 1's general crown-ornament form vs. Position 2's longevity form).

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## Esoteric two-stage interpretation

### Consensus
None — each commentary that offers an esoteric ("hidden-meaning") reading develops its own distinct hermeneutic; no two independently converge on the same specific formulation for this verse (contrast "Identified as the Perfection of Wisdom," above, where two commentaries do converge closely).

### ⚑ Divergences
Not applicable — the two elaborations below do not contradict one another; they are simply independent, non-overlapping hermeneutical projects.

### Unique

**Palden Sherab's four-tier hermeneutic.** Beyond the literal word-meaning (cited under "Identification" and "Doctrinal," above), Palden Sherab gives three further explicitly-labelled tiers unique to this commentary in the corpus:
- Generation-stage visualization: golden Uṣṇīṣavijayā on lotus-and-moon, right hand supreme-giving, left holding a blue utpala topped by a vase of deathless life, from which light purifies saṃsāra/nirvāṇa's essence and grants the accomplishment of deathless life (also cited under "Iconographic form" and "Deathless-life practice tradition," above/below). `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1`
- Completion-stage-with-marks: the Tathāgata as the wisdom of the four joys, drawing great-bliss wisdom to the crown (= Uṣṇīṣavijayā); the ten winds, once purified in their respective places, become dharmakāya wisdom, the great mother of the victors and their heirs. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-3-1`
- Completion-stage-without-marks (the ultimate meaning): the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa as the peak of the nine vehicles, the view of Atiyoga; transference-prone winds dissolving like clouds into ground-liberation, established as the great dharmakāya — this, as the great unobstructed rainbow body, is identified with "the Tathāgata's uṣṇīṣa, Jetsün Ārya Tārā, of boundless victorious conduct." `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-4-1`

**Taranatha's hidden-meaning reading.** Distinct from Palden Sherab's tiers above, Taranatha gives its own "profound meaning" (ཟབ་དོན) analysis: the "ground of joy" of forward and reverse order (`taranatha:c-4-8`); through the elements and the assembly of winds dissolving into the uṣṇīṣa, she is victorious over the limitless objects to be relinquished (`taranatha:c-4-10`); the ten pāramitās are identified with the ten winds, once transformed (`taranatha:c-4-11`); the bodhisattva sons of the victors are identified with the sense-fields (e.g. the eye), likewise transformed into wisdom (`taranatha:c-4-12`); and, concluding, that training in this together with the previous (autumn-moon) visualization constitutes a branch of meditative absorption (`taranatha:c-4-13`).

## Deathless-life (longevity) practice tradition

### Consensus
Two commentaries share the base iconography — golden body, holding a vase of deathless life — but only one of them goes on to describe the nectar-dissolution practice itself. Sangye Nyentrul's own account gives the fuller sequence: the vase rests atop an utpala, and light/nectar radiates from it and dissolves into self and others, granting the accomplishment (siddhi) of deathless life. Tsultrim Namdak's claim (re-read to confirm) attests only the base iconography — golden body and the vase of deathless life, with no utpala, and no light/nectar/dissolution/siddhi content at all; its own additional content is instead that this figure is mother to the ten-pāramitā bodhisattva heirs, who she "infuses ... with the vital essence of merit and longevity," a related but distinct idea from Sangye Nyentrul's first-person generation-stage practice.

— attested (golden body + vase of deathless life, base iconography only): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-7` (2 commentaries)
— attested (the nectar-dissolution practice itself: light/nectar radiates and dissolves into self and others, granting siddhi): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-3` (1 commentary — `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-2` is iconographic detail only, no light/nectar/dissolution content of its own, so it is not counted toward this specific attestation)
— attested (utpala specifically): `sangye-nyentrul:c-5-1-2` (1 commentary)

Palden Sherab's own generation-stage visualization (cited fully under "Esoteric two-stage interpretation," above) independently gives a closely corresponding third attestation of the same golden-body / vase-of-deathless-life / nectar-dissolution practice — and, like Sangye Nyentrul, specifies the vase as resting atop an utpala, with Palden Sherab's claim alone specifying the utpala's colour as blue — differing only in its embedding within a four-tier hermeneutic rather than standing alone. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-4-2-1`

### ⚑ Divergences
None between the three commentaries above on the core practice; Tenzin Dhonzang's practice (below) shares the same "chief activity is extending life" framing but is not close enough in its specific imagery to be merged with theirs (per Rule 10), so it is recorded as a separate contribution to the same broader tradition rather than a fourth attestation of the identical practice.

### Unique
Tenzin Dhonzang states that this deity's chief enlightened activity is extending life, and gives its own distinct visualization: all one's sins and obscurations accumulated since beginningless time exit as soot and thick black smoke, and all illness exits as pus, blood, and lymph. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-9`

Tenzin Dhonzang further gives a visualization of subduing the lord of death (Yama) for longevity practice: obstructing spirits and impure forms exit and sink beneath the earth into the mouth of Yama, who — satisfied — closes his mouth and lies down; this is said to be supreme for accomplishing longevity. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-10`

## Unique comparanda and asides

Taranatha gives a short continuation of its vidyā-mantra identification (cited under "Identification of the object of homage," above): "virtuous one, having made it your own, you enjoy it," and "you definitively bestow the vidyā-mantra on limitless beings" — content about her own enjoyment and bestowal of the mantra, not found in any other commentary consulted for this topic. `taranatha:c-4-5`, `taranatha:c-4-6`

Taranatha separately gives an etymology of "Tathāgata" by citation: "as it is said, 'glorious one, gone to suchness, and likewise come again' — accordingly she is a Tathāgata (thus-gone-one)." `taranatha:c-4-9`

Tenzin Dhonzang independently gives its own, differently-sourced etymology of both "homage" and "Tathāgata" — homage purifies faults of faithlessness/disrespect toward the objects of refuge, and "Tathāgata" is explained via wisdom's equipoise with the suchness-expanse of emptiness — not close enough to Taranatha's citation-based etymology above to be treated as corroborating it (re-read and confirmed: different reasoning, different source). `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-3`

Tenzin Dhonzang alone brings in a citation from the Nāmasaṃgīti ("Recitation of Names"): "acts just as has been spoken," offered as a further etymological support. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-4`

Tenzin Dhonzang alone offers a comparison to sacred geography: Ushnishavijaya is described as the highest object of veneration, "just as the highest land of Jambudvīpa is Tibet, and within it the highest mountain-abode is Jomo Langma [Everest] and Kailash." `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-5`

Tenzin Dhonzang alone brings in a citation connecting this homage to a different deity, Sitatapatra (the White Parasol): "from the invisible crown-protrusion of the Sugata arose the White Parasol, mother of all victors." This page does not treat the cited verse as describing the same figure as this homage — it is offered by the commentary as a parallel/comparandum, not an identity claim. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-4-6`

Tenga Tulku's grammatical observation that the word "homage" (ཕྱག་འཚལ) functions as one of three structural markers (beginning/middle/end) across the verse, and here marks the beginning, is unique to this commentary in the corpus. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-3-2` (also cited under "Identification of the object of homage," above)
