# Tārā 2 — ཕྱག་འཚལ་སྟོན་ཀའི་ཟླ་བ་ཀུན་ཏུ། / Homage to Her Whose Face Is Like the Autumn Moon

## Structural placement

### Consensus
Six commentaries mark this passage with an explicit ordinal — "second" (or, equivalently, "(2)") — identifying it as the second of the homages, before proceeding to gloss the verse.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-1`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-1`, `taranatha:c-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-1` (6 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Three commentaries instead locate the verse inside a nested, multi-tier classificatory scheme rather than labelling it simply "second." Gendun Drub and Tenga Tulku give an almost word-for-word identical four-tier cascade — praise by enjoyment-body vs. truth-body forms → peaceful vs. wrathful forms → (within peaceful) six homages, of which this is the *first* — rather than treating it as the second homage of the praise as a whole; Lobsang Dawa's commentary opens the same node with the bare marker "the first" (དང་པོ་ནི།), consistent with the same nested reading (its node number, 1.2.2.1.1.1, matches Tenga Tulku's own node numbering exactly, suggesting a shared classificatory lineage).

- Position 1 (simple "second of 21"): the six commentaries listed under Consensus above.
- Position 2 (nested "first of six peaceful homages," within a four-tier enjoyment-body/truth-body → peaceful/wrathful cascade): `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-1`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-1`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`.

This is a difference in organizational taxonomy, not a doctrinal disagreement about the verse's content — but per Rule 4 it is recorded here rather than folded into the Consensus, since not every commentary agrees this is simply "the second homage."

### Unique
Konchok Thabkhe's structural announcement additionally characterizes the homage by content — "homage by way of ornament and light rays" (རྒྱན་དང་འོད་ཟེར་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) — rather than a bare ordinal alone. This same claim is also cited under Consensus above, for the shared "second" ordinal it states before adding this further content; both statements are genuinely supported by its text. `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-1`

## Root verse and core simile — "a hundred (full) autumn moons"

### Consensus
Seven commentaries quote the root verse's full four lines verbatim (or near-verbatim, subject to minor orthographic variation across transmissions): "ཕྱག་འཚལ་སྟོན་ཀའི་ཟླ་བ་ཀུན་ཏུ། གང་བ་བརྒྱ་ནི་བརྩེགས་པའི་ཞལ་མ། སྐར་མ་སྟོང་ཕྲག་ཚོགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས། རབ་ཏུ་ཕྱེ་བའི་འོད་རབ་འབར་མ།" — "Homage to her whose face is like a hundred full autumn moons stacked together, blazing with light that outshines the massed gathering of a thousand stars."

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-7` (7 commentaries)

Gendun Drub's own root-verse quotation (`gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-2`) gives only the first two lines — "ཕྱག་འཚལ་སྟོན་ཀའི་ཟླ་བ་ཀུན་ཏུ། །གང་བ་བརྒྱ་ནི་བརྩེགས་པའི་ཞལ་མ།" ("Homage to her whose face is like a hundred full autumn moons all gathered together") — not the full four lines; the "thousand stars"/"rab tu phye" half of the verse appears in this commentary only as paraphrase, at `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3` (see "Radiance surpassing a thousand stars," below). Per Rule 9 this is recorded as a partial attestation rather than folded into the full-verbatim count above.

Fifteen of the sixteen contributing commentaries (all but Tenga Tulku, whose claim on this specific point addresses the simile's *mechanism* rather than restating the numeral image — see the next facet) gloss this image as meaning that her face resembles a hundred full (autumn) moons piled or stacked together.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-10`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-16`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-5`, `taranatha:c-2-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-8` (15 commentaries)

Several of these additionally specify that the resulting face is supremely white and beautiful (e.g. Anon-Utpala's "supremely white and beautiful," Gendun Gyatso's "supremely white and beautiful," Sangye Nyentrul's "dazzlingly white and beautiful"), but since not every commentary in the list above makes that further evaluative addition explicit in this particular claim, it is described here in the body text rather than folded into the count above (per Rule 9).

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed at the level of the base simile itself — no commentary disputes that the verse compares her face to a hundred autumn moons. (For a divergence over how a specific word in the verse — "rab tu phye" — should be read, see "Word-gloss and textual-variant tension," below.)

### Unique
Gendun Gyatso's gloss states the multiplier as "brgya bcu pa" (བརྒྱ་བཅུ་པ), an unusual phrasing distinct from the more common "brgya" (a hundred) found elsewhere in the corpus; quoted here exactly as it appears in the packet, without silent correction. `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-1`

Drakpa Gyaltsen alone glosses the verse word-by-word rather than as a running paraphrase, separately noting that "full" (གང་བ) refers to the full moon — a narrower, sub-lexical point subsumed by the fuller image attested above. `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-9`

## Why autumn — freedom from seasonal obscuration

### Consensus
Sungrab Tulku and Tenga Tulku give an almost word-for-word identical explanation of why the *autumn* moon specifically is the point of comparison: in general the moon has radiance, but in summer it is obscured by cloud and mist, and in winter its brilliance is dimmed by smoke and dust; in autumn, being free of both kinds of obscuration, its radiance is clear — and the Venerable Lady's own face is said to be equally clear and radiant.

— attested: `sungrab-tulku:c-5-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3` (2 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — no commentary offers a competing explanation for why "autumn" is significant.

### Unique
Anon-Trinle-Char gives a related but distinct account: the autumn sky is described as free of "the three flaws" of dust, mist, and cloud (རྡུལ་སྨག་སྤྲིན་གྱི་སྐྱོན་བཅས་སྐྱོན་གསུམ), rather than the two-season (summer/winter) contrast used by Sungrab Tulku and Tenga Tulku, and this commentary adds a distinct claim about effect: seeing the goddess's face in this manner is said to cause faith to increase completely (དང་བ་ཡོངས་སུ་འཕེལ་ལོ). Because the specific framing differs from the two-commentary Consensus above (re-read and confirmed not to match it word-for-word), it is recorded separately here rather than folded in as a third attestation, per Rule 10. This claim is also cited under "Root verse and core simile" above, in the 15-commentary Consensus list, for the "hundred stacked moons" gloss it shares with the rest of the corpus; both statements are genuinely supported by its content. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-4`

## Radiance surpassing a thousand stars

### Consensus
Nine commentaries gloss the verse's second image as meaning that her light or radiance surpasses (outshines, or is more brilliant than) a massed gathering of a thousand stars.

— attested: `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-11`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-6`, `taranatha:c-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4` (9 commentaries)

> Note on reuse: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3` is also cited under "Root verse and core simile" above (its "hundred stacked moons" gloss) and under "Word-gloss and textual-variant tension" below (Tension 1, Position A — the same "surpasses" reading bears directly on that divergence). `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4` and `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6` are likewise cited again in the Unique paragraphs immediately below (for content beyond the shared "surpasses" statement) and, for `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`, again under "Word-gloss and textual-variant tension" below; `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6` is further cited under "Naming and identity" below. Each claim genuinely and fully supports every statement it is cited for; the reuse is deliberate and retained after review, per the pattern established under "Iconographic form" above.

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed on the base image itself. See "Word-gloss and textual-variant tension," below, for two further divergences that bear on several of the claims cited above: (a) whether "a hundred"/"a thousand stars" might instead denote teeth or bodily marks, and (b) whether "rab tu phye" means her light surpasses the stars (the reading most of the Consensus claims above attest) or that she is adorned by them (an opposing reading attested elsewhere in the corpus).

### Unique
Tenga Tulku's gloss adds a specific reason the comparison is apt: when a thousand stars gather, their combined light becomes too dazzling for the eye to bear, and the Venerable Lady's radiance is said to be equally unbearable to look upon directly. `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`

Lobsang Dawa's gloss on this image simultaneously supplies an epithet drawn directly from the verse's own closing words — "Blazing Radiance" ('od rab 'bar ma) — rather than a name drawn from an outside naming tradition; see "Naming and identity," below. `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`

## Word-gloss and textual-variant tension on "rab tu phye"

### Consensus
None — while a majority of commentaries independently converge on one reading of "rab tu phye" (Position A under Tension 1, below), the word's sense is not uncontested across the corpus: at least one commentary reads it the opposite way, and one commentary explicitly criticizes the majority reading as a fault. Per Rule 4 this is recorded as a divergence, not flattened into a consensus.

### ⚑ Divergences

**Tension 1 — does "rab tu phye" mean her light surpasses/outshines the stars, or that she is adorned by them?**

This is a genuine divergence independently attested across the corpus, not merely a criticism with no identifiable target. The Position A claims below are already cited above under "Radiance surpassing a thousand stars"; they are cited a second time here for the word-sense tension itself, cross-referenced rather than restated in full.

**Position A — her light surpasses/outshines the stars, radiating from her face:**

— attested: `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-11`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-3`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`, `taranatha:c-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4` (6 commentaries)

**Position B — she is adorned by the stars, rather than her light surpassing them:** Gendun Gyatso's gloss reads, "blazing with intensely white light, as if adorned by a thousand stars fully opened [around her] in reverence" — an "adorned by" framing, the opposite pole from Position A.

— attested: `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-2` (1 commentary)

Anon-Trinle-Char argues, against an unnamed group it calls "some commentators" (འགྲེལ་མཛད་མཁན་དག), that "rab tu phye" should not be read as meaning that star-like rays of light literally emanate from the goddess's face or body, surpassing even starlight — calling this reading a fault, since starlight is itself an example of dim, unclear light and so becomes, in this commentary's view, an "ornament of denigration" if applied to the goddess. Anon-Trinle-Char's own claim names no specific commentator it is criticizing. This page observes — as its own inference, not a statement made by Anon-Trinle-Char itself (per Rule 13) — that the reading it criticizes is exactly Position A above, independently attested by six other commentaries in this corpus, and that the "adorned by" alternative it implicitly favors over that fault is exactly Position B's framing.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-5` (1 commentary)

Anon-Trinle-Char's own preferred reading — "rab tu phye" means "made vividly manifest to view," i.e. the moon's radiance is brought vividly before the eyes, adorned at its edge by the cluster of stars — shares Position B's "adorned" framing in substance, though its exact wording is not identical to Gendun Gyatso's gloss, so it is recorded as its own position rather than folded into Position B.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-6` (1 commentary)

**Tension 2 — Gendun Drub's internal textual-variant tension ("a hundred stacked" vs. "sixteen teeth, thirty-two marks").**

Separately, Gendun Drub's own commentary records an internal textual-variant tension between two readings of the verse's third and fourth lines, marked ⚑ in the source claims file itself:

- Position 1 ("a hundred stacked," the ordinary reading followed elsewhere in this commentary and across the corpus): her face's light outshines even a stack of a hundred moons.
- Position 2 (alternate textual variant, "brgyad gnyis pa sgrigs," read as "the two eights arranged"): her sixteen moon-like teeth (eight pairs), arranged in her mouth, outshine a thousand gathered stars, with "the two eights arranged" further read as indicating her thirty-two auspicious major marks.

— both positions recorded within the single internal-tension claim `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-4`.

### Unique
Tsultrim Namdak's gloss separately compares her teeth to "a thousand stars ... arranged like a garland," opening as her face opens (rab tu phye) in what reads as a smile — a related but not identical image to Gendun Drub's teeth/thirty-two-marks textual variant above. Re-reading both claims confirms they do not share the same specific content (Gendun Drub's is explicitly a textual-variant argument about an alternate wording of the verse itself; Tsultrim Namdak's is a smile/teeth image within its own paraphrase, without invoking a variant reading of the verse's wording), so this page records them separately rather than treating one as corroborating the other, per Rule 10. `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-9`

## Naming and identity of this Tārā

### Consensus
None at the level of a single shared name — see Divergences. All contributing commentaries agree only that the homage is addressed to Tārā generically (see also Facet "Structural placement").

### ⚑ Divergences
At least four distinct naming traditions for this particular Tārā are attested across the corpus, and they do not agree:

**Position 1 — "White Lady, Radiant One" (དཀར་མོ་མདངས་ལྡན་མ):** Anon-Trinle-Char reports that "Rje Dge 'dun grub" (Gendun Drub) names her this way, and this is independently confirmed by Gendun Drub's own commentary in this corpus, which self-identifies: "འདི་ལ་དཀར་མོ་མདངས་ལྡན་སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞེས་བཤད་དོ།" — "She is explained here as White Tārā, Radiant." Both claims were re-read to confirm this genuine, independent cross-attestation.

— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-7`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-5` (2 commentaries)

**Position 2 — "Dbyangs-can-ma" / Sarasvatī (Melodious One), often "Blo-gter Dbyangs-can-ma," Treasure-of-Intellect Sarasvatī:** attested independently by four commentaries.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-0-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5` (4 commentaries)

Of these, two use the fuller epithet "Blo-gter Dbyangs-can-ma" (བློ་གཏེར་དབྱངས་ཅན་མ) exactly, while Sungrab Tulku's claim uses the bare form "Dbyangs-can-ma"/"Yangchenma" without the "Blo-gter" qualifier.

— attested (fuller epithet, exact "...ཅན་མ" form): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-0-1`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5` (2 commentaries)

Sangye Nyentrul separately uses a near-identical epithet — "སྒྲོལ་མ་བློ་གཏེར་དབྱངས་ཅན" (Blo-gter Dbyangs-can), missing the final "-མ" syllable found in Palden Sherab's and Tsultrim Namdak's exact form above — in a distinct claim cited in "Function, benefits, and practice" below; not quoted here as identical to their spelling. `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-1`

**Position 3 — "Great Peace" (ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ, Zhi-ba-chen-mo):** Tenzin Dhonzang's heading names her this way, sharply diverging from both Position 1 and Position 2.

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-1` (1 commentary)

**Position 4 — names drawn directly from the verse's own closing epithet, "Blazing Radiance" ('od rab 'bar ma), rather than from an external naming tradition:**

— attested: `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6` (1 commentary)

### Unique
Within the same claim that attests Position 1 above, Anon-Trinle-Char also reports two further names from named masters outside this corpus's sixteen commentaries — "Venerable Grags-pa bshad-sgrub" calling her "Tārā of the Great Inquiry" (སྒྲོལ་མ་ཞུ་བ་ཆེན་མོ), and "Nyima Sbaspa" calling her "Noble Tārā, white like the autumn moon" — and on this basis criticizes the common thangka/painting practice of depicting her body colour as dark maroon-red, arguing it contradicts these masters' statements and the tantra itself. Because these two further names are third-party attributions reported within a single claim rather than independently attested by those authors' own commentaries in this corpus, they are recorded here as part of Anon-Trinle-Char's own naming survey rather than as separate corpus-wide positions. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-7`

## Iconographic form: body colour, faces, hands, implements

### Consensus
Four commentaries describe her body or complexion as white.

— attested: `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`, `taranatha:c-2-5`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5` (4 commentaries)

> Note on the claims that also appear under Position A of the Divergence below: `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2` and `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1` each genuinely and fully support both statements — "her body is white" (this Consensus) and "her visualized form has one face and two hands holding an utpala-and-mirror" (Divergence Position A) — since both facts appear together in the same claim text. `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5` supports only the Consensus statement here (body colour) plus the utpala-and-mirror detail; its own claim gives no face/hand count or mudrā, so it is cited separately under Position A rather than folded into the same full-statement group (see the note there). This is not a case of a claim being used on two sides of one disagreement (Rule 11); it is claims supporting two different, non-conflicting statements within the same facet, so the reuse is deliberate and is retained after review.

### ⚑ Divergences
Two distinct iconographic traditions are attested for this Tārā's visualized form:

- **Position A — simple form (one face, two hands):** seated half-cross-legged / archer's posture on a lotus-and-moon seat, right hand in the supreme-giving mudrā, left hand holding an utpala topped by a mirror marked with a seed-syllable. `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2` (seed-syllable HRIH), `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1` (seed-syllable HŪṂ — note this specific variant, not silently harmonized with the others' HRIH). Tsultrim Namdak attests only the body colour and the utpala-with-mirror-marked-HRĪḤ detail of this form, not the seated posture or mudrā — its own claim states no more than that — so per Rule 9 it is cited separately rather than folded into the full-statement list above: `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5` (white body; utpala topped by a mirror marked HRĪḤ; seed-syllable HRĪḤ).
- **Position B — elaborate form (three faces, twelve arms):** root face white, right face blue, left face yellow; two hands in equipoise, with the remaining ten hands (six right, six left) holding — right: khaṭvāṅga, wheel, jewel, vajra, flower garland; left: a vase of medicinal nectar, blue lotus (utpala), bell, vase, and a scriptural volume. This description is attributed by the commentary itself to a verse of "Nyima Sbaspa." `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-8`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-9`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-10`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-11` (1 commentary, 4 claims)

No commentary in this corpus reconciles these two traditions or states that one supersedes the other; Position B is Anon-Trinle-Char's own report of an alternate iconography rather than its own preferred description (compare its rejection, in the same commentary, of thangka depictions in maroon-red under "Naming and identity" above).

### Unique
None beyond the two positions above.

## Esoteric two-stage (generation-stage / completion-stage) interpretation

### Consensus
Taranatha and Palden Sherab independently give a corresponding "hidden meaning" (སྦས་དོན) reading of the verse's two images, keyed to the completion-stage of practice: the "hundred (autumn-moon-like) stacked" image is read as bodhicitta stacking, without leakage, through the body's channels between the crown and the jewel-tip (crown-to-jewel and jewel-to-crown); the "thousand stars" image is read as the pervasion of all the subtle channels. Palden Sherab's own claim further specifies this pervasion as being by the co-emergent wisdom of bliss-emptiness, moving through four cakras rather than two channel-endpoints; Taranatha's own claim states the pervasion-of-channels image without that bliss-emptiness qualifier or the four-cakra detail — per Rule 9 the "bliss-emptiness" wording is not attributed to Taranatha.

— attested (bodhicitta-stacking / channel-pervasion structure): `taranatha:c-2-6`, `taranatha:c-2-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-3-1` (2 commentaries)
— attested (co-emergent bliss-emptiness wisdom specifically, and the four-cakra elaboration): `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-3-1` only

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed between the two commentaries that offer this reading — their formulations correspond closely and were re-read to confirm the correspondence is genuine (not merely superficial similarity of vocabulary).

### Unique
Palden Sherab alone extends the two-stage reading further, giving:
- a generation-stage visualization identifying the deity as white Dorje Yangchenma (Vajra Sarasvatī) at the heart of Yeshe Tsogyal, with the iconographic details recorded under "Iconographic form" above; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`
- a fourth, "completion-stage-without-marks" ultimate-meaning reading, in which the verse's imagery is identified with the direct realization of luminosity, described as "the very face of White Vajravārāhī, like stacked autumn moons" — a distinct deity-identification from the "Dorje Yangchenma"/Sarasvatī identification used at the generation-stage level within the same commentary, reflecting a deliberately tiered (outer/generation-stage/completion-stage/ultimate) hermeneutic rather than a single fixed identity; `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-4-1`
- a concluding statement that, relying on unwavering devotion and the guru's blessing, effortless stainless wisdom — likened again to "a mass of a thousand stars" — masters the four correct discernments and eight great treasuries of confidence. `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-4-2`

No other commentary in this corpus extends the reading to this fourth, "ultimate" tier.

## Function, benefits, and practice

### Consensus
Three commentaries state that light from this form of the goddess (in two cases, specifically light radiating from the mirror held in her hand) dispels ignorance or "the darkness of unknowing."

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-4` (3 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Commentaries do not agree on which fourfold wisdom-scheme her light or intelligence is said to embody:

**Position A — "the four correct discernments" (སོ་སོ་ཡང་དག་པའི་རིག་པ་བཞི: of meaning, of dharma, of definitive words, and of confidence):** Palden Sherab enumerates all four explicitly; Sangye Nyentrul names the same fourfold category without individually itemizing its four members.

— attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-1-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-2` (2 commentaries)

**Position B — "four kinds of wisdom" (vast, clear, swift, and profound wisdom — a different fourfold enumeration from Position A):**

— attested: `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-5` (1 commentary)

### Unique
- Sangye Nyentrul: this form of Tārā, epithetically "Blo-gter Dbyangs-can" (Treasury-of-Wisdom Melodious One), is said to increase the power of insight and expand intelligence. `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-1`
- Pema Namgyal: visualizing her radiant nectar-light dissolving into oneself, filling and stabilizing the body, is said to grant the accomplishments (siddhis) of longevity and wisdom. `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-17`
- Tenzin Dhonzang: a distinct ransom-practice tradition, unattested elsewhere in this corpus — one's downfalls, obscurations, and habitual imprints, together with a ransom-effigy of the guru and a ransom for the life-force of oneself and all sentient beings, are offered into the mouth of the lord of death, satisfying him and settling all karmic debts and harm from obstructing spirits. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-6`
- Tenzin Dhonzang: love, compassion, and bodhicitta, free of harmful intent, are said to constitute the supreme ransom against death; this goddess's chief enlightened activity is described as destroying the harm of obstructing spirits and elemental beings across the three realms, together with bodily illness and mental suffering. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-7`
- Tenzin Dhonzang: brings in a comparative citation from outside this text — a verse from the "Legdrima" praise describing the beauty of Khadiravani Tārā (a distinct tantric form of Tārā, not this text's second homage) — as an external point of comparison for beauty; this page does not treat the quoted verse as describing the same figure as this homage. `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-8`
