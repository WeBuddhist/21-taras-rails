## Name / Epithet

### Consensus
Every commentary treats the homage as addressed to Tārā (སྒྲོལ་མ) herself; the root verse supplies no fixed proper epithet of its own for this homage (unlike, e.g., "Svift Heroine" for Tārā 1), so the great majority of commentaries identify her only generically through the verse's own imagery rather than by a distinct name.
— attested (generic identification only): `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4` (also unnamed in `anon-utpala`, `drakpa-gyaltsen`, `lobsang-dawa` [beyond its unique closing-phrase reading, see Unique below], `pema-namgyal`, `taranatha`)

### ⚑ Divergences
Where commentaries do supply a distinct proper name for this homage, at least four separate naming traditions are attested, and they do not agree with one another:

- **"White Lady, Radiant One" (དཀར་མོ་མདངས་ལྡན་མ, dkar mo mdangs ldan ma)** — the title used throughout anon-trinle-char's own node heading, and there explicitly attributed also to Rje Dge 'dun grub — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-1`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-7`. Independently, gendun-drub glosses her simply as "White Tārā, Radiant" — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-5`.
- **"Treasure-of-Intellect, Melodious One" / Vajra Sarasvatī (བློ་གཏེར་དབྱངས་ཅན་མ / རྡོ་རྗེ་དབྱངས་ཅན་མ)** — palden-sherab's node title and its own identification of her as "goddess Vajra Sarasvatī" radiating the four correct discernments — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-0-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-1-2`. Sangye-nyentrul independently gives the same name, both in full ("Dbyangs-can-ma") and by activity ("Treasury-of-Wisdom Melodious One") — `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-2`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-1`. Tsultrim-namdak's second, independent commentary within the same anthology gives the identical name — `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5`. Sungrab-tulku uses the shorter form "Tārā Yangchenma / Melodious One," without the "Treasure-of-Intellect" qualifier the other three attach — `sungrab-tulku:c-5-3`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-6`.
- **"Great Peace" (ཞི་བ་ཆེན་མོ, zhi ba chen mo)** — tenzin-dhonzang alone — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-1`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3`. This commentary's own structural notes caution that it reuses this identical epithet for a different homage (Tārā 15), so even within this one commentary's naming scheme the title is not unique to this verse.
- **"Tārā of the Great Inquiry" (ཞུ་བ་ཆེན་མོ)** — attributed by anon-trinle-char to Rje btsun Grags pa bshad sgrub, alongside its report of Gedun Drub's and Nyima Sepa's own namings above — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-7`.

### Unique
- Konchok-thabkhe supplies no personal epithet at all, instead titling the whole homage thematically as "homage by way of ornament and light rays" (རྒྱན་དང་འོད་ཟེར་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ) — `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-1`.
- Lobsang-dawa alone treats the root verse's own closing phrase, འོད་རབ་འབར་མ ("Blazing Radiance"), as the operative descriptive title for this homage (type: etymology), rather than supplying an independently-coined name — `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`.

## Colour

### Consensus
Her face/body is white (དཀར་པོ) — attested widely and explicitly: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-8`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-8`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-5`, `taranatha:c-2-5`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-16`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-8` (13 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Anon-trinle-char explicitly records a divergence between this textual/scholarly consensus and attested artistic practice: it states that Gedun Drub, Drakpa Shedrub, and Nyima Sepa all describe her as white ("white like the autumn moon"), and on that basis states that the common thangka/painting convention of depicting this figure's body colour as dark maroon-red (སྐུ་མདོག་དམར་སྨུག) "contradicts these learned masters' statements and is also inconsistent with this tantra" — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-7`.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Face — the Hundred-Autumn-Moons Simile

### Consensus
ཞལ་སྟོན་ཀའི་ཟླ་བ་ཀུན་ཏུ་གང་བ་བརྒྱ་ནི་བརྩེགས་པ — "a face like a hundred full autumn moons stacked/piled together" — the single most universally attested image in the corpus for this homage, given by all sixteen commentaries.
— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-9`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-10`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-2`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-1`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-2`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-2`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-5`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-0-2`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-1-1`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-16`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-1`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-5`, `taranatha:c-2-3`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-2`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-2`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-3`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-7`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-8` (16/16 commentaries)

A distinctive shared elaboration recurs, in near-identical wording, in exactly two commentaries: the moon is normally obscured — by cloud and mist in summer, by smoke and dust in winter — but in autumn, free of both, its brilliance is clear; hence the simile — `sungrab-tulku:c-5-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-3`. Anon-trinle-char and gendun-gyatso independently give the same three-flaws framing (dust, mist/haze, cloud) — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-4`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-1`.

### ⚑ Divergences
Gendun-drub reports a textual variant on the verse's own wording: rather than "a hundred [moons], stacked" (བརྒྱ་ནི་བརྩེགས་པ), an alternative reading has "eight-two arranged" (བརྒྱད་གཉིས་སྒྲིགས), understood as her sixteen moon-like teeth (eight pairs) arranged in her mouth, indicating her thirty-two auspicious major marks — a reading about her *teeth*, not a repeated-moons image at all — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-4`. See also the related divergence on རབ་ཏུ་ཕྱེ་ under Light / Radiance below, where tsultrim-namdak independently reads the same portion of the verse as being about her teeth.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Light / Radiance — the Thousand-Stars Simile and the Reading of རབ་ཏུ་ཕྱེ་

### Consensus
སྐར་མ་སྟོང་ཕྲག་ཚོགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱིས་རབ་ཏུ་ཕྱེ་བའི་འོད་རབ་འབར་མ — "blazing with light that thoroughly outshines/surpasses the massed gathering of a thousand stars" — likewise attested by all sixteen commentaries.
— attested: `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-3`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-6`, `anon-utpala:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`, `drakpa-gyaltsen:c-1-11`, `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-3`, `gendun-gyatso:c-1-2-2`, `karma-maitri:c-1-1-2-3`, `konchok-thabkhe:c-2-2-3`, `lobsang-dawa:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-6`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-1-2`, `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-17`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-0-2`, `sungrab-tulku:c-5-6`, `taranatha:c-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-4`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-9` (16/16 commentaries)

### ⚑ Divergences
Three distinct readings of the verb རབ་ཏུ་ཕྱེ (lit. "thoroughly opened") are attested:

1. **Majority reading — light thoroughly outshines/surpasses the stars.** Most commentaries take the phrase at face value: her light is made "vividly manifest to view," surpassing starlight in brilliance — e.g. `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-6` ("made vividly manifest to view … adorned at its edge by the cluster of stars"), `taranatha:c-2-4`, `tenga-tulku:c-1-2-2-1-1-1-4` ("too dazzling for the eye to bear").
2. **Teeth reading.** Tsultrim-namdak reads the same word as referring to her teeth (ཚེམས) — "opened wide by teeth gathered together like a thousand stars, arranged like a garland" — `tsultrim-namdak:c-2-1-2-1-9`. This parallels gendun-drub's report (above, under Face) of an alternate textual reading of the verse's first half as sixteen moon-like teeth — `gendun-drub:c-2-2-2-2-1-1-1-4`.
3. **Anon-trinle-char's internal critique.** Anon-trinle-char explicitly rejects two other sub-readings it attributes to unnamed "some commentators": that "rab tu phye" means light "surpassing" (lhag pa'i don) the stars, or that it means "adorned by" (brgyan pa'i don) the stars, arguing both are faults because comparing the goddess's face to dim starlight is inadvertently denigrating; it proposes instead that the phrase means her light is "made vividly manifest to view" — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-5`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-6`.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Implements, Mudrā, and Hand-Symbols

### Consensus
None — the commentaries that describe her hands at all split into two incompatible iconographic schemes (see Divergences). Nine of sixteen commentaries give no hand-implement detail whatsoever for this homage, treating it purely as praise of her face and light.

### ⚑ Divergences
- **Complex sādhana-form: three faces, twelve arms.** Anon-trinle-char gives an elaborate iconography, drawn explicitly on a verse it quotes from Nyima Sepa's sādhana: three faces — white (root), blue (right), yellow (left) — and twelve arms; the first two hands rest in equipoise; right hands (2nd–6th) hold a khaṭvāṅga, wheel, jewel, vajra, and flower garland; left hands (2nd–6th) hold a pill/vase [of medicinal nectar], blue lotus (utpala), bell, vase, and a scriptural volume — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-8`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-9`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-10`, `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-11`.
- **Simple generation-stage form: one face, two hands.** Palden-sherab, sangye-nyentrul, and tsultrim-namdak instead each describe a simple peaceful visualization: one face, two hands; the right in the supreme-giving mudrā (མཆོག་སྦྱིན་ཕྱག་རྒྱ); the left holding a blue lotus (utpala) topped by a mirror marked with a seed-syllable — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5`. Within this second scheme, the seed-syllable itself diverges: palden-sherab specifies HŪṂ — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1` — while sangye-nyentrul and tsultrim-namdak specify HRĪḤ — `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5`.

### Unique
- Anon-trinle-char is the only commentary in this corpus to give a full three-face/twelve-arm iconography for this homage — `anon-trinle-char:c-3-2-8` through `c-3-2-11`.
- Palden-sherab alone glosses the left-hand implement as symbolizing the Three Jewels (དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་མཚོན) — a reading not given by sangye-nyentrul or tsultrim-namdak for the same implement — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`.

## Stance / Posture and Seat

Only two of sixteen commentaries describe a stance or seat for this homage at all; the remaining fourteen are silent on this specific facet.

### Consensus
Both commentaries that address a seat agree she sits upon a lotus-and-moon disc (པདྨ་ཟླ་བའི་གདན) — attested: `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`.

### ⚑ Divergences
The two commentaries give different postures on that same seat:
- **Archer's posture** (རྟ་འབབས་སྐྱིལ་མོ་ཀྲུང), both legs — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`.
- **Half-cross-legged / half-vajra posture** (ཞབས་ཕྱེད་སྐྱིལ) — `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

### Consensus
None — this facet is carried by a single unique attestation.

None observed.

### Unique
Tenzin-dhonzang quotes a separate praise, the "Legdrima" (ལེགས་བྲི་མ), giving a wholly different set of physical descriptors for this figure: full-breasted, "a treasury of undefiled bliss," her face "a full moon with a white smile," possessed of a peaceful demeanor, compassionate and wide-eyed, "beauty of the Khadira forest" — none of these descriptors (breast, smile, eyes, forest epithet) appear in any other commentary's treatment of this homage — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-8`.

## Activity / Deed

### Consensus
Her characteristic activity is bestowing or increasing wisdom/insight — attested independently by four commentaries: sangye-nyentrul names her directly "Treasury-of-Wisdom Melodious One" who "increases the power of insight and expands intelligence" (`sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-1`); its own visualization-sequence claim adds that her light "dispels beings' ignorance" and "opens the treasury of wisdom" (`sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-3`); tsultrim-namdak's independent commentary gives the same activity — her intellect's radiant light "dispels the practitioner's darkness of unknowing and opens the door of knowable things" (`tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5`); pema-namgyal states that visualizing her light dissolving into oneself "grants the accomplishments of longevity and wisdom" (`pema-namgyal:c-2-4-17`); and palden-sherab's practice instruction directs the meditator to recite her mantra so as to draw on "her who opens the treasury of wisdom" (`palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-2`). Tenzin-dhonzang partially agrees, describing her white-light form's nature as vast, clear, swift, and profound wisdom that aids skill in debate, exposition, and composition (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-5`).

### ⚑ Divergences
Tenzin-dhonzang alone states that this goddess's *chief* enlightened activity (འཕྲིན་ལས་ཀྱི་གཙོ་བོ) is not wisdom-bestowal but protection: "supreme in destroying the harm of obstructing spirits and elemental beings of the upper, lower, and middle realms, and bodily illness and mental suffering" — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-7`, supported by its own preceding ransom-ritual practice instruction against death and karmic debt (`tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-6`). This does not flatly contradict the same commentary's own wisdom-quality description (`c-4-2-5`) — both appear side by side in tenzin-dhonzang — but it does diverge from every other commentary's exclusive emphasis on wisdom/insight as this Tārā's defining activity, and no other commentary attributes a protective "chief activity" to this specific homage.

### Unique
None beyond the above.

## Doctrinal (Hidden / Ultimate) Meaning

### Consensus
Taranatha and palden-sherab independently give the same completion-stage, subtle-body correlation for this verse's two similes: the autumn-moon face is bodhicitta stacking/travelling without leakage through the central-channel cakras between the jewel[-tip] and the crown of the head; the thousand-stars light is the pervading of all the minor channels with co-emergent bliss-emptiness wisdom.
— attested: `taranatha:c-2-6`, `taranatha:c-2-7`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-3-1`

### ⚑ Divergences
None observed — the two commentaries' hidden-meaning readings agree closely.

### Unique
Palden-sherab alone extends the schema to a fourth, "ultimate meaning" (མཐར་ཐུག) layer, identifying the direct realization of luminosity reached through this practice with the very face of White Vajravārāhī (རྡོ་རྗེ་ཕག་མོ་དཀར་མོ), and states that stainless wisdom "like a mass of a thousand stars" thereby masters the four correct discernments and the eight great treasuries of confidence — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-4-1`, `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-4-2`. No other commentary in this corpus offers a fourth interpretive layer for this homage.

## Associated Mantra Element

### Consensus
No commentary in this corpus ties Tārā 2 to a specific syllable of the root twenty-one-fold mantra (oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā) the way some other homages are correlated to individual mantra syllables elsewhere in this text. **No commentary addresses this facet as such.**

The only mantra-adjacent material found for this homage is the seed-syllable (ས་བོན) marking the mirror held in her left hand in the "simple form" iconography already recorded under Implements, Mudrā, and Hand-Symbols above (HŪṂ per palden-sherab, HRĪḤ per sangye-nyentrul and tsultrim-namdak) — `palden-sherab:c-3-1-2-2-1`, `sangye-nyentrul:c-3-1-2`, `tsultrim-namdak:c-3-5`. This is a hand-implement/visualization detail rather than a mantra-syllable correlation with the recited praise itself, so it is not treated as an answer to this facet's question.

### ⚑ Divergences
Not applicable — no commentary makes a claim here to diverge from.

### Unique
None.

## Verse-Specific Benefit (if any)

Distinct from the text's general closing benefits section (out of scope for this topic page), only two commentaries attribute a benefit specifically to this homage's own verse or its visualization.

### Consensus
None — the two attested benefits are of different kinds and are each attested by only one commentary.

### ⚑ Divergences
Not a strict contradiction, but two different benefit-emphases are attested for the same verse, each by a single commentary:
- **Longevity and wisdom siddhis.** Pema-namgyal states that visualizing her radiant nectar-light dissolving into and filling/stabilizing one's body "grants the accomplishments of longevity and wisdom" (ཚེ་དང་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱི་དངོས་གྲུབ) — `pema-namgyal:c-2-4-17`.
- **Protection from death, spirits, and illness.** Tenzin-dhonzang's ransom-ritual instruction states that meditating on this deity while offering a ransom-effigy satisfies the "lord of death," obstructing spirits, and outstanding karmic debts, becoming "the supreme ransom against death," and that her chief activity is destroying harm from spirits/elemental beings and bodily/mental suffering — `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-6`, `tenzin-dhonzang:c-4-2-7`.

(The wisdom/insight-increase benefit attested by sangye-nyentrul and tsultrim-namdak is recorded under Activity / Deed above rather than repeated here, since in both of those commentaries it is framed as this Tārā's inherent activity rather than as a stated fruit of reciting or visualizing this specific verse.)

### Unique
None beyond the above two positions.
